What is a VDS, and how is it different from a VPS?

A Virtual Dedicated Server is a virtual machine with guaranteed CPU cores. A VPS shares physical cores with other tenants, so under load your available CPU drops and busy neighbours show up as slow queries or stuttering services. A VDS reserves the cores for your server, and the only thing still virtualized is the hardware layer itself.

MoradoSolution VDS plans run on AMD EPYC 7C13 processors with DDR4 ECC memory and NVMe Gen 4 storage inside our own Delhi data centre, with full root access, unmetered bandwidth and one dedicated IPv4 address included. The VDS, VPS and bare metal comparison covers when each one is the right choice.

Common questions.
What is the difference between VDS and Bare Metal?

VDS (Virtual Dedicated Server) gives you guaranteed CPU cores with KVM virtualization. Bare Metal gives you the entire physical machine with no virtualization overhead. Choose VDS for flexibility, Bare Metal for maximum performance.

Can I upgrade from VDS to Bare Metal?

Yes. You can migrate between plans or upgrade to bare metal anytime. Contact support to migrate.

What operating systems are supported?

Ubuntu, Debian, AlmaLinux, Rocky Linux, Fedora, CentOS, and custom ISOs. Select during provisioning or reinstall from the panel.

Do you offer managed services?

Currently we offer unmanaged servers with full root access. Managed services and custom configurations are available on request.

What is the network speed?

All servers include unmetered bandwidth on 10 Gbps uplinks with Tier-1 Indian upstream providers.

Where are the servers located?

Our primary datacenter is in Delhi, India. Low latency to Mumbai, Bangalore, Hyderabad and all major Indian cities.
