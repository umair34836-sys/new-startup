Scope: Pakistani/Indian wedding events (mehndi, dholki, mayun/haldi, baraat, nikah, rukhsati, walima, sangeet, pheras, Anand Karaj), teasers and same-day edits, qawwali/folk/classical music use, Urdu (Nastaliq), Hindi (Devanagari) and Punjabi (Gurmukhi) on-screen text, cultural colours and motifs, Eid/Ramadan/national days, Bollywood/filmi conventions, PK/IN short-video platforms and local-shop reels.
Researched 2026-10-24 via agent-reach (Exa) web search, official docs and local tests. ## Rendering Urdu, Hindi and Punjabi text on this box

5121. **The `subtitles` filter breaks Urdu and Hindi here; the `ass` filter doesn't** — on this box's ffmpeg 6.1 the same .ass file drew Noto Nastaliq Urdu as boxes and broke Devanagari conjuncts (सिर्फ → सरि्फ, श्री → शरी) through `subtitles=`, but rendered both correctly through `ass=` (our 6.1 `subtitles` has no shaping option; complex shaping is "required for correct rendering of complex scripts such as Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari"). Complements skill 2836 (Nastaliq needs complex shaping). **How:** burn Urdu/Hindi captions with `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "ass=caps.ass:fontsdir=library/fonts:shaping=complex" out.mp4`, never `subtitles=` (tested here 2026-10-24, T32/T33 vs T34) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#ass, checked 2026-10-24]
5122. **drawtext cannot draw Nastaliq** — drawtext's `text_shaping` (needs libfribidi) only reverses RTL order and joins Arabic letters; with Noto Nastaliq Urdu, Gulzar, Lateef and Scheherazade New it printed empty boxes or nothing, Mirza half-broken. Those fonts map almost no Arabic Presentation Forms (Noto Nastaliq Urdu 0 of U+FE70-FEFF), which the fribidi path needs. **How:** don't use drawtext for Urdu Nastaliq; render the line in Chrome/HyperFrames or PIL+Raqm and overlay the PNG (tested here 2026-10-24, T25 + fontTools count) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawtext, checked 2026-10-24]
5123. **drawtext also breaks Hindi conjuncts and the i-matra** — `drawtext` with Mukta drew "सिर्फ ₹499" as "सरि्फ ₹499" (pre-base ि in the wrong place, half-form not joined), though plain "शादी मुबारक" looked fine. **How:** for any Hindi with ि, reph or conjuncts (क्ष, श्री) use the `ass` filter (5121) or a Chrome/PIL PNG; keep drawtext for Latin/Roman Urdu (tested here 2026-10-24, T07) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawtext, checked 2026-10-24]
5124. **If drawtext must draw Urdu, use Noto Naskh Arabic or Amiri** — these two Naskh fonts include the presentation forms (141 and 140 of U+FE70-FEFF) and drew "شادی مبارک" joined and in the right order through drawtext. **How:** `drawtext=fontfile=NotoNaskhArabic.ttf:textfile=ur.txt:fontsize=48:borderw=3:x=(w-tw)/2:y=h-90` (UTF-8 text in a file avoids shell quoting) (tested here 2026-10-24, T04) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawtext, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5125. **Pre-check a font for the ffmpeg drawtext path** — count its Presentation Forms-B code points; near zero means drawtext will show boxes. **How:** `python3 -c "from fontTools.ttLib import TTFont;cm=TTFont('f.ttf').getBestCmap();print(sum(1 for u in cm if 0xFE70<=u<=0xFEFF))"` (fontTools 4.67 pip-installed, small) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5126. **HyperFrames/Chrome renders Nastaliq and Hindi correctly** — the repo's headless Chrome (HyperFrames) shaped Noto Nastaliq Urdu, Gulzar and Tiro Devanagari Hindi (क्षत्रिय) correctly from local @font-face files. **How:** in a HyperFrames scene `@font-face{font-family:NNU;src:url(fonts/NotoNastaliqUrdu.ttf)}` and `<p dir="rtl" lang="ur" style="font-family:NNU">…</p>` (tested here 2026-10-24, chrome-headless-shell 152 screenshot) [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"] [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5127. **Transparent Urdu title PNG from headless Chrome** — for an ffmpeg-only edit, let Chrome draw the text on a transparent page and overlay it. **How:** `chrome-headless-shell --no-sandbox --allow-file-access-from-files --hide-scrollbars --default-background-color=00000000 --window-size=640,200 --screenshot=$PWD/t.png file://$PWD/t.html` gives an RGBA PNG with alpha 0 around the text (tested here 2026-10-24, T03) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24]
5128. **Loop a still title before fading it** — a PNG input is one frame, so `fade=...:alpha=1` on it never progresses and the title stays invisible; add `-loop 1 -t <dur>` to the image input and `shortest=1` to overlay. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.mp4 -loop 1 -t 3 -i ur_title.png -filter_complex "[1]format=rgba,fade=in:st=0.3:d=0.5:alpha=1[o];[0][o]overlay=0:180:shortest=1" out.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24, T02: invisible without -loop, visible with it) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#fade, checked 2026-10-24]
5129. **Nastaliq needs a tall line height** — Google Fonts lists line heights of 2.5 for Noto Nastaliq Urdu and 2.7 for Gulzar, against 1.703 for Noto Naskh Arabic; Nastaliq's sloping words stack high. **How:** CSS `line-height:2.5` on Nastaliq lines and extra top/bottom padding on caption boxes; in ASS raise MarginV (tested here 2026-10-24, Chrome render at 2.2-2.5) [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Nastaliq, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nastaliq, checked 2026-10-24]
5130. **Set lang="ur" so Urdu digits get Urdu shapes** — Unicode notes Persian's ۴ differs from the Urdu/Sindhi one and Urdu/Sindhi ۵ ۶ ۷ differ from Arabic; with Noto Naskh Arabic, `lang="ur"` and `lang="fa"` drew ۴ and ۷ differently. **How:** always `lang="ur"` on Urdu elements (PIL: `language='ur'`) (tested here 2026-10-24, T21) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5131. **Urdu digits are U+06F0-06F9** — Extended Arabic-Indic digits, not the Arabic-Indic ٠-٩ block. **How:** convert dates/prices: `'14 August 2026'.translate(str.maketrans('0123456789','۰۱۲۳۴۵۶۷۸۹'))` (tested here 2026-10-24, T22) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5132. **Urdu comma and full stop** — Urdu text uses ، (U+060C ARABIC COMMA) and ۔ (U+06D4 ARABIC FULL STOP), not Latin , and . **How:** type them into caption text; both rendered in every Urdu-capable font in the Chrome sheet and in the ASS test (tested here 2026-10-24, T05) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5133. **Urdu-only letters to test in any "Arabic" font** — ٹ (Tteh, Urdu), ہ (Heh Goal, Urdu) and ھ (Heh Doachashmee, aspirates) plus ڈ ڑ ں ے are what Arabic-only fonts lack. **How:** `need=[0x679,0x688,0x691,0x6BA,0x6BE,0x6C1,0x6CC,0x6D2]; print([hex(u) for u in need if u not in TTFont(f).getBestCmap()])` (tested here 2026-10-24, T26) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Urdu alphabet, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_alphabet, checked 2026-10-24]
5134. **Drone wides for outdoor and destination weddings** — drone shots add epic scale to wedding visuals, especially at outdoor and destination weddings. **How:** open each outdoor chapter (mehndi lawn, baraat street, destination venue) on one drone wide before cutting into the crowd [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24]
5135. **Roman Urdu is the informal-media default** — Roman Urdu (Urdu in the English alphabet) is mostly used for informal communication on the internet, social media and text messages. **How:** keep reel/WhatsApp captions in Roman Urdu (owner rule) and save Nastaliq for titles, invitations and poetry [Wikipedia: Roman Urdu, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Urdu, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Reply in Roman Urdu"]
5136. **Bollywood-style title cards in Roman script** — Bollywood uses a version of Roman script as the main script for its film titles, because the films are watched across South Asia and the Middle East. **How:** main title in Latin (e.g. Rozha One/Anton), a smaller Devanagari or Nastaliq line under it (tested here 2026-10-24, T28 mixed render) [Wikipedia: Roman Urdu, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Urdu, checked 2026-10-24]
5137. **Hinglish lines: one font for both scripts** — Hinglish mixes Hindi and English, once limited to informal contexts and ads, now common on social media. Pick a Devanagari font that also has Latin (all Google Devanagari fonts tested had A-Z, a-z, 0-9) so "Sale शुरू! सिर्फ ₹499" stays in one face. **How:** `<p lang="hi" style="font-family:'Baloo 2'">Sale शुरू! सिर्फ ₹499</p>` (tested here 2026-10-24, T28) [Wikipedia: Hinglish, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinglish, checked 2026-10-24] [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5138. **Check the ₹ sign before an Indian price card** — the rupee sign U+20B9 is in Devanagari's symbol set; Modak, Palanquin, Palanquin Dark, Sarpanch and Sumana lack it, and no Nastaliq font tested has it. **How:** `0x20B9 in TTFont(f).getBestCmap()` before using a font on a price card (tested here 2026-10-24, coverage.tsv) [Wikipedia: Devanagari, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari, checked 2026-10-24]
5139. **Rare Devanagari letters missing in popular fonts** — Hind, Kalam, Tillana and Yatra One lack ऎ ऒ ऩ ऴ (U+090E, 0912, 0929, 0934); Teko also lacks ऍ. **How:** run the coverage check on the exact caption text, not just "Hindi", before locking a font (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia: Devanagari (Unicode block), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari_(Unicode_block), checked 2026-10-24]
5140. **Conjunct test string for Devanagari fonts** — render "शादी मुबारक, क्षत्रिय श्री ₹500" before using a font: in Chrome, Alkatra dropped the conjuncts (श्री as शी) and Dekko showed visible halants (क्‌षत्‌रिय) while 50+ others shaped it. **How:** add the line to a quick Chrome sheet (sk2_20/t/deva.html pattern) and look (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia: Devanagari, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari, checked 2026-10-24] [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5141. **Nastaliq covers Punjabi (Shahmukhi), Pashto, Saraiki captions too** — Nastaliq is used for Urdu, Pashto, Balochi, Punjabi (in Pakistan), Kashmiri and Saraiki; Noto Nastaliq Urdu has all 9 Pashto letters tested and 10/11 Sindhi (missing ڪ U+06AA). **How:** same font route as Urdu; for Sindhi use Noto Naskh Arabic/Amiri/Lateef (11/11) (tested here 2026-10-24, coverage.tsv) [Wikipedia: Nastaliq, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nastaliq, checked 2026-10-24]
5142. **Gurmukhi for Sikh and Indian-Punjabi films** — Sikh wedding terms are written in Gurmukhi (ਅਨੰਦ ਕਾਰਜ); all 9 Google Gurmukhi fonts tested have the 29 checked letters plus ੴ (Ik Onkar). **How:** `<p lang="pa">ਵਿਆਹ ਮੁਬਾਰਕ</p>` with one of the Gurmukhi faces in 5199-5204 (tested here 2026-10-24, guru.html) [Wikipedia: Anand Karaj, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Karaj, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Gurmukhi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurmukhi, checked 2026-10-24]
5143. **Zain draws Urdu digits as Latin** — in the Chrome sheet Zain showed "123" for ۱۲۳ although it covers the Urdu letters. **How:** don't use Zain where Urdu numerals must show (dates, prices); Noto Naskh/Nastaliq show them (tested here 2026-10-24, urdu.html) [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5144. **Colour fonts for Eid/Ramadan titles** — Aref Ruqaa Ink, Blaka Ink and Reem Kufi Ink (OT-SVG + COLRv1) and Cairo Play, Reem Kufi Fun (COLRv0) rendered in colour in the Chrome sheet. **How:** use them through Chrome/HyperFrames; their colour in PIL/ffmpeg was not tested (tested here 2026-10-24: Chrome only) [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5145. **Get any Google font as a TTF in one line** — the CSS API returns a fonts.gstatic.com TTF URL to non-browser clients. **How:** `u=$(curl -s "https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Nastaliq+Urdu" | grep -o 'https://fonts.gstatic.com[^)]*' | head -1); curl -s "$u" -o library/fonts/NotoNastaliqUrdu.ttf` (tested here 2026-10-24 for 119 families) [Google Fonts CSS API, https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Nastaliq+Urdu, checked 2026-10-24]

## Pakistani wedding events: structure and coverage

5205. **Shaadi = Nikah + Walima at its core** — a typical Pakistani wedding (shaadi) consists of two main events, the Nikah (contract) and the Walima (reception); the other functions vary by ethnicity and family. **How:** build the full film around those two anchors and slot the optional functions before them; write an ffmetadata chapter file and `ffmpeg -i film.mp4 -i chapters.txt -map_metadata 1 -map_chapters 1 -c copy out.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24, T08) [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html#metadata, checked 2026-10-24]
5206. **Chapter names in the client's words** — Pakistani pages name the steps mangni/nisbat (engagement), dholki, rasm-e-heena/mehndi, baraat, nikah, rukhsati, walima. **How:** use these exact Roman Urdu labels as chapter titles and section cards (`title=Mehndi` in the ffmetadata file) (tested here 2026-10-24, T08) [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5207. **Mangni: rings, dua, sweets** — at the engagement rings are exchanged (traditionally placed on the bride by the groom's mother or sister, now usually by the couple), then a dua and blessings are recited and the wedding date is fixed. **How:** mark ring placement and the dua as must-have moments; let the dua play with music ducked or out [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5208. **Dholki is often folded into the mehndi** — dholki (named after the drum) was many nights of women singing at the bride's house; today it is often one night and often combined with the Rasm-e-Heena. **How:** ask whether it was a separate night; if combined, cut it as the opening sequence of the Mehndi chapter, not its own chapter [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5209. **Mehndi: two possible hosts** — some regions hold two Rasm-e-Heena functions, one by the groom's family and one by the bride's; others none. **How:** label them "Mehndi (Larki wale)" / "Mehndi (Larke wale)" so the family can find each [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5210. **Mehndi rituals to catch** — married women close to the bride apply henna and feed her sweets (for luck and long married life); performances and family-vs-family competitions are common, and men now take part. **How:** cover rasm (henna + sweets) as one sequence, then each performance as its own beat-cut block [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5211. **Hidden initials in the bride's mehndi** — designs often hide the bride's and groom's names/initials, and the groom may be challenged to find them. **How:** shoot a macro of the hidden initial and slowly push in on it in the edit: `zoompan=z='1+on*0.0015':x='iw/2-(iw/zoom/2)':y='ih/2-(ih/zoom/2)':d=1:s=1920x1080:fps=30` (tested here 2026-10-24, T20) [Wikipedia: Mehndi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehndi, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5212. **Mehndi detail list** — arrive early for henna-stained hands, decorations and anticipation; overhead shots of application, shooting through hanging decor, low angles on the dance floor, reflective surfaces (mirrors, water, glass), lanterns and fabrics as backdrops. **How:** when logging footage, tag these shot types so the mehndi sequence can alternate wide energy with detail inserts (contact sheet every 0.4 s, repo recipe) [Visual Artistry Co, https://www.visualartistry.co/blog-1/bridal-beauty-traditional-makeup-and-hairstyles-in-muslim-weddings-df66c-pl6c2-het63-77743-e7xac-p5wa2-8ehg4-rppm8-s98g4-2mrnc-pcxfp-h4ce2, checked 2026-10-24]
5213. **Keep henna brown** — the standard colour of mehndi is brown (white, red, black and gold designs exist). **How:** after any warm grade, check a henna close-up hasn't turned orange: compare frames with `npx hyperframes grade-compare --for frame.png` (repo) [Wikipedia: Mehndi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehndi, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video" step 4]
5214. **Doodh pilai** — the bride's family (usually her younger sister) presents the groom a decorated glass of milk; he sips, passes it to the bride, and must pay for it. **How:** cut it as a short playful scene with natural audio (the bargaining is the story) between Baraat arrival and Rukhsati [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5215. **Door blocked at the baraat** — the bride's sisters, cousins and friends block the entrance and let the groom in only for money. **How:** cover both sides (sisters and groom's party) for a back-and-forth cut; it plays as comic relief before the nikah [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5216. **Joota chupai** — the bride's sisters/cousins hide the groom's shoes and return them for money (in Hindu/Sikh weddings when he takes them off before the mandap or gurdwara). **How:** shoot the theft early and the payoff at departure; place both in order so the joke lands [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]

5219. **Rukhsati is the emotional peak** — the bride's farewell from her family; the doli marks her leaving, games are juxtaposed with the parents' sadness, and the Qur'an is held over her head as she leaves. **How:** let rukhsati carry the slowest pacing and softest music of the film; the Qur'an-over-head walk is the hero shot [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24] [Visual Artistry Co, https://visualartistry.co/blog-1/69ggwzljs7gffkl-nyj5k-eglnj-yh5rh-j97w4-pxeaz-rl8xh-7zx8k-zljrs-2hddk-3blnt-d9p5r-89nxr-d7nnd-chbgt-xhm8f-tjd5d-3wfpk-fn4cc-mfx2w, checked 2026-10-24]

5221. **Walima: the public reception** — hosted by husband and wife (costs usually borne by the groom's side), it makes the marriage public; once at home, now in halls, restaurants or hotels. **How:** treat it as the "elegant" chapter: formal couple portraits and group photos, slower cuts than baraat [Wikipedia: Walima, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walima, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5222. **Turban ceremony for the groom** — in some regions elders place a turban on the groom's head, formally including him in the "circle of men". **How:** if it happens, it's a strong opening for the groom-side story; capture the elders' hands and the groom's face together [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]

## Indian, Punjabi, Sikh and Bengali wedding events

5229. **Punjabi wedding chapter template** — pre-wedding: Roka, Kurmai, Sangeet, Mehndi, Mayian, Haldi, Jaggo; wedding day: Milni, Varmala, Joota chupai; post-wedding: Vidaai, Reception, Phera Dalna. **How:** start every Punjabi project from this chapter list and delete what didn't happen [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5230. **Roka and Chunni Chadana** — Roka ("to stop") unites the families, gifts like fruit and sweets are exchanged; in Chunni Chadana the groom's family drapes a chunni on the bride to welcome her. **How:** the chunni drape is the hero moment of a Roka reel [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5231. **Kurmai: phulkari, tikka, rings** — the bride is draped in a phulkari (often an heirloom), a dot of henna goes on her palm, the bride's father applies a tikka to the groom, rings and sweets follow. **How:** shoot the heirloom phulkari as a detail insert and mention it in a caption [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5232. **Sangeet: "sung together"** — historically a female-only evening of folk songs and Giddha; now in bigger venues with men joining. **How:** cut each performance to its own song on the beat; keep a few wide shots of the whole family singing [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5233. **Mayian / Vatna / Haldi** — one day before a Punjabi wedding the bride/groom sits on a wooden patri under a red cloth held by four female relatives while married women rub turmeric, flour and mustard-oil paste on face, arms and legs, singing; usually in the morning. **How:** plan this as a daylight, close-up-heavy sequence; the red cloth overhead makes a natural top frame [Wikipedia: Haldi (ceremony) / Mayian, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldi_(ceremony), checked 2026-10-24]
5234. **Four diyas lit for the bride** — four lamps are lit and oil keeps being poured so their glow reflects on her face. **How:** expose for the face lit by the diyas; this is a natural warm close-up for the haldi chapter opener [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5235. **Choora and kaleeras** — the bride's maternal uncle gifts red (and ivory/white) bangles; relatives tie kaleeras; after the wedding the bride shakes the kaleeras over unmarried girls, and whoever a piece falls on is said to marry next. **How:** run the kaleera shake in slow motion (repo minterpolate recipe) and cut to the girl's reaction [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Chura (bangles), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choora, checked 2026-10-24]
5236. **Ghara gharoli** — a sibling's spouse fills a decorated pitcher at a nearby temple and the bride is bathed with it, then dresses in wedding attire (also at the groom's house). **How:** a short transition sequence from haldi to getting ready [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5237. **Jaggo is a night procession** — in the last hours of the night, the maternal aunt carries a brass vessel with lit clay lamps on her head while another woman shakes a stick with bells, moving house to house with dancing and fireworks; also practised in Pakistan. **How:** treat it as a low-light sequence: keep the lamps as the key light and let the bells/fireworks carry the sound [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5238. **Groom-side morning: sehrabandi, varna, ghodi chadna** — the groom's sister ties the sehra (everyone gives gifts), his bhabi lines his eyes with surma, cash is waved to ward off the evil eye, sisters feed and adorn the mare, then he rides off. **How:** cut these four in order as the groom's "getting ready" act [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5239. **The sarbala** — a young nephew/cousin dressed like the groom accompanies him. **How:** a matching two-shot (groom + sarbala) is a reliable cute insert [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5240. **Milni: introductions by seniority** — senior male relatives of both families are introduced and exchange garlands; shagun is given in descending order of age. **How:** cut the pairs in the order they happened (oldest first) — families notice the order [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5241. **Varmala: the lift** — the bride traditionally places the varmala first; friends playfully lift the bride or groom so the other can't reach. **How:** slow-motion the lift and the garland landing; it's a teaser-worthy beat [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Jaimala, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaimala, checked 2026-10-24]
5242. **Hindu ceremony order for the mandap chapter** — Ganesh pooja; the bride brought in by her maternal uncle (antarpat held before the groom); varmala; kanyadaan; offerings to agni and the pheras; sindoor in the hair parting; mangalsutra; exchange of sweets; touching elders' feet. **How:** use this order as sub-chapters of the ceremony so nothing is cut out of sequence [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5243. **Sindoor Daan** — the groom first applies sindoor to the bride on the wedding day; afterwards she applies it herself daily, as the mark of a married woman. **How:** a tight close-up of the first application is the ceremony's key insert [Wikipedia: Sindoor, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindoor, checked 2026-10-24]
5244. **Anand Karaj: four laavaan, gurdwara only** — the Sikh ceremony has four laavaan (hymns) while the couple walks around the Guru Granth Sahib; Sikh authorities say it can take place only in a gurdwara, not destination/resort venues. **How:** make four sub-chapters, one per laav; plan no destination-venue ceremony shots [Wikipedia: Anand Karaj, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Karaj, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5245. **Vidaai** — the bride throws puffed rice over her head as wishes for her parents, says goodbye, relatives throw coins after the procession, and the mother-in-law circles a glass of water around her. **How:** shoot the rice throw from behind the bride toward the family; cut the vidaai slow with B&W as an option (5220) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5246. **Bengali chapter set** — Gaye Holud (turmeric, the groom's family visits the bride's house in the morning), Mala Badal (garland exchange), Sindoor Daan at the chadnatala, Bou Bhat (the groom's family introduces the bride at a reception). **How:** use these names, not Punjabi ones, for Bengali clients [Wikipedia: Bengali Hindu wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindu_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5247. **Baraat shot list** — the groom travels to the venue with family and friends, once on a mare/chariot/elephant, now often vintage cars; a band, dancers, fireworks and the dhol; the groom carries a sword and usually doesn't dance (the baraatis do); he's welcomed with garlands, tilak, aarti and shehnai/nadaswaram. **How:** split coverage: groom stillness vs baraati energy, and intercut them [Wikipedia: Baraat, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraat, checked 2026-10-24]

5249. **30-60 s Instagram highlight is standard** — Indian wedding content is designed for Instagram: short 30-60 s highlight reels with transitions, trending audio and captions; some teams have dedicated social-media editors. **How:** cut a 9:16 reel version alongside the film: `-vf "crop=ih*9/16:ih"` (tested here 2026-10-24, T23) [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24]

## Qawwali, folk and classical music for desi edits

5263. **Qawwali party = shot list** — a qawwali party is usually eight or nine men: lead singer, one or two side singers, one or two harmoniums, tabla and dholak, and a chorus of four or five who repeat key verses and clap; front row singers + harmoniums, back row chorus + percussion. **How:** cover lead singer, harmonium hands, tabla/dholak hands and the clapping chorus separately so you can cut on claps [Wikipedia: Qawwali, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali, checked 2026-10-24]
5264. **Qawwali builds from gentle to very high energy** — qawwalis begin gently and build steadily to a very high energy level. **How:** let shot length shrink as the piece builds: find the hits with `npx hyperframes audio song.wav` and cut every 2 beats late in the piece vs every 2 bars early [Wikipedia: Qawwali, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
5265. **Qawwali sections for chapters** — songs usually open with an instrumental prelude (melody on harmonium with tabla), then the alap (long improvised notes in the raga), then preamble verses before the main song. **How:** mark prelude/alap/verses as chapters or cut points; an excerpt for a reel can start at the end of the alap [Wikipedia: Qawwali, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali, checked 2026-10-24]
5266. **Qawwalis are long** — the longest commercial qawwali runs slightly over 115 minutes and Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan has songs over 60 minutes. **How:** never plan to use a whole qawwali; pick one section boundary to the next (5265) [Wikipedia: Qawwali, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali, checked 2026-10-24]
5267. **Qawwali is devotional** — traditionally performed at Sufi shrines (dargahs) to invoke remembrance of God and spiritual ecstasy. **How:** ask the client before cutting a qawwali under party/dance footage; for mehndi dances use dhol/dholak music instead [Wikipedia: Qawwali, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qawwali, checked 2026-10-24]
5268. **Keherwa: the 8-beat grid** — 8 beats in two divisions; sam (beat 1) starts the cycle, khali (beat 5) is waved; theka "dha ge na ti | na ka dhi na"; qawwali is one of its variations. **How:** cycle length = 8×60/BPM; at 96 BPM a cycle is 5.0 s, so cut on sam every 5.0 s: `python3 -c "bpm=96;cyc=8*60/bpm;print([round(i*cyc,3) for i in range(8)])"` (tested here 2026-10-24, T18) [Wikipedia: Keherwa, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keherwa, checked 2026-10-24]
5269. **Teentaal: the 16-beat grid** — the most common Hindustani taal: 16 beats in 4×4, claps on 1, 5 and 13, a wave on 9 (khali); used for fast (drut) tempo. **How:** cycle = 16×60/BPM (10.0 s at 96 BPM); put big transitions on sam (beat 1) and quieter cuts on khali (beat 9): `cyc=16*60/bpm`, khali at `cyc/2` (tested here 2026-10-24, T18) [Wikipedia: Teentaal, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teentaal, checked 2026-10-24]
5270. **Pick the dhol rhythm by scene** — common dhol rhythms are bhangra, dhamaal (associated with Sufi devotional dance) and kaharva (also called luddi); since the 1990s Punjabi pop evoking bhangra used kaharva. **How:** baraat/bhangra energy → bhangra/kaharva dhol; shrine or Sufi content → dhamaal [Wikipedia: Dhol, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhol, checked 2026-10-24]
5271. **Dhol = baraat** — dhols are used in wedding processions such as the baraat; the baraat reaches the meeting point to the rhythm of the dhol. **How:** if live dhol audio was recorded at the baraat, use it under the arrival instead of a music track (clean it with clean_voice.py only if voices must stay) [Wikipedia: Dhol, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhol, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Baraat, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraat, checked 2026-10-24]
5272. **Bhangra's sound** — the dhol (heavy beater on one side, light stick on the other) is bhangra's best-known instrument, with tumbi, sarangi, dholak and modern synth/guitar; dancers perform to short songs called boliyan; bhangra is tied to the Vaisakhi harvest festival. **How:** for Punjabi energy edits, cut on the dhol's heavy-beater hits [Wikipedia: Bhangra (music), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhangra_(music), checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Bhangra (dance), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhangra_(dance), checked 2026-10-24]
5273. **Luddi at weddings** — a Punjabi circle dance with finger clicks, hand claps, jumps and half-turns, performed at weddings in both Punjabs to dhol and shehnai. **How:** a wide overhead of the circle plus tight clap inserts on the beat [Wikipedia: Luddi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luddi, checked 2026-10-24]
5274. **Attan speeds up** — the Pashtun Attan, danced to a dohol, lasts 5-30 minutes and usually starts slow and finishes fast, with clapping in sync with the drums. **How:** compress it into a montage whose cut rate follows the drum tempo (long shots early, beat cuts at the end) [Wikipedia: Attan, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attan, checked 2026-10-24]
5275. **Dholak/dholki for mehndi nights** — the dholak is a two-headed hand drum used in qawwali, bhangra, Hindi film music and lokgeet; the dholki ceremony is named after it. **How:** a dholak-led track (or the family's live dholki) is the natural mehndi bed; the repo's romance-bollywood track has a dholak-style keherwa [Wikipedia: Dholak, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dholki, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: library/README.md music table]
5276. **Tabla: classical and qawwali** — since the 18th century the tabla is the principal percussion of Hindustani classical music, one of the main qawwali instruments, and features in kathak. **How:** for elegant (walima, nikah) beds prefer tabla-led tracks over dhol; the repo has a CC0 "Electronic Indian tabla tune" [Wikipedia: Tabla, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabla, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: library/music/cc0/README.md]
5277. **Shehnai welcomes the groom** — in traditional baraats the groom is welcomed with shehnai or nadaswaram, considered auspicious at Hindu weddings; the shehnai (double-reed, A3-A5) was one of the nine instruments of the Mughal court. **How:** sketch a shehnai welcome cue with General MIDI program 112 "Shanai" (0-based 111) in FluidSynth: `mido.Message('program_change',program=111)` then `fluidsynth -ni -F cue.wav -r 44100 /usr/share/sounds/sf2/FluidR3_GM.sf2 cue.mid` (tested here 2026-10-24, T24) [Wikipedia: Baraat, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraat, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Shehnai, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehnai, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: General MIDI, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_MIDI, checked 2026-10-24]
5278. **GM sitar for quick desi mock-ups** — General MIDI's ethnic group has Sitar at program 105 (0-based 104); the repo's Magenta arranger already writes for sitar, flute and dholak-style percussion. **How:** `mido` program_change 104 → `fluidsynth -ni -F out.wav -r 44100 /usr/share/sounds/sf2/FluidR3_GM.sf2 in.mid` (tested here 2026-10-24, T24) [Wikipedia: General MIDI, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_MIDI, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: library/README.md magenta/arrange.py]
5279. **Bansuri for romance** — the bansuri is a side-blown bamboo flute of the subcontinent (traditionally seven finger holes) used in many folk songs; the repo's "90s Bollywood romantic" track uses a bansuri melody with sitar intro. **How:** reuse `romance-bollywood-72bpm.wav` (owner-approved own music only when asked; see CLAUDE.md rule) [Wikipedia: Bansuri, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansuri, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: library/README.md]
5280. **Alghoza for Sindhi films** — the alghoza (beenon) is a paired woodwind of Sindhi culture. **How:** for Sindhi wedding/heritage edits ask the family for live alghoza audio or search Freesound/Commons with licence checks [Wikipedia: Alghoza, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algoza, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: library/research/free-music-sources.md]
5281. **Tumbi and chimta for Punjabi colour** — the tumbi is a high-pitched one-string plucked instrument of Punjabi folk, popular in Western bhangra; the chimta (tongs with brass jingles) is used in Punjabi folk, bhangra and Gurbani kirtan. **How:** when choosing a Punjabi track, these two timbres signal "Punjab" even without vocals [Wikipedia: Tumbi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbi, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Chimta, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimta, checked 2026-10-24]

5284. **Old film songs are not public domain** — 1940s-50s film songs are still under copyright (lyricist/composer life + 60 years in India/Pakistan). **How:** never rip filmi classics for client work; the owner rule forbids copyrighted songs [repo: library/research/free-music-sources.md] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Never download copyrighted songs"]

5286. **Playback = audio is the master** — in South Asian cinema songs are pre-recorded by playback singers and actors lip-sync to them. **How:** for lip-sync or song-picturisation reels lay the final audio first and cut picture to it, never the reverse [Wikipedia: Playback singer, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playback_singer, checked 2026-10-24]
5287. **Ghazal/shayari overlays** — a ghazal has five to fifteen relatively independent couplet lines on love and separation; Bollywood lyrics draw heavily on Urdu poetry and the ghazal tradition. **How:** one couplet (two lines) per shot in Nastaliq via Chrome (5126), held for the length of one sung/recited line [Wikipedia: Ghazal, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazal, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Filmi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmi, checked 2026-10-24]
5288. **Naat for Rabi ul Awwal / Ramadan content** — naat is poetry in praise of the Prophet Muhammad, popular in Pakistan, India and Bangladesh in Urdu, Punjabi and Bengali. **How:** treat as devotional: slow, respectful visuals, no beat-cut gimmicks; ask whether the client wants voice-only [Wikipedia: Naat, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naat, checked 2026-10-24]
5289. **Coke Studio-style live sessions** — Coke Studio Pakistan (Rohail Hyatt, from 8 June 2008) is a multi-camera live-performance music show. **How:** for a multi-camera family/band performance, sync cameras by audio cross-correlation (numpy FFT xcorr found a 0.75 s offset exactly) and cut between musicians on phrase ends [Wikipedia: Coke Studio Pakistan, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coke_Studio_Pakistan, checked 2026-10-24]
5290. **Kathak coverage by gharana** — Jaipur gharana stresses technical long pure-dance compositions with fast spins and footwork; Banaras footwork and Krishna stories; Lucknow grace and elegance. **How:** ask the dancer's style: for Jaipur get foot close-ups and spins (slow-mo), for Lucknow longer, wider takes [Wikipedia: Kathak, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathak, checked 2026-10-24]
