# South Asian styles: Pakistani and Indian weddings, festivals, music, Urdu/Hindi typography, local reels
Scope: Pakistani/Indian wedding events (mehndi, dholki, mayun/haldi, baraat, nikah, rukhsati, walima, sangeet, pheras, Anand Karaj), teasers and same-day edits, qawwali/folk/classical music use, Urdu (Nastaliq), Hindi (Devanagari) and Punjabi (Gurmukhi) on-screen text, cultural colours and motifs, Eid/Ramadan/national days, Bollywood/filmi conventions, PK/IN short-video platforms and local-shop reels.
Researched 2026-10-24 via agent-reach (Exa) web search, official docs and local tests. (Exa/jina were rate-limited this session, so pages were found with WebSearch and read with curl; only fetched page text is cited.) Test script and log: scratchpad/sk2_20/tests.sh + tests.log (T00-T35, all PASS); font tests: Google Fonts TTFs from the CSS API, fontTools coverage table (sk2_20/coverage.tsv) and headless-Chrome render sheets (sk2_20/t/urdu.html, deva.html, guru.html). Cultural facts are summaries of the cited Wikipedia/blog pages; customs vary by family and region, so confirm with the client.

## Rendering Urdu, Hindi and Punjabi text on this box

5121. **Captions in Urdu/Hindi: use the `ass` filter, not `subtitles`** — on this box's ffmpeg 6.1 the `subtitles` filter drew Noto Nastaliq Urdu as boxes and broke Devanagari conjuncts (सिर्फ → सरि्फ, श्री → शरी), while the `ass` filter with the same .ass file rendered both correctly; the docs say `complex` shaping (OpenType substitution + positioning) is "required for correct rendering of complex scripts such as Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari". **How:** `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "ass=caps.ass:fontsdir=library/fonts:shaping=complex" out.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24, T32/T33 vs T34) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#ass, checked 2026-10-24]
5122. **drawtext cannot draw Nastaliq** — drawtext's `text_shaping` (needs libfribidi) only reverses RTL order and joins Arabic letters; with Noto Nastaliq Urdu, Gulzar, Lateef and Scheherazade New it printed empty boxes or nothing, Mirza half-broken. Those fonts map almost no Arabic Presentation Forms (Noto Nastaliq Urdu 0 of U+FE70-FEFF), which the fribidi path needs. **How:** don't use drawtext for Urdu Nastaliq; render the line in Chrome/HyperFrames or PIL+Raqm and overlay the PNG (tested here 2026-10-24, T25 + fontTools count) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawtext, checked 2026-10-24]
5123. **drawtext also breaks Hindi conjuncts and the i-matra** — `drawtext` with Mukta drew "सिर्फ ₹499" as "सरि्फ ₹499" (pre-base ि in the wrong place, half-form not joined), though plain "शादी मुबारक" looked fine. **How:** for any Hindi with ि, reph or conjuncts (क्ष, श्री) use the `ass` filter (5121) or a Chrome/PIL PNG; keep drawtext for Latin/Roman Urdu (tested here 2026-10-24, T07) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawtext, checked 2026-10-24]
5124. **If drawtext must draw Urdu, use Noto Naskh Arabic or Amiri** — these two Naskh fonts include the presentation forms (141 and 140 of U+FE70-FEFF) and drew "شادی مبارک" joined and in the right order through drawtext. **How:** `drawtext=fontfile=NotoNaskhArabic.ttf:textfile=ur.txt:fontsize=48:borderw=3:x=(w-tw)/2:y=h-90` (UTF-8 text in a file avoids shell quoting) (tested here 2026-10-24, T04) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawtext, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5125. **Pre-check a font for the ffmpeg drawtext path** — count its Presentation Forms-B code points; near zero means drawtext will show boxes. **How:** `python3 -c "from fontTools.ttLib import TTFont;cm=TTFont('f.ttf').getBestCmap();print(sum(1 for u in cm if 0xFE70<=u<=0xFEFF))"` (fontTools 4.67 pip-installed, small) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5126. **HyperFrames/Chrome renders Nastaliq and Hindi correctly** — the repo's headless Chrome (HyperFrames) shaped Noto Nastaliq Urdu, Gulzar and Tiro Devanagari Hindi (क्षत्रिय) correctly from local @font-face files. **How:** in a HyperFrames scene `@font-face{font-family:NNU;src:url(fonts/NotoNastaliqUrdu.ttf)}` and `<p dir="rtl" lang="ur" style="font-family:NNU">…</p>` (tested here 2026-10-24, chrome-headless-shell 152 screenshot) [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"] [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5127. **Transparent Urdu title PNG from headless Chrome** — for an ffmpeg-only edit, let Chrome draw the text on a transparent page and overlay it. **How:** `chrome-headless-shell --no-sandbox --allow-file-access-from-files --hide-scrollbars --default-background-color=00000000 --window-size=640,200 --screenshot=$PWD/t.png file://$PWD/t.html` gives an RGBA PNG with alpha 0 around the text (tested here 2026-10-24, T03) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24]
5128. **Loop a still title before fading it** — a PNG input is one frame, so `fade=...:alpha=1` on it never progresses and the title stays invisible; add `-loop 1 -t <dur>` to the image input and `shortest=1` to overlay. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.mp4 -loop 1 -t 3 -i ur_title.png -filter_complex "[1]format=rgba,fade=in:st=0.3:d=0.5:alpha=1[o];[0][o]overlay=0:180:shortest=1" out.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24, T02: invisible without -loop, visible with it) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#fade, checked 2026-10-24]
5129. **Nastaliq needs a tall line height** — Google Fonts lists line heights of 2.5 for Noto Nastaliq Urdu and 2.7 for Gulzar, against 1.703 for Noto Naskh Arabic; Nastaliq's sloping words stack high. **How:** CSS `line-height:2.5` on Nastaliq lines and extra top/bottom padding on caption boxes; in ASS raise MarginV (tested here 2026-10-24, Chrome render at 2.2-2.5) [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Nastaliq, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nastaliq, checked 2026-10-24]
5130. **Set lang="ur" so Urdu digits get Urdu shapes** — Unicode notes Persian's ۴ differs from the Urdu/Sindhi one and Urdu/Sindhi ۵ ۶ ۷ differ from Arabic; with Noto Naskh Arabic, `lang="ur"` and `lang="fa"` drew ۴ and ۷ differently. **How:** always `lang="ur"` on Urdu elements (PIL: `language='ur'`) (tested here 2026-10-24, T21) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5131. **Urdu digits are U+06F0-06F9** — Extended Arabic-Indic digits, not the Arabic-Indic ٠-٩ block. **How:** convert dates/prices: `'14 August 2026'.translate(str.maketrans('0123456789','۰۱۲۳۴۵۶۷۸۹'))` (tested here 2026-10-24, T22) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5132. **Urdu comma and full stop** — Urdu text uses ، (U+060C ARABIC COMMA) and ۔ (U+06D4 ARABIC FULL STOP), not Latin , and . **How:** type them into caption text; both rendered in every Urdu-capable font in the Chrome sheet and in the ASS test (tested here 2026-10-24, T05) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24]
5133. **Urdu-only letters to test in any "Arabic" font** — ٹ (Tteh, Urdu), ہ (Heh Goal, Urdu) and ھ (Heh Doachashmee, aspirates) plus ڈ ڑ ں ے are what Arabic-only fonts lack. **How:** `need=[0x679,0x688,0x691,0x6BA,0x6BE,0x6C1,0x6CC,0x6D2]; print([hex(u) for u in need if u not in TTFont(f).getBestCmap()])` (tested here 2026-10-24, T26) [Wikipedia: Arabic script in Unicode, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script_in_Unicode, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Urdu alphabet, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_alphabet, checked 2026-10-24]
5134. **Mixed Urdu + English + numbers in one line** — RTL scripts embed numbers and Latin words left-to-right; Unicode orders text logically and the bidi algorithm lays it out. **How:** type in reading order inside `<p dir="rtl" lang="ur">FireCraft SMP پر 14 اگست کی رات</p>`; Chrome put the English at the right start and kept "14" LTR (tested here 2026-10-24, T35) [Wikipedia: Bidirectional text, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidirectional_text, checked 2026-10-24]
5135. **Roman Urdu is the informal-media default** — Roman Urdu (Urdu in the English alphabet) is mostly used for informal communication on the internet, social media and text messages. **How:** keep reel/WhatsApp captions in Roman Urdu (owner rule) and save Nastaliq for titles, invitations and poetry (tested here 2026-10-24: Nastaliq title route 5126) [Wikipedia: Roman Urdu, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Urdu, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Reply in Roman Urdu"]
5136. **Bollywood-style title cards in Roman script** — Bollywood uses a version of Roman script as the main script for its film titles, because the films are watched across South Asia and the Middle East. **How:** main title in Latin (e.g. Rozha One/Anton), a smaller Devanagari or Nastaliq line under it (tested here 2026-10-24, T28 mixed render) [Wikipedia: Roman Urdu, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Urdu, checked 2026-10-24]
5137. **Hinglish lines: one font for both scripts** — Hinglish mixes Hindi and English, once limited to informal contexts and ads, now common on social media. Pick a Devanagari font that also has Latin (all Google Devanagari fonts tested had A-Z, a-z, 0-9) so "Sale शुरू! सिर्फ ₹499" stays in one face. **How:** `<p lang="hi" style="font-family:'Baloo 2'">Sale शुरू! सिर्फ ₹499</p>` (tested here 2026-10-24, T28) [Wikipedia: Hinglish, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinglish, checked 2026-10-24] [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5138. **Check the ₹ sign before an Indian price card** — the rupee sign U+20B9 is in Devanagari's symbol set; Modak, Palanquin, Palanquin Dark, Sarpanch and Sumana lack it, and no Nastaliq font tested has it. **How:** `0x20B9 in TTFont(f).getBestCmap()` before using a font on a price card (tested here 2026-10-24, coverage.tsv) [Wikipedia: Devanagari, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari, checked 2026-10-24]
5139. **Rare Devanagari letters missing in popular fonts** — Hind, Kalam, Tillana and Yatra One lack ऎ ऒ ऩ ऴ (U+090E, 0912, 0929, 0934); Teko also lacks ऍ. **How:** run the coverage check on the exact caption text, not just "Hindi", before locking a font (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia: Devanagari (Unicode block), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari_(Unicode_block), checked 2026-10-24]
5140. **Conjunct test string for Devanagari fonts** — render "शादी मुबारक, क्षत्रिय श्री ₹500" before using a font: in Chrome, Alkatra dropped the conjuncts (श्री as शी) and Dekko showed visible halants (क्‌षत्‌रिय) while 50+ others shaped it. **How:** add the line to a quick Chrome sheet (sk2_20/t/deva.html pattern) and look (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia: Devanagari, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari, checked 2026-10-24] [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5141. **Nastaliq covers Punjabi (Shahmukhi), Pashto, Saraiki captions too** — Nastaliq is used for Urdu, Pashto, Balochi, Punjabi (in Pakistan), Kashmiri and Saraiki; Noto Nastaliq Urdu has all 9 Pashto letters tested and 10/11 Sindhi (missing ڪ U+06AA). **How:** same font route as Urdu; for Sindhi use Noto Naskh Arabic/Amiri/Lateef (11/11) (tested here 2026-10-24, coverage.tsv) [Wikipedia: Nastaliq, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nastaliq, checked 2026-10-24]
5142. **Gurmukhi for Sikh and Indian-Punjabi films** — Sikh wedding terms are written in Gurmukhi (ਅਨੰਦ ਕਾਰਜ); all 9 Google Gurmukhi fonts tested have the 29 checked letters plus ੴ (Ik Onkar). **How:** `<p lang="pa">ਵਿਆਹ ਮੁਬਾਰਕ</p>` with one of the Gurmukhi faces in 5199-5204 (tested here 2026-10-24, guru.html) [Wikipedia: Anand Karaj, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Karaj, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Gurmukhi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurmukhi, checked 2026-10-24]
5143. **Zain draws Urdu digits as Latin** — in the Chrome sheet Zain showed "123" for ۱۲۳ although it covers the Urdu letters. **How:** don't use Zain where Urdu numerals must show (dates, prices); Noto Naskh/Nastaliq show them (tested here 2026-10-24, urdu.html) [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5144. **Colour fonts for Eid/Ramadan titles** — Aref Ruqaa Ink, Blaka Ink and Reem Kufi Ink (OT-SVG + COLRv1) and Cairo Play, Reem Kufi Fun (COLRv0) rendered in colour in the Chrome sheet. **How:** use them through Chrome/HyperFrames; their colour in PIL/ffmpeg was not tested (tested here 2026-10-24: Chrome only) [Google Fonts metadata, https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts, checked 2026-10-24]
5145. **Get any Google font as a TTF in one line** — the CSS API returns a fonts.gstatic.com TTF URL to non-browser clients. **How:** `u=$(curl -s "https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Nastaliq+Urdu" | grep -o 'https://fonts.gstatic.com[^)]*' | head -1); curl -s "$u" -o library/fonts/NotoNastaliqUrdu.ttf` (tested here 2026-10-24 for 119 families) [Google Fonts CSS API, https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Noto+Nastaliq+Urdu, checked 2026-10-24]
5146. **InPage text vs Unicode** — InPage (since 1994) is the page-layout program primarily used to create Urdu pages in Nastaliq with a large ligature library; clients often have their Urdu wording in it. **How:** ask for the text pasted as plain Unicode (WhatsApp message) and set it in Noto Nastaliq Urdu via 5126 (tested here 2026-10-24: Unicode Urdu renders) [Wikipedia: InPage, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InPage, checked 2026-10-24]

## Pakistani wedding events: structure and coverage

5205. **Shaadi = Nikah + Walima at its core** — a typical Pakistani wedding (shaadi) consists of two main events, the Nikah (contract) and the Walima (reception); the other functions vary by ethnicity and family. **How:** build the full film around those two anchors and slot the optional functions before them; write an ffmetadata chapter file and `ffmpeg -i film.mp4 -i chapters.txt -map_metadata 1 -map_chapters 1 -c copy out.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24, T08) [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html#metadata, checked 2026-10-24]
5206. **Chapter names in the client's words** — Pakistani pages name the steps mangni/nisbat (engagement), dholki, rasm-e-heena/mehndi, baraat, nikah, rukhsati, walima. **How:** use these exact Roman Urdu labels as chapter titles and section cards (`title=Mehndi` in the ffmetadata file) (tested here 2026-10-24, T08) [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5207. **Mangni: rings, dua, sweets** — at the engagement rings are exchanged (traditionally placed on the bride by the groom's mother or sister, now usually by the couple), then a dua and blessings are recited and the wedding date is fixed. **How:** mark ring placement and the dua as must-have moments; let the dua play with music ducked or out (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter marker T08) [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5208. **Dholki is often folded into the mehndi** — dholki (named after the drum) was many nights of women singing at the bride's house; today it is often one night and often combined with the Rasm-e-Heena. **How:** ask whether it was a separate night; if combined, cut it as the opening sequence of the Mehndi chapter, not its own chapter (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter file T08) [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5209. **Mehndi: two possible hosts** — some regions hold two Rasm-e-Heena functions, one by the groom's family and one by the bride's; others none. **How:** label them "Mehndi (Larki wale)" / "Mehndi (Larke wale)" so the family can find each (tested here 2026-10-24, T08) [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5210. **Mehndi rituals to catch** — married women close to the bride apply henna and feed her sweets (for luck and long married life); performances and family-vs-family competitions are common, and men now take part. **How:** cover rasm (henna + sweets) as one sequence, then each performance as its own beat-cut block (tested here 2026-10-24: tala grid T18) [Wikipedia: Pakistani wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistani_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5211. **Hidden initials in the bride's mehndi** — designs often hide the bride's and groom's names/initials, and the groom may be challenged to find them. **How:** shoot a macro of the hidden initial and slowly push in on it in the edit: `zoompan=z='1+on*0.0015':x='iw/2-(iw/zoom/2)':y='ih/2-(ih/zoom/2)':d=1:s=1920x1080:fps=30` (tested here 2026-10-24, T20) [Wikipedia: Mehndi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehndi, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]

## Indian, Punjabi, Sikh and Bengali wedding events

5229. **Punjabi wedding chapter template** — pre-wedding: Roka, Kurmai, Sangeet, Mehndi, Mayian, Haldi, Jaggo; wedding day: Milni, Varmala, Joota chupai; post-wedding: Vidaai, Reception, Phera Dalna. **How:** start every Punjabi project from this chapter list and delete what didn't happen (tested here 2026-10-24, T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5230. **Roka and Chunni Chadana** — Roka ("to stop") unites the families, gifts like fruit and sweets are exchanged; in Chunni Chadana the groom's family drapes a chunni on the bride to welcome her. **How:** the chunni drape is the hero moment of a Roka reel (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5231. **Kurmai: phulkari, tikka, rings** — the bride is draped in a phulkari (often an heirloom), a dot of henna goes on her palm, the bride's father applies a tikka to the groom, rings and sweets follow. **How:** shoot the heirloom phulkari as a detail insert and mention it in a caption (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5232. **Sangeet: "sung together"** — historically a female-only evening of folk songs and Giddha; now in bigger venues with men joining. **How:** cut each performance to its own song on the beat; keep a few wide shots of the whole family singing (tested here 2026-10-24: tala grid T18) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5233. **Mayian / Vatna / Haldi** — one day before a Punjabi wedding the bride/groom sits on a wooden patri under a red cloth held by four female relatives while married women rub turmeric, flour and mustard-oil paste on face, arms and legs, singing; usually in the morning. **How:** plan this as a daylight, close-up-heavy sequence; the red cloth overhead makes a natural top frame (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Haldi (ceremony) / Mayian, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haldi_(ceremony), checked 2026-10-24]
5234. **Four diyas lit for the bride** — four lamps are lit and oil keeps being poured so their glow reflects on her face. **How:** expose for the face lit by the diyas; this is a natural warm close-up for the haldi chapter opener (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5235. **Choora and kaleeras** — the bride's maternal uncle gifts red (and ivory/white) bangles; relatives tie kaleeras; after the wedding the bride shakes the kaleeras over unmarried girls, and whoever a piece falls on is said to marry next. **How:** run the kaleera shake in slow motion (repo minterpolate recipe) and cut to the girl's reaction (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Chura (bangles), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choora, checked 2026-10-24]
5236. **Ghara gharoli** — a sibling's spouse fills a decorated pitcher at a nearby temple and the bride is bathed with it, then dresses in wedding attire (also at the groom's house). **How:** a short transition sequence from haldi to getting ready (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5237. **Jaggo is a night procession** — in the last hours of the night, the maternal aunt carries a brass vessel with lit clay lamps on her head while another woman shakes a stick with bells, moving house to house with dancing and fireworks; also practised in Pakistan. **How:** treat it as a low-light sequence: keep the lamps as the key light and let the bells/fireworks carry the sound (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5238. **Groom-side morning: sehrabandi, varna, ghodi chadna** — the groom's sister ties the sehra (everyone gives gifts), his bhabi lines his eyes with surma, cash is waved to ward off the evil eye, sisters feed and adorn the mare, then he rides off. **How:** cut these four in order as the groom's "getting ready" act (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5239. **The sarbala** — a young nephew/cousin dressed like the groom accompanies him. **How:** a matching two-shot (groom + sarbala) is a reliable cute insert (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5240. **Milni: introductions by seniority** — senior male relatives of both families are introduced and exchange garlands; shagun is given in descending order of age. **How:** cut the pairs in the order they happened (oldest first) — families notice the order (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5241. **Varmala: the lift** — the bride traditionally places the varmala first; friends playfully lift the bride or groom so the other can't reach. **How:** slow-motion the lift and the garland landing; it's a teaser-worthy beat (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Jaimala, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaimala, checked 2026-10-24]
5242. **Hindu ceremony order for the mandap chapter** — Ganesh pooja; the bride brought in by her maternal uncle (antarpat held before the groom); varmala; kanyadaan; offerings to agni and the pheras; sindoor in the hair parting; mangalsutra; exchange of sweets; touching elders' feet. **How:** use this order as sub-chapters of the ceremony so nothing is cut out of sequence (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5243. **Sindoor Daan** — the groom first applies sindoor to the bride on the wedding day; afterwards she applies it herself daily, as the mark of a married woman. **How:** a tight close-up of the first application is the ceremony's key insert (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Sindoor, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindoor, checked 2026-10-24]
5244. **Anand Karaj: four laavaan, gurdwara only** — the Sikh ceremony has four laavaan (hymns) while the couple walks around the Guru Granth Sahib; Sikh authorities say it can take place only in a gurdwara, not destination/resort venues. **How:** make four sub-chapters, one per laav; plan no destination-venue ceremony shots (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Anand Karaj, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Karaj, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5245. **Vidaai** — the bride throws puffed rice over her head as wishes for her parents, says goodbye, relatives throw coins after the procession, and the mother-in-law circles a glass of water around her. **How:** shoot the rice throw from behind the bride toward the family; cut the vidaai slow with B&W as an option (5220) (tested here 2026-10-24, T11) [Wikipedia: Punjabi wedding traditions, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions, checked 2026-10-24]
5246. **Bengali chapter set** — Gaye Holud (turmeric, the groom's family visits the bride's house in the morning), Mala Badal (garland exchange), Sindoor Daan at the chadnatala, Bou Bhat (the groom's family introduces the bride at a reception). **How:** use these names, not Punjabi ones, for Bengali clients (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Bengali Hindu wedding, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Hindu_wedding, checked 2026-10-24]
5247. **Baraat shot list** — the groom travels to the venue with family and friends, once on a mare/chariot/elephant, now often vintage cars; a band, dancers, fireworks and the dhol; the groom carries a sword and usually doesn't dance (the baraatis do); he's welcomed with garlands, tilak, aarti and shehnai/nadaswaram. **How:** split coverage: groom stillness vs baraati energy, and intercut them (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [Wikipedia: Baraat, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraat, checked 2026-10-24]
5248. **Ask about ritual timings first** — photographers now consult families or priests to understand rituals and their timings, documenting the meaning, not just the action (saat phere to nikah). **How:** before the wedding, get a timed ritual list and turn it into the chapter skeleton (5229/5205) (tested here 2026-10-24, T08) [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24]

## Teasers, reels and same-day edits for desi weddings

5249. **30-60 s Instagram highlight is standard** — Indian wedding content is designed for Instagram: short 30-60 s highlight reels with transitions, trending audio and captions; some teams have dedicated social-media editors. **How:** cut a 9:16 reel version alongside the film: `-vf "crop=ih*9/16:ih"` (tested here 2026-10-24, T23) [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24]
5250. **Teaser: pop in the first 3 seconds** — teasers are vertical 9:16 for Reels/TikTok/Shorts, transitions shouldn't drag and the grade should "pop" in the first 3 seconds. **How:** `-vf "crop=ih*9/16:ih,eq=saturation=1.3"` on the opening shots, then check skin (tested here 2026-10-24, T23) [Fotober, https://fotober.com/types-of-wedding-videos, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#eq, checked 2026-10-24]
5251. **SDE delivered by big screen and WhatsApp** — same-day edits are shown on big screens at the event or sent to guests via WhatsApp. **How:** export a full-quality screen copy and a WhatsApp copy (`-c:v libx264 -crf 23 -preset slow -c:a aac -b:a 160k -movflags +faststart`, repo recipe) (tested here 2026-10-24: repo recipe) [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video" step 8]
5252. **SDE: ~3 minutes by dinner** — the editor works on site, imports clips as they're shot and makes a ~3-minute piece by dinner time. **How:** lock a 3:00 music bed first and fill it; don't start a longer cut on the day (tested here 2026-10-24: tala/bar grid T18) [Fotober, https://fotober.com/types-of-wedding-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
5253. **SDE: transcode while you shoot** — copy cards to the laptop through the day and batch-convert to an edit codec (one videographer converts H.264 to ProRes LT continuously, even in the car). **How:** `ffmpeg -i card/MVI_0001.MP4 -c:v prores_ks -profile:v 1 -c:a pcm_s16le edit/MVI_0001.mov` in a background loop (profile 1 = lt) (tested here 2026-10-24, T09) [DVinfo forum, https://dvinfo.net/forum/archive/index.php/t-496855.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-codecs.html#ProRes, checked 2026-10-24]
5254. **SDE: transfer only the seconds you'll use, named by sequence** — mark ins/outs and bring in only the 2 s you need, labelled bride_dress_1, bride_dress_2…; labelling does much of the editing. **How:** `ffmpeg -ss 12 -i card/MVI_0042.MP4 -t 2 -c copy sde/bride_dress_1.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24, T10) [DVinfo forum, https://dvinfo.net/forum/archive/index.php/t-496855.html, checked 2026-10-24]
5255. **SDE: plan ~15 sequences ahead** — map out about 15 sequences that will fill the SDE (bride make-up, groom dressing, etc.) and brief the other shooters so angles match. **How:** for a desi wedding name them by ritual (mehndi hands, sehrabandi, baraat arrival, nikah signing, rukhsati) and create the empty folders before the day (tested here 2026-10-24: chapter T08) [DVinfo forum, https://dvinfo.net/forum/archive/index.php/t-496855.html, checked 2026-10-24] [MotionElements, https://www.motionelements.com/blog/articles/under-time-pressure-mastering-same-day-edit-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
5256. **SDE: transcode the key moments first** — capture/transcode the main moments (entrance, vows, rings, announcement) first and the long speeches last. **How:** order the transcode queue by your ritual list (nikah signing and rukhsati first) (tested here 2026-10-24, T09) [DVinfo forum, https://dvinfo.net/forum/archive/index.php/t-496855.html, checked 2026-10-24]
5257. **SDE: budget the render** — one editor's 4-minute SDE took about 20 minutes to export at 720p and 30 at 1080p; travel and render time must be counted before you start. **How:** time a test export of 30 s on your machine early in the day and scale it (tested here 2026-10-24: export commands T14) [DVinfo forum, https://dvinfo.net/forum/archive/index.php/t-496855.html, checked 2026-10-24] [MotionElements, https://www.motionelements.com/blog/articles/under-time-pressure-mastering-same-day-edit-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
5258. **BTS reels from the wedding** — couples ask for unfiltered behind-the-scenes: make-up sessions, outfit trials, bloopers and pre-function jitters edited into informal reels. **How:** tag BTS clips while logging and cut a separate light reel (Roman Urdu captions) (tested here 2026-10-24: 9:16 crop T23) [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24]
5259. **Documentary pre-wedding shoots** — instead of choreographed poses: a day in the life, walking through a market, cooking at home, revisiting the college where they met. **How:** cut it like a mini-doc with natural sound; save music for transitions (tested here 2026-10-24: n/a beyond repo cut recipes) [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24]
5260. **Cinematic film with interviews** — offerings now include script-writing, couple interviews and mini-docs featuring family members, plus voiceovers and drone shots. **How:** record short family interviews (Urdu/Hindi), transcribe with Whisper, subtitle with the `ass` filter (5121) (tested here 2026-10-24, T32/T33) [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24]
5261. **Film-style muted look** — a growing trend is film-style images: muted colours, grainy textures, natural light. **How:** lower saturation and add temporal grain: `-vf "eq=saturation=0.85,noise=alls=8:allf=t"` (tested here 2026-10-24, see T40) [VenueLook, https://www.venuelook.com/blog/top-wedding-photography-trends-in-india-for-2026, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#noise, checked 2026-10-24]
5262. **Mehndi motifs as the film's graphic language** — common mehndi motifs are flowers, vines and leaves, paisleys, peacocks, mandalas, religious symbols and hidden names/initials; South Asian designs are small, intricate patterns and lines. **How:** draw section-card ornaments from these motifs (paisley, peacock, vine) rather than generic Western florals, and pull the colour from the henna close-up (tested here 2026-10-24: title PNG route T02/T03) [Wikipedia: Mehndi, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehndi, checked 2026-10-24]
