## Shooting green screen so it keys (photography rules)

2961. **Pick the screen colour from the subject, not habit** — the most important factor for a key is colour separation between subject and screen: use blue when the subject is mostly green (plants, a green costume), green otherwise. Bright green is less likely in foregrounds and digital cameras and lossy codecs keep the most detail in the green channel. **How:** before the shoot list the wardrobe/prop colours; if any is green, book a blue screen and key it later with `chromakey` on the sampled blue. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24] [Filmdaft, https://filmdaft.com/guide-how-to-shoot-and-key-green-screen-footage/, checked 2026-10-24]
2962. **Two characters, two screens** — when two subjects need opposite screen colours (a red-blue costume and an all-green costume), shoot each against the screen that is far from its colours and composite both later (Spider-Man was shot on green, the Green Goblin on blue). **How:** shoot separate passes and stack them in ffmpeg with two keyed `overlay` layers or two HyperFrames video layers. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2963. **Blue for night scenes** — blue is still the better screen when green spill would look wrong, for example a night scene, or when a costume or prop is green. **How:** for a night composite ask for a blue screen and key with `chromakey`/`hsvkey` on the blue. [Filmdaft, https://filmdaft.com/guide-how-to-shoot-and-key-green-screen-footage/, checked 2026-10-24]
2964. **Screen dress code** — nothing on the subject may match the backing colour, or that part is keyed out (presenters 'disappear' into the key); also check glasses and watches for green reflections. **How:** send the owner/client a one-line brief: 'no green clothes, jewellery or props; remove shiny watches'. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24] [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24]
2965. **Simple silhouettes key cleanly** — frizzy hair and fine detail may not resolve, and partly transparent costume parts cause problems, so a fairly simple outline gives a clean division. **How:** prefer tied-back hair and solid fabric for a key you will pull with ffmpeg; budget `rembg` alpha matting or a softer `blend` for hair. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2966. **Matte screen material** — shiny surfaces reflect the lights as pale hot areas and dark patches; a matte surface diffuses light and gives a narrow colour range. Non-reflective fabric or vinyl avoids hot spots. **How:** reject shiny paper or glossy paint as a backdrop for owner shoots. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24] [Finchley Studio, https://finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/top-10-tips-for-effective-green-screen-filming, checked 2026-10-24]
2967. **Taut, clean, wrinkle-free screen** — wrinkles and folds cast shadows; dirty marks also stay in the key. Stretch fabric tight on all sides. **How:** check the first frame at 100% before recording; a shadowed fold shows up as a separate colour when you sample it with `crop=...,scale=1:1`. [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24] [Finchley Studio, https://finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/top-10-tips-for-effective-green-screen-filming, checked 2026-10-24]
2968. **Two-stop value difference** — for the cleanest key create a value difference between subject and screen, for example the screen two stops brighter than the subject or the other way round. **How:** ask the shooter to meter screen and face separately and keep the two-stop gap. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2969. **Expose the screen bright and saturated** — under- or over-exposing the backing lowers its saturation, and underexposed video is noisy; the screen must be bright enough for a bright, saturated colour, and too dark or blown out gives no clean key. **How:** check the sampled screen colour: a dull, grey sample means the exposure is off. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24] [Filmdaft, https://filmdaft.com/guide-how-to-shoot-and-key-green-screen-footage/, checked 2026-10-24]
2970. **Soft lights at 45 degrees on the screen** — light the screen with at least two diffused lights a few feet away (out of shot) at about 45 degrees; uncovered lights give harsh falloff. Check where the two beams overlap for hot spots. **How:** softboxes or diffusion gel in front of LED panels; keep fabric away from hot tungsten lamps. [Filmdaft, https://filmdaft.com/guide-how-to-shoot-and-key-green-screen-footage/, checked 2026-10-24] [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24]
2971. **Same lights, same settings, same colour temperature** — screen lights should be the same make and model with the same settings, and every light on the screen should share one colour temperature (3200K tungsten or about 5600K daylight LEDs, not mixed), with camera white balance matched. **How:** mixed lamps make the green shift across the screen, which shows as two different sampled colours. [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24] [Filmdaft, https://filmdaft.com/guide-how-to-shoot-and-key-green-screen-footage/, checked 2026-10-24]
2972. **Light the subject for the target scene** — light the subject for where they are meant to be, not where they are: blue light for a night scene, beach light for a beach. Lighting in both scenes must match: overcast skies are easier to match, direct sun must match in direction and colour. **How:** pick the background plate first, then brief the subject lighting from it. [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2973. **Magenta-gel the backlight** — a backlight separates the subject from the screen, and gelling it with minus-green (magenta) counters green spill on hair and shoulders. **How:** add a magenta-gelled rim light behind the subject. [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24]
2974. **Bigger, farther screen against spill** — the screen scatters its own colour onto the set (spill); a larger screen placed far from the actors avoids it. **How:** if spill remains, plan `despill` in post. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2975. **Defocus the screen slightly** — keeping the screen a little out of focus evens out marks and shadows on it. **How:** use a wider aperture so the backdrop is soft while the subject stays sharp. [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24]
2976. **Shutter for keying** — a fast shutter cuts motion blur and helps with flyaway hair (Artlist); 1/48 at 24 fps is the cinematic default (Finchley). A faster shutter needs exposure rebalanced. **How:** for fast action ask for a faster shutter; for talking heads keep the 180-degree default. [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24] [Finchley Studio, https://finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/top-10-tips-for-effective-green-screen-filming, checked 2026-10-24]
2977. **Shoot at the highest resolution** — more recorded detail keys more accurately, and spare resolution lets you crop and reframe as an emergency fix when the key fails near the frame edge. **How:** shoot 4K for a 1080p delivery when a key is planned. [Filmdaft, https://filmdaft.com/guide-how-to-shoot-and-key-green-screen-footage/, checked 2026-10-24] [Finchley Studio, https://finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/top-10-tips-for-effective-green-screen-filming, checked 2026-10-24]
2978. **Match depth of field to the plate** — the depth of field of the screen shot should match the background, which can mean a larger depth of field than usual. **How:** if the plate is deep-focus, stop down on the subject; if the plate is soft, blur it with `gblur` before the `overlay`. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2979. **Rehearse for shadows and test-key on set** — do a dry run of the whole move to catch shadows and green flashes, and test-key a sample clip before the full shoot. **How:** pull a 5 s test clip and key it here with `chromakey` the same day. [Artlist (Daniela Bowker), https://artlist.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-green-screen-lighting/, checked 2026-10-24] [Finchley Studio, https://finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/top-10-tips-for-effective-green-screen-filming, checked 2026-10-24]
2980. **Tracking markers for a moving camera** — tape crosses, a painted grid or tennis balls on the screen give the software points to compute the camera move so the background matches perspective and motion. **How:** put markers on the screen (not the subject) and remove them later with the garbage matte or keyer. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2981. **Keep a real shadow on purpose** — shadowed areas of the screen can be replaced with a darker version of the new background so the subject seems to cast a shadow. **How:** needs a soft matte where the shadow is: use `blend` in `chromakey` and check the shadow region in the alpha. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2982. **Shoot a clean plate** — capturing the background without actors first lets you cancel it per pixel (a difference matte), but objects similar to the background vanish and camera noise or small changes leave background behind. **How:** record 3 s of empty set from the locked camera before the take; use it with the difference-key skills below. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.DifferencePlugin.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2983. **Reflective props pick up the screen** — green or blue screens caused problems on reflective armour and gave no interactive light (one reason The Mandalorian used LED walls); green screen stays the practical choice for most shoots. **How:** cover or dull reflective props, or plan to roto and grade them. [Filmdaft, https://filmdaft.com/guide-how-to-shoot-and-key-green-screen-footage/, checked 2026-10-24]

## ffmpeg keyers: options and patterns

2984. **Sample the real screen colour** — key on the colour the camera recorded, not on pure 0x00FF00: average a patch of clean screen and print its RGB. **How:** `ffmpeg -v error -i green.png -vf "crop=40:40:0:0,scale=1:1:flags=area" -f rawvideo -pix_fmt rgb24 - | od -An -tu1` printed `0 175 62` for a 0x00b140 screen with noise (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#crop, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#scale, checked 2026-10-24]
2985. **chromakey output carries alpha** — the chromakey output stream is yuva420p, so it can go straight into `overlay`, `alphaextract` or an alpha codec. **How:** `ffprobe -v error -f lavfi -i "movie=green.mp4,chromakey=0x00b140:0.1" -show_entries stream=pix_fmt -of csv=p=0` printed `yuva420p` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chromakey, checked 2026-10-24]
2986. **chromakey on exact YUV values** — with `yuv=1` the colour is read as YUV hex, so you can pass exact Y/U/V values from a scope; colour names like green no longer make sense then. **How:** `chromakey=color=0x8C2C24:similarity=0.1:yuv=1,format=yuva420p` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chromakey, checked 2026-10-24]
2987. **Doc example: key over a black plate** — the ffmpeg docs overlay a green-screen video on a black background with `chromakey=0x70de77:0.1:0.2`; give the lavfi colour source a duration so the job ends. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i color=c=black:s=320x180:d=2 -i green.mp4 -shortest -filter_complex "[1:v]chromakey=0x70de77:0.1:0.2[ckout];[0:v][ckout]overlay[out]" -map "[out]" out.mkv` (without `:d=` on the colour source the run did not end here) (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chromakey, checked 2026-10-24]
2988. **Animate key settings with sendcmd** — chromakey (and colorkey, hsvkey, lumakey, despill, backgroundkey) accept their options as commands, so similarity can change at a given time when the light changes mid-shot. **How:** `-vf "sendcmd=c='1.0 chromakey similarity 0.3',chromakey=0x00b140:0.1:0.05,format=yuva420p"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chromakey, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sendcmd_002c-asendcmd, checked 2026-10-24]
2989. **colorkey defaults to black** — colorkey's default colour is black, so it can drop a dark background from stock elements shot on black. **How:** `colorkey=color=black:similarity=0.1:blend=0.3,format=rgba` on a glow-on-black clip (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#colorkey, checked 2026-10-24] [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/keying/luminance_key.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2990. **lumakey a black background** — threshold=0 keys the darks, the job for elements shot on black (explosions, smoke, debris); for things that emit light, a Screen or Add blend usually looks better than a key. **How:** `lumakey=threshold=0:tolerance=0.05:softness=0.2` then `overlay` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#lumakey, checked 2026-10-24] [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/keying/luminance_key.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2991. **backgroundkey for a static background** — backgroundkey turns a static background into transparency (threshold for scene-change detection, default 0.08; similarity 0.1; blend 0); it exists in this ffmpeg 6.1. **How:** `-vf "backgroundkey=threshold=0.08:similarity=0.1:blend=0.05,format=yuva420p" -c:v png -f nut out.nut` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#backgroundkey, checked 2026-10-24]
2992. **Keep one colour: chromahold/colorhold/hsvhold** — the hold filters remove colour from everything except one colour (chromahold in YUV, colorhold in RGB, hsvhold by HSV range); `blend` 0 makes pixels fully grey or not at all. **How:** `chromahold=color=red:similarity=0.2:blend=0.1`, `colorhold=color=0xff0000:similarity=0.3:blend=0.1`, `hsvhold=hue=0:sat=0.8:val=0.8:similarity=0.3:blend=0.1` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chromahold, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#colorhold, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#hsvhold, checked 2026-10-24]
2993. **despill a blue screen** — for blue screen set type=blue and blue=-1 (green scale back to 0). **How:** `despill=type=blue:blue=-1:green=0` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#despill, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.Despill.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2994. **What the spillmap mix means** — in the Natron Despill (same parameter set, after Steve Wright's unspill) mix 0 limits green by blue, 0.5 by the average of red and blue, 1 by red; expand lowers the subtracted values to remove leftover spill. **How:** try `despill=type=green:mix=0` and `mix=1` against the default 0.5 on a skin close-up (tested here 2026-10-24) [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.Despill.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#despill, checked 2026-10-24]
2995. **Despill brightness and colour push** — despill can add red/blue into the spill area and set its brightness while trying to keep colours, instead of only removing green. **How:** `despill=type=green:brightness=0.3:red=0.2` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#despill, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.Despill.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2996. **Look at the spillmap** — with alpha=1 despill writes the generated spillmap into alpha, which shows where spill is being removed. **How:** `-vf "format=rgba,despill=type=green:alpha=1,alphaextract"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#despill, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.Despill.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2997. **Average-limit despill by expression** — Blender's Color Spill 'Average' limit uses the average of the other two channels as the limit for the spill channel; in ffmpeg that is g = min(g, (r+b)/2). **How:** `-vf "format=gbrp,geq=r='r(X,Y)':g='min(g(X,Y),(r(X,Y)+b(X,Y))/2)':b='b(X,Y)'"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/keying/color_spill.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#geq, checked 2026-10-24]
2998. **The simplest spill fix** — Wikipedia gives a very simple spill function g(r,g,b) = (r, min(g,b), b), close to analogue/film screen pulling. **How:** `-vf "format=gbrp,geq=r='r(X,Y)':g='min(g(X,Y),b(X,Y))':b='b(X,Y)'"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#geq, checked 2026-10-24]
2999. **Colour-difference key by expression** — the simplest green-screen alpha is f = A(r+b) - Bg with A and B user constants defaulting to 1; modern keyers use nested surfaces in RGB space. **How:** alpha from geq: `format=rgba,geq=r='r(X,Y)':g='g(X,Y)':b='b(X,Y)':a='clip(255*((r(X,Y)+b(X,Y))-g(X,Y))/255*4,0,255)'` (the *4 is a gain to taste) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#geq, checked 2026-10-24]
3000. **PIK / IBK key formula in numpy** — PIK's green key is alpha = 0 if (Ag - Ar*rw - Ab*gbw) < 0 else 1 - (Ag - Ar*rw - Ab*gbw)/(Bg - Br*rw - Bb*gbw), A = (denoised) foreground, B = screen colour or clean plate, weights default 0.5; screen subtraction removes (1-alpha) x screen colour. **How:** implement on a float RGB frame with Bc = mean of a clean screen patch, then `fg - (1-alpha)*Bc` for the despilled fill (/home/user/tools/venv/bin/python, numpy) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.PIK.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/ibkgizmo.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3001. **Lower a weight to fix a discoloured edge** — in PIK/IBK a discoloured edge after screen subtraction is not spill but a matte that is too strong; lower the weight of that colour (red edge: lower red weight). **How:** in the numpy PIK, drop rw from 0.5 to 0.3 and compare the edge. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.PIK.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/ibkgizmo.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3002. **Clean-plate keying for uneven screens** — IBK/PIK use an image of the screen (clean plate, or one generated by IBKColor/PIKColor) instead of one picked colour, which gives good results on uneven blue/green screens. **How:** use a shot clean plate (or `tmedian` over a long empty stretch) as B in the numpy PIK formula, per pixel instead of one colour. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/ibkgizmo.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.PIK.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/fr.inria.PIKColor.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3003. **Key a denoised copy, keep the original colour** — feed a denoised or pre-blurred copy to the keyer to pull the matte, and use the original for colour (PIK's PFg input; Keylight's Screen PreBlur for grainy footage). **How:** `[0:v]split[o][k];[k]hqdn3d=4:3:6:4,chromakey=0x00b140:0.12:0.05,format=yuva444p,alphaextract[m];[o][m]alphamerge` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.PIK.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/keylight.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#hqdn3d, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#alphamerge, checked 2026-10-24]
3004. **Screen balance starting points** — Keylight's Screen Balance: blue screens tend to work best around 0.95, green around 0.5; if not, try 0.05, 0.5 and 0.95. **How:** there is no balance option in ffmpeg's keyers; in the numpy colour-difference key, vary the red/blue weights the same way. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/keylight.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3005. **Multi-pass keying** — shots that one key can't handle are keyed in several passes on different parts and combined (Keylight Intermediate Result, Natron 'Add to Inside Mask'). **How:** two chromakeys on two sampled screen colours, mattes combined with the darker (more transparent) value: `[0:v]split=3[o][a][b];[a]chromakey=0x00b140:0.10:0.02,format=yuva444p,alphaextract[m1];[b]chromakey=0x00c060:0.10:0.02,format=yuva444p,alphaextract[m2];[m1][m2]blend=all_mode=darken[m];[o][m]alphamerge` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/keylight.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.ChromaKeyerPlugin.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]
3006. **Uneven screen: sample several screen colours** — Blender's Keying Screen builds a gradient plate from colours sampled at tracks across the screen so each area keys against its own shade, instead of widening tolerance (which also eats the foreground). **How:** sample 3-4 patches with the crop+scale=1:1 command; if they differ a lot, use multi-pass keys or the clean-plate PIK formula. [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/keying/keying_screen.html, checked 2026-10-24]

## Alpha: straight, premultiplied, Porter-Duff

3033. **The over formula** — straight over: alpha_o = a_a + a_b(1-a_a), C_o = (C_a a_a + C_b a_b (1-a_a))/alpha_o; premultiplied over: C_o = C_a + C_b(1-a_a). **How:** checked numerically in numpy: both forms give (0.1, 0.45, 0.4) for green@0.5 over opaque blue-grey (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia Alpha compositing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_compositing, checked 2026-10-24]
3034. **Why premultiply** — premultiplied alpha blends, interpolates and filters correctly; filtering straight alpha leaks RGB from fully transparent areas into borders. Porter-Duff ops are simple only in premultiplied form. **How:** premultiply before blurring or scaling a cutout, unpremultiply before colour work. [Wikipedia Alpha compositing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_compositing, checked 2026-10-24]
3035. **premultiply / unpremultiply in ffmpeg** — premultiply applies the alpha premultiply using the first plane of a second stream, or the frame's own alpha with inplace=1; unpremultiply reverses it. **How:** `chromakey=...,format=yuva444p,premultiply=inplace=1` and `format=yuva444p,unpremultiply=inplace=1` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#premultiply, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#unpremultiply, checked 2026-10-24]
3036. **Tell overlay the alpha is premultiplied** — overlay has an `alpha` option, straight or premultiplied (default straight in this 6.1 build; newer docs add auto). **How:** `[fg]` premultiplied, then `[0:v][fg]overlay=alpha=premultiplied:format=yuv444` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24]
3037. **Overlay in 4:4:4 for clean edges** — overlay's output format defaults to yuv420; format=yuv444 (or rgb/gbrp/auto) avoids subsampling the composite inside the filter. **How:** `[0:v][fg]overlay=format=yuv444[v]` then `-pix_fmt yuv420p` for delivery (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24]
3038. **Emission with no occlusion** — premultiplied alpha can hold additive pixels (flame, glitter) next to normal ones, written as RGB with alpha 0; straight alpha cannot represent that. **How:** for fire overlays use an additive blend (`blend=all_mode=addition`) instead of an alpha cutout. [Wikipedia Alpha compositing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_compositing, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]
3039. **Porter-Duff operators** — over A+B(1-a), under, in Ab, mask Ba, out A(1-b), stencil B(1-a), atop Ab+B(1-a), xor A(1-b)+B(1-a); 'in' and 'out' act like clipping with the second image's alpha. **How:** over = ffmpeg overlay; in/out = alphamerge with a matte or its `negate`. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.MergePlugin.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia Alpha compositing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_compositing, checked 2026-10-24]
3040. **Disjoint and conjoint over** — Disjoint over assumes the background was held out by the foreground (cleaner edges with many layers); conjoint assumes they overlap. Formulas: conjoint A+B(1-a)/b, A if a>b; disjoint A+B(1-a)/b, A+B if a+b<1. **How:** use these terms when stacking several cutouts that share edges. [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/color/alpha_over.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.MergePlugin.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3041. **RGB-only inputs are opaque** — in Natron's Merge an input with only RGB has alpha treated as one, so the output is fully opaque; non-zero RGB with zero alpha is not valid premultiplied RGBA. **How:** add `format=yuva420p` and a real matte before a merge that should stay transparent. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.MergePlugin.html, checked 2026-10-24]

## Tracking: points, objects and stabilised overlays

3070. **Tracks are 2D paths of features** — a feature is a point the tracker can lock onto (bright/dark spots, edges, corners); followed through frames it becomes a series of 2D coordinates, a track, usable for 2D motion tracking or for solving 3D. Template matching with NCC score is a common method. **How:** think of every tracker output here as one (x, y) per frame, saved to CSV/JSON in the scratchpad. [Wikipedia Match moving, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_moving, checked 2026-10-24]
3071. **One point is never enough for a camera solve** — each tracked point narrows the possible camera parameters; many tracks plus a known focal length make the 3D solve tractable; errors call for statistical methods (bundle adjustment). **How:** for 3D camera solves use a dedicated solver (Blender, missing here); this repo stays with 2D tracks. [Wikipedia Match moving, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_moving, checked 2026-10-24]
3072. **Stabilize, match-move, remove or add jitter** — a tracker's output can stabilise the plate, match-move another image to the track, remove jitter (smooth high frequencies) or add jitter (apply the shake to another image). **How:** choose the mode first; in ffmpeg vid.stab stabilises, `invert=1` reverses the transforms. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/fr.inria.built-in.Tracker.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#vidstabtransform, checked 2026-10-24]
3073. **Reference frame and fitting error** — the reference frame is where the exported transform is identity; the fitting error (px) per frame flags a poor model choice (Natron warns at 1 px by default); a robust model drops points that do not fit. **How:** pick the frame where the insert is placed as reference; print per-frame reprojection error from findHomography inliers. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/fr.inria.built-in.Tracker.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3074. **MIL tracker (built into OpenCV)** — TrackerMIL trains an online classifier to separate object from background; init(frame, box) then update(frame) returns (ok, box). ok False may mean the target is really out of sight, not a failure. **How:** `t=cv2.TrackerMIL_create(); t.init(f0,(x,y,w,h)); ok,bb=t.update(f)` (OpenCV 5.0.0 in the venv) (tested here 2026-10-24) [OpenCV header tracking.hpp, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/modules/video/include/opencv2/video/tracking.hpp, checked 2026-10-24]

3102. **CornerPin vs Transform export** — exported to a CornerPin, four or more tracks keep more information and are more stable than a Transform. **How:** store all four corner tracks per frame, not only a centre. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/fr.inria.built-in.Tracker.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3103. **Plane track mask vs image output** — Blender's Plane Track Deform outputs the warped image and a Plane mask; using the warped image moves and skews it, using the original with the Plane mask only shows the part inside the mask. **How:** decide which you need: a pinned insert (warp) or a window onto a fixed image (mask). [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/tracking/plane_track_deform.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3104. **Motion blur on a planar insert** — Plane Track Deform can add motion blur from the plane's motion (samples, shutter in frames; samples from the next frame only, so the blur leads slightly). **How:** ffmpeg's perspective filter has no motion-blur option (its options are the corners, interpolation, sense and eval), so compare the insert with the plate's own blur in snapshots. [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/tracking/plane_track_deform.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tmix, checked 2026-10-24]
3105. **Blur only a tracked region** — feedback crops a region to its second output, lets you filter it, and puts it back on the frame; the docs blur a 100x100 corner with gblur. **How:** `[0:v][fbin]feedback=x=100:y=40:w=100:h=80[out][fbout];[fbout]gblur=sigma=8[fbin]` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#feedback, checked 2026-10-24]
3106. **Variable blur by a matte** — varblur blurs by a second stream that sets the radius per pixel (min_r 0, max_r default 8). **How:** `[a][r]varblur=max_r=12` with a negated matte as the radius map (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#varblur, checked 2026-10-24]
3107. **Phone-screen and device blocks** — HyperFrames has device scenes instead of tracking real footage: `filmed-screen` (scene filmed off a screen with perspective and DOF), `device-frame-stage`, `multi-device-splay`, `yt-screen-warp` (makes footage read as playing on a physical display). **How:** `npx hyperframes catalog --query "screen replacement device mockup"` then `npx hyperframes add filmed-screen` [repo: npx hyperframes catalog v0.8.191, checked 2026-10-24]

## Cleanup: logos, objects, borders

3108. **delogo only for part of the clip** — the docs show a time-limited delogo: split, trim the delogo branch, and overlay it back with eof_action=pass; the timeline enable option is simpler. **How:** `split[m][d];[d]trim=start=0.5:end=1.5,delogo=...[dl];[m][dl]overlay=eof_action=pass` or `delogo=...:enable='between(t,0.5,1.5)'` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#delogo, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24]
3109. **OpenCV inpaint: Telea vs Navier-Stokes** — cv2.inpaint fills masked pixels from the boundary inward (INPAINT_TELEA, fast marching) or by continuing isophotes (INPAINT_NS); the mask is 8-bit, non-zero = fill; usable on stills or video frames. **How:** `cv2.inpaint(frame,mask,3,cv2.INPAINT_TELEA)` (tested here 2026-10-24) [OpenCV tutorial Inpainting, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/doc/py_tutorials/py_photo/py_inpainting/py_inpainting.markdown, checked 2026-10-24] [OpenCV header photo.hpp, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/modules/photo/include/opencv2/photo.hpp, checked 2026-10-24]
3110. **Seamless clone modes** — seamlessClone blends a source region into a destination: NORMAL_CLONE keeps the source look, MIXED_CLONE mixes source structure with destination texture (good with loose selections), MONOCHROME_TRANSFER swaps textures; *_WIDE variants use the whole mask area. **How:** `cv2.seamlessClone(src,dst,mask,(cx,cy),cv2.NORMAL_CLONE)`; all six flags exist in this OpenCV 5.0.0 (tested here 2026-10-24) [OpenCV header photo.hpp, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/modules/photo/include/opencv2/photo.hpp, checked 2026-10-24] [LearnOpenCV Seamless Cloning, https://learnopencv.com/seamless-cloning-using-opencv-python-cpp/, checked 2026-10-24]
3111. **Poisson local edits** — illuminationChange (alpha, beta 0-2; defaults 0.2/0.4) relights a masked area or reduces specular glare, colorChange multiplies channels (0.5-2.5) inside a mask, textureFlattening washes out texture. **How:** `cv2.illuminationChange(img,mask,alpha=0.2,beta=0.4)` (tested here 2026-10-24) [OpenCV header photo.hpp, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/modules/photo/include/opencv2/photo.hpp, checked 2026-10-24]
3112. **Clean plate from a temporal median** — tmedian picks the median of successive frames (radius up to 127; percentile 0 = min, 1 = max), so moving things drop out of a locked shot. **How:** `tmedian=radius=15` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tmedian, checked 2026-10-24]
3113. **Fill garbage at the frame edge** — fillborders fills N pixels at each border without changing size (smear, mirror and other modes). **How:** `fillborders=left=4:right=4:top=2:bottom=2:mode=smear` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#fillborders, checked 2026-10-24]

## Glow, bloom and light wrap

3116. **Glare controls to copy** — Blender's Glare node: threshold (minimum luminance that glows), smoothness (gradual transition), clamp maximum (consistent bloom over varied highlights), strength, saturation and tint, size (1 = whole image); types bloom, fog glow (more physical, slower), streaks, ghosts, simple star, sun beams, kernel. **How:** in ffmpeg the same parts are a highlight cut (`lutyuv=y='if(gt(val,200),val,16)'`), a big `gblur` and a `blend=all_mode=screen` back over the frame. [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/filter/glare.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3117. **Nuke Glow controls** — Glow brightens bright areas with a blur: tolerance (low threshold), saturation, effect only, a box (fastest) or gaussian (smoothest) filter, quality by downscale, and a mask. **How:** 'effect only' means rendering only the blurred-highlight branch and compositing it later. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/filter_nodes/glow.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3118. **ffmpeg blend mode 'glow'** — blend has a mode named glow among about 40 modes (addition, screen, lighten, softlight, vividlight...); the first input is the top layer. **How:** `[g][a]blend=all_mode=glow` with a blurred copy as top (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]
3119. **Blend opacity** — all_opacity (or c0_opacity...) sets how much of the blend mode applies, only together with a mode. **How:** `blend=all_mode=screen:all_opacity=0.6` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]
3120. **Light wrap controls (Nuke)** — LightWrap spills background light onto the foreground's edges; apply it to the foreground before the merge. Diffuse (default 15) spread and Intensity are balanced together; FGBlur 1 sets matte blur, BGBlur 0 background blur, Highlight Merge plus gives a glow; options: wrap only, disable luminance-based wrap, constant colour, enable glow. **How:** start with Diffuse low, raise Intensity, then return to Diffuse. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/draw_nodes/lightwrap.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3121. **Light wrap in ffmpeg** — blurred background inside a thin band just inside the matte edge, laid over the composite. **How:** edge = matte x blurred inverted matte; wrap = blurred bg with that alpha: `[fa]alphaextract,split[m1][m2];[m1]negate,gblur=sigma=6[inv];[m2][inv]blend=all_mode=multiply[edge];[0:v]format=yuv444p,gblur=sigma=10[bgb];[bgb][edge]alphamerge[wrap];[base][fg]overlay[comp];[comp][wrap]overlay` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/draw_nodes/lightwrap.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#alphamerge, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]
3122. **Use background luminance on edges** — IBK/PIK can let the new background affect edge colour and brightness (use bkg luminance/chroma), which also hides fringing; grade the bg input up or down just before it to tune. **How:** ffmpeg's keyers have no such option; the tested light-wrap command above is the nearest stand-in here. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/ibkgizmo.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.PIK.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3123. **Glow from a roto shape** — a matte can guide a glow: the lightsaber blades were roto'd from sticks, enlarged and glowed. **How:** dilate + heavy gblur the matte, tint it, and screen it over the plate. [Wikipedia Rotoscoping, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotoscoping, checked 2026-10-24]
3124. **Screen or Add for light on black** — for elements that emit light shot on a dark background (fire), a Screen or Add mix beats a key. **How:** `[1:v][0:v]blend=all_mode=addition` or screen in gbrp (tested here 2026-10-24) [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/keying/luminance_key.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]
3125. **Screen in RGB for fire** — screen fire over a plate in RGB planes so the mode works on R, G, B, then convert back for delivery. **How:** `[0:v]format=gbrp[b];[1:v]format=gbrp[f];[f][b]blend=all_mode=screen,format=yuv420p` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]

## Lens flares and lens artefacts

3126. **What a lens flare is** — light scattered inside the lens (internal reflections, forward scatter) causes visible artefacts, often aperture-shaped (6 blades -> hexagons), in a row across the image, plus veiling glare that lowers contrast and saturation; flares move with the camera relative to the light and fade as it points away. **How:** place flare elements on a line through the light and the frame centre, and lower contrast slightly while the flare is strong. [Wikipedia Lens flare, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_flare, checked 2026-10-24]
3127. **Anamorphic flare streaks** — anamorphic lenses make horizontal line flares, common with car headlights at night and part of the 'film look'. **How:** tested stand-in (look not judged): a horizontal directional blur of the highlights, screened back: `[b]dblur=angle=0:radius=40[s];[a][s]blend=all_mode=screen` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia Lens flare, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_flare, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#dblur, checked 2026-10-24]
3128. **Cheap flare = textures on a line** — game-style flares draw starburst, ring and disc textures over the image and move them as the light moves; adding a flare implies an unedited photo, but overuse is criticised (Abrams admitted his were overdone). **How:** one soft disc at the light with a geq gaussian: `geq=lum='255*gauss((X/W-0.7)*12)*gauss((Y/H-0.3)*12)/gauss(0)/gauss(0)'`, screened over the plate (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia Lens flare, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_flare, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#geq, checked 2026-10-24]
3129. **Flare elements as donuts** — Nuke's Flare is a donut with inner, middle and outer radii, ring and inner colours (equal colours make discs), chroma spread (red out, blue in), edge flattening for polygonal flares, and repeat flares randomised along the centre-to-light line; 'offset' places a flare between centre (0) and light (1). **How:** a ring in ffmpeg: `geq=lum='clip(255*exp(-pow((hypot(X-220,Y-50)-18)/5,2)),0,255)'` screened over the plate (tested here 2026-10-24) [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/draw_nodes/flare.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#geq, checked 2026-10-24]
3130. **Ghosts mirrored through the centre** — reposition flare elements along the imaginary line between the light position and the image centre. **How:** a second, dimmer disc at centre - (light - centre) x 0.6 in the same geq expression (tested here 2026-10-24) [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/draw_nodes/flare.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#geq, checked 2026-10-24]
3131. **Flare visibility mask** — a visibility mask limits the flare (white = full, black = off) and is blurred by a radius, so the flare dims when the light goes behind something. **How:** multiply the flare layer by a matte of the light source before the screen blend. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/draw_nodes/flare.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3132. **HyperFrames flare and light blocks** — the registry has `lens-flare` (WebGPU star burst and rainbow halo), `vfx-anamorphic-flare` (points with long blue-violet streaks), `godrays`, `sun-burst`, `light-edge`, `light-sweep-pass`, `light-leak` and `organic-light-leak-overlay`. **How:** `npx hyperframes catalog --query "lens flare"`, then `npx hyperframes add lens-flare` [repo: npx hyperframes catalog v0.8.191, checked 2026-10-24]
3133. **Ripple distortion with displace** — displace moves pixels by two map streams (x and y; 128 = no move); the docs build ripple and wave maps with geq sine expressions; edge blank/smear (default)/wrap/mirror. **How:** `[0][1][2]displace` with `nullsrc,lutrgb=128:128:128` and a geq sine map (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#displace, checked 2026-10-24]
3134. **Heat-haze and distortion blocks** — for distortion transitions the registry has `thermal-distortion` (heat haze), `ripple-waves`, `swirl-vortex` and `transitions-distortion`. **How:** `npx hyperframes catalog --query "heat distortion"` [repo: npx hyperframes catalog v0.8.191, checked 2026-10-24]

3143. **Blend modes off the 3D world** — mix-blend-mode, opacity < 1 and filters on a preserve-3d container flatten the 3D world; put blend modes only in separate 2D overlay compositions above it. **How:** fire or flare layers with `mix-blend-mode: screen` live in their own 2D overlay composition. [repo: CLAUDE.md]

[MDN mix-blend-mode, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/mix-blend-mode, checked 2026-10-24]
3146. **mask-image alpha vs luminance** — mask-image hides parts of an element by the mask's alpha; with mask-mode luminance (default for SVG <mask>) the luminance times alpha is used, so white shows and black hides. **How:** use a PNG matte from rembg/GrabCut as `mask-image: url(matte.png)` with `mask-mode: luminance` for a grey matte. ## More keying controls worth knowing (Keylight, IBK, PIK, ChromaKeyer)

3152. **Blur only the colour before keying** — Blender's Keying pre-process blurs only colour (luminosity intact) to cut colour noise, and it affects the matte only, not the result image. **How:** key a copy whose U/V planes are blurred, apply the matte to the original: `[k]format=yuv444p,gblur=sigma=2:planes=6,chromakey=0x00b140:0.12:0.05,format=yuva444p,alphaextract[m];[o][m]alphamerge` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/keying/keying.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#gblur, checked 2026-10-24]
3153. **IBK screen range** — lower IBK's screen range until it stops changing the background; if too much noise remains in the backing, degrain before pulling the key; this may also harden matte edges. **How:** in ffmpeg, denoise the key branch (`hqdn3d`) rather than raising similarity. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/ibkgizmo.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3154. **IBK luminance match** — luminance match adds a luminance factor that captures transparent foreground areas brighter than the background and can firm up alpha in light areas; pushed too far it eats foreground blacks. **How:** watch the darkest foreground parts in the matte when you add any luma term to a key. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/ibkgizmo.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3155. **IBK autolevels for supersaturated colours** — autolevels removes hard edges from strongly saturated foreground colours (a red subject on blue gives a magenta transition); keep yellow/cyan/magenta boxes for real foreground colours. **How:** in the numpy PIK, check saturated edges separately before trusting a single weight setting. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/ibkgizmo.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3156. **Alpha bias for an impure screen** — when the screen colour is not pure (strong global cast), PIK/Keylight's Alpha Bias normalises colours by a grey-reference colour before computing alpha; pick it from the foreground area that went transparent. Despill Bias (often skin or hair colour) can be set separately. **How:** divide A and B by the bias colour in the numpy PIK before the alpha formula. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.PIK.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/keylight.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3157. **Keylight screen gain** — Screen Gain sets how much screen colour is removed to make the matte; increasing it keys more. **How:** the closest knob in ffmpeg's chromakey is similarity: raise it in small steps. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/keylight.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chromakey, checked 2026-10-24]
3158. **Inside-mask replace colour** — when an inside mask raises alpha, Keylight can leave the despilled image (None), bring back the original pixel (Source) or add a replace colour (Hard Color, or Soft Color which keeps the original luminance). **How:** after a core matte, check the patched area does not show the screen colour. [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/keylight.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3159. **Acceptance and suppression angles** — Natron's ChromaKeyer suppresses foreground colours only inside the acceptance angle (default 120); inside the suppression angle (default 40, at most a third of acceptance) chrominance is zeroed against noise; Key Lift raises fewer background pixels, Key Gain lowered classifies more. **How:** the angle model is Keith Jack's; ffmpeg's chromakey uses a similarity distance instead. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.ChromaKeyerPlugin.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3160. **Pick an output mode on purpose** — keyers can output Intermediate (source colour + key, for multipass), Premultiplied, Unpremultiplied, or a Composite over the background; a premultiplied result must be merged with a premultiplied over. **How:** ffmpeg: straight alpha from chromakey, `premultiply=inplace=1` for premultiplied, `overlay` for the composite. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.ChromaKeyerPlugin.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.PIK.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3161. **PIKColor clean-plate workflow** — feed PIKColor the same (denoised) screen as PIK's PFg, then while watching PIK's alpha lower darks.g (green screen) until the garbage area changes, and back off one step; give both nodes the same inside mask. **How:** the numpy equivalent is building the plate from screen-only pixels before the PIK formula. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/fr.inria.PIKColor.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3162. **Key and despill as two jobs** — separating matte generation from spill correction gives more control: the key's alpha merges the despilled foreground over the new background. **How:** branch: `split[a][b]`, alpha from `[a]chromakey...alphaextract`, colour from `[b]despill`, then `alphamerge`. [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.Despill.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3163. **Why per-pixel colour is not enough** — good keyers use more than the pixel's colour: its position, neighbours, reference images, a statistical colour model and user masks; another family finds a 2D path between foreground and background (active contours). **How:** add garbage/core mattes and a clean plate before pushing similarity further. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
3164. **Angle-based chroma key controls** — Blender's Chroma Key: Minimum is a colour-wheel angle of tolerance, Maximum sets what counts as pure background, Falloff softens the edge. **How:** these map to the idea of similarity (tolerance) and blend (falloff) in ffmpeg's chromakey. [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/keying/chroma_key.html, checked 2026-10-24]

## More compositing building blocks in ffmpeg

3165. **Fade a keyed layer by its alpha** — Blender's Alpha Over fade-in animates the Factor 0 -> 1; in ffmpeg the fade filter's alpha=1 fades only the alpha channel. **How:** `[1:v]chromakey=0x00b140:0.12:0.05,fade=t=in:st=0:d=1:alpha=1[fg];[0:v][fg]overlay` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/color/alpha_over.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#fade, checked 2026-10-24]
3166. **Grain extract and grain merge** — grain-extract is B - A + 0.5 and grain-merge B + A - 0.5, a way to lift texture from one plate and lay it on another; ffmpeg's blend has both modes. **How:** `[1:v][0:v]blend=all_mode=grainextract` and `blend=all_mode=grainmerge` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/net.sf.openfx.MergePlugin.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]
3167. **Graduated blend between two plates** — blend expressions mix two layers by position; the docs' linear transition is A*(X/W)+B*(1-X/W). A vertical version blends a sky plate into the ground plate. **How:** `[0:v][1:v]blend=all_expr='A*(Y/H)+B*(1-Y/H)'` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend, checked 2026-10-24]
3168. **Soft radial matte** — the geq docs build a radial gradient with gauss(); use it as a soft round matte for maskedmerge. **How:** `nullsrc=s=320x180:d=2,geq=lum='255*gauss((X/W-0.5)*3)*gauss((Y/H-0.5)*3)/gauss(0)/gauss(0)',format=gray` then `[a][b][m]maskedmerge` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#geq, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#maskedmerge, checked 2026-10-24]
3169. **Transparent colour layer** — the overlay docs add a translucent colour layer with color=red@.3 at the main input's size, a quick way to tint a plate toward the foreground's light. **How:** `color=color=0x203060@0.3:size=320x180:d=2[over];[0:v][over]overlay` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24]
3170. **Premultiply by a separate matte** — premultiply can use the first plane of a second stream as alpha (same size and pixel format). **How:** `[0:v]format=yuv444p[c];[1:v]format=yuv444p[m];[c][m]premultiply` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#premultiply, checked 2026-10-24]
3171. **Clone a person with a locked-off split** — in-camera multiple exposure records one part of the frame at a time (Buster Keaton played nine people at once); the actions must match. With a locked camera, two takes and a split do the same. **How:** `[1:v]crop=160:180:0:0[l];[0:v][l]overlay=x=0:y=0` (left half of take B over take A) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia Compositing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositing, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#crop, checked 2026-10-24]
3172. **Static split matte for a set extension** — a static matte has the same shape every frame (actors in front of a store, a city above the roof: one mask above the roofline, one below); a travelling matte changes per frame. **How:** soft horizontal split: `geq=lum='if(lt(Y,80),255,0)',format=gray,gblur=sigma=3` as the maskedmerge mask (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia Matte (filmmaking), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matte_(filmmaking), checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#maskedmerge, checked 2026-10-24]
3173. **Pull the alpha plane with extractplanes** — extractplanes accepts 'a', so it can pull alpha as a grey stream like alphaextract. **How:** `format=rgba,extractplanes=a` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#extractplanes, checked 2026-10-24]
3174. **Chain overlays in cascade** — the overlay docs build a 2x2 layout by chaining overlays, each adding one layer to the previous result (test the speed of long chains). **How:** `[0:v][c0]overlay=0:0[m0];[m0][c1]overlay=100:0[m1];...` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24]
3175. **Float EXR for pre-rendered plates** — Nuke recommends EXR for a pre-rendered IBKColor plate because 8-bit formats lose colour information; ffmpeg can write EXR. **How:** `ffmpeg -i plate.png -vf format=gbrapf32le -c:v exr plate.exr` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Foundry Nuke 14.1 docs, https://learn.foundry.com/nuke/14.1/content/reference_guide/keyer_nodes/ibkgizmo.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3176. **Where seamlessClone puts the patch** — p is where the centre of the source is placed in the destination; an empty mask means all-non-zero; the result has the destination's size and type. **How:** `cv2.seamlessClone(src,dst,mask,(160,90),cv2.NORMAL_CLONE)` (tested here 2026-10-24) [OpenCV header photo.hpp, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/modules/photo/include/opencv2/photo.hpp, checked 2026-10-24]
3177. **Tracker trade-offs from the refresh** — MIL updates a classifier online and reduces small box errors, but wrong self-updates can drift and full occlusion or moves beyond the search window are hard; NanoTrack keeps a fixed template and searches locally; a returned box is not proof of accuracy. **How:** re-initialise MIL on a fresh box after occlusions and check boxes against frames. [LearnOpenCV Object Tracking, https://learnopencv.com/object-tracking-using-opencv-cpp-python/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Set, screen and HyperFrames helpers

3178. **Retroreflective screen with an LED ring** — a retroreflective curtain lit only by a ring of LEDs around the lens needs no big screen lights or rigging (practical since blue and green LEDs). **How:** an option for small rooms where two softboxes do not fit. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
3179. **Give the talent a monitor** — weather presenters use a field monitor beside the screen to see where their hands are, or a faint image projected onto the screen. **How:** show the presenter the composite on a laptop when they must point at inserted graphics. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
3180. **Patterned backdrop for difference mattes** — a background with a repeating pattern avoids many difference-matte problems and is less sensitive to wardrobe colour than a solid backdrop. **How:** when no green screen exists, shoot a patterned wall plus a clean plate. [Wikipedia Chroma key, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chroma_key, checked 2026-10-24]
3181. **Extend the set beyond the screen** — compositing software can take the screen colour at the backing's edges and extend it to fill the frame, so subjects in a small area can sit in a large virtual vista. **How:** treat everything outside the screen as garbage: black it out in the matte (drawbox on the matte) so the new plate shows there. [Wikipedia Compositing, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositing, checked 2026-10-24]
3182. **Shade the lens against stray flare** — a light just outside the frame causes washed-out haze; a lens hood, a gobo or barn doors on the light, or a filter reduce it. **How:** flag lights that hit the lens on owner shoots; add flares later on purpose instead. [Wikipedia Lens flare, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_flare, checked 2026-10-24]
3183. **Match a handheld plate with camera-shake** — the registry's `camera-shake` gives procedural handheld, telephoto and rig shake for any wrapper (nine measured profiles, seek-safe), useful when a still or keyed layer must sit in a handheld plate. **How:** `npx hyperframes add camera-shake`, then apply it to the wrapper that holds the composite. [repo: npx hyperframes catalog v0.8.191, checked 2026-10-24]
3184. **Feathered spotlight mask block** — `yt-feather-highlight` dims the frame except a feathered ellipse whose position and size are CSS variables, so a helper can glide it between targets. **How:** `npx hyperframes add yt-feather-highlight` [repo: npx hyperframes catalog v0.8.191, checked 2026-10-24]
3185. **Mask-reveal blocks** — `svg-mask-reveal` reveals media through an editable SVG wordmark mask; `texture-mask-text` uses CSS luminance masks (66 masks from ambientCG maps) to cut holes in letters. **How:** `npx hyperframes catalog --query "mask reveal"` [repo: npx hyperframes catalog v0.8.191, checked 2026-10-24]
3186. **Real aperture bokeh pull** — `rack-focus` pulls focus from a near to a far subject with hard-edged aperture bokeh on the defocused one. **How:** `npx hyperframes add rack-focus` when a composite needs a focus shift. [repo: npx hyperframes catalog v0.8.191, checked 2026-10-24]
3187. **SAM point prompts in rembg** — the sam model takes prompts through extra JSON, e.g. a foreground point: `-x '{"sam_prompt": [{"type": "point", "data": [724, 740], "label": 1}]}'`. **How:** needs the sam encoder/decoder download (missing here) and a working rembg CLI. [rembg README (danielgatis), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/danielgatis/rembg/main/README.md, checked 2026-10-24]

## Extra tested patterns

3188. **rembg on OpenCV frames** — remove() also accepts a NumPy array (as cv2.imread gives), so frames read with cv2.VideoCapture go straight in; the result is a 4-channel array. **How:** `out=remove(frame, session=new_session("u2netp"))` returned shape (180, 320, 4) (tested here 2026-10-24) [rembg README (danielgatis), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/danielgatis/rembg/main/README.md, checked 2026-10-24]
3189. **Propagate GrabCut through a clip** — GrabCut accepts a mask initialisation (GC_INIT_WITH_MASK), so the previous frame's labels can seed the next frame as probable foreground/background. **How:** `m=np.where((m==1)|(m==3),cv2.GC_PR_FGD,cv2.GC_PR_BGD).astype(np.uint8); cv2.grabCut(f,m,None,bgd,fgd,2,cv2.GC_INIT_WITH_MASK)`; on the synthetic clip the foreground shrank from 10000 to 5899 px in four frames, so check every few frames (tested here 2026-10-24) [OpenCV tutorial GrabCut, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/doc/py_tutorials/py_imgproc/py_grabcut/py_grabcut.markdown, checked 2026-10-24]
3190. **Evaluate a static overlay position once** — eval=init evaluates x/y only at init (or when a command arrives); t and n are NAN then, so use it only for fixed positions. **How:** `overlay=x=W-w-10:y=H-h-10:eval=init` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24]
3191. **Stop showing the last overlay frame** — repeatlast (default on) extends the last frame of the secondary input past its end; repeatlast=0 lets it disappear. **How:** `[1:v]trim=0:1,...[fg];[0:v][fg]overlay=repeatlast=0` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#framesync, checked 2026-10-24]
3192. **alphamerge inside one -vf chain with movie** — the alphamerge docs read the matte with the movie source inside the filtergraph: `movie=in_alpha.mkv [alpha]; [in][alpha] alphamerge [out]`. **How:** `-vf "movie=matte.mp4,format=gray[alpha];[in][alpha]alphamerge,format=yuva420p"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#alphamerge, checked 2026-10-24]
3193. **Smooth a track before it drives anything** — Natron's Tracker can smooth translation, rotation and scale by averaging N frames and uses a 10-frame jitter period for remove/add jitter; an edge-padded moving average does the same on a tracked CSV. **How:** `np.convolve(np.pad(c,(r,r),'edge'),np.ones(2*r+1)/(2*r+1),'same')[r:-r]` (as in the stabiliser test) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Natron docs, https://natron.readthedocs.io/en/rb-2.5/plugins/fr.inria.built-in.Tracker.html, checked 2026-10-24] [LearnOpenCV video stabilization (updated 2026-07-31), https://learnopencv.com/video-stabilization-using-point-feature-matching-in-opencv/, checked 2026-10-24]
3194. **Filter flare ghosts** — photographic filters can add flare, especially ghosts of bright lights; drop the filter, use a better one, or stop down. **How:** for night shoots with practical lights, take filters off the lens. [Wikipedia Lens flare, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_flare, checked 2026-10-24]
3195. **VitTrack's score tells you when the track is lost** — the opencv_zoo VitTrack model returns confidence values during tracking, and the score drops sharply when the target is lost, while NanoTrack returns about 0.9 in any situation. **How:** needs vitTracker.onnx (missing); with it, `t=cv2.TrackerVit_create(p)` and read `t.getTrackingScore()` per frame. [OpenCV Zoo VitTrack README, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv_zoo/main/models/object_tracking_vittrack/README.md, checked 2026-10-24] [OpenCV header tracking.hpp, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/modules/video/include/opencv2/video/tracking.hpp, checked 2026-10-24]
