# Client and business side of paid video editing
Scope: briefs, quotes and pricing models, contracts, revisions and timecoded feedback rounds, delivery checks, invoicing, file handover, licensing and music rights, releases and disclosure, Fiverr/Upwork practice, Pakistani freelance specifics.
Researched 2026-10-24 via agent-reach (Exa/jina) web reads, WebSearch to find pages, official help centres/docs and local tests (test log: scratchpad/sk2_19/tests.sh). Legal/tax items are summaries of the cited pages, not legal advice; Pakistani tax items from a bank guide and a secondary blog are marked to confirm with FBR/PSEB.

## Briefs, requirements and scope
4881. **Creative brief questions for a video job** — a typical brief covers background, target audience, objectives (and how success is measured), single message, desired viewer action, tone of voice, mandatories (logo, address, phone), deliverables, timeline incl. number of revision rounds, budget, and who approves. **How:** send these as a numbered Roman Urdu/English questionnaire before quoting. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_brief, checked 2026-10-24]
4882. **Use the brief to settle "off-brief" arguments** — the brief is the mutually agreed document to check where a cut diverged from what was asked. **How:** when feedback contradicts the brief, quote the brief line and price the change. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_brief, checked 2026-10-24]
4883. **Video requirement questions (Fiverr's explainer brief)** — ask: product/features to focus on, main concept, business name/contacts/social links, target audience, reference videos, timeline; script ready or needed, platform, format and duration, last-screen content (logo, contact, CTA), voice-over (gender, age, language, accent, tone). **How:** paste these into the gig Requirements tab. [Fiverr Help Center, https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010451397, checked 2026-10-24]
4884. **Requirement questions that prevent revisions** — beyond the two default questions (industry, part of a larger project), add: your expectations, style references, upload logos/files/style guides, what the project will be used for. **How:** add these as questions in the gig's Requirements. [Fiverr Community (Kesha), https://community.fiverr.com/home/blogs/enhancing-your-order-requirements-2025-06-26, checked 2026-10-24]
4885. **One decision-maker on the client side** — AIGA's client responsibilities include coordinating decision-making with other parties (plus supplying content ready to use). **How:** ask the client to name one person who consolidates everyone's notes per round. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4886. **Statement of work sections** — purpose, scope of work, period of performance, deliverables schedule, applicable standards, acceptance criteria, special requirements, payment schedule (upfront vs phased); an SOW is often binding, and a master service agreement can govern several SOWs. **How:** for a repeat client sign one master agreement and a short SOW per video. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_of_work, checked 2026-10-24]
4887. **Objective acceptance criteria** — the SOW's acceptance criteria say how the client decides the work is acceptable, usually with objective criteria. **How:** write e.g. "60 s, 1080x1920, captions burned in, logo end card" so approval isn't a matter of mood. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_of_work, checked 2026-10-24]
4888. **Change orders: add, omit, substitute** — a change order adds, removes or alters agreed work and may change price and/or completion date. **How:** log every client change as a one-line change order (what, price, new date) and get a written OK. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_order, checked 2026-10-24]
4889. **Name scope creep early** — scope creep is uncontrolled growth of scope after a project starts, often from a poorly defined scope, vague documents, or "small, simple" changes that add up; teams also add unasked extras to impress. **How:** write deliverables (length, formats, rounds) in the brief and treat each extra as a priced change. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scope_creep, checked 2026-10-24]
4890. **One-way or mutual NDA** — a unilateral NDA protects one side's disclosures; a bilateral (mutual) NDA protects both. **How:** one-way when only the client shares secrets (e.g. unreleased product footage); mutual when both sides share confidential material. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-disclosure_agreement, checked 2026-10-24]

## Quotes, packages and pricing models
4891. **Fixed price vs time-and-materials** — fixed price = one lump sum for the job regardless of your hours; T&M = paid for time spent (plus materials); many T&M deals add a guaranteed maximum price as a cap. **How:** quote fixed price for a defined reel; quote hourly + cap for open-ended edits like "clean up 3 hours of footage". [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_and_materials, checked 2026-10-24]
4892. **Value-based price** — price set by the client's perceived value and willingness to pay, not by your production cost; works best with a distinct, differentiated, high-quality offer. **How:** for a launch promo that drives sales, price on the outcome for the client rather than hours. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value-based_pricing, checked 2026-10-24]
4893. **Monthly retainer for repeat clients** — a retainer sits between a one-off contract and employment; the fee can be fixed or hourly, a single advance or recurring monthly, and absent agreement otherwise it is refundable if work isn't performed. **How:** for a client wanting e.g. weekly reels, write the monthly fee, included videos and what happens to unused work. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retainer_agreement, checked 2026-10-24]
4894. **Three-tier video packages** — build Basic/Standard/Premium tiers that differ in scope (e.g. length, formats), revisions and extras, each with its own price and delivery time. **How:** Gig edit > Pricing > 3 Packages toggle; packages from $5 up to the tier maximum; upgrades are added at checkout, extras are separate add-ons. [Fiverr Help Center, https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010559138, checked 2026-10-24]
4895. **Standardized category minimums** — some Fiverr categories force a minimum package price/scope (e.g. Video & Animation > 3D Product Animation min $100). **How:** check dashboard notices; fix within 30 days or the gig loses visibility. [Fiverr Help Center, https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010559138, checked 2026-10-24]
4896. **Custom Offer for non-standard edits** — a client whose reel doesn't fit your packages (longer, cheaper, extra formats) gets a Custom Offer instead of you changing gig prices. **How:** reply in Inbox with a Custom Offer at the agreed price/scope; standardized package rules apply to packages only, not custom offers. [Fiverr Help Center, https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010639617, checked 2026-10-24] [Fiverr Help Center, https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010559138, checked 2026-10-24]
4897. **Paid extra for out-of-scope work** — when a revision is really new work (extra format, extra minute), send an order extra. **How:** Activity tab > Offer more extras > custom extra with price and added days (price can't be $0; days may be 0). [Fiverr Help Center, https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010639617, checked 2026-10-24]
4898. **Pad delivery time for revision rounds** — on Fiverr, set the delivery time longer than the real work time so a round or two of revisions doesn't drift far past the due date (which can get flagged as delayed). **How:** e.g. 3 days of work -> list 7-day delivery, as the Fiverr success manager advises. [Fiverr Community (Adrian Rodrigues, Senior Success Manager), https://community.fiverr.com/public/blogs/navigating-client-revisions-while-protecting-your-success-score-metrics-2026-04-09, checked 2026-10-24]
4899. **No "unlimited revisions" packages** — unlimited revisions remove the client's reason to give complete notes and create scattered rounds that hurt the Conflict-Free metric. **How:** set a fixed revision count per package and stick to it. [Fiverr Community (Adrian Rodrigues, Senior Success Manager), https://community.fiverr.com/public/blogs/navigating-client-revisions-while-protecting-your-success-score-metrics-2026-04-09, checked 2026-10-24]
4900. **Gig page sets expectations** — thorough FAQs, package descriptions that say what is and isn't included, a portfolio that matches what the package really delivers, a realistic summary: fewer revisions start here. **How:** don't show a high-end ad reel on a basic $-tier package. [Fiverr Community (Adrian Rodrigues, Senior Success Manager), https://community.fiverr.com/public/blogs/navigating-client-revisions-while-protecting-your-success-score-metrics-2026-04-09, checked 2026-10-24]
4901. **Outside costs billed separately** — AIGA pricing covers the designer's fee only; equipment rental, talent, music licences, hosting etc. are billed to the client unless the proposal says otherwise. **How:** list "music/stock licences billed at cost" as its own line in the quote. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4902. **Out-of-scope changes at an hourly rate** — client changes outside scope are billed time-and-materials at your standard hourly rate, on top of any fixed price, and deadlines may move. **How:** write your hourly change rate into the quote. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4903. **Big changes = new proposal** — if changes exceed a set percentage of the original time/scope, submit a new proposal and don't start until it's signed (and any extra retainer paid). **How:** set the percentage in your terms, e.g. as a blank you fill per client. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4904. **Price for the Upwork fee** — the Freelancer Service Fee is 0-15% per contract, shown on the offer/proposal and locked once sent. **How:** gross rate = wanted net / (1 - fee); Upwork's example: net $20/h at 10% -> charge $22.22/h. `python3 -c "print(round(20/(1-0.10),2))"` -> 22.22 (tested here 2026-10-24) [Upwork Help, https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062538, checked 2026-10-24]
4905. **Fiverr milestones for big edits** — gig milestones need a basic package of at least $100, each milestone at least $50, 2-5 milestones, and only in listed subcategories (e.g. E-Commerce Product Videos, 3D Product Animation); custom offers allow up to 6 milestones regardless. **How:** Inbox > Create an offer > order type Milestones, e.g. 1) rough cut 2) final 3) formats. [Fiverr Help Center, https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360010560178-Working-with-Milestones, checked 2026-10-24]

## Contracts, ownership and legal terms
4906. **Proposal + attached terms = the contract** — AIGA's model is a custom proposal per project with standard terms and conditions attached; together and signed they form the agreement. **How:** keep one reusable terms sheet for edits and write a short proposal (scope, price, schedule) per client. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4907. **Proposal validity window** — state how many days the quote stays valid; if not signed in time, price, terms and deliverables may change. **How:** add "This quote is valid for N days" to every quote. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4908. **Rights only after full payment** — licences/ownership transfer are conditioned on full payment; the designer may withhold deliverables while invoices are overdue. **How:** hold the final master until the invoice is paid, and say so in the terms. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4909. **Client review deadline and written notes** — client agrees to review each deliverable within the stated time and either approve in writing or give written comments clear enough to act on; client delays don't count as your breach. **How:** add "notes in writing (Frame.io/Vimeo comments or numbered list) within N days". [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4910. **Five-business-day acceptance** — under the model's testing-and-acceptance clause, if the client sends no written objection within five business days of receiving a deliverable, it is deemed accepted. **How:** say in the delivery message: "If no notes by <date>, this version counts as approved." [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4911. **Suspension fee for client delays** — when a client stalls the project you reserved time for, the model charges hourly fees for idle time unless you fill that time with other work. **How:** include it in terms for long jobs with fixed slots. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4912. **Client is responsible for final proofreading** — if the client approved a deliverable that still has typos/misspellings, the client pays to correct them; the client also ensures their content/claims are accurate and legal. **How:** ask the client to proof all on-screen text before final approval. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4913. **Portfolio and credit rights** — the model reserves the designer's right to show final and preliminary works in portfolios/websites (unless it contains client confidential info) and to be credited. **How:** write a portfolio clause into the quote; for personal/family footage, never use it (owner rule). [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4914. **Termination fee for convenience** — if the client cancels for convenience, the model pays work done (greater of advance, prorated fee or hourly) plus an early termination fee of 25% of the total project fee, and the client gets no usage rights without consent. **How:** add a cancellation clause to the quote. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4915. **Kill fee for motion work** — AIGA's motion supplement says a lump-sum "kill fee" is standard for motion design/VFX contracts and replaces progress billing if the client cancels an active project. **How:** state a fixed kill fee amount in motion/edit contracts. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4916. **Work stoppage on non-payment** — if the client breaches (e.g. doesn't pay) and doesn't cure within 10 days of written notice, the designer may suspend work until cured and fees/schedule are amended. **How:** send a written breach notice, wait the cure period, then pause. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4917. **Choose the IP option explicitly** — the model offers four IP options: limited-usage licence, exclusive licence, assignment of rights, work made for hire; motion/VFX is typically work made for hire. **How:** tick one option in each contract instead of leaving ownership vague. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4918. **Limited licence fields** — a limited usage licence lists category of use, medium, duration and geographic territory; extra uses need separate pricing. **How:** e.g. "social media ads, 12 months, Pakistan" in the quote. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4919. **Modification right is a choice** — the licence options let you say whether the client may edit/crop/re-cut the final works or must use them as delivered. **How:** tick "may modify" or "original form only" in the IP section. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4920. **Project files stay yours unless sold** — the model reserves preliminary works and working files (project files, drafts) to the designer and has the client return them within 30 days. **How:** quote project-file handover as a separate priced item if the client wants it. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4921. **Third-party materials are licensed, not owned** — third-party materials (stock, music) stay owned by their owners; the designer tells the client which ones need a licence and obtains it for the agreed usage; the client covers missing releases for material it asked to include. **How:** list every third-party asset with its licence in the delivery notes. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4922. **Client warrants its own footage** — the client represents it owns or may use the content it supplies and that it doesn't infringe others' rights. **How:** include this line when clients send footage, logos and music. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4923. **Technical-spec warranty** — the motion supplement warrants delivery meets the client's technical requirements (duration, file format, resolution, colour specs). **How:** get those specs in writing in the brief, then verify with the ffprobe report before delivery. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4924. **"No new live action" clause** — for edit-only jobs, state you won't shoot any new footage. **How:** add "editing of client-supplied footage only; no filming" to the scope. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4925. **Content restrictions clause** — the motion supplement lets you list content you won't work on (e.g. adult, gambling, violence, politics, religion, alcohol) or accept mature content with adult-only staff. **How:** state your no-go list in your gig/terms. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4926. **Not responsible for defects in client footage** — the motion supplement says the designer isn't responsible for defects in client-provided footage or effects. **How:** note "quality depends on supplied footage" in the quote. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4927. **Non-solicitation of your subcontractors** — the model bars the client from hiring your subcontractors for six months, with a 25% commission if they do. **How:** include it if you use helpers on a job. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4928. **Changes authorised by email count** — the model lets invoices include changes/costs the client authorised by email or the project platform, while other modifications need signed writing. **How:** get "yes, go ahead with the extra" in writing before billing extras. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4929. **Your templates stay yours** — under AIGA terms "Designer Tools" (software incl. source code, authoring tools, fonts, application tools) and their IP are owned solely by you; the client gets a non-exclusive, non-transferable licence to use them as part of the final work. **How:** state that HyperFrames blocks, presets and scripts in the edit stay yours. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4930. **Expenses at cost plus markup** — AIGA terms bill incidental out-of-pocket expenses at cost plus your stated markup and travel only with the client's prior approval. **How:** list the markup % in the quote; get travel approval in writing. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4931. **Confidentiality both ways** — each party keeps the other's confidential information secret and uses it only for the project; public-domain info is excluded; confidential info is returned/destroyed at the end. **How:** add this before receiving unreleased product footage. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4932. **Independent contractor and no exclusivity** — you decide the manner and means of the work; the client may hire others and you may work for other clients. **How:** include both lines so a client can't demand exclusivity without paying for it. [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4933. **Originality warranty** — except third-party materials and client content, you warrant the final deliverables are your original work, but you don't run trademark/copyright clearance searches. **How:** say so in the quote when a client asks you to "check if the name is free". [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services (2022 update), https://www.aiga.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Standardformofagreement_2022update.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4934. **"Work made for hire" needs a signed paper** — for a commissioned (non-employee) edit, US law treats the client as author only if the work is in an eligible category (a part of a motion picture/audiovisual work qualifies) AND both parties signed a written agreement expressly calling it a work made for hire; miss any one and it isn't. **How:** if a US client wants full ownership, put an explicit work-made-for-hire clause in the contract and get both signatures. [US Copyright Office Circular 30 (rev. 08/2024), https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4935. **Who owns the edit by default** — unless the contract says otherwise, the commissioned editor is the author; under work-for-hire the commissioning party is author and initial owner. **How:** write in the quote who owns the final video, the project file and raw assets. [US Copyright Office Circular 30 (rev. 08/2024), https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4936. **WMFH term and no take-back** — copyright in a work made for hire lasts 95 years from publication or 120 from creation (whichever first) and the author's termination rights don't apply. **How:** note it when a client asks for a work-made-for-hire clause. [US Copyright Office Circular 30 (rev. 08/2024), https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
4937. **NYC clients: written contract from $800** — under NYC's Freelance Isn't Free Act (Local Law 140 of 2016, effective May 15 2017) a hiring party must give a written contract when work is $800 or more, and pay within 30 days of completion when no date is set; rights include timely full payment and no retaliation, with statutory and double damages. **How:** for NYC clients send the contract before starting and quote the 30-day rule if late. [NYC DCWP, https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/freelance-isnt-free-act.page, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freelance_Isn%27t_Free_Act, checked 2026-10-24]

## Feedback rounds in Frame.io (timecoded notes, shares, approvals)
4938. **Single-frame Frame.io note** — ask the client to pause on the exact frame and type, so every note carries the timecode the editor needs. **How:** tell the client "pause, type in the comment box, Enter"; typing auto-pauses playback; clicking a comment card jumps back to that frame. [Frame.io V4 Knowledge Center, https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105251-commenting-on-your-media, checked 2026-10-24]
4939. **Range comment for a whole scene** — for notes about a stretch (music bed, a full scene), use an A-to-B range comment instead of one frame. **How:** in Frame.io press I to start the range and O to end it, write the note, Send; clicking the card loops only that range, Esc returns to full playback. [Frame.io V4 Knowledge Center, https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105251-commenting-on-your-media, checked 2026-10-24]
4940. **Anchored (pinned) comment on the picture** — when the note is about one spot in the frame (a logo, a face, a typo), the client clicks the picture to pin the note there. **How:** click the white pin icon in the playbar (turns purple), click the spot; works in full screen too and on images/PDFs. [Frame.io V4 Knowledge Center, https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105251-commenting-on-your-media, checked 2026-10-24] [Frame.io Insider, https://blog.frame.io/2024/05/28/frame-io-v4-features-player-and-commenting/, checked 2026-10-24]
4941. **Internal comments the client never sees** — discuss a client note with your team on the same asset without the external reviewer seeing it. **How:** in the comment box switch the tab next to Send from Public Comment to Internal Comment before sending; a lock icon marks it. [Frame.io V4 Knowledge Center, https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105251-commenting-on-your-media, checked 2026-10-24]
4942. **Draw-on-frame annotation** — let the client draw an arrow/box/line on the frame so a vague note ("move that") becomes exact. **How:** write the note first, then click the annotation icon and draw; the annotation shows only when the comment card is clicked, not during normal playback. [Frame.io V4 Knowledge Center, https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105251-commenting-on-your-media, checked 2026-10-24]
4943. **Reference file attached to a note** — client or colourist attaches a reference image/clip to the comment itself. **How:** use Attach Files in the comment (Pro plan or higher, up to 6 attachments per comment; non-media files like ZIPs get no preview). [Frame.io V4 Knowledge Center, https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105251-commenting-on-your-media, checked 2026-10-24]
4944. **General note without a timestamp** — for overall notes ("whole video feels slow") switch off the timestamp so it doesn't pin to a random frame. **How:** click the timestamp toggle next to Send before posting. [Frame.io V4 Knowledge Center, https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105251-commenting-on-your-media, checked 2026-10-24]
4945. **Hashtag notes to sort a feedback round** — tag notes like #music #text #colour and filter the round by tag, so you fix one kind at a time. **How:** type #tag in the comment; use the Hashtag section in the comment filter; hashtags are indexed and suggested later. [Frame.io V4 Knowledge Center, https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9105251-commenting-on-your-media, checked 2026-10-24] [Frame.io Insider, https://blog.frame.io/2024/05/28/frame-io-v4-features-player-and-commenting/, checked 2026-10-24]

5077. **FBR filing threshold for freelancers** — freelancers earning above PKR 600,000 a year must register with FBR and file a return (filing even below it keeps you on the Active Taxpayer List per the second source). **How:** note yearly totals from bank credit advices. [Allied Bank (ABL) guide, https://www.abl.com/?p=292711, checked 2026-10-24] [beancount.io blog (secondary; confirm with FBR/PSEB), https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/13/pakistan-it-freelancers-0-25-percent-pseb-2029-guide, checked 2026-10-24]
5078. **PSEB registration: 0.25% vs 1%** — PSEB-registered IT/IT-enabled exporters get a 0.25% final tax on remittances via banks (reported extended to June 30 2029); unregistered default is 1%. Conditions include remitting at least 80% of export proceeds through formal banking channels. **How:** register with PSEB, renew yearly, keep bank withholding certificates and remittance advices with purpose code. Confirm current rates with FBR/PSEB before advising anyone. [Allied Bank (ABL) guide, https://www.abl.com/?p=292711, checked 2026-10-24] [beancount.io blog (secondary; confirm with FBR/PSEB), https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/13/pakistan-it-freelancers-0-25-percent-pseb-2029-guide, checked 2026-10-24]
5079. **Pakistan National Freelancing Policy (draft)** — a 2023 draft proposed PSEB freelancer registration at Rs 1,000 and Rs 3,500 yearly renewal, SBP freelancer FX accounts retaining 35% of inflows, and an income-tax holiday till 2030 on export income via SBP purpose codes; it was a DRAFT. **How:** treat as background only; check what was enacted before telling anyone. [ProPakistani, https://propakistani.pk/2023/07/13/national-freelancing-policy-draft-includes-incentives-for-registered-freelancers/, checked 2026-10-24]
5080. **PSEB freelancer registration portal** — since Sept 2021 freelancers register with PSEB through MoITT's online portal (pseb.org.pk). **How:** register there before claiming the PSEB-linked tax/bank benefits above. [ProPakistani, https://propakistani.pk/2021/09/17/it-ministry-launches-an-online-portal-for-registration-of-freelancers/, checked 2026-10-24]
5081. **Pakistan: Copyright Ordinance 1962** — copyright covers cinematographic works, records, musical works etc.; the first owner is generally the author (exceptions incl. works for hire); copyright can be assigned (s.14) or licensed (s.35); registration is optional but is prima facie evidence. **How:** for Pakistani clients, put assignment/licence in writing in the contract. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_law_of_Pakistan, checked 2026-10-24]
5082. **Pakistan terms and moral rights** — cinematographic works, records and photographs: 50 years from the year after publication; assigning rights does not assign moral rights (s.62); fair dealing covers research, private study, criticism, review, reporting current events. **How:** don't promise clients US "fair use" for music; rely on licences. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_law_of_Pakistan, checked 2026-10-24]
5083. **Pakistan related rights** — performers' and phonogram producers' rights last 50 years; broadcasting organisations 25 years. **How:** check broadcast/recording rights separately from the composition when a client wants an old TV clip or recording. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_law_of_Pakistan, checked 2026-10-24]

## Music, stock and copyright claims
5084. **CC0 music: no permission, no credit** — CC0 waives copyright worldwide; you may copy, modify, distribute, even commercially, without asking. **How:** prefer CC0 tracks (owner rule: no credit-needed music); note CC0 doesn't clear trademark, publicity or privacy rights. [Creative Commons, https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/, checked 2026-10-24]
5085. **CC BY means a credit line + licence link** — CC BY 4.0 allows commercial use but requires appropriate credit, a link to the licence and noting changes. **How:** because the owner rejects credit-needed music, skip CC BY for paid client videos unless the client accepts a credit. [Creative Commons, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/, checked 2026-10-24]
5086. **YouTube Audio Library "Attribution not required" filter** — for a client's YouTube video, use Audio Library tracks filtered to Attribution not required; they are copyright-safe and won't be Content-ID claimed. **How:** YouTube Studio > Audio library > filter > Attribution not required > Download MP3. CC-licensed Audio Library tracks still need the credit in the description. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882, checked 2026-10-24]
5087. **Monetisation with Audio Library music** — YouTube Partner Program channels can monetise videos that use Audio Library music and SFX. **How:** tell monetising clients YouTube says only Audio Library music is known to it to be copyright-safe and it isn't responsible for "royalty-free" music from other channels/libraries. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882, checked 2026-10-24]
5088. **Searching the YouTube Audio Library** — filter music by title, genre, mood, artist, attribution and duration (seconds), star tracks to a Starred tab, and sort by title/artist/duration/date; new releases arrive twice a month; SFX filter by category and duration. **How:** filter Attribution not required + duration close to the edit length, star candidates and send the client the shortlist. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882, checked 2026-10-24]
5089. **Short over 60 s: music limits** — vertical/square uploads up to 3 min are Shorts; Shorts Audio Library songs mostly allowed up to 90 s (some 60 or 30 s); YouTube Audio Library royalty-free music won't get a Content ID claim; any claimed Short over one minute is blocked globally. **How:** for client Shorts over a minute, use Audio Library "attribution not required" or CC0 music. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877, checked 2026-10-24]
5090. **Content ID claim is not a strike** — a claim (block/monetise/track, can differ by country) affects the video, usually not the channel; strikes come from valid removal requests. **How:** reassure the client, then check Studio > Content > filter Claims > Restrictions > See details > Claimant. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276, checked 2026-10-24]
5091. **Fix a valid claim without re-uploading** — options: Erase song (mute song or all audio), Trim out segment, or Replace song with Audio Library audio; each removes the claim. **How:** use these in YouTube Studio for the client instead of deleting the video. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276, checked 2026-10-24]
5092. **Shorts 1-3 min with a claim are blocked** — any active claim blocks a 1-3-minute vertical Short regardless of policy. **How:** for client Shorts over 60 s, use music that can't be claimed (CC0 or YouTube Audio Library). [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276, checked 2026-10-24]
5093. **Don't dispute without rights** — disputing a claim without a valid reason can lead to a removal request and a copyright strike. **How:** dispute only when you hold the licence (keep the licence PDF/link in the project folder). [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276, checked 2026-10-24]
5094. **Claim dispute timeline** — after a dispute the claimant has 30 days (release, reinstate, removal request, or let it expire); a rejected dispute can be appealed (claimant then has 7 days); "Escalate to Appeal" exists only for claims that block the video; a dispute can't be cancelled. **How:** tell the client the realistic wait before promising the video will be monetised. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797454, checked 2026-10-24]
5095. **Studio edits to claimed videos are permanent** — since June 2025 a saved Studio Editor change can't be reverted; "Erase song" may fail on hard-to-separate music, "Mute all sound in claimed segments" is faster and more likely to clear the claim. **How:** keep the original master on disk before editing in Studio. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2902117, checked 2026-10-24]
5096. **Freesound: pick CC0 for client SFX** — Freesound sounds are CC0 (do almost anything, even sell, but don't claim authorship), CC BY (always credit) or CC BY-NC (credit, no money-making use); old Sampling+ sounds also bar commercial advertising. **How:** for paid client work use only CC0 sounds (owner rule: no credit-needed audio). [Freesound FAQ, https://freesound.org/help/faq/, checked 2026-10-24]
5097. **Freesound credit format (when credit is allowed)** — Freesound's suggested credit lists "sound" by user (link) licensed under <licence>; a long list can point to a credits web page; your account's attribution list shows everything you downloaded. **How:** use the Freesound attribution list to find where each downloaded sound came from. [Freesound FAQ, https://freesound.org/help/faq/, checked 2026-10-24]
5098. **Raw SFX can trigger Content ID** — unedited Freesound effects used inside songs registered with Content ID can cause claims on your video; modified/stretched sounds are less likely to match. Dispute with the Freesound page link and the date proving it predates the claimed song. **How:** keep the source URL of every SFX in the project notes. [Freesound FAQ, https://freesound.org/help/faq/, checked 2026-10-24]
5099. **"No copyright intended" protects nothing** — YouTube: credit lines, "I do not own", "no infringement intended" or "for entertainment only" don't make a use fair; transformativeness and the four factors decide, and Content ID can't judge fair use. **How:** tell clients who want a famous clip/song that a disclaimer won't stop a claim. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148, checked 2026-10-24]
5100. **Other libraries may not cover YouTube** — YouTube warns that some royalty-free/licensing sites don't grant rights to use or monetise on YouTube. **How:** read the library's terms for YouTube/monetisation before using a track in a client channel video; or use the Audio Library. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148, checked 2026-10-24]
5101. **Outside the US it's not "fair use"** — the EU allows specific categories (quotation, criticism, review, caricature, parody, pastiche); other countries use fair dealing. **How:** don't promise Pakistani or UK clients US-style fair use. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148, checked 2026-10-24]
5102. **Four fair-use factors (US)** — purpose/character (non-profit education and transformative uses favoured), nature of the work (factual favoured), amount used (small bits favoured, but not the "heart"), effect on the market. **How:** use this only to explain to clients why a claimed clip is risky; prefer licences. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148, checked 2026-10-24]
5103. **A famous song needs two licences** — syncing a recording needs the composition (publisher) and the master recording (label); producers on tight budgets use covers or commissioned work-for-hire music to avoid the master side. **How:** when a client asks for a hit song, explain both licences and offer CC0/Audio Library/commissioned music instead (owner rule: never download copyrighted songs). [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronization_rights, checked 2026-10-24]
5104. **Pixabay media: free, no credit, but limits** — Pixabay allows free use and modification without attribution, but no standalone resale, no commercial use of content with recognisable trademarks/logos in relation to goods/services, no misleading or illegal use of recognisable people, no use as a trademark. **How:** check stock clips for visible brands before using them in an ad. [Pixabay, https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/, checked 2026-10-24]
5105. **Pexels stock in client ads** — Pexels photos/videos are free, no attribution needed, may be modified; not allowed: identifiable people shown in a bad light, implying endorsement by people/brands in the imagery, reselling unaltered copies, redistributing on stock sites, use in a trademark/business name. **How:** for a client product ad, don't cut a Pexels person into a shot that looks like they endorse the product. [Pexels, https://www.pexels.com/license/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Releases, consent and disclosure labels
5106. **Release is about publishing, not filming** — a model release grants permission to publish someone's image; it is a privacy matter separate from copyright, and the need rises sharply for advertising/commercial use. **How:** for client ad edits ask the client to confirm releases for every recognisable person in their footage before you deliver. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_release, checked 2026-10-24]
5107. **Minor release signed by a parent** — a minor's release must be signed by a parent/legal guardian, otherwise it may give the publisher no protection; group releases add signature lines for several people. **How:** for school/kids footage in a promo, ask for guardian-signed releases. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_release, checked 2026-10-24]
5108. **Free licences don't clear people** — CC0 and CC BY say they don't cover publicity/privacy rights of people in the work. **How:** even a CC0 clip with a recognisable face is risky in a client's commercial ad; prefer shots without identifiable people. [Creative Commons CC0, https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/, checked 2026-10-24] [Creative Commons BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/, checked 2026-10-24]
5109. **Consent to film is not consent to publish** — Wikimedia Commons separates consent to take, to upload/publish and to re-use; private-place shots need consent; minors' consent comes from parents/guardians; many countries need consent for commercial use even in public (its table lists commercial use needing consent in e.g. the UK; India "yes, with exceptions"). **How:** for client ads ask who appears and whether they agreed to an advert, not just to being filmed. [Wikimedia Commons, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Photographs_of_identifiable_people, checked 2026-10-24]
5110. **Paid promotion checkbox for sponsored videos** — if the client's video contains sponsorship/branded content, tick Paid promotion so YouTube adds a disclosure label at the start. **How:** Studio > Upload > Show more > Paid Promotion > "Yes, my video includes branded content"; age/country restrictions appear only after Yes. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235, checked 2026-10-24]
5111. **Disclosure inside the video, not only the description** — FTC: in a video the disclosure should be in the video itself (ideally said and shown), clear words like "ad"/"sponsored", same language as the endorsement, repeated in live streams. **How:** in client influencer edits put an on-screen "Ad"/"Sponsored" caption in the video and have it said in the voice track too. [FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers, checked 2026-10-24]
5112. **Avoid vague disclosure tags** — FTC says don't use "sp", "spon", "collab" or a lone "thanks"/"ambassador", and don't bury the disclosure among hashtags. **How:** write "Sponsored by <brand>" or "#ad" visibly. [FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers, checked 2026-10-24]
5113. **Free product = disclose too** — FTC: a brand relationship includes free or discounted products; if a brand gave free products and the creator then mentions one of its products, disclose even if they weren't asked to mention that product. **How:** for influencer client edits, ask whether the product was gifted and add the disclosure if so. [FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers, checked 2026-10-24]
5114. **Ad scripts: no untried or unprovable claims** — FTC: you can't talk about experience with a product you haven't tried, call it terrific if you thought it terrible, or make claims the advertiser can't prove (e.g. health claims). **How:** flag such lines in a client's voiceover/caption script before editing them in. [FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers, checked 2026-10-24]
5115. **Instagram paid partnership label** — branded content on Instagram must use the paid partnership label tagging the brand; first tag sends the brand an approval request; until approved the label shows without the brand name; brand partner needs a professional account and gets the post's insights. **How:** tell influencer clients to add it in the app when posting your edit. [Instagram Help, https://help.instagram.com/android-app/1109894795810258, checked 2026-10-24]
5116. **AI-use disclosure for realistic AI** — YouTube requires disclosure when AI makes a real person appear to say/do something, alters real events/places, creates a realistic scene that didn't happen, or creates music that is the main focus. **How:** Studio upload > Attributes > AI use > Yes. AI-generated music is on the must-disclose list. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491, checked 2026-10-24]
5117. **What AI edits don't need a label** — colour/lighting filters, background blur, captions, upscaling/repair, voice/audio repair, AI scripts/thumbnails, cloning one's own voice for voiceovers are listed as not needing disclosure. **How:** tell the client which of their edits need the AI-use Yes and which don't. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491, checked 2026-10-24]

## Client channel handover and portfolio
5118. **Get channel access, never the password** — YouTube channel permissions are safer than sharing passwords; roles: Owner, Manager, Editor, Editor (Limited, no revenue data), Subtitle Editor, Viewer. **How:** ask the client to invite you as Editor (Limited) to upload and schedule; invites expire after 30 days; client removes access when done. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9481328, checked 2026-10-24]
5119. **Schedule the client's premiere time** — upload as Private/Scheduled and set date, time and time zone; the watch page "published on" date follows Pacific time. **How:** Studio upload > Schedule card; for a Pakistan launch date near midnight, check the PST date. [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1270709, checked 2026-10-24]
5120. **Showreel length and purpose** — a showreel (demo/sizzle reel) is usually 2-3 minutes of footage from different projects, supplementing a CV. **How:** cut yours to 2-3 min from client work you have permission to show (AIGA-style portfolio clause), never family/personal media. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_reel, checked 2026-10-24]
