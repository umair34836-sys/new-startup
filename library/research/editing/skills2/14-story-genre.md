## Feature-film beat sheets scaled to video length

3681. **Save the Cat beats as runtime percentages** — Blake Snyder's 15-beat sheet gives page numbers for a ~110-page script (Opening Image p1, Theme Stated p5, Catalyst p12, Break into Two p25, B Story p30, Midpoint p55, All Is Lost p75, Break into Three p85, Final Image p110); treat them as fractions of runtime to place beats in any length of video. **How:** `python3 -c "L=90;[print(n,round(L*p/110,1)) for n,p in [('catalyst',12),('break2',25),('bstory',30),('midpoint',55),('allislost',75),('break3',85),('final',110)]]"` prints the second for each beat; put scene boundaries / markers there (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/, checked 2026-10-24]
3682. **Opening Image sets tone, mood, type and scope** — the very first visual should tell the viewer what kind of film this is (The Matrix opens on a blinking command-line cursor). **How:** choose the frame at 0 s for tone, not just for information; with HyperFrames it is also the thumbnail, so check it with `npx hyperframes snapshot --at 0` [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
3683. **Final Image mirrors the Opening Image** — end on a shot that echoes the first one so the change in between is visible. **How:** export the first frame `ffmpeg -ss 0 -i edit.mp4 -frames:v 1 first.png` and reframe/re-shoot or pick a matching last shot with the same composition, grab the last frame with `ffmpeg -sseof -0.1 -i edit.mp4 -frames:v 1 last.png` and compare them side by side (`ffmpeg -i first.png -i last.png -filter_complex hstack pair.png`) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3684. **Theme Stated in passing by a secondary character** — around 5/110 of the runtime someone other than the hero says the theme offhand, in a line the hero doesn't yet grasp ("you need to unplug for a while"). **How:** find an offhand line from a side person that names the theme and place it near 5/110 (~4.5%) of runtime [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/, checked 2026-10-24]
3685. **Set-up shows "things that need fixing"** — the first ~10% shows the hero's world before the change and what is missing or broken in it (flaws, needs, wishes; Snyder calls six an arbitrary number); all main characters are introduced or hinted at. **How:** list what is missing or broken in the subject's world and show it in the first ~10% (p1-10), before the catalyst [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3686. **Debate beat before committing** — after the Catalyst the hero doubts or refuses (Snyder p12-25; Campbell's "refusal of the call"), which makes the later decision weigh more. **How:** keep the doubt/refusal moment between the catalyst (~11%) and the decision (~23%) instead of jumping straight to action [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/, checked 2026-10-24]
3687. **Break into Two is a choice, not an accident** — the hero chooses to go on the adventure (Neo takes the red pill); that choice officially starts the plot. **How:** make the decision itself visible on screen at ~23% (25/110) of runtime [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/, checked 2026-10-24]
3688. **B Story as a breather that carries the theme** — just after Break into Two a second strand (often a relationship) starts; Snyder says it gives the audience a breather and carries the theme. **How:** start the second strand near 30/110 (~27%) of runtime, right after Break into Two [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3689. **Fun and Games = the promise of the premise** — the first half of act two (p30-55) is "the promise of the premise", the fun heart of the movie, less concerned with forward progress and often lighter in tone. **How:** give ~27-50% of runtime (p30-55) to the fun core of the premise, after the set-up and before stakes rise [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3690. **Midpoint false victory or false defeat** — at the middle the stakes rise through an apparent win or loss; the All Is Lost beat often inverts it (Wikipedia's example: a false-defeat midpoint pairs with a false-victory All Is Lost). **How:** decide which one your midpoint (50%, 55/110) is, then make the ~68% beat (75/110) the opposite [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3691. **All Is Lost carries a "whiff of death"** — the lowest point often includes something that suggests death (a mentor dies, a hint of an end). **How:** at ~68% of runtime (75/110), place the moment that suggests an ending or a loss before the comeback [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3692. **Dark Night of the Soul, then Break into Three** — after the low, a stretch of hopelessness, then the A and B stories intertwine to give the solution (p75-85). **How:** after the low point, bring back the B-story person/element as the trigger for the final act [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3693. **Finale deals with obstacles in ascending order** — the hero applies what was learned and the bad guys are dealt with from least to most important; succeeding is not enough, the world must change. **How:** order the climax montage so the biggest win/reveal comes last [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3694. **"Save the cat" moment to make the hero likeable** — early on, the hero does something that makes the audience like them and want them to win (Aladdin shares stolen food with hungry kids). **How:** find a likeable action of the subject in the footage and place it early, before the viewer is asked to root for them [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3695. **"Pope in the Pool" exposition** — Snyder's trick for delivering exposition without boring the audience: put something entertaining on screen while the information is told. **How:** lay exposition over something entertaining on screen rather than over a plain shot [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3696. **The Board: four rows of scene cards** — plan the story on a board with four rows (Act One, first half of Act Two, second half of Act Two, Act Three), one index card per scene with its emotional change and conflict, colour-coded by character/storyline. **How:** before cutting, write four lists (one per row) of scene notes in the scratchpad, each with its emotional change and conflict [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3697. **Logline test before the edit** — Snyder wants a one-or-two-sentence logline (irony, a compelling mental picture, audience and cost, a killer title) written before the script; pitch it and adjust. **How:** write the video's logline at the top of the edit notes and pitch it before building the timeline [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3698. **Pick a story type, not a surface genre** — Snyder sorts films into types like Monster in the House, Golden Fleece, Out of the Bottle, Dude with a Problem, Rites of Passage, Buddy Love, Whydunit, Fool Triumphant, Institutionalized, Superhero, and says to study films of the same type. **How:** name the story type of the piece and watch a few films of that same type before structuring, as Snyder advises [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]
3699. **Beat sheet is a guide, not a formula** — critics say strict adherence to Snyder's beats and page counts makes films feel formulaic; some strong films hit the beats out of order and out of proportion. **How:** use the percentages as a check after a rough cut, not as fixed cut points [Wikipedia "Save the Cat!", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_the_Cat, checked 2026-10-24]

## Other story structures (and when to pick them)

3700. **Hero's journey in three movements** — Campbell's 17 stages group into Departure, Initiation and Return: called from the ordinary world, helped by a mentor, crossing into a special world of trials, the central crisis ("ordeal"), a reward, then the road back home with something that benefits others; not every story uses every stage, or in that order. **How:** sort selects into three bins (Departure, Initiation, Return) before ordering them [Wikipedia "Hero's journey", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero%27s_journey, checked 2026-10-24]
3701. **Vogler's 12-stage writer's version** — Christopher Vogler's adaptation for screenwriters: Ordinary World, Call to Adventure, Refusal, Meeting the Mentor, Crossing the First Threshold, Tests/Allies/Enemies, the Inmost Cave, the Ordeal, Reward, the Road Back, the Resurrection, Return with the Elixir. **How:** use it as a checklist on a rough cut: which of the 12 is missing or too short? [Wikipedia "Hero's journey", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero%27s_journey, checked 2026-10-24]
3702. **Kishōtenketsu for conflict-free stories** — four parts: ki (introduction), shō (development, no major change yet), ten (twist: the unexpected turn and biggest change), ketsu (conclusion); it doesn't need conflict, though it can contain it. **How:** split the material into four parts and keep the biggest change for part 3 [Wikipedia "Kishōtenketsu", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kish%C5%8Dtenketsu, checked 2026-10-24]
3703. **Hold the key element back until "ten"** — kishōtenketsu deliberately withholds a major story element until the third of four parts, and the twist can even change the genre of the piece. **How:** don't show the reveal in parts 1-2; keep it for part 3 of 4 [Wikipedia "Kishōtenketsu", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kish%C5%8Dtenketsu, checked 2026-10-24]
3704. **Ketsu ends quickly, may call back** — in the form's essay use the conclusion should be quick and not linger, and a writer can set up a callback to the beginning. **How:** keep the last part short, optionally with a callback to the opening [Wikipedia "Kishōtenketsu", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kish%C5%8Dtenketsu, checked 2026-10-24]
3705. **Pose a dramatic question at the first plot point** — in three-act structure the end of act one raises a question ("Will X disable the bomb?") that only the climax answers. **How:** write the dramatic question in one line; raise it at the end of act one and answer it in the climax [Wikipedia "Three-act structure", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-act_structure, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia "Story structure", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatic_structure, checked 2026-10-24]
3706. **Second plot point turns into act three** — act two ends at a second plot point; the third act brings the problem to a boil, forces a confrontation and reaches the climax, which answers the dramatic question. **How:** mark the second plot point at the end of the middle block, then let act three force the confrontation [Wikipedia "Story structure", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatic_structure, checked 2026-10-24]
3707. **Freytag's five parts** — Gustav Freytag (Die Technik des Dramas, 1863) outlined Introduction, Rise, Climax, Return or Fall, and Catastrophe, the basis of later three- and five-act forms. **How:** lay out sections as the five parts when the story falls after its peak [Wikipedia "Story structure", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatic_structure, checked 2026-10-24]
3708. **Two-act shape: complication and unbinding** — Aristotle argued for a two-part plot, desis (binding/complication) and lysis (unbinding/dénouement), split by a reversal (peripeteia); the "beginning, middle, end" three-act form is falsely attributed to him. **How:** build two halves (complication, unbinding) with the reversal at the join [Wikipedia "Story structure", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatic_structure, checked 2026-10-24]
3709. **Dan Harmon's Story Circle (8 steps)** — You (comfort zone), Need, Go (unfamiliar situation), Search (adapt), Find (get what they wanted), Take (pay a price), Return, Change; a simplified, character-focused hero's journey that ends back where it started, changed. **How:** label selects with the 8 step names and check each step is present [Dabble, https://dabblewriter.com/the-story-circle, checked 2026-10-24] [Spines, https://spines.com/dan-harmon-story-circle/, checked 2026-10-24]
3710. **Story Circle low point is the false win** — "Find" sits at the bottom of the circle: the hero gets what they wanted but must pay for it in "Take", so the mid-story victory is a false win. **How:** follow the mid-story success with its cost (Take) before the Return [Dabble, https://dabblewriter.com/the-story-circle, checked 2026-10-24]
3711. **The last step is what the hero does with the win** — "Change" is not the victory itself but the choice the changed character now makes; "Return" can be to a routine or community, not a place. **How:** end on what the changed subject now does, not on the victory itself [Dabble, https://dabblewriter.com/the-story-circle, checked 2026-10-24]
3712. **Seven-point structure, planned backwards** — Dan Wells's Hook, Plot Turn 1, Pinch 1, Midpoint, Pinch 2, Plot Turn 2, Resolution; plan the resolution first, make the hook its opposite, then the midpoint, then turns and pinches. **How:** decide the resolution first, then make the hook its opposite [Writing Excuses 7.41, https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-7-41-seven-point-story-structure/, checked 2026-10-24] [TCK Publishing, https://www.tckpublishing.com/seven-point-story-structure/, checked 2026-10-24]
3713. **Pinch points apply pressure** — pinches push the character into action (Pinch 1: the antagonistic force appears; Pinch 2: pressure until it looks lost, often loss of a mentor). **How:** place Pinch 1 after Plot Turn 1 and Pinch 2 after the midpoint [Writing Excuses 7.41, https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-7-41-seven-point-story-structure/, checked 2026-10-24] [TCK Publishing, https://www.tckpublishing.com/seven-point-story-structure/, checked 2026-10-24]
3714. **Midpoint: from reaction to action** — in seven-point structure the first half is the character reacting, the second half acting on purpose; the midpoint is the decision that flips it, and Plot Turn 2 is acted on by free will where Plot Turn 1 was forced. **How:** show the subject reacting before the midpoint and acting by choice after it [TCK Publishing, https://www.tckpublishing.com/seven-point-story-structure/, checked 2026-10-24]
3715. **Story Spine as an 8-line outline** — Kenn Adams's prompts: Once upon a time… Every day… But one day… Because of that… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally… And ever since then… **How:** fill the 8 sentences for the video before cutting [NPR via WLRN, https://www.wlrn.org/npr-breaking-news/2026-06-23/meet-the-creator-of-the-story-spine-an-8-sentence-tool-to-create-and-analyze-stories, checked 2026-10-24] [SessionLab, https://www.sessionlab.com/methods/story-spine, checked 2026-10-24]

## Scene construction

3716. **Scene = goal, conflict, disaster** — Dwight Swain defined a scene as an effort to reach a goal despite opposition, built as goal → conflict → disaster. **How:** for each scene in the timeline write its goal, conflict and disaster [Wikipedia "Scene and sequel", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scene_and_sequel, checked 2026-10-24]
3717. **Sequel = reaction, dilemma, decision** — Swain's linking unit: the character reacts to the disaster, faces a dilemma and decides, which becomes the next goal; it also "controls tempo". **How:** after a scene's disaster, keep the reaction, dilemma and decision beats that lead to the next goal [Wikipedia "Scene and sequel", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scene_and_sequel, checked 2026-10-24] [TCK Publishing, https://www.tckpublishing.com/scene-and-sequel/, checked 2026-10-24]
3718. **Pace with the scene-to-sequel ratio** — Swain: the author controls pacing with the proportion of scene to sequel; more scene = faster, more sequel = more reflection and believability. **How:** change the proportion of scene to sequel material to change the pace [Wikipedia "Scene and sequel", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scene_and_sequel, checked 2026-10-24]
3719. **Enter the scene as late as possible** — skip arrivals, small talk and set-up; open just before the point of the scene (the couple already seated, then the news). **How:** cut as much as possible from the head of each scene and check it still works [Charles Deemer, https://www.ibiblio.org/cdeemer/cfs0401.htm, checked 2026-10-24] [The Story Department, https://www.thestorydepartment.com/screenwriting-in-late-out-early/, checked 2026-10-24]
3720. **Leave on the line plus one reaction** — end the scene on its key line, give a quick look at the listener's reaction, and cut away instead of playing out the argument we've heard before. **How:** cut the tail after the key line and a quick reaction shot [Charles Deemer, https://www.ibiblio.org/cdeemer/cfs0401.htm, checked 2026-10-24]
3721. **Slow scene openings need a reason** — a slow build works only if an element justifies it (Deemer's example: the husband loads a gun before lunch, so every delay adds tension). **How:** keep a long lead-in only when an element (like the gun) justifies the pacing [Charles Deemer, https://www.ibiblio.org/cdeemer/cfs0401.htm, checked 2026-10-24]
3722. **Hold longer for a breather at act starts** — exceptions to "in late, out early" work at the start or end of an act or sequence, to give the audience a breather before rebuilding tension; make such holds interesting. **How:** allow longer holds at act/sequence starts or ends, and make them interesting [The Story Department, https://www.thestorydepartment.com/screenwriting-in-late-out-early/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Editing theory: Dmytryk's working rules

3723. **Content first, then form** — Dmytryk's first rule: substance before technique; technical skill counts for nothing if the film has little to do with humanity. **How:** judge the content of the cut before working on its form [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/seven_rules_for_film_and_video_editors/, checked 2026-10-24] [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3724. **First viewing should be emotional, not critical** — a film's first viewing should evoke an emotional reaction, not a critical one. **How:** watch the cut straight through once for emotional reaction before critiquing it [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3725. **Cut primarily for the picture** — except some musical sequences, images drive the edit and the soundtrack accompanies, because hearing is continuous while viewing is not. **How:** choose cut points by the picture, except in musical sequences [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3726. **When unsure of the frame, cut long** — Dmytryk: undecided about the exact frame, cut long rather than short; the first instinct is usually right, and re-lengthening clipped shots makes jumpy viewing. **How:** when in doubt leave the cut longer and trim later [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/seven_rules_for_film_and_video_editors/, checked 2026-10-24] [NYFA, https://nyfa.edu/student-resources/what-you-can-learn-from-edward-dmytryks-7-rules-of-cutting, checked 2026-10-24]
3727. **Cut at the centre of the movement** — cutting too early makes the next shot feel nonsensical, too late tries patience; the precise point is near the dead centre of the action (the moment a seated man touches the bench). **How:** step through the motion and place the cut near its centre (e.g. the contact frame) [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/seven_rules_for_film_and_video_editors/, checked 2026-10-24]
3728. **Fresh beats stale: pad at the head of the new shot** — if extra frames are needed between actions, add them at the beginning of the incoming shot (the viewer reads a new setting) rather than lingering on an emptied outgoing frame. **How:** don't linger after a subject exits; any extra frames go at the head of the next shot [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/seven_rules_for_film_and_video_editors/, checked 2026-10-24] [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3729. **Begin and end scenes on continuing action** — never start on an actor preparing to act; start as they walk in, pick up a phone, wash dishes, and end with them leaving or slamming the phone down, so the scene feels like a fragment of continuing life. **How:** trim heads and tails so each scene starts and ends on action [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/seven_rules_for_film_and_video_editors/, checked 2026-10-24] [NYFA, https://nyfa.edu/student-resources/what-you-can-learn-from-edward-dmytryks-7-rules-of-cutting, checked 2026-10-24]
3730. **Cut for values, not matches** — when the strongest emotional take doesn't match in continuity, use it anyway; the audience follows the emotional flow and ignores the mismatch. Fallbacks: cut to a close-up to hide unwanted movement, or blow up a weak shot. **How:** pick takes by dramatic value; hide a mismatch with a close-up or a blow-up [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/seven_rules_for_film_and_video_editors/, checked 2026-10-24] [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3731. **Look-off to POV: eyes settle + 3-4 frames** — the viewer accepts a look only once the actor's eyes focus off-screen; find the frame where the eyes freeze, add 3-4 frames, then cut to the POV, which lasts only as long as it takes to read. **How:** at 30 fps that is 0.10-0.13 s after the eyes settle: `python3 -c "print([round(f/30,3) for f in (3,4)])"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3732. **Text inserts: favour the slow reader** — an insert's length depends on how easily it reads; for text favour the slow reader, and a repeat of an earlier shot can be shorter than a new one. **How:** time text inserts for a slow reader; make repeats shorter [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3733. **Cut to the listener before the sentence ends** — the sense of a sentence is clear before it finishes (subject and predicate come first), so listeners react early; cut on the reaction rather than the words where you can. **How:** cut to the listener's reaction before the speaker's sentence ends [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3734. **Reaction shot = first frame of the reaction + 3-4** — find the first frame where the reaction starts and add 3-4 frames before cutting in; a brief freeze on the start of a "frozen" reaction is an option. **How:** at 30 fps start the reaction clip 0.10-0.13 s before the visible change [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3735. **Judge action only with sound in; slow the cut for music** — withhold judgement on an action scene until sound effects are laid in; a music score increases apparent pace, so the editor will probably want to decrease the cutting pace. **How:** lay SFX before judging an action cut; with score under it, consider decreasing the cutting pace [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3736. **No audience cutaways in action** — cutting to onlookers is generally unwise because the viewer doesn't want to be shown a model of how to react. **How:** avoid audience cutaways inside action scenes [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3737. **Suspense: never let a shot be analysed** — mood is everything; a suspense shot must never stay on so long that the audience can analyse it, and should never be repeated. **How:** in suspense passages, cut before a shot can be analysed and don't repeat shots [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk notes), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html, checked 2026-10-24]
3738. **Find script beats in the actors' eyes** — Norman Hollyn: the beats (where characters change) show in what the actors do with their eyes; change pacing, shot size, music or sound around those moments. **How:** mark where the eyes change, and change pacing/shot size/music/sound around those moments [ProVideo Coalition (Hollyn quote), https://www.provideocoalition.com/seven_rules_for_film_and_video_editors/, checked 2026-10-24]
3739. **Keep the great moment even if the cut is rough** — Tim Squyres: if an actor gives a striking moment, use it; nobody cares if the head turn doesn't match. **How:** keep the striking moment even if the matching cut is imperfect [ProVideo Coalition (Squyres quote), https://www.provideocoalition.com/seven_rules_for_film_and_video_editors/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Editing theory: montage and its schools

3740. **Collision (Eisenstein) vs linkage (Pudovkin)** — Eisenstein: montage is an idea arising from the collision of independent shots (meaning multiplies); Pudovkin: images build on one another by addition. **How:** decide whether a sequence should work by collision of opposed shots or by adding shot on shot [Wikipedia "Soviet montage theory", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory, checked 2026-10-24]
3741. **Intellectual montage by juxtaposition** — Strike intercuts violence on workers with a cow being slaughtered, so the pairing produces revulsion and an idea neither shot has alone. **How:** intercut the action with an image that carries the idea you want the pairing to produce [Wikipedia "Soviet montage theory", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory, checked 2026-10-24]
3742. **Choose a dominant stimulus for a sequence** — Eisenstein's "dominant" is the element (movement, light, timing…) that governs a sequence, with secondary elements supporting it; Vertov built one sequence on circular motion. **How:** pick one dominant element for the sequence and select shots that share it [Wikipedia "Soviet montage theory", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory, checked 2026-10-24]
3743. **Montage vs continuity: pick the contract** — continuity editing serves a predetermined narrative and keeps space logical (180° rule); montage makes meaning from associations and may break space on purpose (Man with a Movie Camera). **How:** decide per section whether it follows continuity or montage [Wikipedia "Soviet montage theory", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory, checked 2026-10-24]
3744. **Pudovkin's theme from many angles** — Pudovkin starts from a theme and shows the phenomenon from different angles and distances so the viewer gets both the overview and the detail (his street-demonstration example: roof view plus readable banners). **How:** show the key event from several angles and distances [Wikipedia "Soviet montage theory", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory, checked 2026-10-24]
3745. **Pudovkin's five relational edits** — contrast (opposed scenes compared), parallelism (jump times/places), symbolism (an object stands for an idea), simultaneity (two places at once), leitmotif (a repeated motif tied to a person or idea). **How:** label each transition in the edit with one of the five; if none applies, ask why the cut is there [New School student portfolio, https://portfolio.newschool.edu/lizetteayala/2017/04/25/video-project/, checked 2026-10-24]
3746. **Kuleshov through music** — Baranowski & Hecht intercut faces with neutral scenes under happy, sad or no music and found the music significantly changed how the faces were judged. **How:** test a reaction shot under two music beds (`ffmpeg -i face.mp4 -i bed.mp3 -map 0:v -map 1:a -shortest -c:v copy test.mp4`) and choose the bed that makes the face read right (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Kuleshov effect", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuleshov_effect, checked 2026-10-24]
3747. **Hitchcock's "pure editing" demo** — the same smiling face reads as a kind old man after a woman with a baby and as a "dirty old man" after a woman in a bikini. **How:** put the shot that gives the face its meaning directly before the reaction [Wikipedia "Kuleshov effect", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuleshov_effect, checked 2026-10-24]
3748. **Montage sequence to compress time** — British/American "montage sequence": many short shots (once with dissolves, split screens, superimpositions and music) to show long time passing (Rocky's training weeks, Ferris Bueller's hours). **How:** compress a long stretch into a sequence of short shots with music [Wikipedia "Montage (filmmaking)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montage_%28filmmaking%29, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia "Soviet montage theory", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory, checked 2026-10-24]
3749. **Training-montage conventions** — build-up where the hero faces failing to train, then a serious solo regimen in short cuts, an inspirational (often fast rock) song as the only sound, weeks pass in minutes, hero ready for the big task; it is also a cliché, often parodied. **How:** let the song be the only sound and end with the hero ready for the task [Wikipedia "Montage (filmmaking)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montage_%28filmmaking%29, checked 2026-10-24]
3750. **Newsreel and railroad montage devices** — classic devices: presses rolling and headlines zooming at the screen (Citizen Kane), or engines racing at camera and destination signs filling the screen. **How:** for a news beat, use presses and headlines zooming onto the screen [Wikipedia "Montage (filmmaking)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montage_%28filmmaking%29, checked 2026-10-24]
3751. **Script the montage yourself (Don Siegel)** — Siegel, head of Warner's montage department, turned the script's one-line montage description into his own five-page script instead of throwing stock shots together, and matched the director's style. **How:** write your own detailed script for a montage before pulling clips [Wikipedia "Montage (filmmaking)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montage_%28filmmaking%29, checked 2026-10-24]
3752. **Edit standing, write lying down (Murch)** — Walter Murch edits standing (like conductors, surgeons and cooks) and writes lying down to separate his editing mind from his creating mind. **How:** keep writing (planning) sessions and cutting sessions separate [Wikipedia "Walter Murch", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Murch, checked 2026-10-24]
3753. **Editing as steering the audience's thoughts** — Murch's In the Blink of an Eye treats editing as anticipating and controlling the audience's thoughts and puts emotion above technicalities. **How:** at each cut ask "what is the viewer thinking right now, and what should they think next?" [Wikipedia "Walter Murch", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Murch, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia "In the Blink of an Eye", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_the_Blink_of_an_Eye_(Murch_book), checked 2026-10-24]

## Documentary structures and modes

3754. **Pick a Nichols mode as the film's dominant** — Bill Nichols's six modes: poetic, expository, observational, participatory, reflexive, performative; modes overlap and one usually functions as the dominant without dictating every choice. **How:** name the dominant mode in the edit notes before structuring, then let other modes appear where needed [Wikipedia "Documentary mode", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_mode, checked 2026-10-24] [IDA review of Introduction to Documentary, https://www.documentary.org/node/34718, checked 2026-10-24]
3755. **Poetic mode: associations over continuity** — poetic documentaries favour mood and tone, leave characters and events undeveloped, and edit by "associations and patterns that involve temporal rhythms and spatial juxtapositions"; continuity hardly matters (Ivens's Regen links unrelated shots of a rain shower). **How:** cut by rhythm and visual rhyme instead of chronology for a mood piece [Wikipedia "Documentary mode", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_mode, checked 2026-10-24]
3756. **Expository mode: evidentiary editing** — narration carries the argument, and images "illustrate, illuminate, evoke, or act in counterpoint to what is said"; viewers read the images as evidence for the commentary. **How:** write/record the narration first, then pick each image as proof or counterpoint of its line [Wikipedia "Documentary mode", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_mode, checked 2026-10-24]
3757. **Observational mode bylaws** — pure observational documentaries use no music, no interviews, no staged scenes and no narration, with long takes and few cuts (fly on the wall). **How:** for an observational piece, drop the music bed and the voice-over and keep takes long [Wikipedia "Documentary mode", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_mode, checked 2026-10-24]
3758. **Participatory mode: keep the filmmaker's voice in** — the interaction between filmmaker and subject is on screen; at its simplest the filmmaker's voice is heard in the film (Rouch's Chronicle of a Summer; Errol Morris's Interrotron lets subjects talk to the director while looking into the lens). **How:** leave the interviewer's questions in the cut when the exchange itself is the story [Wikipedia "Documentary mode", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_mode, checked 2026-10-24]
3759. **Reflexive mode: show the making** — reflexive films expose their own construction (Vertov shows footage being shot and edited) and use re-enactment (Morris's The Thin Blue Line), which purists of vérité frown on. **How:** if you include re-enactments, make them read as re-enactments rather than real footage [Wikipedia "Documentary mode", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_mode, checked 2026-10-24]
3760. **Performative mode: subjective truth** — like participatory, but builds truths significant to the filmmaker personally; suits personal stories told without arguing their validity, and allows more visual abstraction. **How:** for a first-person piece, let the narrator's own perspective lead and allow freer, abstract visuals [Wikipedia "Documentary mode", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary_mode, checked 2026-10-24]
3761. **Vérité vs direct cinema** — direct cinema aims for the subject and audience to forget the camera (observational, fly on the wall); cinéma vérité acknowledges the camera and the filmmaker may provoke or set up situations as a catalyst (Perrault asked old people to fish for whale). **How:** decide which contract you keep: hide the crew entirely, or keep moments where subjects react to the camera [Wikipedia "Cinéma vérité", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cin%C3%A9ma_v%C3%A9rit%C3%A9, checked 2026-10-24]
3762. **Nichols's four "legs" of documentary** — Nichols names display/documentation, poetic experimentation, narrative storytelling and rhetorical oratory as the four legs a documentary stands on. **How:** check a doc cut for all four: real record, a poetic passage, a story line and an argument [IDA review of Introduction to Documentary, https://www.documentary.org/node/34718, checked 2026-10-24]

## Interview-driven edits

3763. **Screen hours of interviews at 2x-3x** — Noam Kroll: watch every frame first, but at 2x (sometimes 3x) playback when there are many hours; a clip that jumps out at 2x is good. **How:** make a fast review copy `ffmpeg -i interview.mp4 -vf setpts=0.5*PTS -af atempo=2.0 -c:v libx264 -preset ultrafast -crf 30 review2x.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/?p=8744, checked 2026-10-24]
3764. **Watch everything once before pulling selects** — Kroll: a full pass without pulling clips, then a second pass to pull selects; Andy Grieve also watches everything first and can't cut and screen at the same time. **How:** first pass = notes only; second pass = mark in/out of selects [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/?p=8744, checked 2026-10-24] [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3765. **Ruthless selects for short pieces** — for a 2-3 minute promo "99.9%" of the interview isn't used; if a clip doesn't immediately grab you, stand out or add new insight, leave it. **How:** keep only clips that pass that test into the selects timeline [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/?p=8744, checked 2026-10-24]
3766. **Look for intro, closing and throughline bites** — Kroll's second selects pass keeps only a great introductory statement, a great closing statement, and concise explanations of the throughline; these make up "90%" of a short edit. **How:** tag selects INTRO / CLOSE / THROUGHLINE and drop wordy ones that would need many frankenbites [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/?p=8744, checked 2026-10-24]
3767. **Group selects into beginning, middle, end** — loosely sort selects into the three sections (or by day for a week-in-the-life), then cut from the over-full sections. **How:** three marker colours or three sub-timelines; trim the section with the most material first [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/?p=8744, checked 2026-10-24]
3768. **Pick the best intro bite and let it choose the next** — start the timeline with the very best intro clip; that choice dictates which clip comes next, and so on to the end. **How:** build the sequence one bite at a time from the top, never filling the middle first [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/?p=8744, checked 2026-10-24]
3769. **Music, breathing pauses and B-roll come last** — Kroll: after the bite order is set, drop in music, add pauses for breathing room and lay B-roll for each talking point; this should be the shortest phase. **How:** only start the `voice_over_music.py` / B-roll pass after the bite order is final [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/?p=8744, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Voice and songs with words"]
3770. **Timestamped transcript for every interview** — Todd Boruff has each interview transcribed with question breaks and a timestamp about every 60 s, then highlights candidate bites; automatic transcripts struggle with names and jargon. **How:** transcribe with the repo's Whisper install (word timings) and check names by hand [Todd Boruff, https://toddboruff.substack.com/p/my-interview-editing-process, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Environment facts"]
3771. **Keep the paper edit loose** — how bites look on paper doesn't always match how they're said on video; the stringout reveals energy, stumbles and expressions that change a bite. **How:** after the paper order, listen to each bite in place before committing to it [Todd Boruff, https://toddboruff.substack.com/p/my-interview-editing-process, checked 2026-10-24]
3772. **Build "modules" (Andy Grieve)** — cut each interview to its best parts (removing questions and dead air), organise by character and subject, and build little self-contained modules (a couple of minutes, ten minutes) that you later piece into the film. **How:** make one short sequence per topic first; assemble the film from those sequences [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3773. **Interviews first, archive later** — Grieve watches interviews first because they show which archival footage to look for and which characters share experiences to intercut; he only goes into archival once the story is known. **How:** lock the interview story line before searching B-roll/archive [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3774. **Talking heads as characters, not experts** — Grieve wants interviewees to feel like live characters driving the story, not experts "sitting in a chair", so he lets stories come from their experiences. **How:** prefer bites where people recount what happened to them over bites that explain [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3775. **Never fake cause and effect** — Grieve accepts shaping for emotion or arc but never wants to create a false sense of cause and effect; documentaries pursue truth. **How:** when reordering bites, check you haven't made one event appear to cause another that it didn't [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3776. **Pace a story by where it lands** — a story that seems like an opener may end up later in the film, and then needs different pacing and different archival because its function has changed. **How:** after moving a section, re-pace it and re-pick its B-roll for its new role [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3777. **Searchable locators at each bite** — Grieve's assistant puts a locator at the head (and sometimes middle) of each select with its first words or key words, so bites can be searched. **How:** name each select clip or marker with its first few words (e.g. from Whisper text) [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3778. **Re-screen raw interviews near the end** — late in the edit Grieve passes back through all the raw interviews for anything missed or anything that now means something different in context. **How:** schedule a final raw-interview pass before picture lock [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3779. **Time-of-day map for all-archival stories** — for an all-archival film about one day (the OJ Simpson chase), Grieve mapped a 24-hour timeline of the best footage on layers to see what overlapped. **How:** for multi-source event footage, place clips on a timeline by real clock time (creation time from `ffprobe -v error -show_entries format_tags=creation_time clip.mp4`) before story-cutting (tested here 2026-10-24) [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24]
3780. **Stack alternatives on tracks** — Grieve keeps options for a shot stacked on different tracks while debating them. **How:** in a HyperFrames scene keep the alternate clip as a second element with `visibility:hidden` until chosen [ProVideo Coalition (Andy Grieve), https://www.provideocoalition.com/the-art-of-the-cut-andy-grieve/, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "CSS 3D voxel films"]
3781. **Frankenbite: know the line** — Manfred Becker's term for re-ordering key elements or single words of a statement to create a confession or argument the original didn't contain; he studied the ethical dilemmas of 75 editors over it. **How:** in documentary/testimonial work avoid joins that change what the person meant; Kroll also advises against bites that need many frankenbites [Routledge (Becker), https://www.routledge.com/Creating-Reality-in-Factual-Television-The-Frankenbite-and-Other-Fakes/Becker/p/book/9780367492717, checked 2026-10-24] [YorkSpace (Becker dissertation), https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/items/413d636e-ccaf-4f8c-aae6-f081ce170c13, checked 2026-10-24] [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/?p=8744, checked 2026-10-24]
3782. **B-roll after the interview, shot to support it** — in ENG and documentary, B-roll is often shot after the main interview to supply scenes for what was said; types include inserts, FX shots, establishing shots, stock footage and pickups, usually laid under the continuing primary audio. **How:** list each talking point and the B-roll type that will cover it; keep the interview audio running under it [Wikipedia "B-roll", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-roll, checked 2026-10-24]

## Wedding and event films

3783. **Two deliverables: highlight reel and full film** — wedding editors usually cut a short highlight reel for sharing online and a full-length film that shows the day as it happened; some couples only want the highlight. **How:** build the full chronological timeline first, then derive the highlight from it [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
3784. **Highlight out of order, full film in order** — don't show everything in order in the highlight; weave clips in the most engaging way and save the chronology for the full-length film. **How:** reorder freely in the highlight; keep the long cut chronological [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
3785. **Open the highlight with a speech or the vows** — one of the most frequent openings uses a speech or the vows to introduce the couple, then shows the rest of the day (Joe Simon's reels use the father of the bride). **How:** lay one speech line over the first shots, then cut the day around it [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
3786. **Five-act highlight: opening, build, ceremony, resolution, closing** — RoughCut's arc for a ~7-minute highlight: Opening 0:00-1:00 (one universal emotional line over atmospheric B-roll), Build 1:00-3:00 (speeches telling the love story), Ceremony/Climax 3:00-5:00 (best vow lines, rings, kiss; music peaks), Resolution 5:00-6:30 (exhale, recessional, a light toast line), Closing 6:30-7:00 (one final line, the couple walking away, music fades, black). **How:** scale the act boundaries to the target length: `python3 -c "L=180;[print(n,round(L*t/420)) for n,t in [('build',60),('ceremony',180),('resolution',300),('closing',390)]]"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3787. **Don't open on the vows or a joke** — in RoughCut's arc the opening should be short and evocative; starting with the vows, a joke or a long speech clip doesn't work. **How:** save the vows for the climax section and keep the opening to one line [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3788. **Build section: 2-3 speakers, specific stories** — introduce the couple through other people's eyes (parents, best man, maid of honour); more than 2-3 speakers or generic praise without a specific story weakens it; let each moment breathe. **How:** choose at most three speakers for the build and pick bites that tell an anecdote [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3789. **Let the ceremony own the climax** — use the most honest, personal vow lines, don't rush them, and don't cut the vows with speech audio. **How:** no speech bites inside the ceremony section; the music peak sits here [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3790. **Resolution must exhale** — after the peak, give release (warmth, joy, humour fits here); trying to match the climax's energy doesn't work. **How:** follow the vows with the recessional and a light line, at lower intensity [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3791. **End on one strong line, never logistics** — the last thing heard defines how the audience feels; 1-2 sentences max, not one more toast or "please proceed to the cocktail hour". **How:** pick the closing bite separately and test the ending without it to see if it's earning its place [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3792. **8-15 soundbites for a 7-minute highlight** — RoughCut's biggest-mistake warning: too much; a 7-minute highlight should carry 8-15 soundbites, not 30, and any clip whose removal doesn't hurt the story should go. **How:** count the bites on the timeline; above ~15 for 7 min, cut [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3793. **Score bites by emotion, sync to music last** — transcribe everything, score bites by emotional weight not information, find 2-3 candidates per section, check each bite flows into the next, and only then adjust trims to the music. **How:** pick words first; snap their placement to bar lines only in the final pass [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3794. **Swap a great bite for a better transition** — if two great clips feel jarring back-to-back, swap one for a slightly weaker clip that transitions better. **How:** play each bite join aloud; replace the bite, not the transition effect, when it jars [RoughCut, https://roughcut.com/blog/how-to-structure-wedding-highlight-film, checked 2026-10-24]
3795. **Two-song highlight** — PremiumBeat's standard: a slow, steady song for the intro and ceremony, then a fast dance track for the reception footage; music for online reels must be licensed. **How:** pick both tracks from the repo's CC0 library per the owner's no-credit rule [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Environment facts" (owner music rules)]
3796. **Highlight under ten minutes, best of the best** — keep the highlight short and tight, well under 10 minutes; full ceremony and speeches belong in the full film. **How:** if the highlight passes 10 min, move whole speeches to the long cut [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-cinematic-wedding-highlight-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
3797. **Journalistic vs cinematic vs concept style** — journalistic (documentary) wedding videos keep segments in the order they occurred; cinematic ones add slow motion, transitions, saturated colour, creative angles and dramatic music; concept videos are scripted with pre-wedding shoots and interviews. **How:** agree the style with the client before cutting; it decides ordering and effects [Wikipedia "Wedding videography", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_videography, checked 2026-10-24]
3798. **Event recap: theme decided before the event** — RainFocus: identify the recap's theme and purpose while the event is being planned, know who should watch it and what they should do after, and tell the videographer the channels in advance (that shapes length and structure). **How:** write the recap's one-line theme, audience and channel before the shoot [RainFocus, https://rainfocus.com/blog/15-points-of-a-great-recap-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3799. **Recap length: sources differ** — Canon says few recap videos run more than about a minute; RainFocus advises two to four minutes with a single song. **How:** cut a ~60 s social recap and, if the client wants it, a 2-4 min version on one track [Canon CinemaEOS, https://cinemaeos.canon.ca/resources/five-steps-for-producing-engaging-event-recap-videos, checked 2026-10-24] [RainFocus, https://rainfocus.com/blog/15-points-of-a-great-recap-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3800. **Recap story: who, what, when, where, why — told chronologically** — Canon: find the event's purpose, then generally tell it in order as it unfolded, with evocative visuals, brief interviews of organisers and participants, and sparing text overlays to fill gaps. **How:** order selects by clock time (`ffprobe -v error -show_entries format_tags=creation_time clip.mp4`) and drop in 2-3 short interview bites [Canon CinemaEOS, https://cinemaeos.canon.ca/resources/five-steps-for-producing-engaging-event-recap-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
3801. **No voice-over narration on recaps** — Canon advises avoiding voice-over narration (clichéd) and using explanatory text with the interviews instead. **How:** carry information in short on-screen text, not a narrator [Canon CinemaEOS, https://cinemaeos.canon.ca/resources/five-steps-for-producing-engaging-event-recap-videos, checked 2026-10-24]
3802. **Put the key moment on the song's high point** — RainFocus: tempo and beats speak louder than words; place an important moment at the high point of the song, and keep quotes short and memorable. **How:** find the track's peak (e.g. with `npx hyperframes audio <file>`) and align the event's biggest moment to it [RainFocus, https://rainfocus.com/blog/15-points-of-a-great-recap-video/, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
3803. **Smiles, VIPs and behind-the-scenes** — RainFocus: film attendees smiling and laughing, make sure VIPs are captured, and include behind-the-scenes footage of the people who made the event for context. **How:** check the selects contain each of the three kinds before cutting [RainFocus, https://rainfocus.com/blog/15-points-of-a-great-recap-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3804. **Proof all text and add the logo** — misspelled names and typos distract; the viewer should be able to tell whose event it is from the logo. **How:** spell-check every caption/name card against the client's list; place the logo at least once [RainFocus, https://rainfocus.com/blog/15-points-of-a-great-recap-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3805. **Recap ends on a CTA** — Canon: identify a strong brand-relevant call to action, often an invitation to share memories with a hashtag or to register for the next event. **How:** end card with the hashtag or next-event date [Canon CinemaEOS, https://cinemaeos.canon.ca/resources/five-steps-for-producing-engaging-event-recap-videos, checked 2026-10-24]

## Trailers and teasers

3806. **Trailer skeleton: cold open, three acts, button** — Derek Lieu's breakdown (via Jonny Elwyn) of most modern trailers: Cold Open, Act 1 (introduction), Act 2 (escalation), Act 3 (climax), then the Button. **How:** mark the five sections on the timeline before choosing shots [Jonny Elwyn, https://jonnyelwyn.co.uk/film-and-video-editing/how-to-edit-a-movie-trailer/, checked 2026-10-24]
3807. **Reach act two about a minute in** — Chris St. Pierre (Harry Potter, Gravity trailers): Act One sets the world and characters, Act Two presents the conflict and antagonists, and you typically want to be in act two by about a minute in. **How:** for a ~2:30 trailer, check the conflict starts near 1:00 [MovieMaker, https://www.moviemaker.com/art-of-the-tease-trailer/, checked 2026-10-24]
3808. **Act three is "montage land"** — once the story is out of the way, cut a montage of images to music with cool lines sprinkled in until the main title. **How:** after the conflict is set, switch to bar-driven montage cutting up to the title card [MovieMaker, https://www.moviemaker.com/art-of-the-tease-trailer/, checked 2026-10-24]
3809. **Logo/graphic after the cold open** — the intriguing open is usually followed by a logo or graphic (often the studio logos, or a "This Summer" / "From Director X" card) to separate it from what follows. **How:** place the brand/server logo card right after the cold open [MovieMaker, https://www.moviemaker.com/art-of-the-tease-trailer/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Music videos

3827. **Lay a full-song "bed" performance shot first** — JG Harding (Sound On Sound): sync one of the best whole-song performance shots (e.g. a wide of the band) on the bottom track as a bed, then layer performance takes over it, keeping the parts you like; the bed is something to cut back to (only 10 frames of his remained). **How:** put the full-length wide take as the lowest video layer for the song's whole duration, then add cut sections above it [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278, checked 2026-10-24]
3828. **Take one bar of the best part of each clip** — use only the key part of a clip (e.g. one bar of a 10-second pan, picked for a lens flare or a great expression) so the edit becomes a "best-of" from start to finish; more takes give more serendipitous moments. **How:** bar length = 240/BPM s for 4/4 (`python3 -c "print(240/120)"` → 2.0 s at 120 BPM); trim each clip to its best bar (tested here 2026-10-24) [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278, checked 2026-10-24]
3829. **Beat cuts with occasional 2-beat and 2-bar cuts** — for 4/4 performance pieces, sticking to the beat with occasional two-beat and two-bar cuts keeps it from becoming monotonous (Harding's rule of thumb). **How:** mostly one-bar shots, with a few half-bar and two-bar shots mixed in [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278, checked 2026-10-24]
3830. **Slow songs: cut slightly off the bar** — the Fever Ray video mostly cuts slightly off the start of bars, with many shots about three and a half bars long; cutting out of sync with the main musical events shifts focus to the shot content. **How:** for a slow track, hold shots for several bars and place cuts a little after bar starts [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278, checked 2026-10-24]
3831. **Every shot moves, even in a slow edit** — in Glasvegas's slow video every shot has some motion (pan, track or handheld at similar speed), and still frames contain movement. **How:** if a shot is locked off, add a slow scale/position tween on its wrapper so nothing sits dead [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video" step 5]
3832. **Software zoom on locked-off shots** — Harding: scale up a tripod shot and pan a little or zoom in a few percent; keeping the viewer's interest matters more than a few pixels of sharpness. **How:** `ffmpeg -i still.mp4 -vf "scale=iw*1.05:-2,crop=iw/1.05:ih/1.05" -c:a copy zoom5.mp4` for a fixed 5% punch-in (tested here 2026-10-24) [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278, checked 2026-10-24]
3833. **Cut between wide and intimate close shots** — a static point of view reduces involvement and often happens in amateur videos; cut in and out between wider shots and close-ups. **How:** alternate shot sizes through each section of the song [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278, checked 2026-10-24]
3834. **Name every clip before cutting** — descriptive names like "Band: SOLO 2 (good)" instead of "MOV_4578", sorted into bins, save time when you need "just one more nice shot" late in the edit. **How:** rename source clips by content before building the timeline [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4905278, checked 2026-10-24]
3835. **Echo instruments and riffs with a repeated visual** — Videomaker: whenever an instrument or riff recurs, reinforce it with the same effect or a related visual; mirror verse/chorus/bridge structure with corresponding visual patterns. **How:** assign one visual treatment per recurring riff and reuse it each time the riff returns [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17048-rocking-out-with-a-music-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3836. **Tap markers in real time** — most editors let you add a marker with one keystroke during playback: play the song, tap the beat, then refine marker positions and snap clips to them. **How:** needs an NLE with live markers (missing; e.g. Kdenlive, GPL-3.0): play the song tapping markers, refine them, snap cuts to them [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17048-rocking-out-with-a-music-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3837. **Fade picture or a credit card with the song's fade** — when a song fades out, fade the picture to black with it or fade in a graphic with the artist's name and contact. **How:** `ffmpeg -i mv.mp4 -vf "fade=t=out:st=8:d=2" -c:a copy out.mp4` matching the fade start/length to the song's fade (tested here 2026-10-24) [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17048-rocking-out-with-a-music-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3838. **Establish a look and stick with it** — try wild ideas, but once the video's look is established keep it throughout, building variations off it rather than switching to something entirely different. **How:** pick one grade/treatment for the whole video; vary only within it [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17048-rocking-out-with-a-music-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3839. **The visual story may contradict the lyrics** — use the video to emphasise the song's story; sometimes the visual narrative works best in contradiction to the lyrical story. **How:** decide up front whether visuals follow or counter the lyrics [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17048-rocking-out-with-a-music-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3840. **Audio-reactive effects from the waveform** — the waveform is data: tools such as Trapcode Sound Keys or Boris Beat Reactor, or After Effects audio-to-keyframes with expressions, link effect values to amplitude. **How:** in ffmpeg, `showwaves`/`showfreqs` draw audio-driven visuals that can be overlaid (`ffmpeg -i song.mp3 -filter_complex "showwaves=s=640x120:mode=cline" -t 3 waves.mp4`) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17048-rocking-out-with-a-music-video/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#showwaves, checked 2026-10-24]
3841. **Give the eye time after a big brightness change** — PremiumBeat: cutting between dark and light rooms needs time for the eyes to adjust, and the viewer must understand the new scene before the next cut. **How:** in fast montages, hold the first shot after a dark→bright change a little longer [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/5-tips-editing-fast-paced-video/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Ads and promos

3842. **Hook, body, close for short ads** — TikTok's ad guidance recommends a three-part structure: a hook, a body that highlights the unique selling points, and a close with a clear CTA that tells the audience what to think, feel or do. **How:** split the ad timeline into three labelled blocks before choosing shots [TikTok Ads Help, https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices, checked 2026-10-24] [TikTok Business blog, https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads, checked 2026-10-24]
3843. **Proposition in 3 s, hook within 6 s** — TikTok: introduce the content proposition in the first 3 seconds for recall, and prioritise the hook in the first 6 seconds (it cites 90% of ad recall impact captured in the first six seconds). **How:** make sure the product/offer is named or shown before 3.0 s [TikTok Ads Help, https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices, checked 2026-10-24] [TikTok Business blog, https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads, checked 2026-10-24]
3844. **Show the product on screen in the body** — TikTok reports that ads showing the product on screen drive higher brand affinity and recall ("additive branding cues" through the body). **How:** keep the product/logo visible in several body shots, not only in the end card [TikTok Business blog, https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads, checked 2026-10-24]
3845. **CTA cards instead of interrupting** — TikTok recommends closing with CTA cards rather than interrupting the content. **How:** add an end-card overlay with the action over the last shot instead of cutting to a separate slate [TikTok Business blog, https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads, checked 2026-10-24]
3846. **Ad text at 5-10 words per second** — TikTok suggests displaying 5-10 words per second when using text overlays. **How:** check each card: words ÷ seconds on screen should be ≤ 10 (`python3 -c "w=12;s=1.5;print(w/s)"` → 8.0) (tested here 2026-10-24) [TikTok Ads Help, https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices, checked 2026-10-24]
3847. **Native, not over-polished** — TikTok advises a DIY, not overly polished style that fits user-generated content, featuring creators, employees or customers. **How:** feature real creators, employees or customers and keep a DIY look [TikTok Ads Help, https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices, checked 2026-10-24]
3848. **Before-After-Bridge (BAB)** — show the current painful state (Before), the desired state (After), then the product as the Bridge, then a CTA; Benly's 30 s blocks: Before 0-8 s, After 8-20 s, Bridge 20-27 s, CTA 27-30 s; suits visible transformations and can use split screen. **How:** `ffmpeg -i before.mp4 -i after.mp4 -filter_complex hstack ba.mp4` for a side-by-side Before/After beat (tested here 2026-10-24) [Benly, https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/video-ad-structure-frameworks, checked 2026-10-24]
3849. **UGC testimonial flow** — Benly's 30 s structure: personal hook 0-4 s (start mid-story), problem/context 4-12 s, discovery of the product 12-22 s ("then someone told me about…"), result/proof 22-27 s, direct recommendation 27-30 s. **How:** order a creator's talking-to-camera bites in these five slots [Benly, https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/video-ad-structure-frameworks, checked 2026-10-24]
3850. **Product-demo structure** — lead with the most impressive capability (0-5 s), walk through it solving a real task (5-18 s), show what's different (18-24 s), then social proof + CTA (24-30 s); Benly places it mid-to-bottom funnel, for viewers who already know they need a solution. **How:** open on the "wow" feature shot, then screen recording/close-ups of the task [Benly, https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/video-ad-structure-frameworks, checked 2026-10-24]
3851. **Combine frameworks across one ad** — Benly notes common combinations: PAS for the first 15 s then a product demo, or BAB for emotion then social proof from the UGC format, with smooth transitions. **How:** plan two blocks with a clear hand-off line between them [Benly, https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/video-ad-structure-frameworks, checked 2026-10-24]
3852. **Agitate with vivid consequences** — in PAS the agitation moves from factual to emotional: specific stories of the problem interfering with lives; then present the solution with benefits over features (Dan Kennedy called PAS "the most reliable copywriting formula for sales ever invented"). **How:** in the agitate block use shots of the consequence, not the product [il.ly, https://il.ly/marketing/pas-framework, checked 2026-10-24] [Benly, https://benly.ai/learn/ad-creative/video-ad-structure-frameworks, checked 2026-10-24]
3853. **AIDA's original seller steps** — E. St. Elmo Lewis's model; the original selling stages were: secure attention, hold attention through interest, arouse desire, create confidence and belief, secure decision and action, create satisfaction. **How:** check a promo has a "confidence/belief" beat (proof) between desire and the CTA [Wikipedia "AIDA (marketing)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDA_%28marketing%29, checked 2026-10-24]
3854. **AIDA is a poor predictor, use loosely** — empirical studies found AIDA and other hierarchy models poor predictors of actual consumer behaviour, and they ignore post-purchase effects. **How:** treat AIDA as a script checklist, not a guarantee [Wikipedia "AIDA (marketing)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDA_%28marketing%29, checked 2026-10-24]
3855. **StoryBrand: customer is the hero, brand is the guide** — Donald Miller's SB7: a Character (the customer) with a Problem meets a Guide who gives a Plan and Calls them to Action, helping them avoid Failure and end in Success; the brand is Yoda, not Luke. **How:** cast the customer/player as the on-screen hero and the brand as the helper [Graham Mann (book notes), https://grahammann.net/book-notes/building-a-storybrand-donald-miller, checked 2026-10-24] [IMPACT, https://www.impactplus.com/blog/what-is-the-storybrand-framework, checked 2026-10-24]
3856. **Three problem layers** — SB7: external (the tangible thing), internal (how it makes them feel), philosophical (why it's wrong they have it); most brands only sell the external one. **How:** give the problem block one line or shot per layer [Graham Mann (book notes), https://grahammann.net/book-notes/building-a-storybrand-donald-miller, checked 2026-10-24]
3857. **Guide = empathy + authority** — the brand earns the guide role by showing it understands the problem (empathy) and can solve it (authority), without taking the spotlight. **How:** one empathy line, then one proof of competence (result, credential) [Graham Mann (book notes), https://grahammann.net/book-notes/building-a-storybrand-donald-miller, checked 2026-10-24]
3858. **3-4 step plan plus direct and transitional CTAs** — give a simple plan of three or four steps, a direct CTA (buy, book) and a transitional one (free guide, webinar) for people not ready; spell out failure and success stakes. **How:** end card with the main action plus one low-commitment action [Graham Mann (book notes), https://grahammann.net/book-notes/building-a-storybrand-donald-miller, checked 2026-10-24]
3859. **"If you confuse, you lose"** — Miller's rule: clarity beats clever; marketing should be understood, not impressive. **How:** after a rough cut, have someone say in one sentence what is offered; if they can't, simplify [Graham Mann (book notes), https://grahammann.net/book-notes/building-a-storybrand-donald-miller, checked 2026-10-24]

## Explainers

3860. **Explainer formula with 90 s time budget** — Levitate: Hook 0-5 s, Problem 5-20 s, Solution 20-50 s (no feature list yet), How It Works 50-80 s (two or three steps), Social Proof 80-90 s (one proof point), CTA in the last 10 s; a 60 s version compresses hook, problem and solution. **How:** scale the block edges: `python3 -c "L=60;[print(n,round(L*t/90,1)) for n,t in [('problem',5),('solution',20),('how',50),('proof',80)]]"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Levitate Media, https://levitatemedia.com/learn/how-to-write-an-explainer-video-script, checked 2026-10-24]
3861. **Biteable's 60 s skeleton** — Hook 0-8 s, Problem 8-20 s, Solution 20-45 s (with "here's how it works: step one, two, three"), CTA 45-60 s; about 150 words for 60 s, solution gets the most time. **How:** count script words before recording (`wc -w script.txt`) and aim near 150 per minute (tested here 2026-10-24) [Biteable, https://biteable.com/blog/explainer-video-script-template/, checked 2026-10-24]
3862. **Two-column script: audio left, visuals right** — professional explainer scripts pair each voice-over line with what the viewer sees; write the visual column after the audio is approved. **How:** keep a two-column table (line | shot) in the scratchpad and build scenes row by row [Levitate Media, https://levitatemedia.com/learn/how-to-write-an-explainer-video-script, checked 2026-10-24]
3863. **One idea, one audience, one CTA** — a strong explainer has one core idea, one target audience and one call to action; everything else belongs in a different video. **How:** strike any scene that serves a second audience or second ask [Levitate Media, https://levitatemedia.com/learn/how-to-write-an-explainer-video-script, checked 2026-10-24] [Biteable, https://biteable.com/blog/explainer-video-script-template/, checked 2026-10-24]
3864. **Read the script aloud with a timer** — read at natural pace; if you're rushing, cut the script, not the pace (conversational VO lands around 125-150 words per minute). **How:** `python3 -c "w=210;print(round(w/140*60),'s')"` estimates runtime at 140 wpm, then time a real read (tested here 2026-10-24) [Levitate Media, https://levitatemedia.com/learn/how-to-write-an-explainer-video-script, checked 2026-10-24]
3865. **Open in the viewer's world, not the company's** — start with a pain point or a question the audience already asks; nobody cares about the founding story in the first five seconds. **How:** first line of VO = the viewer's problem; brand name comes later [Levitate Media, https://levitatemedia.com/learn/how-to-write-an-explainer-video-script, checked 2026-10-24]
3866. **Specific numbers, not "saves time"** — replace vague benefits with concrete outcomes ("cuts reporting from three hours to twenty minutes"), pulled from real data. **How:** every benefit line in the VO should contain a concrete, true number or result from the client [Levitate Media, https://levitatemedia.com/learn/how-to-write-an-explainer-video-script, checked 2026-10-24]
3867. **Adapt the hook to the use case** — Biteable: product explainer hooks on the task the buyer dreads; new-hire training on a day-one confusion; internal announcements on what's changing and why now; sales follow-ups on the prospect's goal. **How:** pick the hook angle from the video's use, keep the four-part body [Biteable, https://biteable.com/blog/explainer-video-script-template/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Vlogs

3868. **Give each vlog a repeatable story template** — Fstoppers: successful vloggers often follow traditional storytelling principles; pick a template borrowed from favourite vloggers (e.g. open on a time-lapse, or on a sneak peek of something later in the episode) so you know what to film and where it goes. **How:** fix the episode's opening type and section order before shooting [Fstoppers, https://fstoppers.com/originals/tips-structuring-great-vlog-content-133011, checked 2026-10-24]
3869. **Sneak-peek opening** — start the episode with a short preview of something that happens later. **How:** copy the strongest later moment to 0 s: `ffmpeg -ss 40 -t 3 -i vlog.mp4 -c:v libx264 -c:a aac peek.mp4`, then place it before the start (cut command tested here 2026-10-24) [Fstoppers, https://fstoppers.com/originals/tips-structuring-great-vlog-content-133011, checked 2026-10-24]
3870. **Don't spoon-feed: cut what the viewer can assume** — skip the wake-up-to-arrival details; Neistat often cuts mid-sentence, which shortens, speeds up and lets viewers feel they worked something out (Abrams's "mystery box"). **How:** remove transitional routine footage and test whether the story still reads [Fstoppers, https://fstoppers.com/originals/tips-structuring-great-vlog-content-133011, checked 2026-10-24]
3871. **Don't get attached to your own footage** — great montage shots are often on screen for only a second; lingering because you shot it kills an upbeat edit. **How:** in self-shot montages, trim every shot to its best second first [Fstoppers, https://fstoppers.com/originals/tips-structuring-great-vlog-content-133011, checked 2026-10-24]
3872. **"Neistat cut-off"** — Sven Pape's breakdown: cut abruptly to a new scene in the middle of a sentence or thought. **How:** end the clip on a word mid-sentence and start the next scene's audio immediately [Artlist (Sven Pape breakdown), https://artlist.io/blog/editing-storytelling-techniques-casey-neistat/, checked 2026-10-24]
3873. **Continuous thought across locations** — start a story in one location and finish it in one or more others, stringing small pieces together. **How:** split one spoken thought across clips from different places, keeping the sentence continuous [Artlist (Sven Pape breakdown), https://artlist.io/blog/editing-storytelling-techniques-casey-neistat/, checked 2026-10-24]
3874. **Associative cut** — cut in small bits of referential content (often clips from films) to comment on what's said. **How:** use only licensed/CC0 reference clips per the owner's rules [Artlist (Sven Pape breakdown), https://artlist.io/blog/editing-storytelling-techniques-casey-neistat/, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Rules from the owner"]
3875. **Task-progress montage on sped-up footage** — rapid cuts showing one task progressing, usually on footage that's already sped up. **How:** speed the task clip first (`ffmpeg -i task.mp4 -vf setpts=PTS/4 -an fast.mp4`), then cut it into short pieces (tested here 2026-10-24) [Artlist (Sven Pape breakdown), https://artlist.io/blog/editing-storytelling-techniques-casey-neistat/, checked 2026-10-24]
3876. **In-camera transitions** — putting the camera down/picking it up, a "lens slap", or pushing the camera closer to the face to intensify a moment (an in-camera "edit"). **How:** when filming yourself, cover scene changes with a hand over the lens and cut inside it [Artlist (Sven Pape breakdown), https://artlist.io/blog/editing-storytelling-techniques-casey-neistat/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Tutorials and screen recordings

3877. **Show the end result first** — before the hook, show a ~5-second clip of the finished result so viewers know it's what they searched for. **How:** put a 3-5 s clip of the final output at 0 s, then the hook [TubeMilestone, https://tubemilestone.com/blog/structure-youtube-tutorial, checked 2026-10-24]
3878. **Hook = prerequisite + specific promise** — after the result preview, state the prerequisite level and a specific promise of what the viewer will be able to do. **How:** one line each, in about 15 s total, no channel intro animation [TubeMilestone, https://tubemilestone.com/blog/structure-youtube-tutorial, checked 2026-10-24]
3879. **Steps first, theory after each step** — put explanations in short callouts after the step they explain ("the reason this works is…"), not as preamble. **How:** move any theory segment to right after the step it explains [TubeMilestone, https://tubemilestone.com/blog/structure-youtube-tutorial, checked 2026-10-24]
3880. **Number every step, aloud and on screen** — numbered steps signal progress and create rewatch points. **How:** say "Step 3: …" and show a "Step 3" card at the same moment [TubeMilestone, https://tubemilestone.com/blog/structure-youtube-tutorial, checked 2026-10-24]
3881. **Address the common failure point** — find the 1-2 steps where viewers hit errors and add "if you see this error, here's the fix". **How:** insert a short troubleshooting aside at that step [TubeMilestone, https://tubemilestone.com/blog/structure-youtube-tutorial, checked 2026-10-24]
3882. **End with recap + the next logical step** — one-sentence recap, then the specific next problem the viewer will have and where it's solved. **How:** close on the recap line, then point to the follow-up video [TubeMilestone, https://tubemilestone.com/blog/structure-youtube-tutorial, checked 2026-10-24]
3883. **Tell first, then show silently** — Georgia Tech Library: if the viewer must pay attention to something, don't speak while showing it; tell them first, then show. **How:** leave a narration gap over the key screen action [Georgia Tech Library, https://libguides.library.gatech.edu/videotutorialguidelines/video, checked 2026-10-24]
3884. **No cursor-waving; annotate in the edit** — don't move the cursor around for emphasis; add animation or annotation during editing instead. **How:** draw a highlight box over the target, e.g. `ffmpeg -i rec.mp4 -vf "drawbox=x=100:y=80:w=200:h=60:color=yellow@0.8:t=4:enable='between(t,1,2)'" hl.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Georgia Tech Library, https://libguides.library.gatech.edu/videotutorialguidelines/video, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawbox, checked 2026-10-24]
3885. **Speed up loading; use a still if nothing moves** — speed up or slow down a browser load in the edit; if no movement is needed, use a static screenshot instead of a recording. **How:** freeze a frame for a still segment: `ffmpeg -ss 1 -i rec.mp4 -frames:v 1 shot.png` then `ffmpeg -loop 1 -t 3 -i shot.png -pix_fmt yuv420p still.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24) [Georgia Tech Library, https://libguides.library.gatech.edu/videotutorialguidelines/video, checked 2026-10-24]
3886. **Change the visual at each new point** — use visuals for pacing: change the visual each time a new point is introduced, to cue that something new is being said. **How:** align a visual change to the start of every new script point [Georgia Tech Library, https://libguides.library.gatech.edu/videotutorialguidelines/video, checked 2026-10-24]
3887. **Clean screen before recording** — simplify the screen: no extra browser tabs, clean desktop. **How:** record with a fresh browser profile / single tab [Georgia Tech Library, https://libguides.library.gatech.edu/videotutorialguidelines/video, checked 2026-10-24]

## Pacing per genre: action

3888. **Action needs stakes before choreography** — Eddie Hamilton (Kingsman, Mission: Impossible): the trick to great action is being invested in the outcome — caring about the characters and knowing what happens if the protagonist succeeds or fails; choreography, then sound and music, come after. **How:** before the action section, make sure a line or shot has stated what's at stake [ProVideo Coalition (Eddie Hamilton), https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-cut-eddie-hamilton-ace-kingsman-2/, checked 2026-10-24]
3889. **Cut to faces for the human stakes** — reaction close-ups during action (pain after a punch, fear in a skid) remind us of human stakes; Frame.io's case study is Hamilton's bathroom fight in Mission: Impossible – Fallout. **How:** include faces reacting through the action, not only the moves [Frame.io, https://blog.frame.io/2019/01/31/editing-action-scenes/, checked 2026-10-24]
3890. **Mix long shots with one-second hits** — in Haywire (no music) shots up to ten seconds long sit among one-second cuts of punches and kicks, which sell the speed and violence. **How:** let the fight breathe in longer shots and cut short only on the impacts [Frame.io, https://blog.frame.io/2019/01/31/editing-action-scenes/, checked 2026-10-24]
3891. **Establish geography, then keep it** — map the space early (John Wick 2's garage wide at 0:07), never lose the viewer's orientation, and after a deliberate surprise cut straight back to re-orient; Hamilton adds very wide shots into action for the same reason. **How:** open each action section with a wide that shows where everyone is; re-show it after any disorienting moment [Frame.io, https://blog.frame.io/2019/01/31/editing-action-scenes/, checked 2026-10-24] [ProVideo Coalition (Eddie Hamilton), https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-cut-eddie-hamilton-ace-kingsman-2/, checked 2026-10-24]
3892. **Action sequence as a mini-story** — the best sequences work standalone with a beginning, middle and end (a character wants something and overcomes obstacles); in Wonder Woman's Veld sequence each section opens with an obstacle and every cut shows it being overcome. **How:** split the sequence into sub-goals (each: obstacle, then overcoming it) and drop shots that don't advance one [Frame.io, https://blog.frame.io/2019/01/31/editing-action-scenes/, checked 2026-10-24]
3893. **Let a shot show a complete action** — over-cutting makes action indecipherable (Taken 3's single fence hop in 14 cuts); Atomic Blonde keeps three full actions in one short shot. **How:** don't split one movement across several cuts; keep each action whole in a shot [Frame.io, https://blog.frame.io/2019/01/31/editing-action-scenes/, checked 2026-10-24]
3894. **Start a scene from the wide, ignoring the script** — Hamilton watches a wide shot first to get the story of the scene as shot (the script is a working document), then quickly throws the scene together before refining. **How:** first assembly of any scene = the wide/master, then layer coverage [ProVideo Coalition (Eddie Hamilton), https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-cut-eddie-hamilton-ace-kingsman-2/, checked 2026-10-24]
3895. **Previs the complex sequence before shooting** — Hamilton built Kingsman 2's opening from stunt videos, pencil storyboards and borrowed clips, with sound effects, music and his own voice reading lines, so the director could approve it before filming. **How:** for a planned HyperFrames/voxel sequence, cut an animatic from sketches + temp audio first [ProVideo Coalition (Eddie Hamilton), https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-cut-eddie-hamilton-ace-kingsman-2/, checked 2026-10-24]
3896. **Peaks and troughs over the whole film** — Hamilton aims for a "rollercoaster" with the right amount of excitement, character, humour and breathing space, and may trim a sequence by ~10 s months later. **How:** review the whole video's energy curve and give each peak a quieter trough around it [ProVideo Coalition (Eddie Hamilton), https://www.provideocoalition.com/art-cut-eddie-hamilton-ace-kingsman-2/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Pacing per genre: horror and suspense

3897. **Quick cuts for urgency, held shots for dread** — Videomaker: a fast succession of short clips disorients and feels urgent (best with lots of action and several perspectives); a shot held longer than expected creates discomfort and anticipation that should pay off in a reveal. **How:** hold only inside a sequence that already has tension, then deliver the reveal [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17056-when-editing-a-horror-movie-or-thriller-timing-is-key/, checked 2026-10-24]
3898. **The false scare in steps** — isolated protagonist with limited vision; their anxiety shows; the antagonist is revealed to us (blocking or POV) and hidden again; tension rises (music or slow push-in); a false reveal with a quick move and exaggerated sound; relief; the guard drops; a moment's hesitation and the real reveal. **How:** lay out the beats on the timeline in this order before fine-cutting [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17056-when-editing-a-horror-movie-or-thriller-timing-is-key/, checked 2026-10-24]
3899. **Hold the peak a beat longer** — at the tension peak, don't release yet; let it simmer a beat longer than the audience expects, then hit the scare. **How:** after building tension, add a few extra frames before the scare and compare both versions [Lightworks, https://lwks.com/blog/why-most-filmmakers-fail-at-jump-scares-dont-fall-into-the-trap, checked 2026-10-24]
3900. **The Lewton bus** — Cat People (1942): gradual buildup, then a sudden shock through misdirection and a sharp sonic jolt (an explosive bus entrance), not a monster. **How:** end a dread build on a harmless loud event with a sharp sound [Carleton University (The Conversation), https://carleton.ca/news/story/frightful-films-conjure-terror-quiet, checked 2026-10-24]
3901. **Lull with a steady sound, not only silence** — absolute silence isn't necessary: a low drone or soft repetitive sound (ticking clock, babbling brook, cicadas, the shower water in Psycho) lulls the audience before the jolt. **How:** run a steady ambience bed under the build and cut it dead at the scare [Carleton University (The Conversation), https://carleton.ca/news/story/frightful-films-conjure-terror-quiet, checked 2026-10-24]
3902. **Flip the sound hierarchy** — in horror the usual dialogue-first mix goes out the door: what a sound reveals takes priority, so a faint drip of water can become the dominant element. **How:** for a horror beat, raise a small diegetic sound above the dialogue level [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17056-when-editing-a-horror-movie-or-thriller-timing-is-key/, checked 2026-10-24]
3903. **Pick the take that reveals the threat** — horror blocking often lets the audience see what the protagonist can't (the monster in the window); choose the take where the threat is visible, which needs knowing the director's intent. **How:** check every take of a reveal shot frame by frame for the visible threat [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/17056-when-editing-a-horror-movie-or-thriller-timing-is-key/, checked 2026-10-24]
3904. **Scare when the guard is down** — The Exorcist's demon flash appears during a medical exam, not a "scary" scene; The Descent's creature appears in the camera flash's light, not the dark. **How:** place a scare in a calm moment or a bright frame [Lightworks, https://lwks.com/blog/why-most-filmmakers-fail-at-jump-scares-dont-fall-into-the-trap, checked 2026-10-24]
3905. **Ration jump scares; keep a backup scare** — overusing jump scares makes them predictable; Jaws holds back the primary scare and substitutes a secondary one (Ben Gardner's boat). **How:** count jump scares; replace one with held tension [Lightworks, https://lwks.com/blog/why-most-filmmakers-fail-at-jump-scares-dont-fall-into-the-trap, checked 2026-10-24]

## Testimonials

3906. **Testimonial arc: who, challenge, solution, results, recommendation** — Origin Films: the customer introduces themselves, explains the challenge before, how the business came in, measurable results, then a genuine recommendation; the customer, not the company, is the hero. **How:** order interview bites into those five slots [Origin Films, https://www.originfilms.ca/client-testimonial-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3907. **Specific answers beat generic praise** — "we were losing qualified leads because our website wasn't converting" beats "we needed help"; avoid scripted lines like "they're the best company ever". **How:** pick the bite with concrete detail even if it's less polished [Origin Films, https://www.originfilms.ca/client-testimonial-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3908. **Keep the human imperfections** — slight pauses, smiles and laughter make testimonials feel human; memorable moments often happen between prepared answers. **How:** don't tighten every pause in a testimonial; keep the laugh between answers [Origin Films, https://www.originfilms.ca/client-testimonial-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3909. **Prepare questions, not scripts** — use interview questions about challenges, why they chose you, the experience and the results, rather than scripted responses. **How:** give the client a question list, not lines [Origin Films, https://www.originfilms.ca/client-testimonial-video/, checked 2026-10-24]
3910. **Testimonial length 60 s-3 min** — Origin Films says most high-performing testimonial videos run between 60 seconds and 3 minutes. **How:** cut a 60 s version first, a longer one only if there's material [Origin Films, https://www.originfilms.ca/client-testimonial-video/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Short films and general structure discipline

3911. **Short film rules of thumb** — Tom Barrance: grab attention from the start, keep the story moving, include only what tells the story, keep it simple (one story, one or two places, one or two characters), show don't tell, let the audience imagine. **How:** check the cut against each rule; cut a location or character if it breaks "one or two" [Learn About Film, https://learnaboutfilm.com/making-a-film/story/, checked 2026-10-24]
3912. **Very short films can skip three acts** — a very short movie can rely on an unusual approach, or show a small slice of life the viewer understands is part of a longer story. **How:** for sub-minute pieces, cut a single slice of life instead of forcing setup/confrontation/resolution [Learn About Film, https://learnaboutfilm.com/making-a-film/story/, checked 2026-10-24]
3913. **Be wary of twist-only shorts** — a twist can work, but not on its own; the story must be interesting all the way through, not just at the end. **How:** watch the short with the twist removed; if the rest is dull, rework it [Learn About Film, https://learnaboutfilm.com/making-a-film/story/, checked 2026-10-24]
3914. **Open online shorts with intrigue** — online you have a few seconds: use striking camerawork, evocative music/sound, show something intriguing before revealing what it is, or make it obvious the story is about interesting people/places/things. **How:** open on a detail or partial view and reveal its context a few shots later [Learn About Film, https://learnaboutfilm.com/making-a-film/story/, checked 2026-10-24]
3915. **Retell a known story differently** — change the point of view (Red Riding Hood from the wolf's side), the form (Goldilocks as a news story), the period/place, or add a character from another genre. **How:** for a promo, retell a familiar story beat as a different form (e.g. news report) [Learn About Film, https://learnaboutfilm.com/making-a-film/story/, checked 2026-10-24]
3916. **"Spine and nine"** — Jonny Elwyn's director lays the film out as Part 1, Part 2, Part 3 title cards on the timeline, each holding three short, simple ideas (nine in total), before any B-roll, sound design or titles. **How:** make three placeholder cards and place three bites under each before decorating [RedShark News (Jonny Elwyn), https://www.redsharknews.com/post/item/2736-one-simple-technique-that-will-always-improve-your-editing, checked 2026-10-24]
3917. **Shuffle: an opener may be an ender** — a line chosen to set context at the start often "feels like" a Part 3 ending thought because of how it was said or what it's next to. **How:** try each strong bite in the last section before settling its position [RedShark News (Jonny Elwyn), https://www.redsharknews.com/post/item/2736-one-simple-technique-that-will-always-improve-your-editing, checked 2026-10-24]
3918. **Remove repeated ideas; split conjoined ones** — bites that seem to expand a point often just repeat it (cut one); a long "single" thought may hold two different ideas — keep the right one. **How:** write each bite's idea in a few words; delete duplicates and split doubles [RedShark News (Jonny Elwyn), https://www.redsharknews.com/post/item/2736-one-simple-technique-that-will-always-improve-your-editing, checked 2026-10-24]
3919. **Give the cut a haircut (2:30 → 90 s?)** — ask whether a cut that works at 2:30 would be more digestible at 90 s; come back next day or watch with someone for fresh eyes. **How:** make a 90 s alternative before delivery and compare [RedShark News (Jonny Elwyn), https://www.redsharknews.com/post/item/2736-one-simple-technique-that-will-always-improve-your-editing, checked 2026-10-24]
3920. **Send the client a shorter first cut** — it's easier to add material back than to take it out once the client has seen it ("The mind cannot absorb what the butt cannot endure" — Steve Audette). **How:** deliver the tight version first; keep the trims ready to restore [RedShark News (Jonny Elwyn), https://www.redsharknews.com/post/item/2736-one-simple-technique-that-will-always-improve-your-editing, checked 2026-10-24]
