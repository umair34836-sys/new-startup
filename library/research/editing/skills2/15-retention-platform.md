# Retention, hooks and platform growth
Scope: YouTube retention and analytics (CTR, AVD, APV, engaged views), packaging tests, chapters, end screens, cards, posts, Premieres, Shorts rules, TikTok/Instagram/Facebook ranking signals, and Facebook/LinkedIn/Pinterest/TikTok-ads video specs (thumbnails and export codecs are in other files).
Researched 2026-10-24 via agent-reach (Exa) web search, official docs and local tests. Official help pages were read directly (curl / headless Chromium); Exa was rate-limited for most of the session.


## YouTube retention graph: reading it
3921. **Turn a top moment into the next video** — YouTube recommends expanding the content from your top moments into new uploads. **How:** list the top moments of the last 5 videos, pick the subject that repeats, and brief the next edit around it [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415, checked 2026-10-24]
3922. **A spike can mean confusion** — spikes mark rewatched/shared parts, but the same page says a spike can mean the content was unclear and viewers had to rewatch. **How:** at each spike check: is it a highlight (keep, reuse) or a fast/unclear moment (slow it, add a caption or a zoom)? [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415, checked 2026-10-24]
3923. **Key moments need 60 s and 100 views** — moments are only highlighted if detected, on videos at least 60 seconds long with at least 100 views; retention data takes 1-2 days to process. **How:** don't judge a fresh upload's retention on day 0; check again after 48 h; Shorts under 60 s won't show key moments [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415, checked 2026-10-24]
3924. **Typical retention = your last 10 similar videos** — the retention chart can be compared with "typical retention" of your 10 latest videos of similar length, a fairer yardstick than all of YouTube. **How:** compare a new edit style against typical retention, not against a viral outlier [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415, checked 2026-10-24]
3925. **Split retention by viewer segment** — the Segments drop-down compares new vs returning viewers, subscribers vs non-subscribers, and organic vs paid traffic (skippable ads, display ads). **How:** compare the curves; if non-subscribers drop off early while subscribers stay, test an intro that explains the setup for newcomers [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415, checked 2026-10-24]
3926. **Detailed activity: absolute starts and stops** — the detailed activity report shows the absolute number of views per segment and how many viewers started and stopped watching at each moment; a segment can exceed the total views because one viewer rewatches. **How:** use it to find the exact second people stop, then look at that frame (`ffmpeg -ss T -i master.mp4 -frames:v 1 stop.png`) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415, checked 2026-10-24]

## YouTube analytics metrics (CTR, AVD, APV, views)
3927. **What counts as an impression** — a thumbnail impression counts only when shown for more than 1 second with at least 50% visible, on Home, Search, feeds, Up Next and playlists; NOT on external sites, mobile web, YouTube Kids, cards/end screens inside the player, or notifications. **How:** when most traffic is external or from end screens, judge the video by views/AVD, not by CTR [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314486, checked 2026-10-24]
3928. **Normal CTR range is 2-10%** — YouTube states half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%; new channels or videos under 100 views can swing wider. **How:** don't panic at 3%; compare CTR between your own videos over the long term with their traffic sources [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154, checked 2026-10-24]
3929. **Don't read CTR too early or too finely** — YouTube advises against deciding before a substantial number of impressions, reacting to small CTR changes that aren't statistically significant, or checking right after upload. **How:** wait for a substantial number of impressions (YouTube gives no number) before changing a title/thumbnail [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154, checked 2026-10-24]
3930. **Falling CTR with rising impressions is success** — YouTube's example: 10,000 early impressions at 9% CTR, a week later 100,000 impressions at 3.5% CTR; the drop means the video reached a broader, less familiar audience. **How:** always read CTR next to the impressions count for the same period [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369, checked 2026-10-24]
3931. **CTR differs by surface** — search viewers have high intent so search CTR runs higher with fewer impressions; the Homepage gives many impressions at lower CTR because viewers are browsing. **How:** in Advanced mode break CTR down by traffic source before comparing two videos [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369, checked 2026-10-24]
3932. **Niche-topic impression ceiling** — even excellent CTR and AVD can plateau when the topic is hyper-specific (the pool of interested viewers runs out) or very crowded (competing with established channels). **How:** for a FireCraft-only topic expect a ceiling; widen the angle (a general Minecraft hook) when you want reach [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369, checked 2026-10-24]
3933. **Early CTR is inflated by core fans** — CTR in the first hours comes from the most loyal audience; it often drops when YouTube pushes the video wider. **How:** don't call a title a winner from first-hour CTR; compare at the same lifespan (7 days) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16767369, checked 2026-10-24]
3934. **Views can exceed impressions** — with lots of outside traffic (links, embeds) views can be higher than impressions, and CTR is not views divided by impressions because some views don't come from counted impressions. **How:** never compute CTR yourself from the totals; read the Studio figure [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154, checked 2026-10-24]
3935. **Views counted at play start (from Aug 24 2026)** — since 24 Aug 2026 a view counts the moment a video starts to play on Shorts, long videos and live; YPP earnings still use engaged views/engaged watch hours and eligibility qualified views. **How:** compare pre- and post-August view counts with care; judge edits by engaged views and retention instead [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281, checked 2026-10-24] [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785, checked 2026-10-24]
3936. **AVD and APV only count viewers who stayed** — average view duration (minutes) and average percentage viewed are calculated from engaged views (those who stayed past the initial seconds) and their watch time. **How:** AVD leaves out viewers who left in the initial seconds, so read engaged views / "stayed to watch" next to AVD [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281, checked 2026-10-24]
3937. **Engaged views exclude loops** — engaged views count viewers who stayed past the initial seconds, not including any loops; "Stayed to watch" is the percentage of times viewers stayed past the initial seconds of a Short. **How:** for a Short, the edit's first seconds decide "stayed to watch"; loops raise views but not engaged views [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281, checked 2026-10-24]
3938. **Low-quality playbacks are discarded** — YouTube may slow, freeze or change counts and discard low-quality playbacks, e.g. watching the same video on several devices or in several tabs. **How:** don't ask friends/family to replay a video in loops; counts can take several days to settle [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785, checked 2026-10-24]
3939. **When an ad view counts** — skippable in-stream: full watch of an 11-30 s ad or 30 s of a longer one (or interaction); in-feed: click to play or 10 s of a >10 s ad; Shorts ads: a click or 10 s. **How:** when promoting a FireCraft trailer as an ad, put the server name and hook inside the first 10 s [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785, checked 2026-10-24]
3940. **Unique reach vs co-viewed views** — unique reach estimates individual people (3 people at one TV = 1 view but +3 reach); Views (Co-viewed) counts everyone watching together. **How:** use unique reach to compare TV-heavy content honestly [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281, checked 2026-10-24]

## YouTube traffic sources and reports
3941. **Know the traffic source names** — Browse features (Home, subscriptions, Watch Later), Suggested videos (next to/after videos AND description links), Shorts feed, YouTube search, Channel pages, Playlists, End screens, Video cards, Notifications (belled subscribers separate), Sound pages, Remixed video, External, Direct or unknown. **How:** read "How viewers find this video" first, then decide: search-led → fix title words, suggested-led → match neighbour videos [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355, checked 2026-10-24]
3942. **Mine the YouTube search terms card** — the Reach tab's "YouTube search terms" report lists what viewers typed to find the video. **How:** reuse the top 2-3 terms verbatim in the next title, description first line and spoken intro [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355, checked 2026-10-24]
3943. **Use "Content suggesting this video"** — this report shows which videos viewers were watching when yours was suggested. **How:** study those neighbour videos' length, pacing and packaging; make a follow-up in the same lane [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355, checked 2026-10-24]
3944. **Sound pages bring Shorts traffic** — "Sound pages" is traffic from the shared-audio results page in the Shorts player; "Remixed video" is traffic from remixes of your content. **How:** check these two sources in the Shorts traffic report regularly [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281, checked 2026-10-24]
3945. **End screen element click rate** — the Engagement tab of a single video reports how often viewers clicked each end screen element. **How:** if one element gets almost no clicks, swap it for a "best for viewer" video or a playlist on the next upload [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9313698, checked 2026-10-24]
3946. **Subscribers by content type** — the Content tab "Subscribers" report shows subscribers gained from videos, Shorts, live, posts and others (search, channel page). **How:** put more edit effort into the format that converts best [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281, checked 2026-10-24]
3947. **Viewers across formats** — this Content tab report shows the overlap of viewers watching videos, Shorts and live. **How:** if Shorts viewers rarely watch your long videos, don't count on crossover: promote the long videos on their own [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281, checked 2026-10-24]

## YouTube audience reports
3948. **New, casual and regular viewers** — new = first watch in the period (also private browsing, cleared history, >1 year away); casual = at least monthly for 1-5 months of the past year; regular = at least monthly for over 6 months. Segments do not affect reach or monetization. **How:** read them in Audience tab to see if a video grew the core or only brought strangers [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996, checked 2026-10-24]
3949. **Regular viewers under 1% is normal** — YouTube says regular viewers can be as low as <1%, common for new channels, trending videos and Shorts-heavy channels. **How:** don't treat it as a failure signal; track its trend instead [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996, checked 2026-10-24]
3950. **Grow regulars with consistency** — channels gaining casual/regular viewers usually upload consistent topics or a familiar format, keep consistent hosts, and run popular series. **How:** keep one host (the default blocky host skin), one intro style and a series name across FireCraft videos [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996, checked 2026-10-24]
3951. **How-to videos draw one-time viewers** — YouTube notes "how-to" channels see many new viewers who may not return after getting the answer. **How:** for tutorial edits add a next-step hook ("part 2: ...") and an end-screen playlist to turn answers into return visits [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13615784, checked 2026-10-24]
3952. **Monthly audience beats subscriber count** — monthly audience = unique viewers in the last 28 days, recalculated daily; it is a truer active-audience size than subscribers. **How:** report monthly audience to the owner, not subs, when judging a season's videos [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416, checked 2026-10-24]
3953. **Subscribers watch about twice as much** — YouTube says subscribers have been shown to watch twice as much video as non-subscribers. **How:** compare the "Watch time from subscribers" report; videos with a low subscriber share are reaching new people [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13615784, checked 2026-10-24]
3954. **Average views per viewer** — in Advanced mode "SEE MORE", "Average views per viewer" shows how many of your videos a unique viewer watches. **How:** a rising value after a series launch means the series is binge-able [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7577916, checked 2026-10-24]
3955. **Schedule by "When your viewers are on YouTube"** — this Audience report shows when viewers are online in the last 28 days; YouTube suggests it for scheduling Premieres and live streams. **How:** set the Premiere/publish time inside the darkest band of that chart [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416, checked 2026-10-24]
3956. **Check the device type split** — the Device type report shows the share of watch time on mobile, computer, TV and tablet, also per content type. **How:** if TV watch time is high, check the edit's small text in a full-frame snapshot; if mobile dominates, check it at phone size [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416, checked 2026-10-24]
3957. **Find topics from "What your audience watches"** — shows other videos/Shorts/live/podcasts your viewers watched outside your channel in the past 7 days; "Channels your audience watches" (28 days) suggests collaborators. **How:** take topic and title ideas from it; pitch collabs to channels in that list [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416, checked 2026-10-24]
3958. **Formats your viewers watch** — shows whether your audience watches more videos, Shorts or live over 28 days. **How:** use it to decide whether a promo should be cut as a Short, a long video, or both [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416, checked 2026-10-24]

## YouTube Advanced mode tricks
3959. **Group videos by editing style** — Advanced mode groups hold up to 500 videos; YouTube's own example groups videos with a new editing style and compares their retention against the old style. **How:** Analytics > Advanced mode > channel name > Groups > Create new group "new-cut-style"; then Add comparison against the old group [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9717005, checked 2026-10-24] [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16766491, checked 2026-10-24]
3960. **Compare videos at the same lifespan** — compare videos over their first 24 hours, 7 days or 28 days so old and new uploads are judged fairly (e.g. first 7 days CTR for a new thumbnail style). **How:** Advanced mode > Add comparison > lifespan option [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16766491, checked 2026-10-24]
3961. **Top vs bottom video in one series** — YouTube suggests comparing the best and worst video of the same series on CTR, retention and traffic sources to see what made the difference. **How:** do it regularly per series; note the edit difference in library/research [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16766491, checked 2026-10-24]
3962. **Group by length** — the Advanced-mode tips page suggests grouping all videos under 5 minutes against those over 10 minutes. **How:** use it to choose the target length of the next promo instead of guessing [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16766491, checked 2026-10-24]
3963. **Special reports: first 24 hours and retention rate** — Advanced mode's top navigation has special reports for age/gender, retention rate, first 24 hours of video performance and real-time. **How:** open "first 24 hours" the day after a launch video goes live [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9717005, checked 2026-10-24]
3964. **Export analytics (500-row limit)** — "Export current view" downloads the current report; downloads are capped at 500 rows (the YouTube Reporting API gives more); up to 50 saved reports. **How:** export the retention/traffic view to CSV, keep it in the scratchpad (it is channel data, not for the repo) and read it with Python csv [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9717005, checked 2026-10-24]
3965. **Filter by content type and external traffic** — Advanced mode filters include content type (to isolate Shorts), traffic source (e.g. external) and a title filter. **How:** filter traffic source = External to see which video brings Discord/website visitors best [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16766491, checked 2026-10-24]

## YouTube Shorts rules that shape the edit
3966. **Over 1 minute + a claim: sources disagree** — the three-minute-Shorts page says a Short longer than one minute with an active copyright claim of any type is blocked globally (not playable, recommended or monetized), while the "Create YouTube Shorts" page now says such Shorts will no longer be automatically blocked and may remain playable; the monetization page still lists them as blocked and ineligible. **How:** play safe: keep any Short with third-party music under 60 s, or use library/music CC0 tracks; check `ffprobe -v error -show_entries format=duration -of csv=p=0 short.mp4` before upload (tested here 2026-10-24) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877, checked 2026-10-24] [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10343433, checked 2026-10-24] [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220, checked 2026-10-24]
3967. **Shorts Audio Library songs: up to 90 s** — in a 3-minute Short most Shorts-library songs can run up to 90 s, some only 60 or 30 s; royalty-free YouTube Audio Library music won't get a Content ID claim. **How:** for a 2-3 min Short, plan the music bed in sections (≤90 s per library song) or use our own CC0 track for the full length [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877, checked 2026-10-24]
3968. **Keep it long-form with a wide frame** — YouTube says to stop a video being classified as a Short, use a wider aspect ratio such as 16:9; square or vertical up to 3 min becomes a Short. **How:** a 1:1 teaser under 3 min WILL be a Short; render 16:9 (1920x1080) if it must live on the watch page [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877, checked 2026-10-24]
3969. **Old vertical uploads stay long-form** — vertical/square videos uploaded before 15 Oct 2024 (Official Artist Channels: before 8 Dec 2025) remain long-form videos. **How:** to turn an old vertical clip into a Short, re-cut and upload it as new content rather than expecting it to move [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877, checked 2026-10-24]
3970. **Use the Shorts visual guides for overlays** — in the YouTube app editor, moving text or stickers shows guides where the like button, comments and description appear; white lines mark a non-safe area near the edges. **How:** after rendering, open the Short in the app's text tool once to check our burned-in captions don't sit under those guides [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842, checked 2026-10-24]
3971. **Align tool for match transitions** — Shorts' Align shows a transparent overlay of the last captured frame so the next clip lines up for a seamless transition. **How:** in our pipeline do the same: export the last frame (`ffmpeg -sseof -0.04 -i a.mp4 -frames:v 1 last.png`) and overlay it at 50% in the next shot's preview [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842, checked 2026-10-24] [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948448, checked 2026-10-24]
3972. **Speed range 0.3x-3x** — the Shorts camera Speed tool offers 0.3x to 3x, suggested for slow-mo trick shots and sped-up action. **How:** our equivalent is ffmpeg `setpts` (with minterpolate for slow-mo, see CLAUDE.md recipe) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948448, checked 2026-10-24]
3973. **Sync to beat for photo/video montages** — select at least two photos/videos and Shorts auto-syncs them to music; you can change the track or skip. **How:** offline we cut on bars instead (CLAUDE.md: pick a BPM, cut every bar); use the app feature only for quick phone-made Shorts [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842, checked 2026-10-24]
3974. **Poll sticker: 2 options, stays open** — a poll sticker gives 2 options, stays open while the Short is published, viewers can change votes; not on unlisted/private Shorts. **How:** end a FireCraft Short with a question ("Kaun jeetega?") and add the poll in the app after upload [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842, checked 2026-10-24]
3975. **Quiz sticker** — question up to 3 lines, two answers up to 2 lines each; the first option is the correct one by default; if the video already shows the question, leave the field blank. **How:** burn the question into the end of the Short and add only the answer options as a quiz sticker (the question field can stay blank) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842, checked 2026-10-24]
3976. **Q&A and "Add yours" stickers** — Q&A answers become public comments; "Add yours" lets viewers respond with their own Short to a prompt. **How:** use "Add yours" for community challenges (e.g. "apna best PvP clip dikhao") to spread the prompt through other channels [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842, checked 2026-10-24]
3977. **Reply to comments with a Short** — a Short made as a reply notifies the commenter and appears under the original comment. **How:** turn the best question in comments into an answer Short [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842, checked 2026-10-24] [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12816427, checked 2026-10-24]
3978. **Text keeps viewers with you through fast edits** — YouTube's Shorts tips say on-screen text guides viewers and keeps context through fast edits, can carry calls to action, explain a joke or introduce you. **How:** in HyperFrames time each caption to the clip it explains; drag start/stop like the Shorts timeline [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13380879, checked 2026-10-24]
3979. **Filters stack** — a filter chosen while editing a Short is layered on top of one chosen while recording. **How:** when the owner records with a Shorts filter, don't add our grade on top without checking a frame first (`npx hyperframes grade-compare`) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13380879, checked 2026-10-24]
3980. **Volume mix in the Shorts editor** — Volume balances music, original audio and voiceover. **How:** if the owner adds app music over our render, ask them to lower the app music so our voice stays on top (our render already holds the mix) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16215842, checked 2026-10-24]
3981. **Post a test Short first** — YouTube suggests uploading a test Short (private/unlisted) to learn the steps, then keep a posting schedule you can maintain. **How:** upload the render as private once, check crop/safe zones on a phone, then publish [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12921536, checked 2026-10-24]
3982. **Remixes credit you back** — Shorts made with remixed content are attributed to the original (audio source on the Sound page, video source linked in the Shorts player), which YouTube calls a chance for new viewers to discover you. **How:** make long FireCraft videos remix-friendly: a clean 1-5 s signature moment and a reusable original sound [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10623810, checked 2026-10-24]
3983. **Remix "Cut" = 1-5 s sample** — Cut samples a 1-5 second segment (audio + video); Sound samples up to 90 s of audio (some videos only 30 s); Green Screen uses a video as background. **How:** for a reaction Short, open the source video in the YouTube app > Share > Remix > Cut, then add our commentary [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10623810, checked 2026-10-24]
3984. **Shorts templates spread a format** — any Short can be used as a template (Remix > Use this template) and others drop their clips in. **How:** when a FireCraft Short format works, it is available to others as a template via Remix > Use this template [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10623810, checked 2026-10-24]
3985. **Non-original Shorts earn nothing** — ineligible Shorts views include unedited clips of others' movies/TV, re-uploads of other creators' content, and compilations with no original content added. **How:** every Short we cut must add original edit/voice/commentary; never just re-post someone's clip [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220, checked 2026-10-24]
3986. **Music in a Short cuts the pool, not your share** — Shorts revenue with 1 licensed track sends half the associated revenue to the Creator Pool (2 tracks: one third); creators keep 45% of their allocation, and their allocation itself isn't reduced by music use. **How:** for monetized Shorts, our own/CC0 music keeps the pool larger; licensed partner music doesn't change the creator's 45% [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220, checked 2026-10-24]

## Titles, descriptions and packaging tests (YouTube)
3987. **Front-load the title, branding last** — viewers may see only part of a title, so keep it short, put the most important words first and save episode numbers and branding for the end. **How:** "Lifesteal heart steal trick | FireCraft S2 Ep 3", not "FireCraft S2 Ep 3: ..." [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300, checked 2026-10-24]
3988. **Searchable vs intriguing title** — searchable titles say exactly what to expect (reach searchers); intriguing titles spark curiosity for viewers not hunting that topic. **How:** tutorials/server-join guides get searchable titles; event/drama videos get intriguing ones [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300, checked 2026-10-24]
3989. **Description: the first lines are the preview** — viewers see only the first lines before "Show more"/"more", so describe the video there; the rest can hold channel info; max 5,000 characters. **How:** line 1 = what the video is + the key word; links and credits below [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449, checked 2026-10-24]
3990. **1-2 main words in title AND description** — identify 1-2 main words that describe the video and feature them prominently in both; search terms are matched with the description text. **How:** e.g. "Minecraft lifesteal server" in title and first description line [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449, checked 2026-10-24]
3991. **Unique description per video** — each video should have a unique description to be found in search and stand out from similar videos; a default description can auto-fill channel info. **How:** set Settings > Upload defaults description to the server links/credits, then write a fresh top paragraph each time [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449, checked 2026-10-24]
3992. **Description formatting and bullets** — descriptions support bold, italic and strikethrough; start a line with "*", "+" or "-" and a space for a bullet list. **How:** list chapters/links as bullets for readability [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449, checked 2026-10-24]
3993. **Pretend to search for your own video** — YouTube suggests checking the "How viewers find" search terms and adding missing words a searcher would type. **How:** compare the search-terms card with the description and add the missing ones in natural sentences [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449, checked 2026-10-24]
3994. **Check the description on every surface** — check how the description appears in search results, on the watch page and on mobile, so the important info shows. **How:** open the video on a phone after publishing and read the visible two lines [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449, checked 2026-10-24]

## YouTube recommendation signals and diagnosing drops
3995. **What the system watches** — when recommended, does the viewer click, ignore, or press "not interested"; then do they stay (AVD and average % viewed are both ranking signals); then did they enjoy it (likes, post-watch surveys). **How:** an edit must win all three: packaging (click), retention (stay), payoff (like/satisfaction) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225, checked 2026-10-24]
3996. **No favoured format** — YouTube says its system has no opinion about the type of video and doesn't favour a format; ranking is performance plus personalisation. **How:** pick format by what the audience watches, not by rumours of algorithm preferences [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225, checked 2026-10-24]
3997. **Three external factors: topic, competition, seasonality** — reach depends on topic interest worldwide, competition with all channels, and seasonal swings (holidays, school). **How:** plan launch videos (e.g. a new season) outside exam weeks and big holidays for the audience's country [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225, checked 2026-10-24]

## Content strategy from analytics (YouTube)
4008. **Both AVD and % viewed matter** — YouTube says the algorithm uses both absolute (AVD) and relative (% viewed) watch time, and encourages making videos the appropriate length for the content. **How:** don't pad a 4-minute idea to 10 minutes for AVD, and don't cut a strong 12-minute story just to raise % viewed [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616340, checked 2026-10-24]

## YouTube discovery myths and facts (official FAQ)
4099. **Relative watch time for short, absolute for long** — YouTube: broadly, relative watch time (% viewed) matters more for short videos and absolute watch time (AVD) more for longer videos. **How:** for a 60 s Short aim for near-full completion; for a 12-min video judge minutes watched, not % [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4100. **Title/thumbnail change re-ranks only through viewer behaviour** — changing title or thumbnail can bring more views because viewers react differently, not because the change itself re-ranks; "don't change what's working". **How:** only swap packaging on a video with weak CTR, never on one that is climbing [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4101. **Packaging to avoid** — YouTube lists titles/thumbnails that are deceiving/clickbaity, shocking, disgusting, gratuitously violent, indecent, or loud (ALL CAPS or "!!!!!"); these turn viewers away and can get content removed. **How:** check every title against this list before upload [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4102. **Tags are not important** — tags mainly correct common misspellings (YouTube vs U Tube). **How:** add 2-3 misspelling tags ("firecraft smp", "fire craft") and spend the time on title/description instead [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4103. **Search ranks by match and engagement, not views** — search ranking uses how well title, description and video content match the search, and which videos drive the most engagement for it; results aren't a most-viewed list. **How:** say the searched words in the video too (content match), not only in text fields [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4104. **Unlisted first, public later is fine** — flipping an unlisted upload to public doesn't hurt performance; what matters is viewers' response after publishing. **How:** upload unlisted, check on phone, then publish [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4105. **Publish time and cadence myths** — publish time isn't known to affect long-term performance (it matters for Live and Premieres); growth isn't correlated with time between uploads; breaks showed no correlation with view changes. **How:** schedule for early viewers if you like, but don't rush an edit to hit a weekday [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4106. **Channel location doesn't target viewers** — location settings aren't used for recommendations. **How:** reach Pakistani/Indian viewers with language and topic, not a location setting [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4107. **Monetisation status doesn't affect discovery** — the system doesn't know which videos monetise; violent/graphic content may be recommended less because of the content itself. **How:** no need to fear "limited ads" as a reach penalty; watch the content [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4108. **When an old video takes off** — reasons YouTube lists: topic rising, new viewers binging, more viewers accepting the recommendation, or a new episode sending people back. **How:** when an old FireCraft video spikes, make a follow-up quickly and link it with a card/end screen [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]
4109. **Viral spike that doesn't return** — one listed cause of falling Home/Suggested traffic: a viral video whose viewers didn't come back. **How:** after a viral hit, judge the next uploads against typical retention, not against the hit [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805, checked 2026-10-24]

4144. **Boostable reel rules** — to boost: 90 s or less, full-screen 9:16, no licensed music (royalty-free or original audio), no third-party face/camera effects, GIFs, product tags or interactive stickers, not already shared to Facebook. **How:** if a reel may be boosted, keep it ≤90 s, 1080x1920, with our CC0/own music [Instagram Help, https://help.instagram.com/570215404599013, checked 2026-10-24]
4145. **Boost from the web to skip the Apple fee** — in most regions a 30% Apple service fee may apply when adding funds in the Instagram iOS app; boosting via instagram.com avoids it. **How:** tell the owner to boost from a browser [Instagram Help, https://help.instagram.com/570215404599013, checked 2026-10-24]

## How YouTube recommendation and search work (official explainers)
4146. **Up Next keys on the current video** — YouTube says the video being watched is the main signal for what plays next, while Home relies mainly on watch history. **How:** to win Up Next, make follow-ups that clearly continue a popular video (same topic, series name in title) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387, checked 2026-10-24]
4147. **Satisfaction surveys, not just watch time** — signals include watch and search history, subscriptions, likes, dislikes, "Not interested", "Don't recommend channel" and satisfaction surveys that help the system understand satisfaction beyond watch time. **How:** avoid packaging that misrepresents the video; deliver the promised payoff [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387, checked 2026-10-24]
4148. **AI-made videos recommended equally** — YouTube states videos made with AI can be recommended as broadly as videos made without AI. **How:** our ACE-Step/AI-assisted promos face no recommendation penalty for being AI-assisted; judge them by the same metrics [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387, checked 2026-10-24]
4149. **Search = relevance, engagement, quality** — YouTube search weighs relevance (title, tags, description and video content matching the query), engagement (e.g. watch time of that video for that query) and quality (channels showing expertise/authoritativeness); no paid placement. **How:** for search-driven tutorials, answer the query early so query-specific watch time stays high [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16090438, checked 2026-10-24]
4150. **Two goals of the algorithm** — help each viewer find videos they want, and maximise long-term viewer satisfaction; it "follows the audience", so ask "does my audience like this?" not "does the algorithm like it?". **How:** test cuts on real viewers (Discord preview) rather than chasing rumoured tricks [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387, checked 2026-10-24]
4151. **Consistent title/thumbnail style** — YouTube says a consistent title and thumbnail style makes videos instantly identifiable. **How:** fix one FireCraft title pattern and one thumbnail layout per series [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387, checked 2026-10-24]
4152. **Series + "if you liked this, watch..." CTAs** — YouTube suggests content series, clear CTAs ("If you liked this, then watch..."), playlists and end screens to help viewers find more of your content. **How:** end each episode with a spoken CTA to the next one, matched by an end-screen element [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387, checked 2026-10-24]
4153. **Each video gets fresh judgement, but misses add up** — the algorithm uses fresh performance data per video, so one failed experiment won't necessarily hurt; frequently posting content that doesn't resonate can affect channel performance over time. **How:** experiment freely, but don't upload many weak test videos in a row [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387, checked 2026-10-24]

## Tested pre-upload checks (scripts for this repo)
4154. **Chapter list validator** — YouTube chapters need a first timestamp of 00:00, at least three timestamps in ascending order, each chapter at least 10 s. **How:** `python3 chapters.py "0:Intro" "42:Rules" "95:Heart steal"` prints `00:00 Intro / 00:42 Rules / 01:35 Heart steal` and aborts with an error if a rule is broken (script in scratchpad sk2_15/tools/chapters.py; tested here 2026-10-24) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579, checked 2026-10-24]
4155. **Candidate chapter points from scene cuts** — list hard cuts as possible chapter starts, then pick real topic changes by hand. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.mp4 -vf "select='gt(scene,0.4)',showinfo" -f null - 2>&1 | grep -o 'pts_time:[0-9.]*'` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#select_002c-aselect, checked 2026-10-24] [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579, checked 2026-10-24]
4156. **Will it become a Short? check before upload** — square/vertical up to 3 min is a Short; over 1 min with any claim is blocked. **How:** `python3 -c "import subprocess,json;d=json.loads(subprocess.check_output(['ffprobe','-v','error','-show_entries','stream=width,height:format=duration','-of','json','in.mp4']));w,h=d['streams'][0]['width'],d['streams'][0]['height'];t=float(d['format']['duration']);print('Short' if h>=w and t<=180 else 'long-form',t,'over60' if t>60 else 'under60')"` (tested here 2026-10-24) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877, checked 2026-10-24]
4157. **End-screen length check** — end screens need a video of at least 25 s and sit in the last 5-20 s. **How:** `python3 -c "import subprocess;d=float(subprocess.check_output(['ffprobe','-v','error','-show_entries','format=duration','-of','csv=p=0','in.mp4']));print('ok' if d>=25 else 'too short', 'window from', round(max(0,d-20),2))"` then keep that window free of key on-screen text (tested here 2026-10-24) [YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789, checked 2026-10-24]
4158. **Measure the loop seam** — for clips that will loop (LinkedIn ads under 30 s loop; Shorts loops don't add engaged views), compare the first and last frame numerically. **How:** `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -frames:v 1 first.png; ffmpeg -sseof -0.04 -i in.mp4 -frames:v 1 last.png; ffmpeg -i first.png -i last.png -lavfi ssim -f null - 2>&1 | grep -o "All:[0-9.]*"` (tested here 2026-10-24; this only reports similarity, it doesn't judge how the loop looks) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#ssim, checked 2026-10-24] [LinkedIn Help, https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a424737, checked 2026-10-24]
