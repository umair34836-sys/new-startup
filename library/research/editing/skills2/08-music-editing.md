## Tempo and beat detection (librosa)
2241. **Beat times in seconds straight from the tracker** — `beat_track` returns tempo and beat positions; `units='time'` gives seconds instead of frames, ready for cut lists. **How:** `/home/user/tools/venv/bin/python -c "import librosa;y,sr=librosa.load('song.wav',sr=22050);t,b=librosa.beat.beat_track(y=y,sr=sr,units='time');print(t,b[:6])"` gave 79 beats starting 0.07, 0.464, 0.836 s on a 30 s CC0 lofi clip (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.beat_track.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2242. **librosa's BPM reading is coarse at the default hop** — tempo is estimated from autocorrelation of the onset envelope, measured in frames; with the default hop (512 at 22.05 kHz) an exact 120 BPM click read 117.45, with a finer hop it read closer. **How:** `librosa.feature.tempo(y=y,sr=sr)` gave 117.45 and `librosa.feature.tempo(y=y,sr=sr,hop_length=128)` gave 120.19 on a synthetic 120 BPM click; `npx hyperframes audio click.wav --only bpm --json` gave 120 (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.feature.tempo.html, checked 2026-10-24] [librosa tutorial "Rhythm", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/01-intro/06-rhythm.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2243. **Tempo = first autocorrelation peak** — the rhythm tutorial estimates tempo from the first prominent autocorrelation peak of the onset envelope (not lag 0): a peak at about 0.4 s is 2.5 cycles/s = 150 BPM. **How:** BPM = 60 / lag-of-first-peak; read `librosa.feature.tempogram` to see those lags over time [librosa tutorial "Rhythm", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/01-intro/06-rhythm.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2244. **Three detectors, three tempos: check before you cut** — on the same 30 s lofi clip `beat_track` said 161.5, `feature.tempo` 107.67 and HyperFrames 104.8 BPM; HyperFrames' film guide warns that on calm music a tracker invents a metronome, often at double tempo. **How:** run both `librosa.beat.beat_track` and `npx hyperframes audio <track> --only bpm --json`; if they disagree, cut on phrases and energy changes instead (tested here 2026-10-24) [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/film.md "On music"] [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.beat_track.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2245. **Steer half/double tempo with start_bpm** — `start_bpm` is the tracker's initial tempo guess. **How:** on the lofi clip `start_bpm=60` or `90` gave 78.3 BPM and `120` or `180` gave 161.5 BPM: try the guess that matches what you hear (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.beat_track.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2246. **Cap the tempo with max_tempo** — `feature.tempo` only estimates below `max_tempo` when given. **How:** `librosa.feature.tempo(y=y,sr=sr,max_tempo=100)` moved the lofi reading from 107.67 to 78.3 BPM (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.feature.tempo.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2247. **A tempo prior instead of a guess** — `prior` takes a scipy distribution over BPM and overrides `start_bpm`; the plp docs show a log-normal prior centred on 120. **How:** `pr=scipy.stats.lognorm(loc=np.log(120),scale=120,s=1); librosa.beat.beat_track(y=y,sr=sr,prior=pr)` gave 78.3 BPM on the lofi clip (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.beat_track.html, checked 2026-10-24] [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.plp.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2248. **Known BPM: only place the beats** — pass `bpm=` and the tracker uses it instead of estimating, so you only get the beat phase. **How:** `librosa.beat.beat_track(y=y,sr=sr,bpm=85,units='time')` returned beats at 0.07, 0.836, 1.602 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.beat_track.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2249. **trim=False when the music never goes silent** — by default beat_track assumes silence at the end and trims the last beats; the dynamic-tempo tutorial sets `trim=False` for audio that runs to the end. **How:** `librosa.beat.beat_track(y=y,sr=sr,trim=False)`; on a 16 s click both settings gave 31 beats here, so compare counts on your track (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa tutorial "Beat tracking with time-varying tempo", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/03-advanced/plot_dynamic_beat.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2250. **Beats as a per-frame on/off array** — `sparse=False` returns a boolean array (needed for multi-channel input). **How:** `librosa.beat.beat_track(y=y,sr=sr,sparse=False)` gave 1292 frames with 79 True (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.beat_track.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2251. **Track beats through tempo changes** — estimate a tempo per frame with `aggregate=None` (wider `std_bpm`) and feed that array to `beat_track(bpm=...)`; the tutorial shows a static tempo drifting off an accelerating beat. **How:** `td=librosa.feature.tempo(y=y,sr=sr,aggregate=None,std_bpm=4); librosa.beat.beat_track(y=y,sr=sr,units='time',bpm=td,trim=False)` (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa tutorial "Beat tracking with time-varying tempo", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/03-advanced/plot_dynamic_beat.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2252. **Autocorrelation window size changes the answer** — `ac_size` is the autocorrelation window in seconds (8 s default). **How:** on the lofi clip `ac_size=4` gave 161.5 BPM and `ac_size=16` gave 103.36 BPM: use a longer window for a steadier global tempo (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.feature.tempo.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2253. **PLP beats for drifting tempo or streaming** — predominant local pulse finds a locally stable tempo per frame; the docs prefer it over beat_track when tempo varies a lot, and its local maxima are beat estimates. **How:** `p=librosa.beat.plp(onset_envelope=oe,sr=sr); bp=np.flatnonzero(librosa.util.localmax(p))` found 86 pulses in 30 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.plp.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2254. **Limit PLP to a tempo range** — `tempo_min`/`tempo_max` bound the permissible tempo (30-300 default). **How:** `librosa.beat.plp(y=y,sr=sr,tempo_min=60,tempo_max=100)` gave 40 pulses with a median spacing of 0.766 s on the lofi clip (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.plp.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2255. **Median-aggregated onset envelope** — the beat_track docs build the envelope with `aggregate=np.median` across frequency and pass it in. **How:** `oe=librosa.onset.onset_strength(y=y,sr=sr,aggregate=np.median); librosa.beat.beat_track(onset_envelope=oe,sr=sr)` (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.beat.beat_track.html, checked 2026-10-24] [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.onset.onset_strength.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2256. **Tempogram to see tempo over time** — local autocorrelation of the onset envelope; default window 384 frames is about 8.9 s. **How:** `librosa.feature.tempogram(y=y,sr=sr)` gave a 384 x 1292 matrix for 30 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.feature.tempogram.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2257. **Let the owner hear the beat grid** — the rhythm tutorial sonifies beats as a click track and plays song left, clicks right. **How:** `c=librosa.clicks(times=b,sr=sr,length=len(y)); sf.write('check.wav',np.stack([y,c]).T,sr)` wrote a stereo check file (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa tutorial "Rhythm", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/01-intro/06-rhythm.html, checked 2026-10-24] [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.clicks.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2258. **Click sound you can pick out** — `click_freq` (default 1 kHz) and `click_duration` (default 0.1 s) set the click; the dynamic-beat tutorial uses 660 Hz and 0.25 s. **How:** `librosa.clicks(times=b,sr=sr,click_freq=660,click_duration=0.25,length=len(y))` (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa tutorial "Beat tracking with time-varying tempo", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/03-advanced/plot_dynamic_beat.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2259. **Synthetic click track at an exact BPM** — aevalsrc evaluates an expression per sample; a decaying 60 Hz burst every 60/BPM s makes a test beat for checking tools. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc=exprs='0.8*sin(2*PI*60*t)*exp(-30*mod(t\,0.5))':s=44100:d=16" -ac 2 click120.wav` (HyperFrames read it as 120 BPM) (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aevalsrc, checked 2026-10-24]

## Onsets and slice points
2260. **Onset times in seconds** — `onset_detect` peak-picks the onset strength envelope; `units='time'` returns seconds. **How:** `librosa.onset.onset_detect(y=y,sr=sr,units='time')` found 134 onsets in the 30 s clip (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.onset.onset_detect.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2261. **Backtrack onsets before slicing** — `backtrack=True` rolls each onset back to the preceding energy minimum, "primarily useful when using onsets as slice points". **How:** `librosa.onset.onset_detect(y=y,sr=sr,units='time',backtrack=True)` moved onsets a median 46 ms earlier on the lofi clip (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.onset.onset_detect.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2262. **Backtrack to RMS minima** — `onset_backtrack(events, energy)` matches events to the nearest preceding minimum of any energy function you give it. **How:** `rms=librosa.feature.rms(S=np.abs(librosa.stft(y)))[0]; librosa.onset.onset_backtrack(ev,rms)` (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.onset.onset_backtrack.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2263. **Onsets per frequency band** — `onset_strength_multi` computes one envelope per channel range of spectrogram bins (`channels` = bin boundaries). **How:** `librosa.onset.onset_strength_multi(y=y,sr=sr,channels=[0,8,32,128])` gave 3 envelopes (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.onset.onset_strength_multi.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2264. **onset_detect is not a pure peak picker** — the docs note it may favour earlier points on the rising edge of a peak; naive local maxima give far more events than really occur. **How:** use `onset_detect` (tuned peak_pick) rather than `librosa.util.localmax(onset_env)` for cut points [librosa tutorial "Onset detection", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/01-intro/05-onsets.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2265. **Kick-band onsets in HyperFrames** — `hyperframes audio --band lo,hi` measures onsets only in that band, and `--threshold` (0-1, default 0.1) sets the strength floor. **How:** `npx hyperframes audio music.wav --only onsets --band 40,150 --threshold 0.3 --json` found 42 low-band hits in 30 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [repo: `npx hyperframes audio --help` v0.8.191]
2266. **Fewer false onsets on vibrato** — onset_strength's `max_size` applies a local max filter across frequency (Böck & Widmer vibrato suppression); 1 disables it. **How:** `oe=librosa.onset.onset_strength(y=y,sr=sr,max_size=3)`; on a CC0 tabla/synth clip onsets went from 80 (max_size=1) to 75 (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.onset.onset_strength.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2267. **Energy vs spectral-flux onsets in HyperFrames** — `hyperframes audio --onset-method` is spectral-flux by default or energy. **How:** on the same 20 s CC0 clip spectral-flux found 95 onsets and `--onset-method energy` 201: compare both before choosing hit points (tested here 2026-10-24) [repo: `npx hyperframes audio --help` v0.8.191]

## Finding sections and cut points
2273. **k-section split of a song** — `segment.agglomerative` partitions features into k contiguous segments and returns left boundaries (always starting at 0). **How:** `C=librosa.feature.chroma_cqt(y=y,sr=sr); librosa.frames_to_time(librosa.segment.agglomerative(C,4),sr=sr)` gave 0, 2.95, 24.17, 27.14 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.segment.agglomerative.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2274. **Laplacian structure segmentation (McFee & Ellis)** — the tutorial beat-synchronises a CQT, builds a recurrence matrix (`width=3` blocks links within a bar, `mode='affinity'`), median-filters it with `timelag_filter`, combines it with an MFCC path graph, then clusters eigenvectors with k-means; boundaries are where the label changes. **How:** follow the tutorial code; `librosa.segment.recurrence_matrix(C,mode='affinity',sym=True)` ran here (sklearn 1.9.1 is in the venv) (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa tutorial "Laplacian segmentation", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/03-advanced/plot_segmentation.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2275. **Beat-synchronous features** — `util.sync` aggregates feature frames between beat positions so each column is one beat. **How:** `librosa.util.sync(librosa.feature.chroma_cqt(y=y,sr=sr),beats,aggregate=np.median)` turned 1292 frames into 81 beat columns (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.util.sync.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2276. **Include frame 0 in beat lists** — `util.fix_frames(beats, x_min=0)` adds the start frame so segments cover the whole song (the segmentation tutorial does this for beat times). **How:** `[3 20 36]` became `[0 3 20 36]` (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.util.fix_frames.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2277. **Find the loudest stretch** — `feature.rms` gives frame energy; `times_like` maps frames to seconds. **How:** `rms=librosa.feature.rms(y=y)[0]; librosa.times_like(rms,sr=sr)[rms.argmax()]` gave 27.45 s on the lofi clip (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.feature.rms.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2278. **Picture of the whole song** — showwavespic renders the waveform as one image; `split_channels=1` draws each channel. **How:** `ffmpeg -i song.wav -filter_complex showwavespic=s=1200x200:split_channels=1 -frames:v 1 wave.png` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#showwavespic, checked 2026-10-24]
2279. **Make quiet passages visible in the waveform** — showwavespic `scale` can be lin, log, sqrt or cbrt (default linear), `filter` average or peak. **How:** `showwavespic=s=1200x240:colors=white:scale=sqrt` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#showwavespic, checked 2026-10-24]
2280. **Spectrum picture with a legend** — showspectrumpic draws the whole file's spectrogram with time/frequency axes when `legend=1`. **How:** `ffmpeg -i song.wav -lavfi showspectrumpic=s=800x400:legend=1 spec.png` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#showspectrumpic, checked 2026-10-24]
2281. **Constant-Q view of notes** — showcqt renders a musical-pitch (CQT) spectrum video; librosa's harmony tutorial explains CQT bins are geometrically spaced like notes. **How:** `ffmpeg -i song.wav -t 3 -filter_complex showcqt=s=640x360 -an cqt.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#showcqt, checked 2026-10-24] [librosa tutorial "Harmony", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/01-intro/04-harmony.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2282. **Find gaps and stops** — silencedetect logs silence_start/end when level stays under the noise tolerance (default -60 dB) for at least d seconds (default 2). **How:** `ffmpeg -i in.wav -af silencedetect=n=-50dB:d=1 -f null -` reported 20 to 25 s on a padded clip (prints at info level, so don't add `-v error`) (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#silencedetect, checked 2026-10-24]
2283. **Non-silent intervals in Python** — `effects.split` returns (start, end) sample pairs of non-silent audio, `top_db` below the reference peak. **How:** `librosa.effects.split(y,top_db=40)` returned 0-20.06 s on a clip with 5 s of padding (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.effects.split.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2284. **Trim a track's silent head and tail** — `effects.trim` cuts segments `top_db` below the max RMS and returns the kept index; a fully uniform or silent signal is not trimmed with the default ref. **How:** `yt,idx=librosa.effects.trim(y,top_db=40)` cut 25.0 s to 20.06 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.effects.trim.html, checked 2026-10-24]

## Structure rules for cutting picture to a song (repo guides)
2285. **Music-video tells to avoid** — the same treatment for more than about 6 s, a spectrum bar or waveform as the main picture, and every cut on every beat. **How:** vary treatment per section; cut per strong beat only in rhythmic sections [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/types/music.md]
2286. **Hits start early, reactions late** — a move that must land on a hit starts 40-190 ms early; a reaction starts 0-45 ms after; the hit itself is a 0 ms `tl.set`, because easing a hit kills it. **How:** `tl.set(el,{opacity:1},beat)` for the hit, `tl.to(...,beat-0.12)` for the approach [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/film.md "On music"]
2287. **Words hold a beat, footage follows the section** — a word meant to be read holds at least one beat; on rhythmic sections one clip per strong beat, on calm ones one slow push from 1.0 to 1.08. **How:** schedule captions in whole beats from the beats JSON [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/film.md "On music"]
2288. **Enter at the strongest bar, end on a bar line** — start music at the track's strongest bar rather than a quiet intro, and end on a bar line with a short fade under the end card. **How:** pick the entry with `--only sections` and set `data-media-start` on the `<audio>` [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/sound.md §3]
2289. **Test the opening against later 5-second sections** — for short videos don't assume the file's start is the best edit point; if it opens with a quiet build, trim from a stronger later section with a short fade-in and longer fade-out, and make sure the music covers the whole cut with no silent tail. **How:** re-check whenever the composition length changes [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/audio/references/bgm.md]
2290. **Library loops under a film** — library music often comes as 15-30 s loops: loop the bed to the film with a 0.4 s fade-in and a 1.5 s fade-out. **How:** `-stream_loop -1` or `aloop` to length, then `afade=t=in:d=0.4` and a 1.5 s `afade=t=out` [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/film.md "On music"]

## Cutting and fitting music to length (ffmpeg, pydub)
2291. **Sample-exact trim** — atrim's `start_sample`/`end_sample` count samples, unlike `start`/`end` which read timestamps; add asetpts to restart timestamps at zero. **How:** `ffmpeg -i song.wav -af "atrim=start_sample=44100:end_sample=132300,asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS" bar.wav` gave exactly 2.000 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#atrim, checked 2026-10-24]
2292. **Chain atrims to AND their limits** — with several start/end options atrim is greedy and keeps samples matching any of them; to keep only what matches all, chain several atrim filters. **How:** `atrim=start=10,atrim=end=20` rather than mixing both forms in one atrim [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#atrim, checked 2026-10-24]
2293. **Fast window cut with input seeking** — `-ss` before `-i` seeks the input; when transcoding, accurate seek (default) decodes and drops the part before the position. **How:** `ffmpeg -ss 12.5 -t 6 -i song.wav part.wav` gave 6.000 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Main-options, checked 2026-10-24]
2294. **Grab the real ending** — `-sseof` seeks relative to the end of file (negative = earlier). **How:** `ffmpeg -sseof -8 -i song.wav ending.wav` gave the last 8.000 s, ready to backtime the button (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Main-options, checked 2026-10-24]

## Crossfades between tracks
2319. **Two-track crossfade with exponential curves** — acrossfade fades over the end of the first stream; the docs' first example uses `c1=exp:c2=exp`. **How:** `ffmpeg -i a.wav -i b.wav -filter_complex acrossfade=d=4:c1=exp:c2=exp ab.wav` gave 46 s from 30 + 20 s (4 s overlap) (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossfade, checked 2026-10-24]
2320. **Fade out, then fade in, no overlap** — `o=0` (overlap off) plays the faded end and the faded start back to back. **How:** `acrossfade=d=4:o=0:c1=exp:c2=exp` gave 50 s, the full 30 + 20 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossfade, checked 2026-10-24]
2321. **Check the local ffmpeg before using new doc options** — the online docs list acrossfade `n`/`inputs` for crossfading many inputs, but ffmpeg 6.1.1 here rejects it ("Option not found"); chain pairs instead. **How:** `ffmpeg -h filter=acrossfade` to see options; `[0][1]acrossfade=d=2[a];[a][2]acrossfade=d=2` joined three files to 62 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossfade, checked 2026-10-24]
2322. **Crossfade length in samples** — `nb_samples`/`ns` (default 44100) sets the crossfade when no duration is given. **How:** `acrossfade=ns=88200` overlapped 2 s at 44.1 kHz (30 + 20 s became 48 s) (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossfade, checked 2026-10-24]
2323. **Keep the outgoing track at full level** — curve `nofade` leaves that input as-is while the other fades. **How:** `acrossfade=d=3:c1=nofade:c2=tri` brings the new track in under an unchanged old one (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade, checked 2026-10-24]
2324. **Constant power for different songs, constant gain for the same song** — SoX's table: t (linear) and h (half-cosine) are constant-gain fades for correlated audio; q (quarter-cosine) is constant power for uncorrelated audio. Measured ffmpeg fade-outs at the midpoint: qsin 0.707 (-3.0 dB), hsin and tri 0.500 (-6.0 dB). **How:** two different tracks: `c1=qsin:c2=qsin`; the same track to itself: `c1=hsin:c2=hsin` (tested here 2026-10-24) [SoX manual, https://manpages.ubuntu.com/manpages/noble/man1/sox.1.html, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade, checked 2026-10-24]
2325. **Square-root curve is the other -3 dB fade** — `squ` also sits at 0.707 at the midpoint but holds more at the end (0.866 / 0.707 / 0.500 at 25/50/75 %, vs qsin 0.924 / 0.707 / 0.383). **How:** measured with `afade=t=out:st=0:d=1:curve=squ` on a constant signal (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade, checked 2026-10-24]
2326. **Curves that hold the old track up** — measured fade-out gain at the midpoint: log -0.5 dB, quatr -1.5 dB, cbr -2.0 dB, ipar -2.5 dB; they drop late. **How:** pick one for c1 when the outgoing music must stay present until the end of the crossfade, e.g. `c1=log:c2=qsin` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade, checked 2026-10-24]
2327. **Curves that get the old track out fast** — measured midpoint gain: exp -50 dB, quat -24 dB, cub -18 dB, qua and hsin2 -12 dB; the docs' acrossfade examples use exp. **How:** `c1=cub` clears space quickly for a new track's downbeat (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade, checked 2026-10-24]
2328. **S-shaped crossfades** — desi (double-exponential sigmoid) and losi (logistic sigmoid) cross at 0.5 like linear but are flatter at both ends: 0.938/0.500/0.062 and 0.882/0.500/0.118 at 25/50/75 %, against tri 0.75/0.5/0.25. **How:** `acrossfade=d=2:c1=desi:c2=desi` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade, checked 2026-10-24]
2329. **Dip the music instead of fading it out** — afade's `silence` sets the final gain of a fade-out (default 0), `unity` the starting gain. **How:** `afade=t=out:st=5:d=1:silence=0.3` held the music at 0.30 of its level after 6 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade, checked 2026-10-24]
2330. **Sample-counted fades** — afade takes `start_sample`/`nb_samples` as well as times. **How:** `afade=t=in:ss=0:ns=4410` fades in over exactly 100 ms at 44.1 kHz (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade, checked 2026-10-24]
2331. **Bass swap during a transition** — Wikipedia's beatmatching notes a smoother transition when the low frequencies are taken out of one song while the other's are kept. **How:** high-pass the outgoing tail, then crossfade: `[0]atrim=0:26,asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS[a1];[0]atrim=26:30,asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS,highpass=f=200[a2];[a1][a2]concat=n=2:v=0:a=1[A];[A][1]acrossfade=d=4:c1=qsin:c2=qsin` (ran; listen-check by the owner) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Beatmatching", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatmatching, checked 2026-10-24]
2332. **Match tempos before the overlap** — beatmatching adjusts the incoming track's tempo so beats (and usually bars) coincide; mismatched BPMs make one song sound too fast or slow. **How:** `atempo=<target BPM>/<source BPM>`: a 126 BPM click with `atempo=120/126` then read the same as the 120 BPM click in librosa (117.45, its grid value) (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Beatmatching", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatmatching, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#atempo, checked 2026-10-24]
2333. **pydub joins crossfade 100 ms by default** — `append()` uses a 100 ms crossfade to avoid pops; `crossfade=0` or `a + b` joins with none. **How:** 30 s + 20 s gave 49900 ms with `append`, 45000 ms with `crossfade=5000`, 50000 ms with `+` (tested here 2026-10-24) [pydub API docs, https://github.com/jiaaro/pydub/blob/master/API.markdown, checked 2026-10-24]
2334. **Crossfades in HyperFrames are two tracks** — a crossfade is two clips on different tracks that overlap, each with a fade (`data-fade-in`, `data-fade-out` in seconds). **How:** put the second `<audio>` on another `data-track-index` starting before the first ends, fade-out on the first and fade-in on the second [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/mixing.md]
2335. **Join AI song parts at instrumental gaps** — the website-promo pipeline joins separately generated song parts at instrumental gaps with 1.5 s acrossfades. **How:** `acrossfade=d=1.5` at a gap found in the parts' vocal timing [repo: library/templates/website-promo/README.md] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Long AI songs"]

## Keys for transitions (harmonic mixing)
2336. **Mix in key** — harmonic mixing matches keys to avoid dissonance: same key, relative keys, or keys in a dominant/subdominant relationship; the Camelot wheel is based on the circle of fifths. **How:** before a long musical crossfade, compare the two tracks' keys [Wikipedia "Harmonic mixing", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_mixing, checked 2026-10-24]
2337. **Camelot codes: number and letter** — each key gets a number (its place on a 12-step wheel) and a letter: A = minor, B = major. **How:** note each track's code next to its BPM in the edit notes [Mixed In Key, https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/, checked 2026-10-24]
2338. **Safe Camelot moves** — from 8A try 8A, 7A, 9A or 8B: same code, one step either way with the same letter, or the same number with the other letter. **How:** shortlist next tracks that way, then audition the actual overlap; key compatibility does not guarantee the blend [Mixed In Key, https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/, checked 2026-10-24]
2339. **One step clockwise = up a fifth** — moving 8B to 9B (C major to G major) shifts the key centre up a perfect fifth, the strongest interval connection; anticlockwise (9B to 8B) is a fourth, almost as strong. **How:** prefer these for a lift that still sounds connected [DJ.Studio, https://dj.studio/blog/compatible-keys, checked 2026-10-24]
2340. **Relative major/minor swap changes mood, not notes** — 8B to 8A (C major to A minor) uses the same notes; relative keys share a key signature and the minor tonic is three semitones below the major's. **How:** use the swap to go happy to sad (or back) at a story turn [DJ.Studio, https://dj.studio/blog/compatible-keys, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia "Relative key", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_key, checked 2026-10-24]
2341. **Energy boost (+2) and Jaws (-5) moves** — two steps up on the same side lifts energy (a whole-tone rise, sometimes a little clashy); the Jaws mix, 5 steps anticlockwise (one semitone up, e.g. 1A to 8A), is dissonant and only for deliberate surprise. **How:** use +2 for a final-chorus lift; test any Jaws move by ear first [DJ.Studio, https://dj.studio/blog/compatible-keys, checked 2026-10-24] [Mixed In Key, https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-use-the-camelot-wheel/, checked 2026-10-24]
2342. **Shift a track into a compatible key** — DJ software can pitch-shift a track to a key compatible with the playing one. **How:** shift by the semitone difference with `rubberband=pitch=2^(n/12)` (see pitch skills below) [Wikipedia "Harmonic mixing", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_mixing, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia "Audio time stretching and pitch scaling", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_time_stretching_and_pitch_scaling, checked 2026-10-24]

## Time-stretch and retime without pitch change
2343. **Slow a track down, pitch kept** — atempo changes tempo in the 0.5-100 range. **How:** `ffmpeg -i song.wav -af atempo=0.8 slow.wav` turned 30.0 s into 37.49 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#atempo, checked 2026-10-24]

2351. **Percussive transient detector** — `detector` can be compound (default), percussive or soft. **How:** `rubberband=tempo=1.05:detector=percussive` on a 16 s click gave 15.24 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#rubberband, checked 2026-10-24]

2408. **Cleaner HPSS with a margin** — a margin requires one filter to beat the other by that factor; noise and vocals then go to neither part (residual); the tutorial finds margins of 4 or more give strictly harmonic/percussive parts. **How:** `librosa.effects.hpss(y,margin=4)` left a residual of rms 0.060 (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa tutorial "Harmonic-percussive source separation", https://librosa.org/doc/latest/auto_tutorials/03-advanced/plot_hprss.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2409. **Only the percussive part** — `librosa.effects.percussive(y, margin=)` returns just the drums-like component (`harmonic` the rest). **How:** `librosa.effects.percussive(y,margin=3)` (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.effects.harmonic.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2410. **REPET-SIM style repeating-background filter** — nn_filter replaces each spectrogram column by aggregating its nearest neighbours; `aggregate=np.median` gives REPET-SIM de-noising (Rafii & Pardo, music/voice separation). **How:** `S,ph=librosa.magphase(librosa.stft(y)); Sf=np.minimum(S,librosa.decompose.nn_filter(S,aggregate=np.median,metric='cosine',width=int(librosa.time_to_frames(2,sr=sr))))` ran (tested here 2026-10-24) [librosa 1.0 docs, https://librosa.org/doc/latest/api/generated/librosa.decompose.nn_filter.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2411. **Classic centre cancel ("invert and mix")** — subtracting one channel from the other removes centre-panned audio, often the lead vocal, giving dual mono. **How:** `ffmpeg -i song.wav -af "pan=stereo|c0=c0-c1|c1=c1-c0" nocentre.wav`; on our wide AI song it removed only 1.8 dB of level, so check the result (tested here 2026-10-24) [Audacity Manual "Vocal Reduction and Isolation", https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/vocal_reduction_and_isolation.html, checked 2026-10-24]

2413. **Check stereo correlation before trying centre tricks** — Audacity's Analyze reports channel correlation and how likely centre removal is to work; ffmpeg's aphasemeter exports mean phase (-1 out of phase to 1 in phase). **How:** `aphasemeter=video=0,ametadata=mode=print:key=lavfi.aphasemeter.phase:file=phase.txt` averaged 0.334 on our AI song, where centre cancel removed only 1.8 dB (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aphasemeter, checked 2026-10-24] [Audacity Manual "Vocal Reduction and Isolation", https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/vocal_reduction_and_isolation.html, checked 2026-10-24]

## Hit points and scoring to picture
2419. **3-4 frames is close enough** — when syncing music to picture a leeway of 3-4 frames early or late is generally sufficiently accurate. **How:** at 30 fps that is about 100-133 ms; don't over-stretch music to hit closer than that [Wikipedia "Film score", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score, checked 2026-10-24]
2420. **Written click: seconds per bar** — W = (60/bpm) x beats-per-bar, rounded to 1/3 s in written clicks. **How:** 88 BPM in 4/4: 60/88 x 4 = 2.727 s, written as 4 beats in 2 2/3 s (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Film score", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score, checked 2026-10-24]
2421. **Which beat a sync point falls on** — B = bpm x sp / 60 + 1 (sp = sync point in seconds). **How:** at 88 BPM a hit at 33.7 s falls on beat 50.43, i.e. just after beat 50 (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Film score", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score, checked 2026-10-24]
2422. **Pick the tempo that lands a hit on a beat** — rearranging the same formula: bpm = (B - 1) x 60 / sp. **How:** to put a reveal at 12.0 s on beat 17 (bar 5's downbeat in 4/4) use 80 BPM (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Film score", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score, checked 2026-10-24]
2423. **Frames per beat can be fractional** — a beat lasts 60/bpm s (the written-click formula with one beat), so frames per beat = fps x 60 / BPM: 24 fps gives whole numbers at 72/90/120 BPM (20/16/12), 25 fps does not (20.833/16.667/12.5), 30 fps gives 25/20/15. **How:** at 25 fps place hits from the beat time (`round(t*fps)`) instead of adding a rounded frame count (tested here 2026-10-24) [Wikipedia "Film score", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score, checked 2026-10-24]
2424. **Fit one tempo to several hit points** — Frans Absil's film-cue tempo tool takes event hit points and finds the best fixed tempo and beat fit (with the matching error), within a tempo range, for 24/25/30 fps or 50 ms timing. **How:** enter your cue's hit times at fransabsil.nl, then generate or choose music at that BPM [Frans Absil, https://fransabsil.nl/htm/eventhit.htm, checked 2026-10-24]
2425. **Clicks need a tempo map for expressive cues** — a click track without a tempo map does not allow natural tempo shifts (fermata, accelerando, ritardando). **How:** for an emotional cue, let it breathe (PLP or dynamic tempo, skills above) rather than forcing a grid [Wikipedia "Click track", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Click_track, checked 2026-10-24]
2426. **Mickey-mousing: use it on purpose** — syncing music to every on-screen action is called Mickey Mousing; it is also used as a pejorative for being too simplistic and is associated with melodrama. **How:** it fits cartoon and game-style moments (where the article says it is common); in pieces for adults it can read as too simplistic, so sync only key hits there [Wikipedia "Mickey Mousing", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mousing, checked 2026-10-24]
2427. **Animate to pre-recorded music** — animators time movement to pre-recorded music with an exposure sheet marking beats frame by frame. **How:** export beat frames (`round(beat_time*fps)`) from the beats JSON and key GSAP moves to them [Wikipedia "Mickey Mousing", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mousing, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes-cli/references/beats.md]
2428. **Cutting picture to finished music is legitimate** — some directors edit the film to the music (Baby Driver; Koyaanisqatsi to Philip Glass; E.T.'s finale re-edited to John Williams' cue). **How:** for music-led reels lock the music edit first, then cut picture to it [Wikipedia "Film score", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score, checked 2026-10-24]
2429. **Temp track = guide, not final** — a temp track is existing music used during editing to show the tempo and mood wanted; it is usually replaced before release. **How:** if the owner's rough cut uses a copyrighted song as temp, replace it with CC0/own music of the same tempo before delivery [Wikipedia "Temp track", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temp_track, checked 2026-10-24] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Environment facts"]
2430. **Count desi cycles, not just bars** — Teental has 16 beats in four groups of 4 (sam on beat 1, khali on 9); Keherwa has 8 beats in two groups (sam 1, khali 5); the sam is the most emphasised beat where phrases resolve. **How:** for tabla-led music cut big moments on the sam: every 16 beats in teental, 8 in keherwa [Wikipedia "Teental", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teental, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia "Keharwa", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keherwa, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia "Tala (music)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tala_(music), checked 2026-10-24]
2431. **Phrases can start before the downbeat** — in Indian classical music a composition may be offset so the most accented word falls on the sam, or start with an anacrusis on the last beats of the previous cycle. **How:** when cutting a sung phrase, keep its pickup notes before the cycle's first beat instead of cutting exactly on the sam [Wikipedia "Tala (music)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tala_(music), checked 2026-10-24]

## Placing music in HyperFrames and pydub
2432. **Preview where a cue's hits will land before placing it** — `hyperframes audio <file> --place-at <film s> --skip-file-seconds <s>` analyses a file not yet in the film as if placed there. **How:** `npx hyperframes audio cue.wav --place-at 5 --skip-file-seconds 2 --only onsets --json` returned placement {dataStart 5, mediaStart 2} and onsets in file seconds from 2 s, so film time = 5 + (t - 2) (tested here 2026-10-24) [repo: `npx hyperframes audio --help` v0.8.191]
2433. **Start inside the file with data-media-start** — the repo's riser crests 3.47 s into its file, so `data-media-start` is used to make that moment land on the hit; every `<audio>` also needs `data-duration` equal to the clip's real length. **How:** `<audio src="assets/music.mp3" data-start="0" data-media-start="12.5" data-duration="30" data-track-index="0">` [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
2434. **Overlapping music clips need their own lanes** — overlapping clips need different `data-track-index` values or lint warns `duplicate_audio_track`. **How:** old cue on track 0, incoming cue on track 1 during the crossfade [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
2435. **Volume envelopes on the music clip** — `data-automation` lanes target `volume`, `rate` or an effect parameter; `t` is seconds from the clip's start, and a lane holds its first value back to the clip start, so add an explicit full-level point at t 0. **How:** `data-automation='{"version":1,"lanes":[{"target":"volume","points":[{"t":0,"v":1},{"t":2.5,"v":0.35}]}]}'`; don't also tween volume with GSAP [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/mixing.md]
2436. **Check the render, not the timeline** — the sound guide says to run `npx hyperframes audio <video> --json` on the rendered file: sound present, hits where the picture hits, loudness in range, and to say it was measured, not heard. **How:** run it on every final render [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/sound.md]
2437. **Lower the bed under an overlay in one call** — pydub's `overlay(..., gain_during_overlay=dB)` changes the base audio by that many dB while the overlaid sound plays. **How:** `base.overlay(sting,position=4000,gain_during_overlay=-8)` took that 2 s window from -21.1 to -29.1 dBFS (tested here 2026-10-24) [pydub API docs, https://github.com/jiaaro/pydub/blob/master/API.markdown, checked 2026-10-24]
2438. **Partial fade to a new level** — `fade(to_gain=-6.0, start=ms, duration=ms)` fades from 0 to -6 dB and keeps everything after at -6 dB. **How:** `song.fade(to_gain=-6.0,start=7500,duration=1000)` read -25.0 dBFS after the fade vs -19.0 before (tested here 2026-10-24) [pydub API docs, https://github.com/jiaaro/pydub/blob/master/API.markdown, checked 2026-10-24]
2439. **Fade in/out and export at a bitrate** — `fade_in(ms)` / `fade_out(ms)` then `export(..., format='mp3', bitrate='192k')`. **How:** `song.fade_in(2000).fade_out(3000).export('bed.mp3',format='mp3',bitrate='192k')` (tested here 2026-10-24) [pydub API docs, https://github.com/jiaaro/pydub/blob/master/API.markdown, checked 2026-10-24]
2440. **pydub gain past 0 dBFS clips** — `segment + dB` / `apply_gain` changes level; a track peaking at -0.4 dBFS read max 0.0 dBFS after `+6`, i.e. clipped. **How:** check `seg.max_dBFS` before boosting (tested here 2026-10-24) [pydub API docs, https://github.com/jiaaro/pydub/blob/master/API.markdown, checked 2026-10-24]
2441. **Reverse a phrase into a hit** — areverse plays a clip backwards and buffers the whole clip in memory, so trim first (the docs' example: `atrim=end=5,areverse`). **How:** `ffmpeg -i song.wav -af "atrim=end=2,areverse" rev.wav`; pydub's `segment.reverse()` does the same (both tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#areverse, checked 2026-10-24] [pydub API docs, https://github.com/jiaaro/pydub/blob/master/API.markdown, checked 2026-10-24]
2442. **Music at a quarter weight under a vocal** — the amix docs' example mixes vocals and music with weights "1 0.25", normalize off, duration longest. **How:** `amix=inputs=2:duration=first:dropout_transition=0:weights=1 0.25:normalize=0` (with duration=first it kept the 30 s song length) (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#amix, checked 2026-10-24]
2443. **Resample stems with soxr** — aresample takes resampler options such as `resampler=soxr`. **How:** `ffmpeg -i in48k.wav -af aresample=44100:resampler=soxr out.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24) [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aresample, checked 2026-10-24]
