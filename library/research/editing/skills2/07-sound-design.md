# Sound design skills (#2001-#2236)

Scope: sound effects (whoosh, riser, impact, pop, glitch, UI, foley), layering, ambience beds, sound for transitions, futz (phone/radio/TV), distance and rooms, reverse reverb, tape stop, Doppler, pitch/time tricks, synthesizing SFX with ffmpeg lavfi sources and numpy, and free CC0 SFX sources. Music editing is out of scope (file 08).
Researched 2026-10-24 via agent-reach (Exa) web search, official docs and local tests.

Notes for this file: "tested here" lines were run on ffmpeg 6.1.1 (this container) with synthetic test audio (flite speech, a sine chord, lavfi noise, the bundled repo SFX); the full command list and pass/fail log are in the session scratchpad (`sk2_07/tests.py`, `results.json`: 185/185 commands passed, plus 5 numpy scripts). "Tested" means the command ran and produced a non-empty file of the expected length; where a number is given it was measured (FFT peak, spectral centroid, astats, silencedetect). Nobody listened to the results: a tested command is a working starting point, not a judged sound. Where a recipe comes from a web source, the numbers are the source's; where a line says "tested command only", no claim is made about how it sounds. Docs are the ffmpeg 8.x pages; where local ffmpeg 6.1 differs, the line says so.

2018. **Level SFX by loudness, not only peak** — peak (dBFS) says whether a file can clip, and packs often normalize to about −1 dBFS; LUFS says how loud it sounds, and two files with the same peak can differ a lot; level mixed-source sounds to a common target, then balance categories. **How:** measure with `ffmpeg -i x.wav -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -` (tested here 2026-10-24: a synthesized whoosh read I −32.0 LUFS, peak −17.8 dBFS). [Cybermax Tools, https://cybermaxtools.com/glossary/sfx-loudness/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#ebur128-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2019. **Keep a personal hit library** — build a personal library of 10-15 whooshes, risers and impacts you like and reuse them; most editors work from a curated library and build custom hits only when nothing fits. **How:** keep favourites in `library/sfx/` with names by length and use (see #2016), listed in `library/README.md`. [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-sound-design-video, checked 2026-10-24]
2020. **Name presets by length, not vibe** — save designed FX as "Riser 2bar", "Riser 4bar", "Downlifter", "Sub Drop", "Impact Boom"; mid-session "Riser 4bar dark" beats a fancy name. **How:** name the generated files the same way, e.g. `riser_4bar_72bpm.wav`. [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Placement on picture and transitions

2021. **Peak one frame after the picture** — place an SFX so its peak lands one frame after its picture (light arrives before sound; early feels wrong sooner than late); most files peak after their start. **How:** read the hit time with `npx hyperframes audio <file> --json`, then set `data-start` = picture frame time + 1/30 s − peak offset. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/sound.md]
2022. **Which sound for which picture event** — repo table: click only on a real cursor click, whoosh on a scene cut or a large card moving fast, soft key tick for text arriving letter by letter, pop for a badge/chip/number landing, riser into impact once per film on the payoff. **How:** list every visible event in the storyboard and assign at most one of these. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/sound.md]
2023. **No synthesized dings in HyperFrames films** — the repo sound rules say "No synthesised tones, chimes or dings"; hits sit 3-6 dB over the bed, and a bed with most energy under 150 Hz plays as nothing on a phone. **How:** use library chimes/pings for UI moments; keep synthesized tones (this file's lavfi recipes) as layers or raw material, not as the visible "ding". [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/sound.md]
2024. **Rotate takes of repeated sounds** — the same whoosh on every cut is the most common tell; rotate two or three takes. **How:** make variants with `ffmpeg -i hit.wav -af "asetrate=44100*1.06,aresample=44100" hit_b.wav` (±6 %; tested here 2026-10-24 for 0.94/1.0/1.06) or pick other library files. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/sound.md] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#asetrate, checked 2026-10-24]
2025. **SFX volume ~0.35 under voice and music** — the media-use rule is SFX volume about 0.35, sitting under narration and BGM; a missing effect is skipped, never a render blocker. **How:** start `<audio data-volume="0.35">` for SFX, then adjust by measurement. [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/audio/references/sfx.md]
2026. **Air pass vs rush into a hit** — an "air pass" (air moving past with nothing at the end) can be slid either side of the edit point; a "rush into a hit" puts the rush before the cut and the hit on the first frame after it. **How:** air pass for soft cuts; rush+hit for reveals: end the rush at the cut frame, start the impact clip on the next frame. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/transition-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2027. **Swell into silence** — a build that cuts out at its loudest point; the hole does the work and the next shot starts on clean air and feels louder than it is. **How:** end the riser with a 5 ms fade exactly on the cut (`afade=t=out:st=<len-0.005>:d=0.005`, tested here 2026-10-24) and leave the first frames of the next shot without SFX. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/transition-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2028. **Downward fall reads as an ending** — a tone sliding down and gaining weight reads as an ending, where a rising sound reads as an arrival, so it is the natural close of a sequence. **How:** put a downlifter (#2073) or sub drop (#2074) under the last shot, not the first. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/transition-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2029. **Short swish for small elements** — a light quick brush of air is sized for small elements (a title card); a full rush on a title card sounds like far more than what moves. **How:** use 120-250 ms swishes (#2058) for titles and chips, long whooshes only for whole-frame moves. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/transition-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2030. **Transitions set the pace** — short swishes read as light and quick, a heavy fall reads as final; the chosen transitions tell the audience how fast the piece means to move. **How:** pick one transition family per video's pace and stay with it. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/transition-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2031. **Off-screen arrival with sound alone** — a rush ending on a dull hit implies something arriving from outside the frame; nothing has to be animated. **How:** lay a whoosh + soft impact over a static shot where an off-screen event is implied. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/transition-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2032. **Match the whoosh to the move** — a whoosh/swoosh fits a whip pan or fast-moving scenes better than a riser or slow-motion effect; keep transition SFX lower than BGM and dialogue, and don't overuse them. **How:** whoosh on whip pans and fast slides; risers only before reveals. [FlexClip, https://www.flexclip.com/learn/transition-sound-effects.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2033. **Match energy, loosely match style** — a slow gentle text fade doesn't need an aggressive impact; bright energetic edits want sharper, more digital whooshes, moody cinematic edits deeper, more organic ones. **How:** choose whoosh brightness by the grade and pace, not by the subject. [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-sound-design-video, checked 2026-10-24]
2034. **Duck the bed under each big hit** — sidechain or manually duck the music slightly under each major hit so whooshes/impacts read; keep it subtle: fast attack, 50-150 ms release, 3:1-6:1. **How:** `ffmpeg -i music.wav -i hit.wav -filter_complex "[0]aformat=channel_layouts=stereo[m];[1]aformat=channel_layouts=stereo,asplit[k][h];[m][k]sidechaincompress=threshold=0.05:ratio=4:attack=1:release=100[d];[d][h]amix=inputs=2:normalize=0" out.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-sound-design-video, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sidechaincompress, checked 2026-10-24]
2035. **Music cut-off before a key line** — trailers cut the music to leave a big auditory space before a quote (Cruella trailer at 0:20) and cut scenes to the beat so impacts work like a light switch. **How:** stop the bed (5 ms fade) one beat before the key line; put impacts on beat-aligned cuts. [BOOM Library, https://www.boomlibrary.com/blog/top-tips-for-trailer-sound-design/, checked 2026-10-24]
2036. **Long tails carry into silence** — before a switch to silence, long drawn-out tails carry the viewer forward and keep attention a little longer. **How:** let the last impact's reverb tail (#2120) run 1-3 s into the silent section instead of cutting it. [BOOM Library, https://www.boomlibrary.com/blog/top-tips-for-trailer-sound-design/, checked 2026-10-24]
2037. **Place many cues in one ffmpeg call** — delay each SFX to its time and mix without normalization, padded to the video length. **How:** `ffmpeg -i vid.mp4 -i whoosh.wav -i pop.wav -i hit.wav -filter_complex "[1]adelay=400:all=1[a];[2]adelay=1200:all=1[b];[3]adelay=2000:all=1[c];[a][b][c]amix=inputs=3:normalize=0,apad=whole_dur=3[s]" -map 0:v -map "[s]" -c:v copy -c:a aac -shortest out.mp4` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#adelay, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#amix, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#apad, checked 2026-10-24]
2038. **Verify cue positions with silencedetect** — silence ends mark where each cue begins. **How:** `ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -af "silencedetect=n=-45dB:d=0.1" -f null - 2>&1 | grep silence_` (tested here 2026-10-24: cues placed at 0.4/1.2/2.0 s reported silence_end 0.46, 1.20, 2.03 s — the whoosh file's own lead-in shows as +0.06 s). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#silencedetect, checked 2026-10-24]
2039. **Verify cue positions with librosa onsets** — `librosa.onset.onset_detect(..., units='time')` lists hit times of a mix. **How:** extract audio (`ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -vn out.wav`), then in `/home/user/tools/venv/bin/python`: `librosa.onset.onset_detect(y=y, sr=sr, units='time')` (tested here 2026-10-24: found 0.51, 1.21, 2.07 s for cues at 0.4/1.2/2.0 s). [librosa docs, https://librosa.org/doc/0.11.0/generated/librosa.onset.onset_detect.html, checked 2026-10-24] [librosa 1.0.0 docstring in /home/user/tools/venv: `units : {'frames', 'samples', 'time'}`]
2040. **Find picture cuts to hang whooshes on** — `scdet` writes scene-change times as metadata. **How:** `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "scdet=threshold=10,metadata=print:file=scdet.txt" -f null -`, then read `lavfi.scd.time` lines (tested here 2026-10-24 on a 2-clip join: 1 cut found) and place whooshes per #2021. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#scdet-1, checked 2026-10-24]

## Layering and impacts

2041. **Four layers: sub, body, detail, tail** — sub = low weight you feel (0.5-2 s), body = midrange that says what it is (0.5-2 s), detail = high-frequency specifics (short, inside the body), tail = room/distance (1-4 s); a single library file disappoints because it tries to do all four. **How:** build hero impacts from four files on separate tracks/lanes. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/how-to-layer-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2042. **Balance order for a layer stack** — set the body first as anchor, bring the sub in under it, add detail lower so it flavours, let the tail run past the others, roll off lows of detail/body if the sub carries the bottom; mute layers one at a time to hear each one's job. **How:** in HyperFrames use separate `data-track-index` lanes per layer and adjust `data-volume`. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/how-to-layer-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2043. **Align transients, let detail arrive a hair late** — align layer transients to the same frame so they read as one event; the tail can extend and a detail layer can arrive slightly late for texture. **How:** same `data-start` for all layers; detail +5-20 ms if wanted. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/how-to-layer-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2044. **Impact timing map** — transient 0-10 ms (mostly high frequencies), body 10-100 ms (mid frequencies, material character), sub below 80 Hz concurrent with the body, tail 100 ms+; a body delayed 20-30 ms feels "explosive". **How:** for an explosive variant, `adelay=25:all=1` the body layer before mixing. [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24]
2045. **Filter each impact layer to its band** — high-pass the transient layer at 2 kHz (maybe 4 kHz), low-pass the sub at 60-80 Hz (rumble above 120 Hz fights the body), band-pass the body roughly 150 Hz-2 kHz, and notch body energy at 200 Hz 6-12 dB if it masks the sub. **How:** body: `ffmpeg -i hit.wav -af "highpass=f=150,lowpass=f=2000,equalizer=f=200:t=q:w=4:g=-9" body.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24]
2046. **Split one hit into bands with acrossover** — `acrossover` splits audio into bands that sum back flat. **How:** `ffmpeg -i hit.wav -filter_complex "acrossover=split=150 2500:order=4th[l][m][h]" -map "[l]" low.wav -map "[m]" mid.wav -map "[h]" high.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24); per-band output gain is option `gain` in local ffmpeg 6.1 (`gain=1.5 1 0.5`, tested), named `gains` in the current docs. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossover, checked 2026-10-24]
2047. **Synthesized click transient** — a sine with instant attack and 2 ms decay at 4 kHz makes a clean click to shape a transient layer. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc='sin(2*PI*4000*t)*exp(-t/0.002)':d=0.02:s=44100" click4k.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aevalsrc, checked 2026-10-24]
2048. **Stagger several transients** — soft click at 0, harder crack 2-3 ms later, a wide panned transient pair ~5 ms later for complexity without losing definition. **How:** `[crack]adelay=2.5:all=1`, `[pair]adelay=5|6` then mix with `amix=normalize=0` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#adelay, checked 2026-10-24]
2049. **Sub layer: sine, phase-aligned, checked in mono** — use a sine or triangle for sub layers (saw/square harmonics invade the body); at 50 Hz a 5 ms offset is a 90° shift, so align zero-crossings and check in mono; tune the sub to the key, or below 30 Hz where pitch perception is minimal. **How:** mono check `pan=mono|c0=0.5*c0+0.5*c1` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#pan-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2050. **Sub pitch envelope: an octave in 50 ms** — a quick one-octave drop over 50 ms then sustain emphasizes the impact; an exponential drop sounds more natural than linear; starting high and dropping adds weight, starting low and rising adds urgency. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc='0.9*sin(2*PI*(45*t+90*0.05/log(2)*(1-pow(2,-t/0.05))))*exp(-t*2.5)':d=1.5:s=44100" -af lowpass=f=80 sub.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; measured 59→45 Hz). [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg-utils docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-utils.html#Expression-Evaluation, checked 2026-10-24]
2051. **Reverb per layer, none on the sub** — tight room 0.3-0.8 s on the body, long hall 2-5 s on mids/highs only, no reverb on the sub (it builds up and muddies later hits); reverb pre-delay 20-80 ms keeps the attack, 100 ms+ suggests distance. **How:** convolve only the body/high layers with a synthetic IR (#2120) and leave the sub dry. [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24]

## Whooshes, risers, downlifters and sub drops

2052. **Whoosh = moving filter on noise** — start with white or pink noise and sweep a band-pass or low-pass quickly up and back down (or just up); the filter movement, not the noise, makes the whoosh. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=0.8:c=pink:r=44100:a=0.5" -af "asendcmd=c='0.0-0.8 [expr] bandpass f 300+3500*sin(PI*TI)',bandpass=f=300:w=1.2:t=o,afade=t=in:d=0.4:curve=qsin,afade=t=out:st=0.4:d=0.4:curve=qsin" whoosh.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; measured centroid 2.9→5.5→3.3 kHz). [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-sound-design-video, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sendcmd_002c-asendcmd, checked 2026-10-24]
2053. **asendcmd [expr] sweeps a filter per frame** — inside an `asendcmd` interval, the `[expr]` flag evaluates the argument with `TI` (0→1 across the interval), so any filter with a `frequency` command can be swept. **How:** `asendcmd=c='0.0-0.6 [expr] lowpass f 200+6000*sin(PI*TI)',lowpass=f=200` (tested here 2026-10-24); avoid commas inside the expression (use `exp(log(a)*TI)` instead of `pow(a,TI)`; a `\,` escape failed here). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sendcmd_002c-asendcmd, checked 2026-10-24]
2054. **Neutral cinematic whoosh recipe** — pink noise → HPF 30-50 Hz 24 dB/oct → band-pass sweep 300 Hz→5 kHz, Q 0.8-1.5 → envelope 120 ms attack, 15 ms hold, 420 ms release → light saturation (the source also adds 4 Hz random AM at 10 % and a 0.3-0.8 s room at 5-15 %, not done here). **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=0.6:c=pink:r=44100:a=0.6" -af "highpass=f=40,highpass=f=40,asendcmd=c='0.0-0.555 [expr] bandpass f 300*exp(log(5000/300)*TI)',bandpass=f=300:t=q:w=1.2,volume='if(lt(t,0.12),t/0.12,if(lt(t,0.135),1,max(0,1-(t-0.135)/0.42)))':eval=frame,asoftclip=type=tanh" whoosh.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; centroid rose 1.8→5.8 kHz). [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2055. **Whoosh envelope ranges** — length 300 ms-1.5 s (UI shorter, cinematic longer), attack 50-300 ms, peak hold 0-60 ms, release 150-900 ms; add 1-3 dB slow random level variation (2-8 Hz) so it isn't flat. **How:** set these in the `volume=...:eval=frame` envelope of #2054. [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2056. **Noise colour for whooshes** — white = equal power per Hz, bright/hissy (sci-fi, fast); pink = ~1/f, fuller, more wind-like; brown = low-heavy, can get boomy, high-pass it. **How:** `anoisesrc` colours `white|pink|brown|blue|violet|velvet` (tested here 2026-10-24 for white/pink/brown/velvet). [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#anoisesrc, checked 2026-10-24]
2057. **Filter ranges for whoosh sweeps** — band-pass centre ~200 Hz→6 kHz for broad cinematic whooshes; Q 0.5-2 for natural wind, 2-8 for stylized "laser-swish"; high shelf +2 to +8 dB above 4-8 kHz on approach, down on exit. **How:** in #2052 set `t=q:w=1` (natural) or `w=5` (stylized). [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#bandpass, checked 2026-10-24]
2058. **UI swipe whoosh for phones** — 120-250 ms swish: white noise → steep band-pass 1-6 kHz → fast envelope; keep energy in 1.5-5 kHz where small speakers reproduce, no sub. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=0.2:c=white:r=44100:a=0.6" -af "highpass=f=1000,highpass=f=1000,lowpass=f=6000,lowpass=f=6000,volume='sin(PI*t/0.2)':eval=frame" swipe.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2059. **High-pass whooshes at 30-50 Hz** — a whoosh rarely needs energy below 40-60 Hz unless it is a deliberate "sub swoop"; start with a 24 dB/oct HPF at 30-50 Hz; uncontrolled sub steals headroom and triggers limiters. **How:** `highpass=f=40,highpass=f=40` (two 2-pole stages; each is 12 dB/oct per the docs' 6 dB per pole) (tested here 2026-10-24). [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#highpass, checked 2026-10-24]
2060. **Doppler matters on tones, not on noise** — real pass-by Doppler is noticeable on tonal parts (engines, whistles), not purely broadband noise; for noise whooshes the motion cues are level envelope, spectral tilt and inter-channel cues. **How:** add a tonal layer if you want audible Doppler (#2188); otherwise sweep filters and pan. [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2061. **Bounded randomness for whoosh variants** — randomize sweep time ±15 %, end frequency within 4-7 kHz, modulation depth 8-14 % to get variants that still match. **How:** loop #2054 with `d` and the end frequency drawn per variant in a shell/Python loop. [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2062. **Big trailer whoosh in three layers** — 1.2 s: air (pink noise band-pass 250 Hz→4.5 kHz, mild saturation), body (noise through a "large metal tube" IR, HPF 60 Hz, LPF 6-8 kHz), edge (white noise burst high-passed at 2 kHz, 150-250 ms, near the peak). **How:** build the air layer with #2054, the body with `afir` + an IR (#2120), the edge with a short `anoisesrc` + `highpass=f=2000`. [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2063. **Two band-passes in parallel for a swish** — a short swish: noise into two parallel band-pass filters with different Q, both driven by the amplitude envelope with different ranges, plus a low-pass after. **How:** `... asplit[a][b];[a]asendcmd=c='0.0-0.4 [expr] bandpass f 800+3000*sin(PI*TI)',bandpass=f=800:t=q:w=0.8[x];[b]asendcmd=c='0.0-0.4 [expr] bandpass f 1500+5000*sin(PI*TI)',bandpass=f=1500:t=q:w=4[y];[x][y]amix=inputs=2:normalize=0,lowpass=f=9000` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tsugi, https://tsugi-studio.com/blog/2024/09/17/design-sounds-from-noise-source-1/, checked 2026-10-24]
2064. **Flanger for a "magic" whoosh** — a flanger emphasizes movement in stylized whooshes (try different LFO rates). **How:** `ffmpeg -i whoosh.wav -af "flanger=delay=3:depth=6:speed=2:regen=40" magic.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tsugi, https://tsugi-studio.com/blog/2024/09/17/design-sounds-from-noise-source-1/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#flanger, checked 2026-10-24]
2065. **Whoosh sources beyond noise** — base whooshes on ambience recordings (character), cymbals (bright swells that exaggerate the peak), synthesized noise, and reversed cymbals; layer several and sync their peaks. **How:** reverse a CC0 cymbal with `areverse` and stack it under a noise whoosh with matched peak. [Krotos Sound Magazine, https://sound.krotosaudio.com/whoosh-sound-effects/, checked 2026-10-24]
2066. **Layered volume automation shapes a whoosh** — sculpt fades at start and end; layering several volume automations gives precise control of rise and tail; subtle pitch rises raise tension, drops release it. **How:** multiply two `volume=...:eval=frame` envelopes in series. [Krotos Sound Magazine, https://sound.krotosaudio.com/whoosh-sound-effects/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#volume, checked 2026-10-24]
2067. **Four-layer whoosh with Doppler moves** — automate volume, panning and pitch to emulate Doppler, layer a sub oscillator, a reversed crash cymbal and a reversed snare. **How:** pitch/pan moves per #2188, sub from #2050, reversed hits with `areverse`. [Thales Matos, https://thalesmatos.com/blog/whoosh-sound-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2068. **Noise riser: high-pass sweep 200 Hz→8 kHz** — white noise, highpass swept from ~200 Hz to 8 kHz, long amp attack; match length to the music: 2 bars at 128 BPM ≈ 3.75 s, 4 bars 7.5 s. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=3.75:c=white:r=44100:a=0.5" -af "asendcmd=c='0.0-3.75 [expr] highpass f 200*exp(log(40)*TI)',highpass=f=200,volume='pow(t/3.75,2)':eval=frame,highpass=f=180,afade=t=out:st=3.745:d=0.005" riser.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]
2069. **Tonal riser: saw climbing an octave** — slide a saw under the noise with a pitch envelope climbing one octave over the same duration; a tonal riser pulls harder than plain noise. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc='0.3*(2*mod(110*3.75/log(2)*(pow(2,t/3.75)-1),1)-1)*pow(t/3.75,2)':d=3.75:s=44100" -af highpass=f=180 saw_riser.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; 110→220 Hz exponential). [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]
2070. **Exponential pitch curves sound less stock** — most of the riser's pitch movement belongs in the final bar (lazy start, steep finish); for downlifters fall fast first then a long lazy tail; symmetric curves sound like stock sounds. **How:** use `exp()`/`pow()` pitch laws in `aevalsrc` (as in #2069) instead of linear chirps. [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]
2071. **Riser gets wetter as it climbs** — automate the reverb mix upward during the riser; a sound getting wetter signals "something is coming" even quietly. **How:** mix dry riser with an `afir` wet copy whose `volume` rises with `t` (`volume='t/3.75':eval=frame` on the wet branch). [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]
2072. **High-pass risers at 150-200 Hz** — riser low end argues with the bassline for no benefit; high-pass around 150-200 Hz, give a light sidechain to the kick, and keep them quieter than instinct says (if the listener notices the riser as its own sound, it is too loud). **How:** end every riser chain with `highpass=f=180`. [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]
2073. **Downlifter = riser reversed** — white noise, filter sweeping down, pitch sinking, reverb drying up; keep downlifters 6-10 dB below risers. **How:** `ffmpeg -i riser.wav -af "areverse,volume=-8dB" downlifter.wav` (areverse tested here 2026-10-24). [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#areverse, checked 2026-10-24]
2074. **Sub drop recipe** — one sine, no filter, pitch diving 1-2 octaves over 1-2 s, fired on the downbeat, starting around G1-C2; audition in mono and on small speakers and stop the fall around 35-40 Hz (below 30 Hz nobody hears it). **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc='0.8*sin(2*PI*65.4*1.5/log(65.4/37)*(1-exp(-t*log(65.4/37)/1.5)))*min(1,(1.5-t)/0.3)':d=1.5:s=44100" -af "afade=t=in:d=0.005" subdrop.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; measured 63→39 Hz). [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]
2075. **Impact boom = oversized kick** — low boom: a sine with a quick pitch envelope dropping an octave in 100-200 ms; add a white-noise burst with fast decay and generous reverb; a third textured layer is easier to take from a recording. **How:** boom `aevalsrc='0.9*sin(2*PI*(55*t+55*0.15/log(2)*(1-pow(2,-t/0.15))))*exp(-t*3)':d=1.2` (measured 90→55 Hz), splash `anoisesrc=d=1:c=white:a=0.7,volume='exp(-t*12)':eval=frame` into `afir` with a synthetic IR, then `amix=weights='1 0.5':normalize=0,alimiter=limit=0.89:level=0` (all tested here 2026-10-24). [Monosounds, https://monosounds.studio/serum-2-transition-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]
2076. **Riser over 1-4 s into a reveal** — an oscillator sweeping up in pitch over 1-4 s, often with rising volume and some noise, builds anticipation before a reveal or drop. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc='0.5*sin(2*PI*(200*t+300*t*t))*pow(t/3,2)':d=3:s=44100" -af "afade=t=out:st=2.995:d=0.005" riser.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; measured 390→1860 Hz). [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-sound-design-video, checked 2026-10-24]
2077. **Riser into the drop, not the bundled file blindly** — the bundled `riser.mp3` is 10.03 s and its manifest says trigger at climax − 10.03 s, but CLAUDE.md measured its crest at 3.47 s and silence after 4.5 s. **How:** trust the measurement: `data-media-start` so 3.47 s lands on the hit. [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/audio/assets/sfx/manifest.json] [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
2078. **Trailer sub boom = hit through a low-pass** — sub booms contain only sub frequencies (60 Hz down to 20 Hz and below, felt more than heard); make one by putting a huge hit through a low-pass; use sparingly (act one, stop-downs, the end). **How:** `ffmpeg -i hit.wav -af "lowpass=f=60,lowpass=f=60,volume=6dB" subboom.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Richard Pryn, https://richardpryn.com/trailer-hits/, checked 2026-10-24]
2079. **Distant hit: taiko + 500 Hz cut + 8 s plate** — a distant hit keeps more highs than a sub boom; one recipe: taiko roll, high-cut everything above 500 Hz, into an 8-second plate reverb; use it to introduce a new section, not the biggest change. **How:** `ffmpeg -i hit.wav -i ir8s.wav -filter_complex "[0]lowpass=f=500,lowpass=f=500,apad=pad_dur=8[d];[d][1]afir" distant.wav` with an 8 s synthetic IR (#2120) (tested here 2026-10-24). [Richard Pryn, https://richardpryn.com/trailer-hits/, checked 2026-10-24]
2080. **Swish hit = reverse tail into the hit** — a swish hit is a large hit with a rise before it; the "swish" just has to be a reverse tail into the attack (a reversed drum or synth works). **How:** `ffmpeg -i hit.wav -af "areverse,afade=t=in:d=0.5" pre.wav` then `ffmpeg -i pre.wav -i hit.wav -filter_complex "[0][1]concat=n=2:v=0:a=1" swishhit.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Richard Pryn, https://richardpryn.com/trailer-hits/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#concat, checked 2026-10-24]
2081. **Pre-impact element 100-500 ms** — a reversed part of the impact, a rising sweep or a building noise before the hit creates anticipation; 100-500 ms usually works. **How:** trim the reversed pre-hit of #2080 to 0.1-0.5 s with `atrim`. [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24]
2082. **Impact variations at five intensities** — make full, medium (less sub/body, shorter tail), light (transient-focused), sub-only and transient-only versions so not every moment gets maximum force. **How:** export layer subsets from the same stack (#2041) as separate files. [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24]
2083. **Parallel compression on the impact stack** — copy the mixed impact through heavy compression (10:1, fast attack/release), blend under the original; limit the final at about −1 dB. **How:** `ffmpeg -i impact.wav -filter_complex "asplit[a][b];[b]acompressor=threshold=-30dB:ratio=10:attack=1:release=50:makeup=6[c];[a][c]amix=inputs=2:weights='1 0.35':normalize=0,alimiter=limit=0.89:level=0" out.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#alimiter, checked 2026-10-24]
2084. **Don't fast-compress transient layers** — fast-attack compression clamps the initial peak and reduces impact; use slow attack 30-100 ms or parallel compression. **How:** `acompressor=attack=50` on transient layers. [Tonalux, https://tonalux.org/blog/layering-impact-sounds, checked 2026-10-24]
2085. **Think in four slots: frequency, sustain, tail, transient** — trailer designer advice: think in those four layers and don't stack too many in the same slot or the sound gets muddy; max out the layers, clean unneeded frequencies and make space between elements with sidechain ducking. **How:** one file per slot, EQ each, duck the bed under the stack (#2034). [BOOM Library, https://www.boomlibrary.com/blog/top-tips-for-trailer-sound-design/, checked 2026-10-24]
2086. **Build impacts bottom to top** — start with a kick or taiko, cut its highs, add metal or orchestral percussion for highs, EQ every layer individually; the Ableton example uses distorted kick, high percussion, processed cymbal, sub and an atmosphere. **How:** `lowpass=f=200` the kick layer, `highpass=f=2000` the metal layer, mix. [Ableton, https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/learn-how-to-make-high-impact-sounds-for-movies-and-trailers/, checked 2026-10-24]
2087. **Distort, then EQ and compress** — distortion is effective on cinematic sounds but adds many overtones, so EQ and compress after it; compress elements and limit the final sound. **How:** `asoftclip=type=tanh,equalizer=...,acompressor,alimiter` in that order. [Ableton, https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/learn-how-to-make-high-impact-sounds-for-movies-and-trailers/, checked 2026-10-24]
2088. **Braaam starting point** — the braaam is a low-register brass sound (popular since the Inception trailers); record a long loud low note around C1-C2 and stack octaves (C0, C1, C2). **How:** needs a brass sample library (missing; see Missing tools); with a CC0 brass note, stack octaves with `asetrate` copies at 0.5x and 2x. [Ableton, https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/learn-how-to-make-high-impact-sounds-for-movies-and-trailers/, checked 2026-10-24] [Professional Composers, https://professionalcomposers.com/sound-design-how-to-make-trailer-braaam-fx/, checked 2026-10-24]
2089. **Bass drop as transition** — the trailer "bass drop/bass bend" (from dubstep and drum'n'bass) transitions from something big to weighty dialogue, like an old record scratch. **How:** sub drop (#2074) under the last frame of the action, dialogue after. [Vulture, https://www.vulture.com/2017/03/how-a-handful-of-sound-effects-took-over-movie-trailers.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2090. **Phase-check layered impacts** — when stacking two or three impact samples, align the transients so they trigger together and don't cancel each other's frequencies. **How:** zoom the waveform (`showwavespic`, #2234) and nudge with `adelay` in ms. [Native Instruments, https://blog.native-instruments.com/5-quick-tips-for-epic-movie-trailer-impacts/, checked 2026-10-24]
2091. **Mids first, then lows, then highs** — start with a mid-range sound as the primary, stack a low sound for weight, sprinkle a high-frequency sound for clarity. **How:** pick the body file first, then add sub and detail. [TBird Sound, https://www.tbirdsound.com/post/sfx-with-impact, checked 2026-10-24]
2092. **Fix clashing layers by cutting the other layer** — solo each layer to find its dominant range; if two layers both peak at the same frequency, cut that frequency on the other by 2-3 dB. **How:** `equalizer=f=400:t=q:w=2:g=-3` on the layer that should yield. [Prosonic Studios, https://www.prosonic-studios.com/songcraft/articles/sound-design-troubleshooting-a-symptom-to-cause-guide-for-synthesis-layering-envelopes-modulation-and-texture, checked 2026-10-24]
2093. **Steep crossover between layers** — split layers by band (example kick: sub low-passed ~100 Hz, body 100-400 Hz, click high-passed ~2 kHz) and cut steeply: 24 dB/oct makes a real handoff, 6 dB/oct leaves both in the crossover region. **How:** `acrossover=split=100 400 2000:order=4th` (24 dB/oct per the docs). [Veena (search text), https://www.veena.studio/blog/layering-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossover, checked 2026-10-24]
2094. **Compare with references, rest your ears** — imitate the shape of reference sounds you like, and re-listen to yesterday's work after a long break because you get used to wrong sounds. **How:** keep a reference clip next to the edit and A/B at matched loudness (#2018). [BOOM Library, https://www.boomlibrary.com/blog/top-tips-for-trailer-sound-design/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Pops, clicks and UI sounds

2095. **Mouth pop: 380 Hz sine sliding up 12 semitones in 70 ms** — Notify Sound's "Mouth Pop" is a 380 Hz sine with a +12 semitone slide over 0.07 s; a negative slide gives a falling plop (Cork Pop body at 420 Hz); most of a pop happens in the first few hundredths of a second. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc='0.8*sin(2*PI*380*0.07/log(2)*(pow(2,t/0.07)-1))*exp(-t*50)':d=0.09:s=44100" -af "afade=t=in:d=0.001,afade=t=out:st=0.085:d=0.005" pop.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; measured 409→855 Hz). [Notify Sound, https://notifysound.com/sound-effect/pop/, checked 2026-10-24]
2096. **Sharp vs round pop = attack time** — Balloon Pop: white noise, attack 0.0005 s (a wide crack); Pop Up: triangle wave, attack 0.003 s (rounded front edge). **How:** set the `afade=t=in:d=` to 0.0005 for sharp, 0.003 for round. [Notify Sound, https://notifysound.com/sound-effect/pop/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2097. **Cork pop needs its airy tail** — the tail is the escaping pressure; without it the pop reads as a click. **How:** don't trim the tail of cork/bubble pops; trim it on UI pops. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/pop-sound-effect, checked 2026-10-24]
2098. **Pop = fast attack, short tail** — a pop is short and bright with a fast attack and little sustain; the transient says "something happened", then the tail should get out of the way, or it stops acting as punctuation. **How:** `afade=t=out` the last 20-30 ms of pops longer than 0.3 s. [Add.app, https://add.app/sound-effects/pop-sound-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2099. **Sticker and emoji pops** — Pop Up and Mouth Pop mark a word, sticker or emoji landing in a motion-graphics video; Bubble Up marks a panel opening, Bubble Down closing. **How:** pop on chip/badge landings (see #2022); pair rising pops with opens, falling with closes. [Notify Sound, https://notifysound.com/sound-effect/pop/, checked 2026-10-24]
2100. **When not to use UI sound** — sound is usually unsuitable for frequently performed actions, users who asked for no interruptions, and UIs needing privacy; silence gives other elements focus. **How:** in app-demo videos, sound only the key actions, not every tap. [Material Design 2, https://m2.material.io/design/sound/applying-sound-to-ui.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2101. **Sound only as a supplement** — users may be in noisy places, have sound off, hearing loss, or no speakers, so sound must supplement visuals, never carry the only signal. **How:** every SFX'd event in a video also has a visual change (pairs with the sound-off test). [Microsoft Learn, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/uxguide/vis-sound, checked 2026-10-24]
2102. **Urgency sets duration, volume, character** — a sound's duration, volume and character should follow the event's urgency; avoid sounds that annoy after repetition; keep them distinct and succinct. **How:** short soft cues for info, longer brighter cues only for alerts in a demo. [Shopify Polaris (search text), https://polaris-react.shopify.com/design/sounds, checked 2026-10-24]
2103. **Custom notification sound for identity** — a default notification sound says nothing about where it comes from; a custom one identifies the app. **How:** for a brand promo (e.g. FireCraft), reuse one signature ping across videos. [Apple WWDC17 "Designing Sound", https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2017/803/, checked 2026-10-24]
2104. **Camera shutter from scissors** — Pixel's shutter sound is based on a recording of scissors opening and closing, not a camera. **How:** record or find a CC0 scissors snip for a phone-camera moment. [Google blog, https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/google-pixel-sound-design/, checked 2026-10-24]
2105. **Match the shutter to the camera** — phone capture: short polite click; DSLR: firmer click with mirror slap; film camera: gears and a winding stop; close-ups punchy and direct, distant ones softer. **How:** pick the shutter sample by the camera in shot. [Media.io, https://www.media.io/audio-effects/camera-sound-effects.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2106. **Mechanical motion-graphics layer template** — tracks for clicks/stops, motors/servos, gears/ticks, air/whooshes, impacts/weight, sweeteners and a shared reverb return keep revisions easy. **How:** map them to fixed `data-track-index` lanes in HyperFrames. [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/mechanical-sounds-design-for-motion-graphics, checked 2026-10-24]
2107. **Noise click (tested command only)** — a 30 ms decaying noise burst. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc='(random(0)*2-1)*exp(-t*800)':d=0.03:s=44100" click.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; no claim about how it sounds). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aevalsrc, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg-utils docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-utils.html#Expression-Evaluation, checked 2026-10-24]
2108. **Beep every second from `sine`** — `sine` with `beep_factor` adds a periodic beep every second at b times the carrier (doc example `sine=220:4:d=5`). **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "sine=f=600:b=2:d=4:r=44100" beeps.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24); note the repo rule against synthesized dings in films (#2023). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sine, checked 2026-10-24]
2109. **Robot announcer placeholder with flite** — ffmpeg's `flite` source speaks text (voices include `kal` and `slt`; output 16 kHz here). **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "flite=text='Season two starts now':voice=kal" -af aresample=44100 vo_temp.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24); only for timing drafts — real voice per CLAUDE.md voice rules. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#flite, checked 2026-10-24]

## Ambience beds and noise textures

2110. **Beds vs specifics** — beds are groups of layered sounds that set the base (room tone, wind, crickets, traffic washes) and can carry the scene's emotion; specifics are distinct to the location (vehicles); establish backgrounds at the top of every scene and never let them overpower intimate moments. **How:** one bed lane for the whole scene + specifics placed on visible/plausible events. [Pro Sound Effects, https://blog.prosoundeffects.com/how-to-sound-design-immersive-backgrounds-video, checked 2026-10-24]
2111. **Two to four ambience layers per scene** — build at least two to four layers, match one quiet layer to the original dialogue's tone, place specific sounds in the gaps between lines, balance with level, EQ, panning and reverb. **How:** e.g. room tone + distant city + one specific (car horn) between lines. [Pixflow (search text), https://pixflow.net/blog/ambient-sound-design-layering/, checked 2026-10-24]
2112. **Ambience must not pump between edits** — ambience should run smoothly under the scene without pumping in and out between dialogue edits; it is the steady bed that hides cuts. **How:** run the bed as one continuous clip across all dialogue cuts of a scene. [Filmmaker Genius, https://filmmakergenius.com/academy/sound-design-for-film/ambient-sound-design, checked 2026-10-24]
2113. **Pink for water, brown for rumble** — white noise contains all frequencies and suits subtractive sculpting; pink is more natural and good for water sounds; brown generates low frequencies, ideal for rumble and room tones. **How:** `anoisesrc=c=white|pink|brown` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tsugi, https://tsugi-studio.com/blog/2024/09/17/design-sounds-from-noise-source-1/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#anoisesrc, checked 2026-10-24]
2114. **Synthetic stereo room tone** — room tone is often just filtered noise: noise into two EQ filters at slightly different frequencies around 300 Hz gives a small stereo effect. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=5:c=brown:a=0.3:seed=1" -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=5:c=brown:a=0.3:seed=2" -filter_complex "[0]bandpass=f=290:w=1:t=o[l];[1]bandpass=f=310:w=1:t=o[r];[l][r]join=inputs=2:channel_layout=stereo" roomtone.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tsugi, https://tsugi-studio.com/blog/2024/09/17/design-sounds-from-noise-source-1/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#join, checked 2026-10-24]
2115. **Fan / ventilation from noise + LFO** — noise low-passed around 110 Hz for rumble, an LFO on its amplitude for rotation. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=4:c=white:a=0.6" -af "lowpass=f=110,lowpass=f=110,tremolo=f=6:d=0.6,volume=3" fan.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; tremolo = sine LFO, the source used a TwoSines shape). [Tsugi, https://tsugi-studio.com/blog/2024/09/17/design-sounds-from-noise-source-1/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tremolo, checked 2026-10-24]
2116. **Distant helicopter: moving notch on brown noise** — brown noise into a bell EQ with negative gain (a notch) whose centre is moved by an LFO gives a rotor sound. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=4:c=brown:a=0.7" -af "asendcmd=c='0.0-4.0 [expr] equalizer f 400+300*sin(2*PI*TI*4*5)',equalizer=f=400:t=q:w=2:g=-20" heli.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tsugi, https://tsugi-studio.com/blog/2024/09/17/design-sounds-from-noise-source-1/, checked 2026-10-24]
2117. **Cloth foley from pink noise** — pink noise into a band-pass whose centre is modulated to mimic movement, then EQ toward a friction sound, with amplitude driven together. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=1.5:c=pink:a=0.6" -af "asendcmd=c='0.0-1.5 [expr] bandpass f 1500+1200*sin(2*PI*TI*3)',bandpass=f=1500:w=1:t=o,volume='0.5+0.5*sin(2*PI*t*2)':eval=frame" cloth.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Tsugi, https://tsugi-studio.com/blog/2024/09/17/design-sounds-from-noise-source-1/, checked 2026-10-24]
2118. **Seamless ambience loop with acrossfade** — make a loop whose end flows into its start: take seconds 1-6 and crossfade its last second with seconds 0-1. **How:** `ffmpeg -i amb.wav -af "atrim=1:6,asetpts=N/SR/TB" a.wav; ffmpeg -i amb.wav -af "atrim=0:1,asetpts=N/SR/TB" b.wav; ffmpeg -i a.wav -i b.wav -filter_complex "[0][1]acrossfade=d=1:c1=qsin:c2=qsin" loop.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; 5.0 s loop). Splitting one input inside a single `filter_complex` produced an empty file here; separate files worked. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossfade, checked 2026-10-24]
2119. **Loop an ambience to length** — `-stream_loop N` loops an input (−1 = infinite); `aloop` loops samples in the graph. **How:** `ffmpeg -stream_loop 3 -i loop.wav -t 12 bed.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; `-stream_loop -1` with a WAV did not finish within 120 s here, so use a finite count) or `-af "aloop=loop=2:size=220500"` (tested). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Main-options, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aloop, checked 2026-10-24]

## Reverb, echo and space

2120. **Convolution reverb with afir** — `afir` convolves the first stream with an IR stream (up to 60 s; doc example: input + IR wav → reverb). Without an IR file, a decaying noise burst works as a synthetic IR. **How:** IR: `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=1.5:c=white:a=0.5:seed=7" -af "volume='exp(-t*4)':eval=frame,lowpass=f=6000" ir.wav`; reverb: `ffmpeg -i dry.wav -i ir.wav -filter_complex "[0]apad=pad_dur=1.5,asplit[a][b];[b][1]afir[w];[a][w]amix=inputs=2:weights='1 0.35':normalize=0" wet.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; the synthetic IR is a tested command, not a judged room). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afir-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2121. **Decay time = RT60** — reverb time is usually the time to drop below −60 dB (RT60); short decay = small space, long = big; in real spaces highs die faster than lows. **How:** for an exponential IR `exp(-t*k)`, amplitude falls 60 dB at t = 6.91/k (k=4 → ~1.7 s; k=0.86 → ~8 s, used for #2079) and low-pass the IR to darken the tail. [FabFilter, https://www.fabfilter.com/learn/reverb/basic-reverb-controls, checked 2026-10-24]
2122. **Pre-delay changes size and distance** — pre-delay (time between dry sound and first reflections) affects perceived room size and distance and separates direct sound from reverb for clarity; avoid slap-back. **How:** delay only the wet branch: `[w]adelay=20:all=1` before `afir`. [MusicRadar, https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/reverb-simulate-warp-room, checked 2026-10-24] [Apple Logic Pro Space Designer, https://support.apple.com/guide/logicpro-ipad/main-parameters-lpipe9ff881d/ipados, checked 2026-10-24]
2123. **More wet = farther** — the further away a source, the quieter the direct sound compared with its reverb, so raising reverb level pushes it back. **How:** raise the second `amix` weight for distant sounds. [Mixing Lessons, https://www.mixinglessons.com/reverb-plugin-paramaters/, checked 2026-10-24]
2124. **Early-reflection spacing = room size** — greater spacing of early reflections makes the virtual room sound bigger. **How:** with `aecho`, wider tap spacing (`aecho=0.8:0.7:30|70|110:0.5|0.4|0.3` vs `10|20|30`) (aecho tested here 2026-10-24). [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/advanced-reverberation-part-1, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aecho, checked 2026-10-24]
2125. **aecho doc presets** — ffmpeg's own examples: `aecho=0.8:0.88:60:0.4` doubles instruments; `0.8:0.88:6:0.4` (very short) gives a metallic robot; `0.8:0.9:1000:0.3` open-air mountains; `0.8:0.9:1000|1800:0.3|0.25` one more mountain. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -af "apad=pad_dur=3,aecho=0.8:0.9:1000|1800:0.3|0.25" canyon.wav` (pad so the echoes aren't cut; tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aecho, checked 2026-10-24]
2126. **Switch IRs with afir nbirs** — `afir` can hold several IR streams (`nbirs`, up to 32) and pick one with `ir` (changeable at runtime). **How:** `ffmpeg -i dry.wav -i small.wav -i big.wav -filter_complex "[0]apad=pad_dur=1[d];[d][1][2]afir=nbirs=2:ir=1" out.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afir-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2127. **Reverse reverb (swell into a word)** — copy the phrase, reverse it, add 100 % wet long reverb, print it, reverse again so the tail precedes the voice, then align with a fade-in just before the original; it also works with delays. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -i ir.wav -filter_complex "[0]atrim=0:0.8,asetpts=N/SR/TB,apad=pad_dur=1.5,areverse[r];[r][1]afir=dry=0:wet=1,highpass=f=300,areverse" swell.wav`, then place the swell so it ends where the word starts (`adelay` on the dry) (tested here 2026-10-24). [REAPER Blog, https://reaper.blog/2016/05/reversed-reverb-effect/, checked 2026-10-24] [Nail The Mix, https://www.nailthemix.com/architects-vocal-fx-henrik-udd-metalcore, checked 2026-10-24]
2128. **Reverse-reverb details** — end the isolated snippet on a vowel for a cleaner swell, cut the reverb's lows to reduce mud, and automate pan/volume of the swell to blend. **How:** the `highpass=f=300` in #2127 is that low cut; trim the snippet at a vowel. [Splice, https://splice.com/blog/what-is-reverse-reverb/, checked 2026-10-24]
2129. **Reverse delay swell variant** — the reversed-reverb trick also works with delays. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -af "atrim=0:0.6,asetpts=N/SR/TB,apad=pad_dur=1.2,areverse,aecho=0.8:1:120|240|360|480:0.6|0.45|0.3|0.2,areverse" swell_delay.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [REAPER Blog, https://reaper.blog/2016/05/reversed-reverb-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2130. **Reverse and pitch for horror** — reversing and pitch-shifting make natural sounds surreal; time-stretching and (reversed) reverbs/delays add the other-worldly touch; metal/wood squeaks, whistling wind and voices are good ghostly sources. **How:** `areverse,rubberband=pitch=0.7` on a CC0 creak (both tested here 2026-10-24). [A Sound Effect, https://www.asoundeffect.com/how-to-create-horror-sound-effects/, checked 2026-10-24]
2131. **areverse needs a trimmed input** — `areverse` buffers the whole clip in memory; trim first (`atrim=end=5,areverse`). **How:** always `atrim` before `areverse` on long files (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#areverse, checked 2026-10-24]

## Futz: phone, radio, TV, PA, walkie

2132. **Landline phone recipe** — HPF 300 Hz and LPF 3.4 kHz, both 24 dB/oct; a 4-6 dB peak at 1.5-2 kHz; hard compression (fast attack, 6-10 dB gain reduction, release 60-150 ms); light distortion after the filter, then a second 3.4 kHz low-pass (distortion leaks energy above the band); mono; no reverb. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -af "highpass=f=300,highpass=f=300,lowpass=f=3400,lowpass=f=3400,equalizer=f=1700:t=q:w=1.5:g=5,acompressor=threshold=-30dB:ratio=8:attack=5:release=100:makeup=4,asoftclip=type=tanh,lowpass=f=3400,lowpass=f=3400,aformat=channel_layouts=mono" phone.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/telephone-effect-on-vocals/, checked 2026-10-24]
2133. **Why 300-3400 Hz** — the analog phone channel carried about 300-3400 Hz; once digital it was sampled 8,000 times a second (content up to 4 kHz), 8 bits → 64 kbit/s; modern wideband calls carry roughly 50 Hz-7 kHz with digital artifacts instead. **How:** `aresample=8000,aresample=44100` after the band-pass imitates the 8 kHz rate (tested here 2026-10-24). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/telephone-effect-on-vocals/, checked 2026-10-24]
2134. **Slope decides phone vs "bass cut"** — at 12 dB/oct a 300 Hz HPF leaves 200 Hz only 7.8 dB down (still chesty); 24 dB/oct gives 14.3 dB, 48 dB/oct 28.2 dB; a 24 dB/oct LPF at 3.4 kHz puts 6 kHz 19.8 dB down and 8 kHz 29.7 dB down. **How:** cascade two `highpass`/`lowpass` (2-pole each) for 24 dB/oct, four for 48. [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/telephone-effect-on-vocals/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#lowpass, checked 2026-10-24]
2135. **Phone corner off the singer, not a preset** — a voice at 110 Hz loses its fundamental to a 300 Hz HPF but keeps its pitch (the ear uses harmonic spacing); a 220 Hz voice loses only 11.1 dB of its fundamental, so move the HPF to 400 Hz (−20.8 dB) or 500 Hz (−28.5 dB) for higher voices. **How:** `highpass=f=400,highpass=f=400` for higher voices. [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/telephone-effect-on-vocals/, checked 2026-10-24]
2136. **Band-pass makes the voice peakier** — on their test line the 24 dB/oct band-pass dropped RMS 4.20 dB but peak only 1.84 dB (crest factor 13.25→15.61 dB), so compression is needed twice over; budget 4-5 dB make-up. **How:** compare `astats` RMS/peak before and after (astats tested here 2026-10-24). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/telephone-effect-on-vocals/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#astats-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2137. **Phone check: sweep and mono** — solo the effect and sweep a narrow boost: anything answering below 250 Hz or above 4 kHz means soft slopes; sum to mono and it should not change; A/B against a real phone voice memo. **How:** check the band on a spectrogram: `ffmpeg -i phone.wav -lavfi "showspectrumpic=s=640x320:fscale=log:start=50:stop=8000" spec.png` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/telephone-effect-on-vocals/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#showspectrumpic, checked 2026-10-24]
2138. **Phone effect on a duplicate, crossfaded in** — build it on a copy of the voice with sends off; crossfade in on a consonant at a phrase start with a 10-20 ms fade; dip the instrumental 1 dB for the length of the phone line; use it for a line or hook, not a whole verse. **How:** cut the voice into dry/phone pieces and join with `acrossfade=d=0.015`. [Mesh Plugins, https://shop.meshplugins.com/blogs/the-hidden-sauce/vocal-telephone-effect, checked 2026-10-24]
2139. **Phone alternative: sinc FIR band-pass** — the `sinc` source makes Kaiser-windowed FIR coefficients (band-pass when hp < lp) for `afir`. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -f lavfi -i "sinc=hp=300:lp=3400:r=44100" -filter_complex "[0][1]afir" phone_fir.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sinc, checked 2026-10-24]
2140. **Futz band table by device** — landline/mobile 300 Hz-3.4 kHz; mobile HD voice 100 Hz-7 kHz; small TV/clock radio 200-400 Hz to 5-8 kHz + cabinet peak 800 Hz-1.5 kHz; laptop 400 Hz-10 kHz (thin, not muffled); PA/megaphone 400 Hz-4 kHz + strong 1-2 kHz honk + real distortion; intercom/walkie 400 Hz-3 kHz + heavy compression + squelch at each transmission's top and tail; all with steep filters, 24 dB/oct+. **How:** e.g. TV `highpass=f=300,highpass=f=300,lowpass=f=6000,lowpass=f=6000,equalizer=f=1100:t=q:w=4:g=6,acompressor=threshold=-25dB:ratio=6`; laptop `highpass=f=400,highpass=f=400,lowpass=f=10000,lowpass=f=10000`; PA `...lowpass=f=4000,lowpass=f=4000,equalizer=f=1500:t=q:w=1.5:g=8,volume=2,asoftclip=type=hard:threshold=0.5` (all tested here 2026-10-24). [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/futz-phones-radios-and-tvs/, checked 2026-10-24]
2141. **Walkie with squelch at both ends** — intercom/walkie: 400 Hz-3 kHz, heavy compression, a noise squelch at the start and end of each transmission. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=0.2:c=white:a=0.25" -filter_complex "[0]highpass=f=400,highpass=f=400,lowpass=f=3000,lowpass=f=3000,acompressor=threshold=-30dB:ratio=10:attack=2:release=60:makeup=6,agate=threshold=0.0056[v];[1]highpass=f=1000,asplit[s1][s2];[s1][v][s2]concat=n=3:v=0:a=1" walkie.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; gate 0.0056 ≈ −45 dB from #2142). [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/futz-phones-radios-and-tvs/, checked 2026-10-24]
2142. **Two-way radio preset numbers** — a walkie preset cuts below 500 Hz and above 3 kHz, boosts the mids 6 dB, adds drive for the clipped edge and gates at −45 dB so the line is quiet between phrases. **How:** `highpass=f=500,lowpass=f=3000,equalizer=f=1500:t=o:w=2:g=6,asoftclip,agate=threshold=0.0056` (agate threshold is linear; 0.0056 ≈ −45 dBFS). [VoiceFX, https://voicefx.io/radio-voice-effect, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#agate, checked 2026-10-24]
2143. **Radio distortion: analog vs digital** — a bitcrusher is distortion you'd only hear on digital devices; for an analog radio feel use a 3-band EQ band-pass and then distortion, optionally a compressor before it to even out levels. **How:** `bandpass` + `acompressor` + `asoftclip` (analog); `acrusher` only for digital radio. [FMOD Forums, https://qa.fmod.com/t/help-achieving-a-military-style-walkie-talkie-effect/21264, checked 2026-10-24]
2144. **Futzed voice must sit in a room** — the dry, centred futz is why it sounds fake; after filtering add a short reverb matching the device's room, pan to where the device is on screen, drop the level; if the camera moves, the device's sound should change too. **How:** futz chain → `afir` small IR at low mix → `pan` toward the device side. [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/futz-phones-radios-and-tvs/, checked 2026-10-24]
2145. **Codec dropout sells a mobile call** — a modern mobile call sounds slightly granular and metallic and occasionally drops out; one very short dropout per call does more than any EQ; gentle top-octave bitcrushing gets close. **How:** one dropout: `volume='if(between(t,1.2,1.32),0,1)':eval=frame` (volume eval tested here 2026-10-24). [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/futz-phones-radios-and-tvs/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#volume, checked 2026-10-24]
2146. **Noise matched to the device** — a landline has faint hum and hiss, a walkie a squelch burst, a PA room noise around it; silence between words gives the plugin away. **How:** mix a very low `anoisesrc` (pink, a≈0.02) under the futzed line (tested in old-radio chain here 2026-10-24). [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/futz-phones-radios-and-tvs/, checked 2026-10-24]
2147. **Futz definition** — futzing is intentional processing of a sound to fit a scene and environment (cell-phone voice, a voice inside a space suit, Darth Vader). **How:** decide what device/space the voice passes through before picking filters. [TSM Studio, https://tsmstudio.com/tsmstudio/the-art-of-futzing-processing-examples-included/, checked 2026-10-24]
2148. **Worldizing** — Walter Murch's technique: play a recording through a speaker in a real space and re-record it with microphones so it takes on that place's acoustics; it still beats plugins. **How:** play the futzed file from a phone in a real room, record with another phone, use that take. [filmsound.org, https://filmsound.org/terminology/worldizing.htm, checked 2026-10-24] [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/futz-phones-radios-and-tvs/, checked 2026-10-24]
2149. **Old radio with iZotope-style band limits** — band-limiting makes music sound like small radio speakers (wider band = bigger set), mechanical noise/dust/scratch simulate interference, and mono helps for a radio in a bar or a phone call. **How:** `ffmpeg -i m.wav -af "highpass=f=400,lowpass=f=4000,aformat=channel_layouts=mono" radio.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24), plus a low pink-noise layer. [iZotope, https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/using-vinyl-for-an-old-radio-sound, checked 2026-10-24]
2150. **Telephone mid boost alternative** — a simpler phone recipe: LPF at 2 kHz, HPF at 500 Hz and a 1 kHz boost. **How:** `highpass=f=500,lowpass=f=2000,equalizer=f=1000:t=o:w=1:g=6`. [Thales Matos, https://thalesmatos.com/blog/telephone-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]

## Distance, walls and underwater

2151. **Next-room music recipe** — LPF 500 Hz-2 kHz at 24 dB/oct (start ~1 kHz), drop 6-12 dB, collapse toward mono, small room reverb 0.6-1.2 s with pre-delay under 20 ms at 30-50 % mix, a small 100-200 Hz boost for wall resonance, roll off below 40 Hz; don't high-pass the low end away — the mud is the effect. **How:** `ffmpeg -i m.wav -i ir_small.wav -filter_complex "[0]aformat=channel_layouts=mono,lowpass=f=1000,lowpass=f=1000,equalizer=f=150:t=q:w=1:g=4,highpass=f=40,volume=-9dB,asplit[d][w];[w]adelay=15:all=1[wd];[wd][1]afir[r];[d][r]amix=inputs=2:weights='0.6 0.4':normalize=0" nextroom.wav` with a 0.9 s synthetic IR (tested here 2026-10-24). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-music-sound-like-another-room/, checked 2026-10-24]
2152. **Room-effect variations** — door open: cutoff 3-4 kHz, less reverb, keep some stereo; underwater: LPF ~400 Hz with slow cutoff modulation; car outside: LPF ~250 Hz, heavy compression, slow fade as it drives away; club from the smoking area: LPF ~800 Hz, long reverb, and when the door opens automate the cutoff up 2 kHz for a bar. **How:** car: `lowpass=f=250,lowpass=f=250,acompressor=threshold=-30dB:ratio=8:makeup=4,afade=t=out:st=1:d=3`; club door: `asendcmd=c='1.0-2.0 lowpass f 2800;2.0 lowpass f 800',lowpass=f=800,lowpass=f=800` (both tested here 2026-10-24). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-music-sound-like-another-room/, checked 2026-10-24]
2153. **Muffled presets with numbers** — Next Room 850 Hz, −8 dB, +6 dB bass, mono; Bathroom at a Party 1100 Hz, −6 dB, +4 dB, stereo; Through the Wall 600 Hz, −12 dB, +8 dB, mono; Underwater 400 Hz, −4 dB, 0 dB, low reverb; Daydream 3000 Hz, −3 dB, +2 dB; all with a 24 dB/oct low-pass and a 1.4 s small-room reverb. **How:** `lowpass=f=850,lowpass=f=850,lowshelf=f=120:g=6,volume=-8dB` (all four presets tested here 2026-10-24; reverb not included). [Slowed Reverb Maker, https://slowedreverbmaker.net/tools/muffled-song-maker, checked 2026-10-24]
2154. **Phone filters steep, wall filters gentle** — a wall is a gentle physical roll-off, a phone line a designed electronic band limit with hard edges; that is why futz advice is "steep" and through-wall advice differs. **How:** 2-pole (12 dB/oct) `lowpass` for walls, cascaded 4-pole for phones. [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/futz-phones-radios-and-tvs/, checked 2026-10-24]
2155. **Through a wall: low-pass ~300 Hz, plus resonance and reflections** — try a low-pass around 300 Hz (maybe lower); building materials resonate, so a bump somewhere 180-400 Hz (careful of 300 Hz mud), reverb/IR, or a short slap delay (early reflections tell the ear the space) help. **How:** `lowpass=f=300,equalizer=f=220:t=q:w=2:g=3,aecho=0.6:0.4:40:0.3`. [Sound Design Stack Exchange, https://sound.stackexchange.com/questions/24641/how-to-create-the-muffled-sound-effect-like-hearing-through-walls/42140, checked 2026-10-24]
2156. **Underwater EQ by depth** — HPF at 20 Hz (optional), the key move is a LPF at 300 Hz; go to 200 Hz for deeper, 400-500 Hz nearer the surface; add chorus after the EQ at about 4 Hz rate, 50/50 dry/wet for warble. **How:** `ffmpeg -i m.wav -af "highpass=f=20,lowpass=f=300,lowpass=f=300,chorus=0.5:0.5:20:0.5:4:2" underwater.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Music Guy Mixing, https://www.musicguymixing.com/underwater-eq/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chorus, checked 2026-10-24]
2157. **Swim to the surface = automate the cutoff up** — automate the underwater low-pass upward when the character surfaces; going from filtered to full spectrum feels refreshing. **How:** `asendcmd=c='2.0-3.0 [expr] lowpass f 300*exp(log(60)*TI)',lowpass=f=300` (tested here 2026-10-24 on pink noise; centroid 0.28→6.3 kHz across the move). [Music Guy Mixing, https://www.musicguymixing.com/underwater-eq/, checked 2026-10-24]
2158. **Underwater transition: dip the level too** — cut highs (above ~1 kHz for "deep"), and when going from above to under water, drop the level a bit at the transition; add water sounds for depth. **How:** combine #2157 with `volume=-4dB` at the dive point. [Voice Over Tip, https://voiceovertip.com/how-to-make-underwater-sound-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2159. **Distance: quieter, duller, thinner** — a sound at the back needs to be quieter and have less top end (air absorbs highs); also roll off below 150-200 Hz; use a diffuse reverb with its top rolled off; distant sounds can take more reverb. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -i ir.wav -filter_complex "[0]highpass=f=180,lowpass=f=3000,volume=-8dB,asplit[d][w];[w]apad=pad_dur=1.5,adelay=100:all=1[wd];[wd][1]afir,lowpass=f=4000[r];[d][r]amix=inputs=2:weights='1 0.5':duration=longest:normalize=0" far.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/creating-sense-depth-your-mix, checked 2026-10-24]
2160. **Distance with filtered echoes** — echo repeats with the top rolled off above ~4 kHz and bottom below ~200 Hz give an impression of distance; long pre-delay (90-110 ms) reverb or delay instead of reverb keeps a vocal forward. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -af "apad=pad_dur=1.5,asplit[a][b];[b]aecho=0.8:0.6:350|700:0.4|0.2,highpass=f=200,lowpass=f=4000[e];[a][e]amix=inputs=2:normalize=0" echo_far.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/creating-sense-depth-your-mix, checked 2026-10-24]
2161. **Automated low-pass instead of a volume dip** — a 12 or 18 dB/oct low-pass automated down while something else plays pushes a sound back more subtly than level automation. **How:** `asendcmd=c='1.0 lowpass f 2500;3.0 lowpass f 20000',lowpass=f=20000` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/creating-sense-depth-your-mix, checked 2026-10-24]
2162. **Darker = farther** — highs lose more level over distance than mids, lows much less (a distant helicopter still rumbles), so a low-pass moves sounds back; bright, airy, dry sounds sit up front. **How:** dull background SFX with `lowpass`, keep hero SFX full-band. [iZotope, https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/what-is-mix-depth-how-to-create-front-back-space, checked 2026-10-24]
2163. **Game-audio distance curves** — Wwise maps distance to both volume (e.g. −17 dB at 30 m in the course example) and a low-pass curve, because highs diminish over distance. **How:** for an approaching object, ramp `volume` and a `lowpass` frequency together with `asendcmd`. [Audiokinetic Wwise 101, https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/courses/wwise101/?id=adjusting_attenuation_curves, checked 2026-10-24]
2164. **Bass travels: sub booms read as distant** — bass travels further than highs, so a distant event sounds like it has a high-cut filter on it. **How:** low-pass a hit to make it read distant (#2078). [Richard Pryn, https://richardpryn.com/trailer-hits/, checked 2026-10-24]
2165. **Ear-ringing after an explosion** — after the explosion, low-pass all other sounds and fade in an ear-ringing tone; the trope (often credited to Saving Private Ryan) puts the audience in the character's hearing. **How:** `ffmpeg -i hit.wav -i bed.wav -f lavfi -i "sine=f=3500:d=4:r=44100" -filter_complex "[1]asendcmd=c='0.5-1.0 [expr] lowpass f 18000*exp(log(300/18000)*TI)',lowpass=f=18000,volume=0.6[m];[2]volume=0.06,afade=t=in:st=0.5:d=0.3,afade=t=out:st=1.5:d=2.5[r];[0]aformat=channel_layouts=mono[i];[i][m][r]amix=inputs=3:duration=longest:normalize=0" ring.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; the 3.5 kHz tone is our choice). [David Dumais, https://www.daviddumaisaudio.com/how-to-create-a-tinnitus-sfx-in-phase-plant/, checked 2026-10-24] [Sound Design Stack Exchange, https://sound.stackexchange.com/questions/33129/name-and-examples-for-the-tinnitus-effect-after-explosions-in-films, checked 2026-10-24]
2166. **Ring types: steady, blast, slow recovery** — a steady ring holds; a blast ring arrives at full level with no ramp plus dull pressure underneath; a slow recovery thins out and lets the world back in. **How:** for "blast" skip the fade-in in #2165; for "recovery" fade the bed's low-pass back up. [Morphic, https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/ear-ringing-sound-effect, checked 2026-10-24]

## Pitch and time: slow-mo, tape stop, creature voices

2167. **Slow-mo SFX: an octave down, 30 % slower** — pitch slow-mo effects at least one octave down (about as far as you go without severe artifacts) and also time-shift them at least 30 % slower; treat backgrounds the same; add low-end elements; use a low whoosh or bass flutter into and out of slow-mo; water doesn't pitch well (try lava or mud). **How:** `ffmpeg -i hit.wav -af "rubberband=pitch=0.5:tempo=0.7" hit_slomo.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Boom Box Post, https://www.boomboxpost.com/blog/2017/10/26/lunch-and-learn-sound-effects-editing-slo-mo, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#rubberband, checked 2026-10-24]
2168. **Varispeed vs independent pitch/time** — time stretching changes duration without pitch, pitch scaling changes pitch without duration, "pitch control" (varispeed) changes both together; varispeed is the simpler method and degrades the sound less. **How:** varispeed: `asetrate=44100*0.5,aresample=44100` (octave down, twice as long; tested here 2026-10-24); independent: `rubberband`. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_time_stretching_and_pitch_scaling, checked 2026-10-24] [O'Reilly "The Audio Expert" ch.12, https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-audio-expert/9780240821009/xhtml/Ch12.xhtml, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#asetrate, checked 2026-10-24]
2169. **Semitones from speed** — pitch in semitones = 12 × log2(speed): half speed is 12 semitones down and twice as long; a quarter is 24 down; a tenth −39.9. **How:** for n semitones use `asetrate=44100*2^(n/12)` (math checked here 2026-10-24: 0.5→−12.0, 0.25→−24.0, 0.1→−39.9). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-a-tape-stop-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2170. **rubberband pitch with formant preservation** — `rubberband` shifts pitch (`pitch` scale factor) and tempo independently and has `formant=preserved`. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -af "rubberband=pitch=0.7937:formant=preserved" v_down4.wav` (−4 semitones; tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#rubberband, checked 2026-10-24]
2171. **rubberband transient and window modes** — options include `transients=crisp|mixed|smooth`, `detector=compound|percussive|soft`, `window=standard|short|long`, `smoothing`, `pitchq=quality|speed|consistency`. **How:** hits: `rubberband=tempo=0.5:transients=crisp:detector=percussive`; textures: `rubberband=tempo=0.5:transients=smooth:window=long` (both tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#rubberband, checked 2026-10-24]
2172. **atempo for speed without pitch** — `atempo` takes 0.5-100; above 2 it skips samples instead of blending, so chain instances (`atempo=sqrt(3),atempo=sqrt(3)`). **How:** `atempo=0.5` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#atempo, checked 2026-10-24]
2173. **Stretch and pitch for drones** — time-stretch existing files to make atmospheres/drones; it works best with high sample-rate sources, and recordings with lots of highs survive pitching down two or three octaves better than sub-heavy ones. **How:** `ffmpeg -i src.wav -af "rubberband=tempo=0.125:window=long:smoothing=on,rubberband=pitch=0.5" drone.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Ableton, https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/learn-how-to-make-high-impact-sounds-for-movies-and-trailers/, checked 2026-10-24]
2174. **High-resolution sources survive extreme pitch** — material recorded at high sample rates (192 kHz/24-bit, sold as 96 kHz) can be pitched and processed to extremes while keeping quality. **How:** prefer 96 kHz CC0 sources for heavy slow-mo/creature work. [Daan Hendriks, https://daanhendriks.co.uk/sound-design/creature-sound-design-tutorial/, checked 2026-10-24]
2175. **Creature voice = pitch down + layered animal** — the core of creature voices is pitch shifting your own roar low and layering animal roars on top. **How:** `rubberband=pitch=0.6:formant=shifted` on the voice, then `amix` with a CC0 animal growl (pitch chain tested here 2026-10-24). [Krotos, https://www.krotosaudio.com/creature-voice-effects-tutorial/, checked 2026-10-24]
2176. **Tape stop: speed and pitch fall together** — a tape stop slows audio to a halt so pitch and speed fall together (unlike wow/flutter or a pitch bend); length a quarter note to one bar; shed speed fastest at the start; fade the last moment (≤5 ms) so it doesn't click; high-pass at 25 Hz after the stop. **How:** numpy: read position = cumulative sum of a speed ramp, `np.interp` the source, 5 ms end fade, `butter(2,25,'highpass')` (tested here 2026-10-24, `sk2_07/np_tests.py`). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-a-tape-stop-effect/, checked 2026-10-24] [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/how-to-make-a-tape-stop/, checked 2026-10-24]
2177. **Tape stop curve: exponential = straight pitch line** — a linear speed ramp drops slowly then off a cliff in pitch (first half = one octave, last tenth = three more); a speed decaying as e^(−t/τ) falls 17.3 semitones per τ in a straight line but never reaches zero, so you choose where to cut. **How:** `v = np.exp(-t/0.25)` instead of `1 - t/T` in #2176 (both tested here 2026-10-24). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-a-tape-stop-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2178. **Tape stop eats half its length of material** — a linear stop of length T consumes T/2 of source, so anything after is T/2 late; keep at least that much material past the stop point. **How:** measured here: a 1.0 s linear stop consumed 0.5 s of source (2026-10-24). [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-a-tape-stop-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2179. **Tape stop length in beats** — set the stop in beats, not milliseconds (bar = 240/BPM s): two beats at 87 BPM is 1.379 s; the same gesture then reads right at any tempo. **How:** stop length = n × 60/BPM. [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-a-tape-stop-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2180. **Tape start / spin-up** — the reverse move: speed rises from a dead stop into pitch; good as an intro into a phrase or drop. **How:** numpy speed ramp 0→1 (`v = t/T`, tested here 2026-10-24). [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/how-to-make-a-tape-stop/, checked 2026-10-24] [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-a-tape-stop-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2181. **Where tape stops land** — into a drop (quick stop, a beat of silence, then the drop), ending a phrase/track, or short repeated as a stutter; it wears out fast if used on every transition. **How:** once per video, at the biggest section change. [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/how-to-make-a-tape-stop/, checked 2026-10-24]
2182. **Check a tape stop on a phone** — small drivers give up in the hundreds of Hz, so on a phone the second half of a stop is hats over near-silence; put the drama in the top of the ramp and finish sooner if it must work there; test on a sustained sine (period should lengthen smoothly). **How:** run #2176 on `sine=f=440` and look at `showwavespic`. [Gazillion Industries, https://gazillionindustries.com/how-to-make-a-tape-stop-effect/, checked 2026-10-24]
2183. **Wow and flutter are not tape stop** — wow/flutter is a small ongoing pitch wobble that never stops the sound. **How:** a tested stand-in: `vibrato=f=0.8:d=0.05,vibrato=f=7:d=0.02` (tested here 2026-10-24; rates are our choice). [Tunary Instruments, https://tunaryinstruments.com/guides/how-to-make-a-tape-stop/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#vibrato, checked 2026-10-24]
2184. **Frequency shift ≠ pitch shift** — a true frequency shift moves the whole spectrum and gives a metallic, inharmonic sound (a special effect); pitch shifting keeps harmonic relations. **How:** `afreqshift=shift=50` (metallic) vs `rubberband=pitch=1.06` (both tested here 2026-10-24). [Stephan Bernsee (Zynaptiq), https://blogs.zynaptiq.com/bernsee/time-pitch-overview/, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afreqshift, checked 2026-10-24]
2185. **Transposing noise in a sampler** — white noise can't be transposed at source (pitch doesn't change on a noise oscillator), but transposing a noise recording in a sampler gives a lo-fi effect. **How:** `asetrate=44100*0.5,aresample=44100` on a recorded noise file. [MusicRadar, https://www.musicradar.com/tuition/tech/how-to-create-custom-sweeps-and-fx-using-noise-611502, checked 2026-10-24]
2186. **Granular textures** — granular synthesis chops a sound into grains (tens of ms; NI says 1-100 ms) and reassembles them; each grain gets a smooth window (Hann) because raw rectangular grains click; results tend to be cloudy, smeared, frozen or glitchy. **How:** numpy: 50 ms Hann grains from random positions, overlap-add at 1/4 grain hop (tested here 2026-10-24, `np_tests.py`). [CMU Intro to Computer Music, https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~15322/book/ch10/03.html, checked 2026-10-24] [Native Instruments, https://blog.native-instruments.com/granular-synthesis/, checked 2026-10-24] [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/granular-synthesis-practical-introduction, checked 2026-10-24]

## Doppler, panning and stereo width

2187. **Doppler formula** — observed f = ((v_m ± v_r)/(v_m ∓ v_s)) × f0: source speed subtracted from the wave speed when approaching, added when receding. **How:** for a 440 Hz source at 30 m/s in air (343 m/s): 482.2 Hz approaching, 404.6 Hz receding; a numpy pass-by (delay = r/c, gain 1/r) measured 478.4 and 401.7 Hz (tested here 2026-10-24, `np_tests.py`). [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppler_effect, checked 2026-10-24]
2188. **Pass-by = pitch + level + pan** — automate pitch up and volume up while panning in, then pitch and volume down as it moves away. **How:** ffmpeg approximation on a tonal source: `aformat=channel_layouts=stereo,asendcmd=c='0.0-3.0 [expr] rubberband pitch 1.12-0.24/(1+exp(-(TI-0.5)*12))',rubberband=pitch=1.12,aeval='val(0)*(1-t/3)*(0.3+0.7*exp(-pow((t-1.5)*2,2)))|val(1)*(t/3)*(0.3+0.7*exp(-pow((t-1.5)*2,2)))'` (tested here 2026-10-24). [SoundCy, https://soundcy.com/article/how-to-make-audio-sound-like-its-passing-by, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aeval, checked 2026-10-24]
2189. **Doppler needs pre-delay for far sources** — a Doppler simulation adds an initial delay (a source a mile away arrives seconds late) that must be compensated to land the peak at a point. **How:** in the numpy pass-by, offset the read index by the distance delay and shift the result so the closest point lands on the frame. [Full Compass Doppler manual, https://www.fullcompass.com/common/files/86223-DOPPLERManual.pdf, checked 2026-10-24]
2190. **Equal-power pan moves** — pan automation with equal-power law; avoid hard-left-to-hard-right moves in stereo unless you want a stylized wipe (natural pass-bys arc through the field). **How:** `aeval='val(0)*cos(PI/2*min(t/0.8,1))|val(0)*sin(PI/2*min(t/0.8,1))':c=stereo` (tested here 2026-10-24); keep the end angle short of 90° for a natural pass. [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2191. **Static pan of a mono SFX** — `pan` sets per-output-channel gains. **How:** `pan=stereo|c0=0.8*c0|c1=0.3*c0` (tested here 2026-10-24); use `<` instead of `=` to renormalize gains and avoid clipping. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#pan-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2192. **Mono SFX are point sources** — mono is the default for effects without width (footsteps, animal calls, flickering bulbs, UI beeps); they are easier to pan than stereo files and ideal for movement effects. **How:** keep spot SFX mono and pan them; keep beds stereo. [Pro Sound Effects, https://blog.prosoundeffects.com/when-and-how-to-use-sound-effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2193. **Haas zone and its numbers** — two copies within roughly 30-40 ms fuse into one event located at the first arrival; around 2-5 ms location comes almost only from the first sound, beyond ~5 ms it gets louder and wider; the reflection can be ~10 dB louder and still not shift localization. **How:** `haas` filter on a mono SFX (default delays 2.05/2.12 ms; local 6.1 allows 0-40 ms): `ffmpeg -i mono.wav -af "haas=left_delay=0:right_delay=15:middle_source=left" wide.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [iZotope, https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/what-is-the-haas-effect, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#haas, checked 2026-10-24]
2194. **Haas is not mono-safe: keep it off key sounds** — Haas-widened parts disappear or change in mono through phase cancellation; going outside 5-35 ms fixes cancellation but becomes an echo; use it only on non-critical extras; M/S widening, chorus, double-tracking or early-reflection reverbs are mono-compatible alternatives. **How:** never Haas the main whoosh/impact; mono-check with `pan=mono|c0=0.5*c0+0.5*c1` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/sound-advice/q-can-haas-delays-be-mono-compatible, checked 2026-10-24]
2195. **Stereo-ising a mono sound** — duplicate it, route hard left/right and make the copies different; the more different, the wider; delay, pitch shift and pitch modulation are the usual tools. **How:** `aformat=channel_layouts=stereo,adecorrelate=stages=6` (tested here 2026-10-24) or a small `chorus`. [Production Expert, https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/how-to-create-stereo-from-mono-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [MusicRadar, https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/add-width-to-mono-sounds, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#adecorrelate, checked 2026-10-24]
2196. **Widen beds with stereowiden / extrastereo** — `stereowiden` suppresses common signal and cross-delays L/R (defaults delay 20 ms, feedback 0.3, crossfeed 0.3, drymix 0.8); `extrastereo` scales L/R difference (m=1 unchanged, 0 mono, −1 swaps). **How:** `stereowiden=delay=20:feedback=0.3:crossfeed=0.3` or `extrastereo=m=2.0` on stereo ambience (both tested here 2026-10-24); mono-check after. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#stereowiden, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#extrastereo, checked 2026-10-24]
2197. **Whoosh width and stereo micro-delays** — stereo decorrelation with micro-delays 0.2-1.5 ms or all-pass networks widens without obvious echo; if it drops more than ~3 dB or turns hollow in mono, reduce inter-channel delay and keep energy in the mid. **How:** `adelay=0|1:all=0` (1 ms on the right) then mono-check RMS with `astats`. [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2198. **Auto-pan with apulsator** — `apulsator` modulates L/R volume with an LFO; offset_r 0.5 (default) acts as an autopanner, equal offsets as a tremolo; modes sine/triangle/square/sawup/sawdown; timing in bpm, ms or hz. **How:** `ffmpeg -i amb.wav -af "aformat=channel_layouts=stereo,apulsator=hz=0.5" pan.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#apulsator, checked 2026-10-24]
2199. **Headphone crossfeed options** — `crossfeed` (strength default 0.2, cut-off near 1550 Hz), `bs2b` (profiles default 700 Hz/50, cmoy 700/60, jmeier 650/95) and `earwax` (moves headphone image in front, for 44.1 kHz stereo) reduce extreme headphone separation. **How:** `crossfeed=strength=0.4`, `bs2b=profile=cmoy`, `earwax` (all three tested here 2026-10-24) on an exported headphone check copy, not the master. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#crossfeed, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#bs2b, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#earwax, checked 2026-10-24]

[ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sofalizer, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#headphone, checked 2026-10-24]

## Robot, glitch and lo-fi

2201. **Ring-mod robot voice** — ring modulation multiplies the voice by a steady sine; 30-80 Hz gives the clanging metallic sci-fi robot; lower (30) harsher and more mechanical, higher (120+) more alien. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -f lavfi -i "sine=f=30:r=44100:d=3.2" -filter_complex "[0][1]amultiply,volume=6" robot.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24 at 30 and 120 Hz; `sine` has amplitude 1/8, hence the gain). [VoiceFX, https://voicefx.io/robot-voice-changer, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#amultiply, checked 2026-10-24]
2202. **Dalek origin: 30 Hz tape loop** — the BBC Radiophonic ring modulator multiplied an actor's voice by a 30 Hz sine from a tape loop; its diodes added distortion; ring modulation can make speech robotic though it rarely suits melodic sounds. **How:** #2201 at 30 Hz plus a light `asoftclip`. [BBC R&D Web Audio, https://webaudio.prototyping.bbc.co.uk/ring-modulator/, checked 2026-10-24] [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotic_voice_effects, checked 2026-10-24]
2203. **tremolo above 20 Hz is a ring modulator** — ffmpeg's `tremolo` docs: below 20 Hz it is tremolo, higher frequencies make it a ring modulator. **How:** `tremolo=f=60:d=1` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tremolo, checked 2026-10-24]
2204. **FFT robotize and whisper (doc recipes)** — `afftfilt` doc examples: robotize `real='hypot(re,im)*sin(0)':imag='hypot(re,im)*cos(0)':win_size=512:overlap=0.75`; whisper with random phase, `win_size=128:overlap=0.8`. **How:** `ffmpeg -i v.wav -af "afftfilt=real='hypot(re,im)*sin(0)':imag='hypot(re,im)*cos(0)':win_size=512:overlap=0.75" robot_fft.wav` (both tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afftfilt, checked 2026-10-24]
2205. **Vocoder robot (monotone)** — a vocoder measures the voice's loudness in bands (16 in VoiceFX's) and imposes it on a buzzing carrier so each syllable lands on one flat pitch; Audacity's Vocoder takes the voice in the left channel and the carrier in the right, and white-noise carrier gives a robot voice. **How:** no vocoder filter in ffmpeg; use Audacity's Vocoder (missing here; see Missing tools). [VoiceFX, https://voicefx.io/robot-voice-changer, checked 2026-10-24] [Audacity Manual, https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/vocoder.html, checked 2026-10-24]
2206. **What a glitch is** — the audible by-product of digital failure: buffer overruns, bit-depth truncation, sample-rate mismatches, corruption → stutters, zipper noise, bitcrushed distortion, granular fragments; harsh and aliased rather than warm. **How:** combine stutter (#2207) and crush (#2209). [Selekt (search text), https://selektaudio.com/sound-lab/textures/glitch, checked 2026-10-24]
2207. **Buffer-repeat stutter** — repeat a short slice (Selekt: buffer-repeat at 1/32-note intervals); a glitch module records a circular buffer and loops a clock-sized slice. **How:** `ffmpeg -i m.wav -af "atrim=0:0.0625,asetpts=N/SR/TB,aloop=loop=15:size=2756" stutter.wav` (a 1/32 note at 120 BPM = 62.5 ms; tested here 2026-10-24). [Selekt (search text), https://selektaudio.com/sound-lab/textures/glitch, checked 2026-10-24] [Phazerville Glitch, https://firmware.phazerville.com/Glitch, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aloop, checked 2026-10-24]
2208. **Progressive bitcrush 16→4 bit** — bitcrush progressively from 16-bit down to 4-bit over two bars for a transition. **How:** `acrusher` accepts commands: `asendcmd=c='0.0-4.0 [expr] acrusher bits 16-12*TI',acrusher=bits=16:mode=lin:mix=1` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Selekt (search text), https://selektaudio.com/sound-lab/textures/glitch, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrusher, checked 2026-10-24]
2209. **acrusher modes** — `acrusher` reduces bit resolution (without changing real bit depth); `mode=log` is more natural and doesn't gate quiet signals; `aa` gives softer crushing; `samples` reduces sample rate; built-in `lfo`. **How:** `acrusher=bits=6:mode=log:aa=1:mix=1`; rate crush `acrusher=samples=12:bits=16`; moving `acrusher=bits=8:samples=6:lfo=1:lforange=10:lforate=0.5` (all tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrusher, checked 2026-10-24]
2210. **Bitcrush for a bad radio / broken robot** — crushing to ~13 bit gives grit; heavier gives broken-robot sounds, a bad radio signal (like C&C radio) and digital breakup; compress before the crusher so the grit sits evenly. **How:** `acompressor,acrusher=bits=8:mode=log` on a futzed voice. [Isocube BITCRUSHER-FX, https://isocube.itch.io/bitcrusher-fx, checked 2026-10-24]
2211. **Random gating for digital dropouts (tested command only)** — `volume` with `eval=frame` and `random()` mutes random 1024-sample frames. **How:** `ffmpeg -i m.wav -af "volume='gt(random(0),0.35)':eval=frame,acrusher=bits=8:samples=4" glitch.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; no claim about how it sounds). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#volume, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg-utils docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-utils.html#Expression-Evaluation, checked 2026-10-24]
2212. **Trance gate / rhythmic chop** — `apulsator` with `mode=square`, `offset_r=0` (both channels together) and BPM timing chops a bed in tempo. **How:** `apulsator=mode=square:offset_r=0:timing=bpm:bpm=120:amount=1` (tested here 2026-10-24); or `amultiply` with a square LFO from `aevalsrc='0.5+0.5*sgn(sin(2*PI*4*t))'` (tested). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#apulsator, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#amultiply, checked 2026-10-24]
2213. **Lo-fi gramophone ingredients** — gramophone/lo-fi emulators combine low- and high-pass band limiting (thin, phone-like), warbly modulation (depth/rate) and hum and crackle (noise/dust). **How:** `highpass=f=400,lowpass=f=4000` + `vibrato` + a low velvet-noise crackle layer (#2214) (pieces tested here 2026-10-24). [Delicious Audio, https://delicious-audio.com/glowfly-albert-gramophone-simulator/, checked 2026-10-24]
2214. **Velvet noise for sparse crackle (tested command only)** — `anoisesrc` has a velvet colour with `density` (0-1, default 0.05). **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "anoisesrc=d=4:c=velvet:density=0.002:a=0.8" -af "highpass=f=1500" crackle.wav` (tested here 2026-10-24; no claim about how it sounds). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#anoisesrc, checked 2026-10-24]
2215. **RIAA/vinyl emphasis curves** — `aemphasis` applies or restores emphasis curves (Columbia, EMI, BSI 78 RPM, RIAA, CD, FM 50/75 µs); production mode applies, reproduction restores. **How:** `aemphasis=mode=production:type=riaa` (tested here 2026-10-24; no claim it sounds "vintage"). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aemphasis, checked 2026-10-24]
2216. **Saturation vs clipping** — clipping distortion suits single sounds (thicker, more highs) but on complex material every frequency makes harmonics until it turns toward noise; gentler saturation suits groups and buses; saturation adds compression and harmonics (even harmonics of 100 Hz at 200, 400, 600 Hz). **How:** single hits: `asoftclip=type=hard`; groups: `asoftclip=type=tanh:threshold=0.6:oversample=4` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/new-harmonics, checked 2026-10-24] [iZotope, https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/what-is-audio-saturation, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#asoftclip, checked 2026-10-24]
2217. **Oversample nonlinear stages** — nonlinear saturation widens bandwidth and can alias above Nyquist; whoosh guides advise oversampling (2×-8×) for aggressive drive. **How:** `asoftclip=...:oversample=4` (tested here 2026-10-24). [Maynooth (Timoney et al.), http://eprints.maynoothuniversity.ie/4099/1/EAApaper-JT-30-03.pdf, checked 2026-10-24] [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/sounddesign/how-to-create-whooshes-from-scratch, checked 2026-10-24]
2218. **Transient boost survives limiting** — boosting a snare's attack and then limiting back to the same peak does not fully cancel the perceived punch; clipping back to the peak keeps the enhancement more obviously (with more grit). **How:** `crystalizer=i=4` or `aderivative` blend as a crude attack lift, then `alimiter`/`apsyclip=clip=0.7` (all tested here 2026-10-24). [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/using-transient-processors-audio-examples, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#apsyclip, checked 2026-10-24]
2219. **Transient shaper vs compressor** — a transient shaper splits attack and sustain and adjusts each (boost attack for crack, pull sustain to tighten a boomy hit) without the level-riding of a compressor. **How:** no dedicated transient shaper in ffmpeg; approximate attack lift with `crystalizer` and tail cut with `agate` (#2221). [Top VST Plugins, https://topvstplugins.com/guides/transient-shapers/, checked 2026-10-24]
2220. **Exciter adds missing highs** — `aexciter` creates harmonics in a limited range (freq 2-12 kHz, default 7.5 kHz) and adds them, raising the top end differently from EQ. **How:** `aexciter=amount=3:drive=8.5:freq=3000` on a dull SFX (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aexciter, checked 2026-10-24]

## Envelopes, dynamics and synthesis building blocks

2221. **Cut an impact's tail with agate** — `agate` reduces signal below a threshold (range, ratio, attack/release in ms). **How:** `agate=threshold=0.05:range=0.001:attack=1:release=60` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#agate, checked 2026-10-24]
2222. **Gate a bed by another sound** — `sidechaingate` opens/closes the first input by the level of the second. **How:** `[bed][key]sidechaingate=threshold=0.05:range=0.1:attack=5:release=150` (both mono; tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sidechaingate, checked 2026-10-24]
2223. **compand hard limiter for stacked hits** — doc example "Hard limiter at −6 dB": `compand=attacks=0:points=-80/-80|-6/-6|20/-6`. **How:** after `amix` of layers (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#compand, checked 2026-10-24]
2224. **alimiter: set level=0** — `alimiter` uses lookahead (delay = attack) and by default auto-levels the output back to 0 dB (`level` enabled); disable it to keep your gain staging. **How:** `alimiter=limit=0.89:level=0` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#alimiter, checked 2026-10-24]
2225. **amix: weights and normalize=0** — `amix` normalizes inputs by default; for layered SFX with set levels use `weights` and `normalize=0` (doc example vocals 1, music 0.25). **How:** `amix=inputs=3:weights='1 0.7 0.5':normalize=0` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#amix, checked 2026-10-24]
2226. **Fade curves for SFX** — `afade` curves include tri (default), qsin, hsin, esin, log, exp, par, qua, cub, losi, desi and more; `silence` sets the end gain of a fade-out. **How:** natural-sounding tail: `afade=t=out:st=0.3:d=1.2:curve=exp`; partial fade `afade=t=out:st=1:d=1:silence=0.25` (both tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade-1, checked 2026-10-24]
2227. **Custom envelopes with volume eval=frame** — `volume` evaluates an expression per frame with `t`; doc example fades after 10 s over 5 s. **How:** `volume='if(lt(t,1),t,if(lt(t,4),1,max(1-(t-4),0)))':eval=frame` (tested here 2026-10-24); resolution is one audio frame (1024 samples by default for lavfi sources). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#volume, checked 2026-10-24]
2228. **aevalsrc chirps and exponential pitch** — `aevalsrc` evaluates per-sample expressions (`t`, `n`, `s`; functions from ffmpeg-utils like `exp`, `pow`, `mod`, `random`, `sgn`); a pitch law f(t) needs its integral as phase: linear f0+k·t → `sin(2*PI*(f0*t+k*t*t/2))`. **How:** `aevalsrc='0.5*sin(2*PI*(200*t+300*t*t))':d=3:s=44100` (200→2000 Hz; measured 390→1860 Hz across 6 windows, tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aevalsrc, checked 2026-10-24] [ffmpeg-utils docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-utils.html#Expression-Evaluation, checked 2026-10-24]
2229. **FM tones in aevalsrc (tested command only)** — phase modulation inside `sin()` gives FM sidebands. **How:** `aevalsrc='0.5*sin(2*PI*500*t+4*exp(-t*3)*sin(2*PI*700*t))*exp(-t*2)':d=2:s=44100` (tested here 2026-10-24; no claim about how it sounds). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#aevalsrc, checked 2026-10-24]
2230. **Heartbeat for tension** — a synth heartbeat: simple sine/triangle, low-pass for warmth, realistic tempo 60-100 BPM; layer soft noise or reverb for realism. **How:** `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i "aevalsrc='0.9*sin(2*PI*55*t)*(exp(-mod(t,0.9)*25)+0.7*gt(mod(t,0.9),0.25)*exp(-(mod(t,0.9)-0.25)*25))':d=3.6:s=44100" -af lowpass=f=150 heartbeat.wav` (~67 BPM two-thump pattern; tested here 2026-10-24). [SoundCy, https://soundcy.com/article/how-to-make-a-heartbeat-sound-on-a-synth, checked 2026-10-24]
2231. **Shape a sound with firequalizer curves** — `firequalizer` takes a gain curve via `gain_entry='entry(f,g);...'`. **How:** small-speaker shape `firequalizer=gain_entry='entry(0,-40);entry(250,-30);entry(500,0);entry(4000,0);entry(8000,-30)'` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#firequalizer, checked 2026-10-24]
2232. **Tilt a whole SFX darker/brighter** — `tiltshelf` boosts/cuts lows and does the opposite to highs around a centre frequency (default 3 kHz), like hi-fi tone controls. **How:** `tiltshelf=g=6:f=1500` (tested here 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tiltshelf, checked 2026-10-24]
