# Editing skills 02: transitions, camera moves and speed (#111-#200)

What each transition, camera move and speed change means, when to use it, and how to do it here (ffmpeg or HyperFrames). Every item has a source.
Researched 2026-10-24 via agent-reach (Exa) web search. Every ffmpeg command below was run once on a 640x360 test clip (testsrc2 + smptebars, ffmpeg in this container) and marked "(ran OK locally)".

## Transitions

111. **Cut by default** — most shot changes should be straight cuts, because a cut goes unnoticed while a transition pulls attention to itself, so use a transition only when you can name the reason. Here: in HyperFrames, hard cuts go inside a scene (punch-ins), and no more than a third of the joins between beats should be hard cuts. [Primer disparo, https://www.apovedam.com/en/free-video-editing-course/video-transitions.html] [Sharpy, https://sharpy.live/blog/effects-and-transitions-in-sharpy, 2026-10-18] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
112. **Cross dissolve for time or place** — overlap two shots so one fades into the other when time has passed, the location changes or a flashback starts. Here: `ffmpeg -i a.mp4 -i b.mp4 -filter_complex "[0:v][1:v]xfade=transition=fade:duration=0.5:offset=2.5[v]" -map "[v]" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolve_(filmmaking)] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade]
113. **Dissolve length** — scene dissolves usually last 1-2 s (24-48 frames), and short 6-12 frame dissolves can soften a jarring hard cut or jump cut. Here: set `xfade` `duration=` in seconds (0.2-0.4 for a soft cut, 1-2 for a scene change). [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolve_(filmmaking)] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade]
114. **Dissolve needs matching shots** — both images share the screen for a few frames, so their composition, lighting level and film grain should suit each other. Here: grade both clips the same way before the xfade (CLAUDE.md step 4: apply the winning grade to every clip). [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/transitions/dissolve.html] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a professional edit"]
115. **Leave handles** — a dissolve shows frames from after the outgoing shot's out point and before the incoming shot's in point, so trim each clip a little long. Here: with `xfade`, `offset` = length of the first clip minus `duration`, and the output runs for the two lengths added together minus `duration` (two 3 s clips with a 0.5 s fade gave 5.5 s; ran OK locally). [JeffAPierson.com, https://jeffapierson.com/av-theory.php?topic=what-a-transition-says] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade]
116. **xfade input rules** — both inputs must have a constant frame rate and the same resolution, pixel format, frame rate and timebase, or the filter fails. Here: run `fps=30,scale=W:H,format=yuv420p` on each input first. [ffmpeg 8.0 filter docs (ayosec mirror), https://ayosec.github.io/ffmpeg-filters-docs/8.0/Filters/Video/xfade.html]
117. **No dissolve inside dialogue** — a crossfade inside a dialogue scene looks like a mistake, but a few frames of crossfade on the audio is a different job: it prevents pops. Here: `acrossfade=d=0.5` next to `xfade`, e.g. `-filter_complex "[0:v][1:v]xfade=...[v];[0:a][1:a]acrossfade=d=0.5[a]"` (ran OK locally). [Descript, https://www.descript.com/blog/article/what-is-a-video-crossfade-effect, 2022-08-16] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossfade]
118. **Fade to black means an ending** — taking the picture away says separation: under 0.5 s is a dip (punctuation), about 1 s ends a scene, 2-3 s ends an act, and more than 3 s ends the film. Here: `-vf "fade=t=in:st=0:d=0.5,fade=t=out:st=2:d=1" -af "afade=t=in:d=0.5,afade=t=out:st=2:d=1"` (ran OK locally). [ZOOOP, https://zooop.ai/glossary/editing/fade-to-black] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#fade]
119. **Decide what the audio does over the black** — carrying the sound across a fade turns it into a pause, while cutting the sound with the picture makes the ending absolute. Pick one on purpose. Here: either keep the `afade` longer than the video fade, or end both together. [ZOOOP, https://zooop.ai/glossary/editing/fade-to-black]
120. **Fade to black between every shot** — this is the most common mistake in a first edit, like ending every sentence with a full stop and a blank page. Here: keep fades for the ends of sections. [JeffAPierson.com, https://jeffapierson.com/av-theory.php?topic=what-a-transition-says]
121. **Dip to black plus silence** — a dip to black is a full stop for a chapter break or a big time jump, and it builds the most anticipation when the music drops out at the same moment. Here: `xfade=transition=fadeblack` (ran OK locally). [Drone Filming Hertfordshire, https://www.dronefilminghertfordshire.co.uk/droning-on-blog/choosing-the-right-transition-between-cuts/] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade]
122. **Dip to white** — fading through white reads as memory, dream, a flashback or a moment of impact, and feels lighter and more upbeat than black. Here: `xfade=transition=fadewhite` between clips, or `fade=t=out:st=2.5:d=0.5:color=white` at the end of a clip (both ran OK locally). [Primer disparo, https://www.apovedam.com/en/free-video-editing-course/video-transitions.html] [Spotlight FX, https://spotlightfx.com/blog/how-to-add-transitions-in-premiere-pro-2025-guide, 2025-01-30] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#fade]
123. **Flash transition** — a very short burst of brightness like a camera flash, made from a white matte with opacity keyframes 0 → 100 → 0, to mark a change of time or place or to stress a moment. Here: `library/blocks/flash-warm.html` (warm radial white, opacity 0 → 0.85 in 0.2 s, then 0 in 0.45 s; the phone-clip-reel template drops it at a cut for 0.7 s). [Filmora, https://filmora.wondershare.com/adobe-premiere/how-to-create-flash-transition.html] [Tella, https://www.tella.com/definition/flash-transition] [repo: library/blocks/flash-warm.html] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
124. **Dip cut** — darken the outgoing frame by about 40% for 3 frames before a hard cut, so the cut reads as intended. Here: HyperFrames, a 3-frame opacity/brightness tween on the outgoing wrapper. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
125. **Whip pan shot in camera** — whip out of one shot and into the next in the same direction, then hide the cut inside the blur. This moves the viewer to another time or place. Here: put the cut on the blurriest frame of the two whips. [No Film School, https://nofilmschool.com/how-to-do-a-whip-pan, 2024-06-11]
126. **Whip pan faked in post** — move the outgoing clip off-screen over its last ~4 frames and bring the incoming clip in from the other side over ~4 frames, with directional blur 0 → 20 → 0 at the same angle. Here: the phone-clip-reel template tweens `x: -260, filter: blur(16px)` over 0.2 s on the way out and `x: 260 → 0`, blur 16 → 0 over 0.28 s on the way in; motion.md says a whip is a 0.13-0.3 s smear that lands blurred and sharpens in ~0.13 s. [xeremy, https://xere.my/tutorials/whip-pan-transition/] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
127. **Whip from a speed-up** — with no whip footage, speed up the last part of a clip by 500-4000% so any small camera movement turns into a blur, then cut. Here: in HyperFrames, a `rate` lane in `data-automation` (rate goes up to 10). [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/create-seamless-transitons-whip-pan/, 2018-04-05] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
128. **Zoom blur transition** — at the cut, scale up hard (Size 1.0 → 3.0 at the cut → back to 1.0) over a 12-frame span (6 frames before the cut, 6 after) with an S-curve and directional blur; it is widely used for YouTube, TikTok and fast commercials to keep viewers watching. Here: `xfade=transition=zoomin` as a quick ffmpeg version (ran OK locally). [Cutsio, https://cutsio.com/blog/zoom-transition-tutorial-in-davinci-resolve-smooth-zoom-blur-transition] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade]
129. **Zoom-through** — clip A scales up fast until it is all blurred pixels, then clip B appears mid-zoom and scales down to rest, as if travelling through one scene into the next. Here: the recipe that worked is A scale → 2.2 with blur 14 px over 0.3 s `power3.in`, then B settles from 1.7 (blur 12 px) to 1.22 over 0.45 s `power3.out`; the CLAUDE.md version settles from 2.0 to 1.7. [Disruptiv-e, https://www.disruptiv-e.com/article/how-to-create-a-smooth-zoom-transition-in-premiere-pro, 2025-07-10] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a professional edit"]
130. **Zoom through an object** — the camera tracks to a button with blur (0.6 s), then the button scales from ~8% to ~250% of the frame in 0.35 s with a strong ease-in, and its fill becomes the next frame. Here: HyperFrames, measured on the official films. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
131. **Light leak at the cut** — put an orange/red light-leak overlay above the cut in Screen blend mode, with its brightest point on the cut, so the flare "blinds" the viewer and they accept the new scene. Here: `library/blocks/light-leak-portrait.html` (screen-blended amber/red/gold layers); the template places it 0.6 s before a cut for 1.8 s; ffmpeg equivalent `blend=all_mode=screen` (ran OK locally). [ANFX, https://anfx.co/blog/complete-guide-to-video-overlays/, 2020-06-20] [repo: library/blocks/light-leak-portrait.html] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend]
132. **Match the leak to the light** — tint the leak toward the scene's light (warm at sunset), lower the saturation if it fights the grade, and mask faces and titles if it gets distracting. Here: change the block's gradient colours and opacity for each video. [CineUni, https://cineuni.com/guides/light-leak-overlays, 2026-08-16]
133. **Light leaks need a reason** — they fail as a generic transition pack and tint skin badly over faces. Use short bursts tied to a change in time, memory, energy or place. Here: one or two leaks per reel, never over the face. [We Design Motion, https://wedesignmotion.com/blog/productivity/video-overlays-explained-grain-dust-light-leaks-paper/, 2026-04-01]
134. **Object (mask) wipe** — an object or person must cover the whole frame top to bottom as it crosses, and a mask that follows it reveals the next shot; if it doesn't fill the frame, the wipe looks confusing. Here: HyperFrames, tween `clip-path` or a mask on an inner wrapper; the source time is untouched. [Premiere Bro, https://premierebro.com/premiere-in-post/justin-odisho-walk-by-transition-effect-custom-wipe-reveal-with-masking-in-adobe-premiere-pro-cc, 2017-02-03] [VideoStudio, https://www.videostudiopro.com/en/pages/video-masks/unique-transitions/] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
135. **Shoot for the mask wipe** — look for straight objects (doors, poles, chair arms) that start off-frame and cross the whole screen; a speed ramp at the crossing helps hide the mask. Here: plan it at the shoot; the geometric fallback is `xfade=transition=wipeleft` or `circleopen` (ran OK locally). [Mario So, https://www.mariosomedia.com/blog/maskingtransition, 2025-05-26] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade]
136. **Morph cut for talking heads** — after cutting out "umm"s and pauses, Morph Cut uses face tracking and optical-flow interpolation to smooth the jump cut so it looks like one take. Here: no ffmpeg equivalent found; on a single camera use a punch-in instead (Camera section). [Adobe Help, https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-video-effects/apply-video-transitions/morph-cut-overview.html, 2026-01-07]
137. **When morph cut works** — one subject in a talking-head shot, a fixed camera and a static background with no lighting changes; heavy hand or body movement gives blotchy results. It must span both sides of the edit and takes the default transition duration set in Preferences; Adobe recommends somewhat shorter durations, and 12-15 frames worked well in Larry Jordan's tests. Here: check that the footage meets these conditions before trying. [Adobe Blog, https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2015/06/18/morph-cut-tips-tricks-for-best-results, 2015-06-18] [Larry Jordan, https://larryjordan.com/articles/adobe-premiere-pro-cc-2105-morph-cut/, 2015-06-28]
138. **Glitch transition** — a 0.1-0.3 s burst of RGB channel split and displaced horizontal blocks, so the cut reads as a signal glitch. It suits tech, sci-fi and suspense. Here: `-vf "rgbashift=rh=8:bh=-8,noise=c0s=30:c0f=t"` on the 3-6 frames around the cut (ran OK locally). [Design Atlas, https://design.solhun.com/en/motion-graphics/glitch-transition/] [AInForce, https://www.ainforce.com/help/knowledgebase/use-case-33-how-and-when-to-use-glitch-transitions/, 2019-12-02] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#rgbashift]
139. **Sound bridge (J-cut / L-cut)** — let the next scene's sound start under the current picture (J-cut), or let the sound carry on over the next picture (L-cut), so the join feels less abrupt. Here: `[1:a]adelay=2000|2000[a1];[a0][a1]amix=inputs=2:duration=first` starts clip B's audio 2 s in, under clip A (ran OK locally). [Filmmakers Academy, https://www.filmmakersacademy.com/glossary/audio-bridge/, 2021-07-16] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-sound-bridge-definition/, 2023-08-06] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#adelay]
140. **Try a J/L-cut before a dissolve** — to smooth a join, try a sound transition first. A dialogue sound bridge plays the next scene's dialogue before the visual cut, over the image of the scene before. Here: same adelay/amix pattern; the voice placement tool is `library/tools/voice_over_music.py` (it places lines in numpy because `adelay` after `concat`+`atempo` silently fails in ffmpeg 6.1). [Primer disparo, https://www.apovedam.com/en/free-video-editing-course/video-transitions.html] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-sound-bridge-definition/, 2023-08-06] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Voice and songs with words"]
141. **Match dissolve** — dissolve between two images that keep the same composition (or share a shape or colour), showing change over time while the frame holds still. Here: line up the subjects (scale/x/y on the wrapper) then `xfade=transition=fade`. [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-dissolve-in-film-definition/, 2022-06-12] [CapCut, https://www.capcut.com/resource/dissolve-transition-in-video, 2025-06-16] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade]
142. **Match cut is a hard cut** — a real match cut joins two shots with a shared shape (graphic match), a continued movement (match on action) or a carried-over sound. If the images blend, it is a match dissolve, not a match cut. Here: plain cut, no xfade. [Filmdaft, https://filmdaft.com/the-best-match-cut-examples-in-film-10-genius-transitions-from-cinema-history/, 2026-06-24] [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/match-cut.html]
143. **Cut on action** — start an action in one shot and finish it in the next, so the movement hides the cut. Here: HyperFrames rule, cut at the fastest frame of a move with the same direction and speed on both sides. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_on_action] [BBC Bitesize, https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zxdmmnb/revision/2, 2017-01-30] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
144. **Morph between similar images** — a morph reshapes the first image into the second; it is showy and works best when the two images are similar (the same face at two ages, a building by day and night). Here: HyperFrames morph, one container widens into the next (~110 → ~880 px in 0.2 s `power3.out`) with horizontal blur 12 → 0. [davinci, https://getdavinci.ai/types-of-camera-transitions/, 2026-10-12] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
145. **Push, slide and wipe sparingly** — these moving transitions feel busy back to back and recall retro TV and sci-fi, so use them in moderation. Here: `xfade=transition=slideleft` / `wipeleft` / `coverleft` / `revealleft` (all ran OK locally). [Spotlight FX, https://spotlightfx.com/blog/how-to-add-transitions-in-premiere-pro-2025-guide, 2025-01-30] [Primer disparo, https://www.apovedam.com/en/free-video-editing-course/video-transitions.html] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade]
146. **xfade catalogue** — ffmpeg has about 60 built-in types (fade, dissolve, fadeblack, fadewhite, fadegrays, wipe*/slide*/smooth*/cover*/reveal*, circleopen/close, radial, pixelize, hblur, zoomin, squeezeh/v and more); `ffmpeg -h filter=xfade` lists what your build has. Here: 15 types tested in this container: fade, dissolve, fadeblack, fadewhite, wipeleft, slideleft, smoothleft, circleopen, zoomin, hblur, pixelize, radial, fadegrays, coverleft, revealleft (all ran OK locally). [FFmpeg source, https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/master/libavfilter/vf_xfade.c] [salivity, https://salivity.github.io/ffmpeg/article/how-to-list-f-fmpeg-xfade-transition-types]
147. **Overuse is the beginner tell** — putting a transition between every clip (spins, zooms, page curls) draws attention to the edit itself; if a transition has no specific story job, delete it and cut. Here: a review pass that checks for accidental transitions on the exported file. [VideoEditingTips.pro, https://videoeditingtips.pro/video-editing-tips-for-beginners/common-video-editing-mistakes-beginners/, 2026-06-21] [Reddit r/finalcutpro, https://www.reddit.com/r/finalcutpro/comments/1lils20/beginner_editor_here_how_do_i_use_transitions/, 2025-06-23]
148. **3-5 transition kinds, each with a cause** — in one film, use 3-5 kinds of transition, each caused by something on screen, and mirror the easing across the join (`power3.in` out, `power3.out` in) or the speeds won't match. Here: HyperFrames review rule. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md] [Vimeo, https://vimeo.com/fr/blog/post/adding-video-transitions, 2025-10-01]
149. **Transitions on the music** — a swoosh on a cymbal swell, a flash on an impact, a slow dissolve on a held note and a hard cut on a staccato hit. Here: place them on the beat grid (frames per beat = fps × 60 / BPM, computed from the beat index, never by adding a rounded beat again and again). [Beat2Cut, https://beat2cut.com/blog/beat-sync-video-editing-complete-guide/, 2026-01-20] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]

## Camera moves & reframing

150. **Punch-in on a jump cut** — after cutting filler out of a single-camera talking head, punch in 10-25% on the kept side so the head jerk disappears and one camera feels like two angles. Change framing only on a cut and keep the eyes in the upper third. Here: `-vf "crop=iw/1.15:ih/1.15,scale=640:360"` for a 15% punch (ran OK locally); in HyperFrames, tween `scale` on a wrapper, never on the media element. [Vyra, https://www.usevyra.com/prompts/talking-head-punch-ins, 2026-09-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#crop] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
151. **Punch-in timing** — either pop in and back out, or creep in slowly before the cut and pop out after it; use it in moderation. Trim dead air and retakes first, then decide where punch-ins go. Here: punch-ins come after the silence-cut pass. [Screencasting.com, https://screencasting.com/how-to-screencast/watch/cover-cut] [Monty, https://monty.fast/blog/how-to-edit-talking-head-videos/, 2026-10-08]
152. **Digital zoom headroom from 4K** — delivering 1080p from a 4K source lets you punch in to 200% at full delivery resolution, reframe after the fact, stabilise (which costs a 5-15% crop) and crop to 9:16 without going soft. Here: shoot or keep the highest-resolution source; crop in ffmpeg before downscaling. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/resolution-and-the-frame] [Duke DDMC, https://sites.duke.edu/ddmc/2014/01/23/editing-4k-content-in-hd-workflow/, 2014-01-23]
153. **Scale from the native source** — Resolve sizes a 4K clip in a 1080 project from the native 4K pixels, in one step (not from an HD copy), so a zoom of up to 200% uses the native pixels; soft, mushy zooms come from scaling in the wrong order. Here: in ffmpeg, scale up first and crop second when filling a larger frame. [Blackmagic Forum, https://forum.blackmagicdesign.com/viewtopic.php?t=82530, 2018-11-24] [Creative Video Tips, https://creativevideotips.com/tutorials/reframing-in-davinci-resolve, 2025-03-13] [kinopipe, https://kinopipe.com/ffmpeg/crop-video]
154. **Ease keyframed zooms** — ease out of the static start of a zoom and ease into its static end, so slow zooms and pans don't start or stop with a jerk. Here: HyperFrames camera moves use `power2.inOut`/`sine.inOut` (the template uses `sine.inOut` drifts across each bar). [DPReview Forums, https://www.dpreview.com/forums/threads/4k-to-fhd-in-premiere.4781185/post-67976454, 2024-10-21] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
155. **Ken Burns = two eased crop rectangles** — the effect is a crop with a start setting and an end setting, eased out at the start and in at the end to mimic friction and inertia. Here: `-loop 1 -i still.png -vf "scale=3200:-2,zoompan=z='min(zoom+0.0015,1.2)':x='iw/2-(iw/zoom/2)':y='ih/2-(ih/zoom/2)':d=150:s=640x360:fps=30" -t 5` (ran OK locally); in HyperFrames, tween scale and x/y on a wrapper. [Apple Final Cut Pro User Guide, https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/pan-and-zoom-clips-verb8e5de9c/10.5/mac/10.15.6] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#zoompan] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
156. **Ken Burns amounts** — change the scale by 10-20% (e.g. 100 → 110-120%): below ~8% the move is invisible, and above ~30% people watch the camera instead of the photo. Here: zoompan `min(zoom+step,1.2)` caps the zoom at 20%. [43frames, https://www.43frames.com/blog/ken-burns-effect, 2026-09-04]
157. **Ken Burns to guide the eye** — when there is no video, zoom slowly toward the subject of interest and pan from one subject to the next (e.g. face to face across a team photo), coming to rest on the one that matters. Here: animate zoompan `x`/`y`, or the wrapper's x/y, toward the target. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Burns_effect]
158. **zoompan jitter fix** — zoompan snaps its crop to whole pixels on every frame, so slow moves shudder; upscale the image a lot before zoompan (or `trunc()` the x/y expressions) and never downscale it first. Here: the `scale=3200:-2` before zoompan in the command above. [Super User, https://superuser.com/questions/1112617/ffmpeg-smooth-zoompan-with-no-jiggle, 2016-08-11] [glide-ffmpeg, https://github.com/Loomos-hub/glide-ffmpeg] [still-motion, https://github.com/tomastimelock/still-motion]
159. **2.5D parallax** — cut a photo into foreground and background layers (fill the hole behind the subject) and move them at different rates, or through a 3D camera, to fake depth. This differs from Ken Burns, which moves the whole picture. Here: one wrapper per layer, each with its own x/scale tween (the same wrapper rule as Ken Burns). [Motion Array, https://blog.motionarray.com/learn/after-effects/animate-flat-2d-images-after-effects/, 2021-02-09] [Adobe Help, https://helpx.adobe.com/ph_fil/after-effects/how-to/animate-picture.html] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
160. **3D Ken Burns from one image** — research methods estimate depth from a single photo and synthesise a camera move with parallax between two crop windows. Here: reference only; no tool for it in the repo. [Niklaus et al., arXiv, https://arxiv.org/html/1909.05483v1, 2019]
161. **Shake for impact** — a short camera shake behind a punch, crash or other action event adds energy and increases the impact. Here: the phone-clip-reel template lands a cut with a yoyo shake (`x: -16, y: 10 → x: 12, y: -8`, 0.06 s, `repeat: 3`); GSAP's "rough" ease isn't in core GSAP, so use the yoyo; in ffmpeg, `crop=iw-24:ih-24:12+12*(random(1)-0.5):12+12*(random(2)-0.5),scale=640:360` (ran OK locally). [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/features/shake-effect-premiere-pro.html] [hathibelagal-dev gist, https://gist.github.com/hathibelagal-dev/65fdbdd84cfafbba2845ce9d3a9b6483, 2025-12-30] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Client phone-video edits"]

170. **Rule of thirds when reframing** — put the subject on a third line or an intersection rather than dead centre, with an interviewee's eyes on the top third line and the horizon on the upper or lower third. 171. **Headroom** — too little space above the head feels cramped, and too much leaves the person sunk at the bottom. ## Speed

174. **Slow motion marks what matters** — slow-mo tells the audience this moment matters more than the ones around it (triumph, loss, impact, a first look); used too often, it loses its power. Here: `setpts=2.0*PTS` for 0.5x (ran OK locally). [ScreenWeaver, https://www.screenweaver.ai/film-techniques/time-motion/slow-motion] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#setpts]
175. **Slow-mo for emotion and steadier handheld** — use it sparingly for emotional scenes (celebrating, despair); it also slows the camera's own movement, so handheld footage looks steadier. Here: CLAUDE.md recipe, 1.5x for setup and slow-mo for emotion. [Studio 91 Media, https://www.studio91media.co.uk/2021/04/30/how-to-use-slow-motion-to-create-emotion/, 2021-04-30] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a professional edit"]
176. **Slow-mo lifts short-form** — in field data and six experiments, slow motion raised likes, views and brand liking of short videos, most of all for pleasant clips with complex movements that are hard to see at normal speed. Here: slow the complex action moments, not static talking. [Stuppy, Landwehr & McGraw, Journal of Marketing Research, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00222437231179187, 2023-05-16]
177. **Shoot for the slowdown** — 60 fps for moderate slow-mo (40-60% speed), 120 fps or more for 25% or below, and a shutter of twice the frame rate (180° rule) for natural blur. Here: check the real fps first; WhatsApp clips can claim 28.4 fps while really being ~12 fps (`ffprobe -show_entries frame=pts_time`). [EditorVault, https://editorvault.online/guides/speed-ramp] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Client phone-video edits"]
178. **Frame sampling vs frame blending vs optical flow** — duplicating frames (sampling) looks choppy, blending mixes neighbouring frames' pixels for smoother playback, and optical flow analyses pixel motion to build new in-between frames. Here: duplicate `setpts=2.0*PTS`, blend `minterpolate=fps=60:mi_mode=blend,setpts=2.0*PTS,fps=30`, flow `mi_mode=mci` (all ran OK locally). [Apple Final Cut Pro X manual, https://apple.manymanuals.com/software/final-cut-pro-x-10-0-9/user-manual-31887/292] [Boris FX, https://borisfx.com/blog/optical-flow-vs-frame-blending-main-difference/, 2024-02-29] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#minterpolate]
179. **minterpolate settings** — `mi_mode` takes dup, blend or mci (motion-compensated); with mci, `mc_mode=aobmc` adapts the block weighting to how reliable the motion vectors are, which reduces over-smoothing. Here: `minterpolate=fps=60:mi_mode=mci:mc_mode=aobmc:vsbmc=1,setpts=PTS/0.5,fps=30` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg 8.1 filter docs (ayosec mirror), https://ayosec.github.io/ffmpeg-filters-docs/8.1/Filters/Video/minterpolate.html] [ffmpeg-engineering-handbook, https://github.com/endcycles/ffmpeg-engineering-handbook/blob/main/docs/advanced/speed.md]
180. **Repo slow-mo recipe** — `ffmpeg -ss S -t T -i in.mp4 -an -vf "minterpolate=fps=<30/rate>:mi_mode=mci:mc_mode=aobmc:vsbmc=1,setpts=PTS/<rate>,fps=30,trim=0:3.333" -r 30 -crf 15 clip.mp4` gave smooth slow-mo on a phone video; fast hand motion smears a little. Here: tested in the repo. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a professional edit"]
181. **Interpolate low-fps clips too** — interpolate every low-fps phone clip to 30 fps (`minterpolate ...:scd=none`), not only the slow-mo ones, or the 1x shots look choppy. Here: tested on a client reel. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Client phone-video edits"] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#minterpolate]
182. **Optical flow limits** — interpolation breaks into warping or "bubbling" artifacts where there is occlusion, large motion or a sudden brightness change (e.g. a hand passing close to the lens); the further apart the real frames, the more it has to invent. Here: on such shots, use a shorter slow-mo, frame blend, or a cut. [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/slow-mo-blues-fixing-bad-optical-flow-retimes/, 2020-03-09] [ACM TOMM survey, https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3556544, 2023-05-11] [Reddit r/premiere, https://www.reddit.com/r/premiere/comments/uib9ou/im_new_to_premiere_how_do_i_fix_this/, 2022-05-04]
183. **Pick the retime engine for deep slow-mo** — Resolve offers Nearest, Frame Blend and Optical Flow, and below ~50% speed you need Enhanced Better or Speed Warp, or slow-mo stutters. Here: in ffmpeg the motion-compensated mode is `minterpolate` `mi_mode=mci`, with `mc_mode=aobmc` (adaptive, to reduce oversmoothing). [Pulse Edit, https://pulseedit.com/blog/speed-ramps-on-the-beat-davinci-resolve.html, 2026-08-29] [YouTube, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gdevOduygro, 2019] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#minterpolate]
184. **Speed ramp** — change speed within a clip with time-remapping keyframes (e.g. start at 250-300%, then drop into slow motion). Here: HyperFrames `rate` lane in `data-automation` on the media (`t` in clip seconds, `v` 0.1-10), which overrides `data-playback-rate`. [Motion Array, https://motionarray.com/learn/premiere-pro/premiere-pro-speed-ramping-tutorial/, 2021-09-09] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
185. **Smooth the ramp curve** — drag the speed-point handles out so the change eases instead of snapping. A ramp looks choppy when the source fps is too low for the slowdown, frame blending is off, or the ease handles are too short. Here: space the automation points over several frames, and interpolate low-fps sources first. [Video with Jens, https://videowithjens.com/how-to-speed-ramp-in-davinci-resolve/, 2022-09-14] [DJordanMedia, https://djordanmedia.com/blogs/news/after-effects-speed-ramp-tutorial, 2026-04-21]
186. **Ramp into the hit** — for an impact, ramp into slow motion and snap back, landing the slowest frame on the beat; cut on phrases of 2/4/8 beats with frames per beat = (60/BPM) × fps. Here: put the rate dip's lowest point on the computed beat frame. [iart-ai motion-design-skills, https://github.com/iart-ai/motion-design-skills/blob/main/skills/beat-sync-editing/SKILL.md] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
187. **Ramps to the music** — use slow motion in quiet sections, a ramp into the beat drop and a freeze on key beats, and don't cut on every beat (it feels frantic). Here: CLAUDE.md cuts on bars (72 BPM: every 3.333 s). [Beat2Cut, https://beat2cut.com/blog/beat-sync-video-editing-complete-guide/, 2026-01-20] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a professional edit"]
188. **Speed up with sound** — scale the video timestamps and change the audio tempo by the inverse amount; `atempo` has a lower limit of 0.5, so chain it for bigger slowdowns. Here: `-filter_complex "[0:v]setpts=0.5*PTS[v];[0:a]atempo=2.0[a]"` (ran OK locally). [Super User, https://superuser.com/questions/1247462/speed-up-video-x1-5-but-keep-all-frames] [ffmpeg-engineering-handbook, https://github.com/endcycles/ffmpeg-engineering-handbook/blob/main/docs/advanced/speed.md] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#atempo]
189. **"Speed this up" means shorten the beat** — when asked to speed things up, speed the content and shorten its beat by the same amount, so later beats move earlier; when told "too fast", slow every zoom and pan to 0.7x. Here: HyperFrames feedback table. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
190. **Time-lapse from video** — `setpts=PTS/10` drops frames to reach 10x; to keep every frame, set a higher output rate (input fps × factor). Here: `-vf "setpts=PTS/10" -r 30` (ran OK locally). [Stack Overflow, https://stackoverflow.com/questions/24270501/change-mp4-video-duration-using-ffmpeg] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#setpts]
191. **Time-lapse interval math** — interval = event duration ÷ (clip length × playback fps), e.g. 2 hours into 10 s at 24 fps = 240 frames at 30 s intervals (720x). The interval must be longer than the shutter, and shutter ≈ interval ÷ 2 gives natural blur. Here: work out the interval before the shoot. [CineMath, https://www.cinemath.app/features/time-lapse] [Tools for Film, https://www.toolsforfilm.com/tools/time-lapse] [AeroTimelapse, https://aerotimelapse.com/timelapse-interval-calculator/]
192. **Interval by subject** — 1-2 s for fast subjects like traffic and crowds, 30-60 s for clouds and sunsets; shoot on a steady tripod. Here: same calculator. [AeroTimelapse, https://aerotimelapse.com/timelapse-interval-calculator/] [Adobe Premiere Elements Help, https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere-elements/desktop/special-effects-basics/time-lapse-best-practices.html]
193. **Hyperlapse** — set shutter, aperture and ISO manually so nothing auto-adjusts (an exposure change between shots makes the video flicker), realign to one fixed anchor point at every step, and keep the steps the same size (uneven steps make the speed look jerky). Here: assemble the stills with `-framerate 30 -i %d.jpg`, then stabilise or crop. [Spotlight FX, https://spotlightfx.com/blog/hyperlapse-explained, 2026-04-13] [Shotkit, https://shotkit.com/hyperlapse-video/, 2023-09-19]
194. **Freeze frame uses** — hold one frame to focus attention (with on-screen text for product details or names), as a story beat (a character takes a photo), or for an emotional or comic stop. Here: mid-clip freeze `-vf "loop=loop=45:size=1:start=45,setpts=N/FRAME_RATE/TB"` (ran OK locally). [Motion Array, https://motionarray.com/learn/premiere-pro/freeze-frames-in-premiere-pro/, 2021-03-05] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#loop]
195. **Pick the freeze frame** — choose a frame just after the peak of a move that is sharp and well composed, keep the hold short unless text needs reading, and slow down before the stop or pair the freeze with silence. Here: export the exact frame as a PNG first. [Rushilo, https://rushilo.com/blog/freeze-frame, 2026-10-22] [Filmora, https://filmora.wondershare.com/basic-video-editing/freeze-frame.html]
196. **Freeze-frame ending** — ending on a freeze (The 400 Blows, 1959) leaves the story unresolved and fixes the character's expression. A freeze can pull viewers out of the story by calling attention to the fact that it is only a movie, which matters least at the very end of the film. Here: end-hold `-vf "tpad=stop_mode=clone:stop_duration=2"` (ran OK locally). [BFI, https://www.bfi.org.uk/film/8803865c-9135-5c8f-b36f-6e50ffcb4873/the-400-blows] [The Take, https://the-take.com/read/ask-the-professor-why-does-a400-blowsa-end-on-a-freeze-frame-what-was-influential-about-it, 2016-09-09] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-freeze-frame-the-best-examples-why-they-work/, 2024-04-28] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tpad]
197. **Seamless freeze in HyperFrames** — end the last slow-mo clip exactly on the chosen frame, export that frame as a PNG and place it with the same framing so the freeze is invisible. A freeze mid-source needs the still exported first and placed as its own clip. Here: tested in the repo. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a professional edit"] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
198. **Reverse for replay, rewind or the uncanny** — reversed footage works for instant replays, going back in time, spilt liquid or shattered glass reassembling, and unsettling moments (Memento's bullets flying back into the gun). Here: `-vf reverse -af areverse` (ran OK locally). [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/video-reverser.html] [Boris FX, https://borisfx.com/blog/how-to-put-a-video-in-reverse/, 2024-06-28] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#reverse]
199. **Reverse uses RAM: trim first** — `reverse` and `areverse` hold the whole input in memory before writing the first frame, so cut a short segment first (`-ss 0 -t 4`) or reverse pieces and join them in reverse order. Re-encoding is required. Here: same command with `-ss`/`-t` (ran OK locally). [kinopipe, https://kinopipe.com/ffmpeg/reverse-video] [FFmpeg Cookbook, https://ffmpeg-cookbook.com/en/articles/reverse-video/, 2026-09-02] [Stack Exchange, https://video.stackexchange.com/questions/17738/how-to-use-ffmpeg-command-for-reverse-video, 2016-02-22]
200. **Boomerang and reverse ramps** — play a clip forward then backward for a boomerang loop.
