Skills for on-screen text, animated graphics and layered/keyed shots, each with the HyperFrames or ffmpeg way to do it here.

## Captions & typography

381. **Characters per line** — keep a subtitle line to about 42 characters and at most two lines, usually one line unless it is too long. Here: size each caption group with `window.__hyperframes.fitTextFontSize(text, { fontFamily, fontWeight, maxWidth })` (maxWidth 1600 landscape, 900 portrait). [Netflix Timed Text Style Guide, https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
382. **Reading speed limit (CPS)** — set each subtitle's on-screen time from its length: Netflix allows up to 20 characters per second for adult programmes and 17 for children's, counting spaces and punctuation. Here: hold a line you must read about 0.5 s plus 0.3 s a word, and the film's last line at least 2 s. [Netflix Timed Text Style Guide, https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide] [Netflix, https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001352212-How-is-reading-speed-measured-Do-punctuation-and-spaces-count] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
383. **Words-per-minute ceiling** — UK guidance (Ofcom, quoted by BBC R&D) says subtitles should not go over 160-180 WPM, and over 200 WPM is hard for many viewers to follow; when trimming for speed, cut and condense rather than paraphrase. Here: split long lines into more groups instead of shortening their hold. [BBC R&D, https://www.bbc.co.uk/rd/blog/2015-09-how-fast-should-subtitles-be, 2015-09-11] [Netflix Timed Text Style Guide, https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
384. **Line breaks and pyramid shape** — break two-line subtitles after punctuation or before conjunctions and prepositions, never between an article and its noun or a first and last name, and prefer a bottom-heavy pyramid. Here: break caption groups at a sentence end, a pause of 150 ms or more, or the word limit. [Netflix, https://studiopartner.netflix.net/studio/us-english-timed-text-style-guide] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
385. **Timing to audio** — start a subtitle on the first frame of speech (within 1-2 frames), keep it about half a second after the audio ends, and leave at least 2 frames between events. Here: a group opens at or before its first word's start and closes at or after its last word's end, never at exactly 0 s (start 0.1-0.3 s in). [Netflix Subtitle Timing Guidelines, https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051554394-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-Subtitle-Timing-Guidelines] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
386. **Don't cross shot changes** — if extending a subtitle's out-time would cross a cut that the dialogue doesn't cross, end it on the cut instead; close tiny gaps by "chaining" or keep gaps of half a second or more. Here: one group visible at a time, with a hard kill (`tl.set(group, { opacity: 0, visibility: "hidden" }, end)`) after each exit. [Netflix Subtitle Timing Guidelines, https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051554394-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-Subtitle-Timing-Guidelines] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
387. **Word-level timestamps first** — word-by-word captions need real per-word timing from transcription; splitting a line evenly over its duration drifts visibly. Here: `npx hyperframes transcribe <file> --json` (pass `--model small --language <code>` for non-English, since `small.en` translates), match transcript words to caption words by position, and drop Whisper's "Thank you." over a final silence. [iart-ai caption-animation skill, https://github.com/iart-ai/tiktok-video-skills/blob/main/skills/caption-animation/SKILL.md] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
388. **Word-by-word (karaoke) highlight** — keep a short phrase on screen and change the active word's colour, outline or box as it is spoken, so the eye has a moving target; the usual recipe is bold sans, 3-4 words a line, centred. Here: install a caption style from `npx hyperframes catalog --tag caption-style --json` (e.g. `caption-clip-wipe`) and restyle it. [OpenClip, https://openclip.app/use-cases/karaoke-captions] [CaptionFit, https://captionfit.com/features/karaoke-captions] [Capto, https://capto.video/styles/karaoke] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes-registry/SKILL.md]
389. **Karaoke in ASS with \k tags** — put `{\kNN}` (centiseconds) before each syllable or word; `\K`/`\kf` sweep-fill it and `\ko` outlines it, and the file burns in with ffmpeg's `subtitles`/`ass` filter. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "subtitles=k.ass" out.mp4` with a line like `{\k60}Har {\k60}lafz {\k70}chamakta {\k60}hai` (ran OK locally). [Aegisub ASS Override Tags, https://aegisub.org/docs/latest/ass_tags/] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#subtitles-1] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#ass]
390. **Burn an SRT with a forced style** — when you only have an SRT, style it at burn time with `force_style` (font size, outline, margin). Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "subtitles=s.srt:force_style='Fontsize=28,Outline=3,BorderStyle=1,MarginV=30'" out.mp4` (ran OK locally); HyperFrames can also write a sidecar with `transcribe --to srt|vtt`. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#subtitles-1] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
391. **Per-word blur-in captions** — reveal each word from blur to sharp with a short stagger, over a bottom gradient, with one gold accent word per line. Here: the phone-video recipe uses a 0.12 s stagger; the template tweens `.w` spans `{ opacity: 0, y: 26, filter: "blur(10px)" }` to sharp over 0.34 s `power3.out`, stagger 0.1, and marks the accent with `.gold` (#ffc94a). [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a "professional" edit"] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
392. **Caption size** — bold sans at roughly 7-9% of frame height reads best on phones; thin or light weights vanish over busy video. Here: base 78 px, minimum 42 px, step 2 in `fitTextFontSize`; the reel template sets Plus Jakarta 800 at 78 px. [OpenClip, https://openclip.app/guides/caption-styling-guide] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
393. **Safe zones for Reels/TikTok UI** — every short-video app draws its caption, account name and buttons over the video, so keep text and logos out of those edges. Here: portrait captions sit about 600-700 px from the bottom, inside the 80% title-safe box, never over the speaker's face. [Fluencify, https://fluencify.io/tools/safe-zone-templates] [Meta Business Help, https://www.facebook.com/business/help/980593475366490] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
394. **Measured UI masks (1080x1920)** — CapQA's masks give TikTok organic a 350 px bottom bar and side buttons from x 918, Reels ads a 330 px bottom and side buttons from x 996, Shorts ads a 345 px bottom with Subscribe from y 1480; SafeZoneCheck gives TikTok margins of 131 px top, 367 px bottom, 120 px each side. Here: keep the caption band roughly y 700-1350 as one guide suggests, and check a snapshot against these numbers. [CapQA, https://capqa.app/check] [CapQA masks, https://capqa.app/guides/social-media-safe-zones/masks.csv] [SafeZoneCheck, https://safezonecheck.com/] [SoCaptions, https://socaptions.com/blog/social-media-subtitle-best-practices, 2026-06-23]
395. **Lower thirds: what they are** — a name/title graphic in the lower region of the frame, inside the title-safe area, so context arrives without pulling attention off the action. Here: a lower third is the bottom 30% of the frame; lower-third scenes need a transparent root or they hide the speaker; registry block `lt-clean-bar`. [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/lower-third-graphics.html] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/overlays.md] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes-registry/SKILL.md]
396. **Lower thirds: design rules** — keep them to one concise line (names, chapters), on brand fonts and colours, and readable over anything with a solid background or shadow rather than bare white text. Here: cards fade in over 0.4 s (`power2.out`) and out over 0.35 s (`power2.in`); glass over bright footage needs a dark scrim and a base like `rgba(15,16,22,.78)` with `backdrop-filter: blur(22px)`. [Vimeo, https://vimeo.com/blog/post/what-is-lower-thirds, 2026-08-10] [IBM Video, https://blog.video.ibm.com/streaming-video-tips/creating-lower-thirds-for-live-video/, 2018-08-09] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/overlays.md]
397. **Lower-third template** — define field types (name+role, location+date, fact, CTA) with set lengths, and one master template fixing typeface, size, safe margins, max width, entrance/hold/exit and long-name behaviour per aspect ratio. Here: portrait type is landscape x 1.3 and padding drops to 24-36 px; set per-mount text with `data-variable-values` (`add --vars '{"title":"…"}'`). [Polimake, https://polimake.com/en-us/kb/lower-thirds] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/overlays.md] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes-registry/SKILL.md]
398. **Lower third with ffmpeg drawtext** — draw text over a semi-opaque box for a quick lower third. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "drawtext=fontfile=/usr/share/fonts/truetype/dejavu/DejaVuSans-Bold.ttf:text='Lower third':x=40:y=h-90:fontsize=36:fontcolor=white:box=1:boxcolor=black@0.6:boxborderw=12" out.mp4` (ran OK locally; give `fontfile` the full path: with a bare `DejaVuSans-Bold.ttf` that is not in the working folder ffmpeg silently drew regular DejaVu Sans instead, measured here on 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawtext-1] [ffmpeg-engineering-handbook, https://github.com/endcycles/ffmpeg-engineering-handbook/blob/main/docs/advanced/subtitles.md]
399. **Font pairing by contrast** — pair a bold display face for headings with a clean neutral face for body text; two equally loud display fonts, or two near-identical body fonts, look accidental. Here: the reel template pairs Anton (titles) with Plus Jakarta Sans (captions) via `@font-face`. [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/app-font-pairing] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
400. **Proven pairs, 2-3 styles max** — examples: Montserrat + Open Sans, Playfair Display + Source Sans Pro, Oswald + Lato, Merriweather Sans + Abril Fatface; keep a design to 2-3 complementary text styles and keep the fonts consistent through the video. Here: declare `font-display` as a root composition variable so the pair can be re-branded from Studio. [VideoScribe, https://blog.videoscribe.co/font-pairing-text-animation-guide, 2025-08-27] [wave.video, https://wave.video/blog/font-pairs/, 2019-10-16] [Filmora, https://filmora.wondershare.com/customize-video/font-pairings.html] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/look.md]
401. **Contrast ratio** — text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast against its background (3:1 for large text), and on-screen video text should meet 4.5:1 sized for the smallest screen. Here: light text on a bright wall fails: use a deep colour (#6e0c20) with a light glow, or captions over the darker lower frame with a bottom gradient veil. [W3C WCAG 2.2, https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/, 2024-12-12] [UC Davis, https://communicationsguide.ucdavis.edu/departments/video/best-practices/accessibility/text-on-screen, 2023-01-06] [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
402. **Box vs stroke vs shadow** — a 60-80% black box is the most reliable, a 2-4 px contrasting outline is next and is the short-form look, a tight shadow (2-4 px offset, 4-8 px blur, 60-80%) is softest; an outline works whatever side the bright area is on, a shadow does not. Here: ffmpeg `drawtext=...:borderw=4:bordercolor=black:shadowx=3:shadowy=3:shadowcolor=black@0.7` (ran OK locally); in HyperFrames plain subtitles over footage brighter than 60 get a 30-40% scrim sized to the text, never a full-width bar. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/type-on-moving-pictures] [Blitzcut, https://blitzcutai.com/blog/caption-background-vs-outline-vs-shadow, 2026-05-20] [KiroClip, https://lite.kiroclip.com/blog/outline-or-shadow-on-subtitles] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#drawtext-1] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
403. **Text behind the speaker** — stack video, then captions, then a cutout of the person so the big word sits behind the head, best 30-55% covered. Here: `npx hyperframes remove-background` for the cutout, `data-layout-allow-occlusion` on it, and judge from `snapshot`; measure word boxes against the matte per caption window. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
404. **Kinetic typography basics** — animated text sets tone and carries the message; timing gives energy or weight, easing makes it natural, and hierarchy gives the main message the biggest motion; text should stay readable at least 0.5 s after it settles. Here: registry "Title cards and kinetic type" blocks (`canopy-part-title`, `code-slice-hero`) and components like `blur-in`, `bottom-up-letters`. [IK Agency, https://www.ikagency.com/graphic-design-typography/kinetic-typography/, 2024-08-21] [CUNY OpenLab MMP 460, https://openlab.bmcc.cuny.edu/mmp460/kinetic-typography/] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes-registry/SKILL.md]
405. **Sync type to audio with markers** — kinetic type pieces are built by syncing animation to the voice/music, using layer markers and precomps for re-use, and type animators that animate one offset across letters/words/lines. Here: take hit times from `npx hyperframes beats --json` (minus the mount's `data-start`) and put titles on a beat grid computed from each beat's index. [School of Motion, https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/kinetic-typography-after-effects-part-1, 2018-01-05] [Motion Tutorials, http://www.motiontutorials.net/blog-tutorials/after-effects-type-animators, 2015-11-10] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes-registry/SKILL.md] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
406. **Vary title entrances** — don't fade-and-slide-up every title: per word blur(6px)->0 in 0.13 s, 0.067 s apart; grey-to-ink per word; the whole line blurring in 8 px -> 0; per letter for a name; or a slot-machine word roll about 0.1 s a roll. Here: these are the title entrances listed in the HyperFrames motion reference; a landed title stays dead still. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
407. **Masked text reveal** — give each word/line an overflow-hidden box and slide it up into view (often from blur), staggering 0.05-0.1 s with a hard ease-out, so type looks set rather than faded in. Here: pad the mask about 0.2 em above and 0.25 em below (pulled back with negative margins) so descenders and overshoot aren't clipped; in HyperFrames tween `clip-path` or a mask on an inner wrapper. [Impractical, https://impractical.ai/motion/masked-text-reveal] [vawe, https://vawe.dev/video-components/mask-rise] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
408. **Typewriter text** — reveal characters one by one; a fixed interval looks robotic because nobody types at a monotonous rate, so vary the gaps. Here: put the reveals on the GSAP timeline, not `setInterval`: anything measured in `onUpdate`, `requestAnimationFrame` or clock time stutters or drifts between renders. [Motion.dev, https://motion.dev/docs/text-animation] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
409. **Title size and accent** — titles at 4-9% of frame height in one or two lines, quiet lines 2.5-4%, hero words filling 60-90% of the width, one accent-coloured word that carries meaning. Here: don't tween `letterSpacing` (lint), use `scaleX`; the reel's "MODE" line settles from scaleX 1.14 to 1. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Client phone-video edits"] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
410. **Title on the first frame** — the first frame is the thumbnail, so put a title or key image on screen at 0 s. Here: the reel template's scene 1 title is visible at t=0 and exits with a zoom-through. [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]

## Motion graphics

411. **Why easing** — nothing in nature moves at constant speed; easing controls acceleration and deceleration so motion stops feeling mechanical. Here: linear only for rotation, progress bars and deliberate constant speed. [Figma Learn, https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/41238219562007-Motion-design-fundamentals-Easing] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
412. **Ease-out for arrivals, ease-in for exits** — ease-out (fast start, gentle stop) suits things entering; ease-in (slow start, fast end) suits things leaving (Figma); ease-in-out suits moves on screen. Open Designer differs for UI: ease-out for both entering and leaving, and never ease-in on interface motion. Here: arrivals on `expo.out`/`power4.out`, camera moves and colour floods on `power2.inOut`, zoom-throughs and exits on `power3.in`. [Figma Learn, https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/41414048690839-Adjust-an-animation-s-easing] [Open Designer, https://opendesigner.dev/docs/motion/] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
413. **Asymmetric curves** — Material's standard curve accelerates quickly and decelerates slowly; asymmetric acceleration reads more natural, and in UI long ease-in/ease-in-out feels sluggish. Here: don't use the same `power2.out` 0.5 s on everything, the flat default that makes a film look generated. [Material Design, https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html] [web.dev, https://web.dev/articles/the-basics-of-easing] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
414. **Twelve principles, origin** — Disney animators Ollie Johnston and Frank Thomas set out the 12 basic principles in "The Illusion of Life" (1981) so characters seemed to obey physics; motion designers adapt them to shapes and type. Here: use the principles below through GSAP tweens on the timeline. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation] [Aaron Whitney Björk, https://aaronbjork.com/12-Principles-of-Animation-for-Motion-Design]
415. **Timing defines weight** — duration communicates mass: a 24 px badge pops in over 150 ms, a full-screen panel slides over 500 ms with the same easing. Here: a fast move takes 0.3-0.6 s; exits are 40-50% shorter than their entrances. [iart-ai twelve principles, https://github.com/iart-ai/motion-design-skills/blob/main/skills/animation-principles/references/twelve-principles.md] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
416. **Anticipation** — a wind-up of about 20-30% of the main action (a 750 ms build gets 150-220 ms): a brief dim/scale-down to 0.96 before the pop, or a 1-2 px counter-move; motion with none reads as a glitch. Here: on the GSAP timeline that is a short tween to `scale: 0.96` placed just before the main tween, as the source describes. [pixel2motion, https://github.com/nolangz/pixel2motion/blob/main/references/twelve-principles-for-logos.md]
417. **Overshoot** — the element passes its target and pulls back once, like a high-friction spring, unlike a bouncing spring; a cubic-bezier can overshoot once but can't oscillate. Here: `back.out(1.4)` or `elastic.out(1, 0.5)`; the reel's check-mark pops with `back.out(2.4)`; position and size may bounce, opacity and colour never. [UX Collective, https://uxdesign.cc/how-to-use-overshoot-to-upgrade-your-ui-animations-afe5526fdeac, 2020-12-09] [css-animation.com, https://www.css-animation.com/core-css-animation-fundamentals/timing-functions-easing-curves/approximating-spring-physics-with-css-easing/] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
418. **Spring damping by mood** — damping 1.0 never bounces, 0.7-0.85 reads premium, 0.4-0.6 playful, under 0.3 cartoon; overshoot belongs in a playful register and never on blocks of text. Here: GSAP `elastic.out` springs and oscillates, `bounce.out` lands and rebounds without passing the target. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md] [Showtime motion craft, https://faviovazquez.github.io/showtime/docs/motion-craft.html] [Annnimate, https://annnimate.com/learn/easing/elastic-ease]
419. **Follow-through and overlapping action** — parts don't stop together: trailing elements settle after the lead (shadow at 450 ms, title 460 ms, subtitle 500 ms after a 400 ms card). Here: the next element starts when the previous is 30-50% done; fully sequential looks robotic, all at once cheap. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follow_through_and_overlapping_action] [iart-ai twelve principles, https://github.com/iart-ai/motion-design-skills/blob/main/skills/animation-principles/references/twelve-principles.md] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
420. **Stagger values** — offsets between items should be much shorter than each item's duration or it turns into a slow march. Here: letters a frame apart, words 60-120 ms, list items 40-80 ms (GSAP `stagger`); the reel's caption words use `stagger: 0.1`. [Figma Learn, https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/41239278373271-Motion-design-fundamentals-Sequencing] [CSS-Tricks, https://css-tricks.com/repeatable-staggered-animation-three-ways-sass-gsap-web-animations-api/, 2017-07-04] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
421. **Staging** — lead the eye to one thing: dim the background (e.g. to 40% over 200 ms) while the target scales 0.9 -> 1.0, and avoid competing actions. Here: a landed title is dead still and the motion moves to the next element. [IBM Design Language, https://www.ibm.com/design/language/animation/classic-principles/] [iart-ai twelve principles, https://github.com/iart-ai/motion-design-skills/blob/main/skills/animation-principles/references/twelve-principles.md] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
422. **Transitions with a cause** — use 3-5 transition kinds per film, each caused by something on screen (zoom through a button, object becomes the world, whip, iris, dip cut, blur swap). Here: ready-made ones in `npx hyperframes catalog --tag transition --json`; the reel template uses zoom-through, whip, light leak and warm flash. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]
423. **Infographic hierarchy** — lead with one hero number, add 3-5 supporting stats, cut everything that doesn't change the takeaway. Here: hero at 60-90% width, quiet lines 2.5-4% of height, one accent colour. [iArt.ai, https://www.iart.ai/blog/how-to-make-an-animated-infographic, 2026-06-24] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
424. **Reveal data in narration order** — each figure should appear when the voiceover reaches it, so the animation carries the argument (Hans Rosling's animated health/wealth chart is the classic). Here: place each reveal at its word's time from the transcript JSON. [Fliki, https://fliki.ai/features/animated-infographic-maker] [BBC The Joy of Stats, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jbkSRLYSojo, 2010-11-26] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
425. **Number count-up** — start at zero (it makes the destination feel earned), pick a duration that is neither under a second nor drawn out, and ease out so the final number slows and is readable. Here: search the registry first, `npx hyperframes catalog --query "number counter" --json`, and report a gap with `hyperframes feedback --search-miss` if nothing fits. [Reochart, https://www.reochart.com/blog/how-to-create-an-animated-number-counter, 2026-07-21] [Motion Cheatcode, https://motioncheatcode.com/guides/animated-charts-and-counters/] [Velements, https://velements.store/blog/animated-number-counters, 2026-09-18]
426. **Logo reveal ingredients** — timing, easing, spacing, overlaps and polish (motion blur, subtle secondary motion); start from the final resting layout and work backwards. Here: the reel template's end card lands each line with `fromTo` from blur/offset into its final place, staggered 0.35-1.4 s. [OlafMotion, https://olafmotion.com/tutorials/how-to-animate-logo-reveals-smoothly/, 2026-02-20] [KinoViz, https://kinoviz.com/blog/logo-animation-tutorial, 2026-04-13]
427. **Logo reveal timing** — stroke draw-on 0.6-1.2 s, mask wipe 0.5-0.9 s, per-piece build 0.4-0.6 s staggered 0.06-0.10 s, final settle 1.04 -> 1.00 in 0.12-0.20 s, whole reveal 0.8-2.5 s, then hold the settled mark at least 0.4 s. Here: the reel's check-mark draws with `strokeDashoffset: 0` over 0.3 s. [iart-ai logo patterns, https://github.com/iart-ai/motion-design-skills/blob/main/skills/logo-animation/references/logo-patterns.md] [repo: library/templates/phone-clip-reel/build.py]

## Compositing & masking

431. **Green screen lighting** — two jobs: light the screen evenly and saturated (within half a stop, about one stop under the key light) and light the subject separately to match the new background; same lights for both cause shadows and spill. Here: keep the subject 6-10 ft from the screen and add a backlight to fight the green halo. [BOLD Cine, https://www.boldcine.com/university/lessons/lighting-for-green-screen] [Digital Cinema Society, http://www.digitalcinemasociety.org/downloads/ShootingGreenscreens.pdf] [Pro Cyc, https://www.procyc.com/how-to-reduce-spill-in-your-greenscreen-setup/, 2021-05-13]
432. **chromakey in ffmpeg** — keys a colour in YUV; options color:similarity:blend (similarity 0.01 matches only the exact colour, higher blend gives softer semi-transparent edges), then `overlay` on the new background. Here: `ffmpeg -i bg.mp4 -i green.mp4 -filter_complex "[1:v]chromakey=0x00FF00:0.1:0.2[fg];[0:v][fg]overlay[v]" -map "[v]" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chromakey] [FFmpeg Cookbook, https://ffmpeg-cookbook.com/en/articles/chromakey-filter/, 2026-04-17]
433. **colorkey + despill** — `colorkey` keys in RGB (hex colour, similarity, blend); follow it with `despill` to remove the green cast left on edges. Here: `-filter_complex "[1:v]colorkey=0x00FF00:0.3:0.15,despill=type=green[fg];[0:v][fg]overlay[v]"` (ran OK locally; despill the keyed foreground before the overlay: the gist's order, `overlay,despill`, also despilled the background, a pure green bar in it went from RGB 0,254,0 to 0,0,0, measured here on 2026-10-24). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#colorkey] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#despill] [kerbeh gist, https://gist.github.com/kerbeh/fbe0cd0d89c424708c119e0a0d00ca88]
434. **Spill vs transparency** — green on a fine edge is partly background (the key removes it), green bounced onto opaque skin/hair is spill (only despill fixes it): key first, then despill, and restore lost luminance. Here: ffmpeg `hsvkey` (hue/sat/val ranges) is another keyer to try before despill: `hsvkey=hue=120:sat=0.7:val=0.5:similarity=0.2:blend=0.1` (ran OK locally). [Andrea Geremia, https://www.andreageremia.it/tutorial_math_despill.html] [Hawaiki Keyer manual, https://hawaiki.co/keyer-manual-4] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#hsvkey]
435. **Garbage matte, core and edge mattes** — mask out rigs, stands and screen edges that can't be keyed, use a holdout mask to restore foreground the key ate, and split a hard core matte from a separate edge matte. Here: in ffmpeg build a mask with `geq` and apply it with `alphamerge` or `maskedmerge`. [flylib Garbage Mattes, https://flylib.com/books/en/2.452.1/blue_screen_garbage_mattes.html] [Apple Motion Help, https://motion.skydocu.com/en/composite-with-color-and-luma-keys/correct-or-refine-an-existing-matte/overview/] [flylib AE Studio Techniques, https://flylib.com/books/en/2.105.1.57/1/] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#alphamerge]
436. **Light wrap and edge blending** — after key and despill, let background light bleed onto the keyed edge (light wrap) and blur across the edge so the subject sits in the plate. Here: `ffmpeg -filters` on this container lists no light-wrap filter (checked 2026-10-24), so do it in a compositor or match the foreground to the plate. [Mixing Light, https://mixinglight.com/color-grading-tutorials/advanced-chroma-keying-light-wrap-edges-davinci-resolve-fusion/, 2024-11-06] [Vindral Light Wrap, https://composer-docs.vindral.com/operators/operators-light-wrap.html]
437. **Rotoscoping** — trace a path around an object frame by frame and use it as a mask to treat object and background separately; start on the frame with the most detail, use as few points as possible, and let mask motion blur match fast moves. Here: HyperFrames crop/mask reveals tween `clip-path` or a mask on an inner wrapper, leaving source time untouched. [Adobe After Effects manual, https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/753844/adobe-after-effects.html?page=330] [Apple Motion, https://support.apple.com/guide/motion/rotoscoping-tips-motn976948aa/mac] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/motion.md]
438. **Point, planar or camera tracking** — point tracking follows one feature (labels, mask drivers), planar follows a flat surface with perspective (screen/sign replacement, corner pins), camera tracking solves the 3D camera path; a point tracker needs two or more points for scale/rotation. Here: Mocha is the reference planar tracker; drive roto splines from the track data. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/tracking-and-match-move] [Boris FX, https://borisfx.com/blog/learn-how-to-do-planar-tracking-with-mocha-pro/, 2023-12-07] [Blackmagic Forum, https://forum.blackmagicdesign.com/viewtopic.php?f=21&t=170381, 2022-11-09]
439. **Picture-in-picture** — shrink one clip over another, about a quarter of the width, away from faces and text, and decide which audio plays. Here: HyperFrames PiP rectangles sit 28 px from the edges (landscape canvas, 16:9 source: 1432, 28, 460x258); ffmpeg `"[1:v]scale=160:-2[p];[0:v][p]overlay=W-w-24:H-h-24[v]" -map "[v]" -map 0:a` (ran OK locally). [Woven, https://www.woven.video/guides/split-screen-video] [Kinopipe, https://kinopipe.com/ffmpeg/picture-in-picture] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#overlay-1] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/overlays.md]
440. **Circle facecam** — a PiP cut to a circle with an optional ring border is a softer version of the same layout. Here: ffmpeg `[1:v]scale=160:160,format=gray,geq=lum='if(lt(hypot(X-80,Y-80),78),255,0)'[m];[1:v]scale=160:160[s];[s][m]alphamerge[c];[0:v][c]overlay=W-w-20:20` (ran OK locally). [Woven, https://www.woven.video/guides/split-screen-video] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#alphamerge] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#geq]
441. **Split screen basics** — put two clips on one canvas, side by side on a horizontal canvas and top/bottom on a vertical one; on 9:16 a stacked 16:9 clip keeps about 63% of its width, side by side on 16:9 keeps 50%. Here: HyperFrames split layout puts the video at 960, 0, 960x1080 landscape or 0, 960, 1080x960 portrait, sized to the source aspect. [Woven, https://www.woven.video/guides/split-screen-video] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/overlays.md]
442. **Match the panels** — set crops so the eyes sit at the same height, match brightness, and give panels a thin white or black border so they read as separate. Here: grade both panels the same way and check the seam in a `snapshot`. [Woven, https://www.woven.video/guides/split-screen-video] [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
443. **hstack / vstack in ffmpeg** — stack videos side by side or top/bottom; the inputs must share the same height (hstack) or width (vstack). Here: `ffmpeg -i a.mp4 -i b.mp4 -filter_complex "[0:v][1:v]hstack=inputs=2[v]" -map "[v]" out.mp4`, and the same with `vstack` (both ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#hstack] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#vstack] [FFmpeg Cookbook, https://ffmpeg-cookbook.com/en/articles/side-by-side-video/, 2026-09-02]
444. **xstack grids** — any custom layout (2x2 mosaic) from N inputs with a `layout` string. Here: `[0:v][1:v][2:v][3:v]xstack=inputs=4:layout=0_0|w0_0|0_h0|w0_h0[v]` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xstack] [Stack Overflow, https://stackoverflow.com/questions/11552565/vertically-or-horizontally-stack-mosaic-several-videos-using-ffmpeg]
445. **Before/after split compare** — crop the left half of one clip and the right half of the other and stack them for a vertical-split comparison. Here: `"[0]crop=iw/2:ih:0:0[l];[1]crop=iw/2:ih:iw/2:0[r];[l][r]hstack"` (ran OK locally); in HyperFrames the registry component `before-after-wipe` does a wipe compare. [AVpres, https://avpres.net/FFmpeg/split.html] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes-registry/SKILL.md]
446. **Multiply and Screen** — Multiply multiplies the layers' 0-1 values (darker; white disappears), Screen inverts, multiplies and inverts (lighter; black disappears); Overlay combines them and leaves 50% grey unchanged. Here: so black drops out under Screen and white drops out under Multiply. [Wikipedia Blend modes, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blend_modes] [web.dev, https://web.dev/learn/css/blend-modes]
447. **Blend modes in ffmpeg** — the `blend` filter mixes two streams with `all_mode` (normal, multiply, screen, overlay and more); the first input is the top layer. Here: `[0:v][1:v]blend=all_mode=screen[v]`, also `multiply` and `overlay` (all three ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend-1] [ffmpeg 9.0 blend docs, https://ayosec.github.io/ffmpeg-filters-docs/9.0/Filters/Video/blend.html]
448. **Blend modes on captions** — choose the text blend from the brightness (0-255) behind it: under 60 `screen`, 60-180 `overlay`, over 180 `normal` with opaque colour. Here: in CSS 3D scenes never put `mix-blend-mode` on the 3D world (it flattens it); put blends in a separate 2D overlay composition. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md] [repo: CLAUDE.md "CSS 3D voxel films"]
449. **Sky replacement: three parts** — a sky matte that holds on trees and hair, a track so the new sky moves with the camera, and a sky whose light direction, colour and time of day match the foreground (grade or relight the ground to match). Here: check the matte edges in `snapshot --at` frames before render. [Nolan Labs, https://nolanlabs.io/blog/sky-replacement-in-video, 2026-10-08] [PHLEARN, https://phlearn.com/tutorial/replace-sky-photoshop/] [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
450. **Sky selection tools** — in Resolve, select the sky with a Qualifier, Window or Magic Mask, then feed it to the Sky Replacement FX and match exposure, lens softness and noise. Here: a bright, clean sky can be keyed by luma in ffmpeg: `[0:v]lumakey=threshold=0.9:tolerance=0.1:softness=0.05[k];[1:v][k]overlay` with the new sky as the bottom input (ran OK locally). [videomontazher, https://videomontazher.com/en/color/resolve-fx/sky-replacement] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#lumakey]
451. **AI background removal** — AI removers separate the moving subject frame by frame with no green screen and export transparent video (WebM/MOV), a green-screen clip or a mask; better ones smooth across time so edges don't flicker. Here: `npx hyperframes remove-background` (first run downloads 168 MB, ~2 fps at 1080p on CPU; drops mic arms and flickers on held objects). [Poof, https://poof.bg/video-background-removal-api] [Adobe Express, https://www.adobe.com/express/feature/ai/video/remove-background] [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
452. **Robust Video Matting (open source)** — RVM is a recurrent network with temporal memory for human video matting, needing no trimap or clean plate (4K 76 fps, HD 104 fps on a GTX 1080 Ti). Here: there is no GPU in this container; rembg can also be run per extracted frame (rembg_from_video), and on this box rembg's u2net_human_seg worked where the default BRIA model was OOM-killed at 14 GB. [RobustVideoMatting, https://github.com/PeterL1n/RobustVideoMatting/] [arXiv 2108.11515, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.11515] [rembg_from_video, https://github.com/seth-tribbey/rembg_from_video] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Environment facts (cloud container)"]
453. **Cutout placement and occlusion** — a WebM cutout over captions fails `check` with `text_occluded`, because `check` treats a `<video>` as opaque across its box. Here: add `data-layout-allow-occlusion` to the cutout and judge from `snapshot`; shake or punch in video and cutout together in one wrapper. [repo: .claude/skills/hyperframes/references/captions.md]
454. **Fix the key on set, not with tolerance** — shadows and hot spots widen the range of "screen colour" the keyer must accept, so even lighting beats widening tolerances; use edge softening so the cut doesn't look like cardboard. Here: start chromakey with low similarity (0.1) and raise blend for softer edges before raising similarity. [Vindral HSV Keyer, https://composer-docs.vindral.com/operators/operators-hsv-keyer.html] [Litepanels, https://www.litepanels.com/en/applications/how-to-light-for-green-screen/, 2023-02-01] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#chromakey]
455. **Export a keyed clip with alpha** — key once and save the result in a codec with an alpha channel, then composite it later over any background. Here: `ffmpeg -i green.mp4 -vf "chromakey=0x00FF00:0.1:0.2" -c:v png alpha.mov` gave a png/rgba stream (ran OK locally).
