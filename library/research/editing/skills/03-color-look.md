# Colour and look skills (#201-#275)

Colour correction, grading looks and finishing skills for phone and client edits, each with a local ffmpeg or HyperFrames way to do it.

Researched 2026-10-24 via agent-reach (Exa) web search. ffmpeg commands were each run once on a 640x360 testsrc2 clip (ffmpeg 6.1.1) in the session scratchpad.

## Correction

201. **Correct first, grade second** — colour correction is the technical pass that brings every shot to a neutral, consistent start (fix too dark, too blue, too flat) and grading is the creative pass after it; a look built on uncorrected shots falls apart when two shots are cut together. Here: correct with ffmpeg `eq`/`colorbalance` or a HyperFrames `adjust` block first, then add a preset or LUT look. [Sequence Handbook, https://sequence.film/handbook/post-production/color-grading] [Dehancer, https://www.dehancer.com/learn/article/color-grading-color-correction-a-practical-guide-for-beginners]
202. **Full order of operations** — one colourist's neutralising order: black point, white point, gamma, white balance, saturation, match shots to scopes, check the skin tone line, grade to a look, then effects (grain, halation, vignette). Here: build the ffmpeg chain in that order, e.g. `colorlevels` -> `curves` -> `colortemperature` -> `eq=saturation` -> look -> `noise`/`vignette`. [Adobe Community, https://community.adobe.com/questions-729/should-white-balance-always-be-the-first-step-in-color-correction-1360726, 2020-03-13]
203. **Primary correction in five steps** — neutralise the white balance first, set gain so the brightest part sits just below clipping, set lift so the darkest part just touches black (unless you want a matte look), place gamma by the skin, and restore saturation last because contrast changes perceived saturation. Here: ffmpeg `colorlevels=rimin=0.04:gimin=0.04:bimin=0.04:rimax=0.96:gimax=0.96:bimax=0.96` sets the end points (ran OK locally). [Luttie, https://luttie.app/tools/lift-gamma-gain] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#colorlevels]
204. **Know which steps commute** — saturation and contrast give the identical image in either order (as do a global exposure lift and a global saturation change), so spend attention on the orders that do matter. Here: in an ffmpeg `eq` chain the order of `contrast` and `saturation` needs no thought. [Luttie, https://luttie.app/blog/does-color-grading-order-matter, 2026-08-29]
205. **Read the scopes, not the screen** — monitors can be badly calibrated and eyes adapt to a colour cast within minutes, while the waveform (brightness) and vectorscope (colour) don't lie. Here: render ffmpeg `waveform` and `vectorscope` PNGs of a frame and look at them with the frame. [Primer disparo, https://www.apovedam.com/en/free-video-editing-course/color-grading-basics.html]
206. **Read a luma waveform** — left to right is the image and bottom to top is brightness; in HD black is 0 and white 100, and levels outside that range clip, so an image peaking at 80 has no true white. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "waveform=mode=column" -frames:v 1 -update 1 wf.png` (the default `mirror=1` puts higher values at the top, as on a normal scope; only with `mirror=0` is the top value 0 and the bottom 255 — measured here 2026-10-24: a black frame's trace sat at row 239 and a white frame's at row 20 of 256) (ran OK locally). [Frame.io, https://blog.frame.io/2017/09/27/introduction-to-video-scopes/, 2017-09-27] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#waveform]
207. **RGB parade for colour casts** — the parade is the same waveform split into red, green and blue; anything that should be white or grey needs the three columns at the same height. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "waveform=mode=column:display=parade:components=7" -frames:v 1 -update 1 parade.png` (ran OK locally). [Primer disparo, https://www.apovedam.com/en/free-video-editing-course/color-grading-basics.html] [Envato Tuts+, https://photography.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-read-scopes-in-resolve-waveform-parade-histogram-vectorscope--cms-41868, 2022-06-15] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#waveform]
208. **Read a vectorscope** — it drops brightness and plots only colour: the centre is neutral (an off-centre trace where you expect neutral is a cast), the angle is hue and the distance from the centre is saturation. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "format=yuv444p,vectorscope=mode=color3:graticule=color" -frames:v 1 -update 1 vs.png` (ran OK locally). [Luttie, https://luttie.app/tools/vectorscope] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#vectorscope]
209. **Skin tone line** — the line between the red and yellow targets (about 11 o'clock, often drawn at 123°, the +I axis) is where faces of a wide range of complexions cluster; it is a guide within a band of about ten degrees, not a law, and melanin mostly changes how far out the cloud sits and how dark the face is. Here: check a face crop on the ffmpeg vectorscope (crop first with `crop=w:h:x:y`). [Versely, https://www.versely.studio/blog/read-skin-tones-on-the-vectorscope, 2026-08-21] [Luttie, https://luttie.app/tools/vectorscope]
210. **Skin hue band and drift fixes** — all skin sits in a narrow orange hue band (about 20-40° on the hue wheel) and complexions differ mainly in lightness and saturation; skin that is too orange or red usually means over-warm temperature or too much saturation, so pull temperature back before touching saturation. Here: lower HyperFrames `adjust.temperature` before `adjust.saturation`. [Luttie, https://luttie.app/blog/how-to-color-correct-skin-tones, 2026-07-04]
211. **Skin in Lab numbers** — skin should keep a positive Lab 'a' (red side) and positive 'b' (yellow side); a grade that pushes it negative (green or blue cast) looks unflattering however good the rest of the image is. Here: sample a face pixel from a snapshot frame and convert to Lab with PIL to check. [Luttie, https://luttie.app/tools/skin-tone-balance]
212. **Two checks before touching a face** — sample the face on the vectorscope against the skin tone line and check its exposure on the waveform, especially in the shadows where darker skin tones live; fix white balance and primaries first and use secondaries only for subtle refinement. Here: ffmpeg `vectorscope` + `waveform` on the same frame. [Ken Jones, https://www.kenjonesnyc.com/skin-tone-color-grading/, 2026-09-14]
213. **Grey-scale first, then colour** — for skin, adjust grey-scale values (waveform: shadows 0-33%, mid-tones 33-66%, highlights 66-100%) before hue and saturation (vectorscope). Here: set `eq`/`curves` before `colorbalance`. [Larry Jordan, https://larryjordan.com/articles/color-correction-make-people-look-normal/, 2014-03-09]
214. **White balance with colortemperature** — ffmpeg simulates a change in ambient colour temperature (1000-40000 K, default 6500 K) with `mix` for strength and `pl` to preserve lightness. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "colortemperature=temperature=5000:mix=1:pl=0.5" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#colortemperature]
215. **Range-specific cast fixes with colorbalance** — shift red-cyan, green-magenta and blue-yellow separately in shadows, midtones and highlights (positive values toward the primary, negative toward its complement). Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "colorbalance=rs=-0.08:bs=0.10:rh=0.08:bh=-0.06" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#colorbalance]
216. **Expose for the subject** — for a backlit person, brightening until every shadow shows can ruin the mood or blow the sky; a slightly darker background with a readable face is the better compromise, and watch clouds, white clothes and skin reflections (unverified: the cited page returned an empty response on 2026-10-24 and no replacement source was found). Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "eq=brightness=0.03:contrast=1.1:saturation=1.05:gamma=1.0" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#eq]
217. **False colour exposure targets** — one false-colour key marks about 56 IRE for skin, 45 IRE for a grey card and 70-80 IRE for white objects. Here: measure average luma with `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "signalstats,metadata=print:key=lavfi.signalstats.YAVG:file=ss.txt" -f null -` (ran OK locally; test clip YAVG 125 of 255). [IKAN, https://ikancorp.com/Downloads/FalseColorChart.pdf] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#signalstats]
218. **Check exposure before any LUT** — false colour maps luma to a few bands so a clipped highlight or a face in the near-black band shows up before a LUT stretches the mistake into something you cannot undo; a generated talking head can look "well exposed" in a 320-pixel poster and still be a clip on the waveform. Here: HyperFrames `media-treatment --selector '#hero' --analyze --json` returns ffmpeg/ffprobe signalstats evidence (deterministic clipping and signal evidence) without modifying the composition. [Versely, https://www.versely.studio/blog/false-color-exposure-check-before-grading, 2026-08-21] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/grading.md] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/media-treatments.md]
219. **S-curve contrast** — a tone curve maps input to output; between two control points the line gets steeper than the diagonal, so small mid-tone differences (where faces live) come out larger, and no detail is added. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "curves=master='0/0 0.25/0.20 0.75/0.80 1/1'" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/curves-and-contrast] [Mixing Light, https://mixinglight.com/color-grading-tutorials/creating-s-curve-using-contrast-pivot/, 2014-05-16] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#curves]
220. **ffmpeg curve presets** — `curves` ships presets (color_negative, cross_process, darker, increase_contrast, lighter, linear_contrast, medium_contrast, negative, strong_contrast, vintage) for a quick contrast pass. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "curves=preset=increase_contrast" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#curves]
221. **Legal video levels** — in 8-bit Rec.709, luma sits in 16-235 and chroma in 16-240 (10-bit: 64-940 luma); values outside are sub-black or super-white, and a legal luma/chroma triple can still make an RGB channel go below 0 or over 1 on saturated cyans and skies. Here: ffmpeg `zscale=...:r=tv` (as in the tonemap chain below) writes limited ("tv") range (the docs list `limited`; `ffmpeg -h filter=zscale` shows `tv` = `limited` = 0). [Versely, https://www.versely.studio/blog/broadcast-safe-legalizer-pass-before-delivery, 2026-08-21] [TV Forum, https://www.tvforum.co.uk/mediatech/broadcast-safe-colours-40639, 2015-02-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#zscale]
222. **Full vs limited range mix-ups** — legal-range video shown as full range on an sRGB monitor looks washed out (and the reverse crushes it), so check which range a file and a player assume. Here: ffprobe the file's range and convert with `zscale` when it is wrong. [Avid KB, https://kb.avid.com/articles/en_US/Knowledge/Washed-out-or-crushed-luminance-levels-in-Media-Composer-or-your-exported-file, updated 2026-01-23] [EBU R 103, https://tech.ebu.ch/files/live/sites/tech/files/shared/r/r103.pdf]
223. **Shot matching order** — grade one reference shot, then match every other shot to it in a fixed order (black point, white point, midtones, colour balance, saturation) on the waveform and RGB parade, not the picture. Here: parade PNGs of the hero shot and the shot being matched side by side. [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-color-matching-cameras]
224. **Colour space first when cameras differ** — most mismatch between cameras is a colour space problem: convert each camera's log/gamut into one shared timeline space before matching, then match to a hero shot and finish on scopes. Here: convert each source with its own LUT through ffmpeg `lut3d` before the shared look. [Basecut, https://basecut.app/guides/match-colors-across-cameras-davinci-resolve]
225. **Auto match is only a start** — Resolve's Shot Match aims to make clips look the same as the chosen clip, not good; it is a starting point for the scene look. Here: use HyperFrames `media-treatment --analyze` suggestions the same way, as a starting point for visual review. [Larry Jordan, https://larryjordan.com/articles/how-to-color-match-clips-in-davinci-resolve-20/, 2025-11-29] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/grading.md]
226. **Phone HDR is HLG and never looks like SDR** — iPhones shoot HDR as HLG (Dolby Vision over HLG); converted to SDR it will always look less bright, less contrasty and less saturated than on an HDR screen. Here: `ffprobe -v error -show_entries stream=color_transfer,color_primaries` (arib-std-b67 = HLG). [Larry Jordan, https://larryjordan.com/articles/editing-hdr-iphone-video-in-final-cut-pro/, 2024-07-06] [4K Shooters, https://www.4kshooters.net/2021/07/28/how-to-color-grade-iphone-12-hdr-10-bit-video-in-resolve-17/, 2021-07-27]
227. **HDR on SDR screens looks wrong** — HDR highlights look clipped and unpleasant on SDR displays and most users want SDR Rec.709 output, so convert phone HDR to SDR before editing for normal screens. Here: tonemap first (next item), then grade the SDR file. [Adobe, https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere-pro/using/working-with-color-managed-iphone-media.html]
228. **HDR->SDR tonemap in ffmpeg** — `tonemap` works only on linear light in float, so linearise with `zscale` first and convert back after. Here: `ffmpeg -i hlg.mp4 -vf "zscale=t=linear:npl=100,format=gbrpf32le,zscale=p=bt709,tonemap=tonemap=hable:desat=0,zscale=t=bt709:m=bt709:r=tv,format=yuv420p" sdr.mp4` (ran OK locally on an HLG-tagged 10-bit test clip; an untagged 8-bit input failed with "no path between colorspaces"). [Jimmy's Blog, https://jimmyhoke.net/wp/?p=412, 2024-04-27, updated 2026-03-21] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tonemap]
229. **Pick the tonemap curve by eye** — ffmpeg's `hable` preserves dark and bright detail at the cost of slightly darkening everything (use it when detail matters more than colour and brightness accuracy), while `mobius` keeps contrast and colours of in-range material (use it when colour accuracy matters more; its `param` is the linear-to-mobius transition point, default 0.3); with libplacebo and Vulkan set up, a `libplacebo=tonemapping=bt.2390` chain gives a better result. Here: `zscale=transfer=linear,tonemap=mobius,zscale=transfer=bt709,format=yuv420p` (ran OK locally on an HLG-tagged 10-bit clip); the ITU-R BT.2446 report presents example HDR-to-SDR tone-mapping methods. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#tonemap] [Jimmy's Blog, https://jimmyhoke.net/wp/?p=412, 2024-04-27, updated 2026-03-21] [ITU-R BT.2446-1, https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-BT.2446-1-2021-PDF-E.pdf] [GitHub bbimer, https://github.com/bbimer/iphone-hdr-to-sdr-ffmpeg]
230. **HyperFrames grades SDR only** — the realtime treatment path is Rec.709/SDR, so do not silently process HDR, HLG, PQ or camera LOG sources through it. Here: tonemap or LUT-convert in ffmpeg first; `media-treatment --analyze` reports HDR/unknown-LOG warnings. [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/media-treatments.md] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/grading.md]
231. **Log to Rec.709 is three fixed operations** — decode the log curve to scene-linear, matrix the camera gamut into Rec.709, then display-encode (a shoulder brings scene-linear values back into range, then the Rec.709 OETF); done right, middle grey from all eight log formats the page tests lands on the same Rec.709 value (0.373). Here: one conversion `.cube` per camera profile via ffmpeg `lut3d`. [FestivalLuts, https://festivalluts.com/academy/log-to-rec709-conversion-done-right]
232. **Conversion LUT must match the camera** — a conversion LUT maps one camera's log curve and gamut to Rec.709, so an S-Log3 LUT on D-Log M (or Apple Log on Apple Log 2) looks wrong; apply the conversion first and a creative LUT on top, never a creative LUT straight on log. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "lut3d=file=convert.cube:interp=tetrahedral,lut3d=file=look.cube" out.mp4` (single `lut3d` ran OK locally). [Bransford Media, https://bransfordmedia.com/luts/learn/what-is-log] [Aprture, https://aprture.com/blogs/news/why-your-lut-looks-bad, 2026-09-16] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#lut3d]
233. **LUT intensity** — intensity mixes the graded and original picture (60% = each pixel moves 60% of the way); lower it on a creative look but leave a conversion LUT at 100%. Here: HyperFrames `"lut": {"src": ".media/luts/grade_001.cube", "intensity": 0.85}`, animatable with `--hf-color-grading-lut-intensity` (0 to 1). [Batchtone, https://batchtone.com/blog/lut-intensity, 2026-10-08] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/grading.md]

## Grading & looks

234. **Warm grades feel close** — raising temperature accentuates reds, yellows, oranges and browns and makes subjects seem heavier, denser and closer, useful for cosy, relatable, trustworthy images. Here: the owner's "Romantic glow" grade uses `"temperature":0.12` on indoor phone footage. [CSU Social, https://social.colostate.edu/best-practices/true-colors/, 2015-07-22] [repo: library/README.md "Colour grades that worked"]
235. **Warm arouses, cool relaxes** — colour-psychology theory links longer-wavelength colours to arousal and warmth and shorter-wavelength colours to relaxation and coolness. Here: a cool shift with ffmpeg `colortemperature=temperature=9000` (used in the day-for-night chain below). [Elliot & Maier review, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383146/, 2015-04-02]
236. **Lightness and saturation carry emotion** — a review of 132 articles found systematic colour-emotion links (e.g. light colours with positive and dark with negative emotions) driven by lightness, saturation and hue. Here: raise exposure/saturation for happy edits, lower them for sad ones. [Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-024-02615-z, 2025-01-13]
237. **Saturation follows the story's valence** — colour photos were rated more pleasant than grey ones for positive images and more unpleasant for negative ones; cutting saturation 50% made positive images less pleasant. Here: keep saturation up on happy client reels. [PMC study, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10867063/, 2023-08-24]
238. **Desaturated look** — lowering every colour to greys, olives and faded browns while keeping a trace of hue reads as fatigue, loss, grief, war or collapse. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "hue=s=0.6" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [Screenweaver, https://www.screenweaver.ai/film-techniques/color/desaturated] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#hue]
239. **Don't desaturate by default** — so many TV shows and movies now have a dull grey filter on every scene that a film Twitter critic (Katie Stebbins, quoted by Vox) calls it the "intangible sludge"; in her Dexter comparison the skin goes pale and even yellowish and a blue shirt turns an indefinable blue/green/black/brown. Here: compare against the ungraded frame with `grade-compare` before keeping a muted look. [Vox, https://www.vox.com/culture/22840526/colors-movies-tv-gray-digital-color-sludge, 2022-01-10] [VideoUniversity, https://www.videouniversity.com/uncategorized/desaturated-gray-movies/, 2022-02-01]
240. **Teal and orange** — push teal/blue into the shadows and orange/yellow into the highlights; most skin sits in the orange range, so the complementary teal background makes faces stand out. Here: HyperFrames LUT look `teal-orange-blockbuster` (intensity 0.85; `resolve --type grade --intent "teal orange blockbuster"`). [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/features/why-use-orange-teal-grading.html] [PetaPixel, https://petapixel.com/2017/02/23/orange-teal-look-popular-hollywood/, 2017-02-23] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/luts/index.json]
241. **Motivate the teal-orange** — teal and orange is a complementary split, not a requirement; warm amber and cool blue should be motivated by lamps and sky or they look pasted, and don't teal-orange a scene that was tungsten five seconds ago. Here: use one look per scene across all its shots. [Melies, https://melies.co/cinematic-techniques/color]
242. **Bleach bypass** — skipping the bleach stage leaves silver on the film, raising contrast, intensifying grain and lowering saturation (used in 1984, Three Kings, Saving Private Ryan). Here: HyperFrames LUT look `bleach-bypass` (intensity 0.8), or ffmpeg `split[a][b];[b]hue=s=0[g];[a][g]blend=all_mode=overlay:all_opacity=0.5,eq=saturation=0.6` (UNVERIFIED look: ran OK locally 2026-10-24, but this exact chain is our own combination of documented filters, not taken from a guide — compare with grade-compare before use). [O'Reilly, Color Correction Look Book ch. 2, https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/color-correction-look/9780133818482/ch02.html] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/luts/index.json] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#blend]
243. **Bleach bypass character** — the look gives deep blacks and a crunchy texture, often some white crushing and slight desaturation; Deakins revived it for the desaturated Nineteen Eighty-Four. Here: keep it for gritty, hard-edged stories, not soft family edits. [Filmworkz, https://filmworkz.com/color-grading-103/, 2022-02-26] [BFI Sight and Sound, https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/remain-light-roger-deakins-ten-shots, 2024-05-22]
244. **Film fade / matte blacks** — lift the blacks so nothing reaches true black, mute the colour and let shadows go slightly milky; it reads as old faded stock and forgives imperfect footage. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "curves=all='0/0.08 1/0.95'" out.mp4` (ran OK locally) or HyperFrames LUT look `film-fade` (intensity 0.75). [Luttie, https://luttie.app/looks/faded-film] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/luts/index.json]
245. **Soft highlight roll-off** — the lifted blacks, soft highlight roll-off and gentle contrast of analog film come from the tone curve, which the exposure/contrast/blacks sliders alone cannot recreate. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "curves=preset=vintage" out.mp4` (ran OK locally), or a custom `curves` top point below 1. [The Editing Studio, https://theeditingstudio.co/blog/how-to-use-the-tone-curve-for-soft-film-highlights, 2026-04-07] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#curves]
246. **Film Memory recipe** — only when the story calls for a warm memory, restrained flashback, personal archive or film-like recollection: `{"preset":"vintage-wash","intensity":0.6,"details":{"vignette":0.12,"grain":0.12,"grainSize":0.2,"grainRoughness":0.6}}`, kept within intensity 0.5-0.75; the strong end can flatten dark skin and black clothes. Here: apply with `hyperframes media-treatment --grading '<json>' --apply`. [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/media-treatment-recipes.md]
247. **Film-look colour** — blues turn slightly cyan, greens cool off and warm tones nudge toward red and orange, with grain adding texture. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "selectivecolor=reds='0 0 -0.1 0':blues='0.2 0 0 0'" out.mp4` (CMYK adjust per colour range; ran OK locally). [VSCO, https://www.vsco.co/learn/how-to-make-photos-look-like-film, 2024-05-10] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#selectivecolor]
248. **Grain alone is not film** — film character comes from the stock's spectral sensitivity, exposure curve, dye colour, highlight handling, grain and optics together; reproducing one of them is decoration. Here: HyperFrames notes parametric LUTs (`buildCube`) cannot reproduce real film stocks; use a scanned `.cube` for that. [HolyGrain, https://www.holygrain.com/blog/what-is-film-emulation-in-digital-cinema/, 2026-09-16] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/grading.md]
249. **Split toning** — push one colour into the shadows and another into the highlights; warm highlights with cool shadows evoke a cinematic or nostalgic look. Here: ffmpeg `colorbalance=rs=-0.08:bs=0.10:rh=0.08:bh=-0.06` (cool shadows, warm highlights; ran OK locally). [Picfair Focus, https://focus.picfair.com/articles/how-to-use-split-toning-in-your-photography, 2020-10-29] [Blender Manual, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/compositing/types/creative/split_toning.html] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#colorbalance]
250. **Split tone late and lightly** — split toning is usually one of the last steps and a common pair is yellow highlights with blue shadows (or the reverse); Lightroom's Color Grading replaced and extends Split Toning. Here: HyperFrames `teal-orange-blockbuster` params use `splitTone` with intensity 0.62. [Shotkit, https://shotkit.com/lightroom-split-toning/, 2021-01-11] [Digital Photography School, https://digital-photography-school.com/use-split-toning-make-photos-stand/, 2024-04-30] [Adobe Blog, https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2020/10/20/introducing-color-grading, 2020-10-20] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/luts/index.json]
251. **Day for night** — shoot in daylight and grade to night; it is as much about framing and lighting on set as about post, and never quite identical to real night. Here: plan shots without bright sky in frame. [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/color-grading-tutorial-creating-a-day-for-night-look/, 2020-02-25]
252. **Day-for-night node order** — normalise, crush contrast, cool the balance, isolate and darken the sky, protect practical lights, finish; a colour shift added before the contrast crush looks blue and daytime, not dark. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "eq=brightness=-0.12:saturation=0.5:gamma=0.8,colortemperature=temperature=9000" out.mp4` (darken first, then cool; ran OK locally). [TryUncle, https://tryuncle.com/learn/davinci-resolve/davinci-resolve-day-for-night-color-grading-tutorial, 2026-08-02]
253. **Day-for-night exposure** — bring highlights down until skin is just visible without straining, then raise midtones to recover some shadow detail. Here: `curves` with a lowered top point plus a small mid lift. [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/5-easy-tips-for-achieving-excellent-day-for-night-shots-on-set-and-in-post/, 2015-08-14]
254. **B&W: don't just desaturate** — simple desaturation gives flat results; the Channel Mixer sets each colour channel's share of the grey image. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "colorchannelmixer=.3:.4:.3:0:.3:.4:.3:0:.3:.4:.3" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [DPChallenge, https://www.dpchallenge.com/tutorial.php?TUTORIAL_ID=43] [Adobe Photoshop help, https://helpx.adobe.com/photoshop/using/color-monochrome-adjustments-using-channels.html, 2023-05-24] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#colorchannelmixer]
255. **Channel mix sums to 100%** — keep the red+green+blue shares at 100% (less darkens, more brightens); the red channel mostly moves skin tones. Here: raise the first value of each `colorchannelmixer` row for brighter skin and lower the others to keep the sum at 1. [Giggster, https://giggster.com/guide/retouching/black-white-channel-mixer/]
256. **Luminosity weights for B&W** — the sRGB weights (21% R, 71% G, 8% B) give a slightly punchy look and a good starting point for a B&W conversion. Here: `colorchannelmixer=.21:.71:.08:0:.21:.71:.08:0:.21:.71:.08` (ran OK locally), or ffmpeg `monochrome` (custom colour filter; ran OK locally). [Digital Photography School, https://digital-photography-school.com/black-and-white-conversions-an-introduction-to-luminosity/, 2013-06-05] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#monochrome]
257. **Secondary correction** — primary correction changes every pixel equally; a secondary works on one region (sky, skin, an over-saturated object) through an alpha selection. Here: ffmpeg `huesaturation=saturation=-0.4:colors=b+c+g` changes only chosen colour ranges (ran OK locally). [UC3M, http://hdl.handle.net/10016/28493, 2019-06-12] [cinapex, https://cinapex.pro/davinci-resolve-qualifier-secondary-correction/, 2026-05-17] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#huesaturation]
258. **Qualifier skin protection** — sample the skin with the HSL qualifier eyedropper and correct only that range (temperature, tint, luminance, saturation) so a strong look can sit on the rest of the frame. Here: in ffmpeg, leave reds/yellows out of `huesaturation colors=` so skin keeps its colour. [Luttie, https://luttie.app/tools/skin-tone-balance] [Noam Kroll, https://noamkroll.com/how-to-match-skintones-perfectly-using-qualifiers-power-windows-in-davinci-resolve/, 2015-05-31]
259. **Why skin breaks first** — skin mixes subsurface scattering with speculars, so heavy saturation or hue shifts look plastic or waxy, and crushed filmic midtones remove the modelling that shows vitality. Here: reject any HyperFrames candidate that makes skin implausible or flattens dark skin. [Coloristic, https://coloristic.org/professions/article/film-television-color-grading-skin-tone-protection/, 2024-11-26] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/media-treatment-recipes.md]
260. **Vibrance over saturation for people** — saturation moves all colours equally, while vibrance ("smart saturation") changes only some colours: Larry Jordan uses it to boost colour while preserving skin tones, and Filmbox's vibrance saturates skin tones less than other tones. Here: HyperFrames `skin-soft` preset reduces vibrance math for skin-like colours (it does not mask or track a face). [Larry Jordan, https://larryjordan.com/articles/saturation-vs-vibrance-whats-the-difference-for-video/, 2024-08-17] [Video Village Filmbox, https://videovillage.com/learn/filmbox/full-guide/lab-module] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/media-treatment-recipes.md]
261. **Natural Portrait grade** — for talking heads: `{"preset":"skin-soft","intensity":0.58,"adjust":{"highlights":-0.08,"shadows":0.08,"temperature":0.02,"vibrance":0.02},"details":{"vignette":0.03}}`, with no grain/blur and vignette 0-0.05. Here: `hyperframes media-treatment --selector '#clip' --grading '<json>' --apply`. [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/media-treatment-recipes.md]
262. **Grades that worked on owner jobs** — "Cinematic muted" tames loud cyan/blue office surfaces while keeping skin warm (skin-soft 0.6, saturation -0.22, vignette 0.28, grain 0.06); "Romantic glow" suits indoor phone footage in harsh window light (warm-daylight 0.7, highlights -0.25, shadows 0.2). Here: copy the JSON from the README into `media-treatment --grading`. [repo: library/README.md "Colour grades that worked"]
263. **Compare candidates on a real frame** — compare 2-3 grades with `npx hyperframes grade-compare --for frame.png --grades grades.json` and apply the winner to every clip. Here: `grade-compare` then `media-treatment ... --apply`. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a professional edit"] [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/grading.md]
264. **HyperFrames grades are whole-frame** — realtime grading applies to the whole `<img>`/`<video>` and cannot isolate or track a face or region; make a separate cropped/masked layer for that. Here: crop the region into its own clip with ffmpeg before grading it differently. [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/media-treatments.md]

## Finishing

265. **Finishing goes last** — grain, halation and vignettes come after the look in the order of operations. Here: put `noise`, `vignette` and halation at the end of the ffmpeg chain. [Adobe Community, https://community.adobe.com/questions-729/should-white-balance-always-be-the-first-step-in-color-correction-1360726, 2020-03-13]
266. **Subtle vignette** — darkening the frame edges pulls focus to the subject; keep it soft so viewers notice the subject, not a dark ring. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "vignette=angle=PI/5" out.mp4` (default lens angle PI/5; ran OK locally) or HyperFrames `details.vignette`. [Boris FX, https://borisfx.com/blog/how-to-add-a-video-vignette-effect-in-premiere-pro/, 2025-05-22] [Resolve Academy, https://editing.nahidrains.com/how-to/create-a-subtle-vignette/] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#vignette]
267. **Vignette isn't in a LUT** — a vignette depends on pixel position, which a LUT cannot know, so it is lost when you export a look as a LUT. Here: keep vignette in HyperFrames `details` or ffmpeg `vignette`, never in a `.cube`. [Luttie, https://luttie.app/docs/vignette]
268. **Halation** — red-orange glow around bright highlights and high-contrast edges, from light bouncing off the film base back into the red layer. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -filter_complex "[0:v]split[a][b];[b]lutrgb=g=0:b=0,gblur=sigma=12[h];[a][h]blend=all_mode=screen:all_opacity=0.25" out.mp4` (UNVERIFIED look: ran OK locally 2026-10-24, but this exact chain is our own combination of documented filters, not taken from a guide — check frames before use). [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-film-look-emulation-grain-halation-density] [Color Finale, https://docs.colorfinale.com/docs/inspector/film-emulation/] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#gblur]
269. **Grain to hide banding and compression** — grain (noise/dither) masks banding and compression artefacts of 8-bit H.264, especially flat-profile footage. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "noise=alls=8:allf=t+u" out.mp4` (temporal + uniform noise, strength 0-100; ran OK locally). [Garret Harkawik, https://www.garretharkawik.com/how-to-add-grain-to-online-video] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#noise]
270. **Grain on YouTube** — interframe compression is designed to kill grain, so it will change; uploading at 1440p or 4K (up-rezzing 1080p) gets a higher bitrate and keeps more of it. Here: scale the grained master to 2560x1440 before upload. [Wolfcrow, https://wolfcrow.com/the-best-settings-in-davinci-resolve-to-upload-your-film-to-youtube-or-vimeo/, 2023-11-15] [RGrain, https://www.rgrain.com/tutorials/retain-the-film-grain-structure-on-vimeo-and-youtube, 2020-02-13]
271. **Sharpening is local contrast** — too much contrast around edges gives visible halos; start from a clean source with a small radius and restrained amount, check high-contrast edges and motion flicker. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "unsharp=5:5:0.6:5:5:0.0" out.mp4` (luma only; ran OK locally) or `cas=strength=0.4` (ran OK locally). [Digital Anarchy, https://blog.digitalanarchy.com/video-editing-plugins/sharpening-video-footage/] [VideoUpscaler, https://videoupscaler.dev/guides/how-to-sharpen-video, 2026-08-18] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#unsharp] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#cas]
272. **Sharpen at export size, locally** — sharpen after upscaling, only the soft subject area if possible, and review at the final size and on a phone screen; sharpening and contrast also make YouTube compression artefacts stand out more. Here: sharpen last, after `scale`. [CapCut, https://www.capcut.com/create/sharpening-vs-clarity-for-video-and-image-enhancement, 2026-06-12] [r/VideoEditing, https://www.reddit.com/r/VideoEditing/comments/10jri7l/sharpening_and_contrast_correction_on_video_makes/, 2023-01-23]
273. **Deband** — banding comes from too few code values in gradients (8-bit, dark scenes), earlier compression or 8-bit conversion without dither; a debander smooths the steps and adds dither, and encoding at a higher bit depth also helps. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "deband,noise=alls=4:allf=t+u" out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [Encode Wiki, https://encode.wiki/tools/filtering/deband] [mediacodec.dev, https://mediacodec.dev/wiki/bit-depth-and-banding/] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#deband]
274. **gradfun is for playback only** — ffmpeg's `gradfun` interpolates and dithers 8-bit banding, but lossy compression after it loses the dither and brings the bands back. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf "gradfun=strength=1.2:radius=16" out.mp4` (ran OK locally), but prefer `deband` + grain for files you export. [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#gradfun]
275. **Verify before and after** — capture a three-frame contact sheet before and after the treatment, compare them and stop when clearly better; if it isn't clearly better, keep the source. Here: `hyperframes snapshot <project> --frames 3 --no-end --describe false --output snapshots/treatment-after`. [repo: .claude/skills/media-use/references/media-treatments.md]
