# Audio, SFX and music skills (#276-#380)

Dialogue cleanup, mixing and loudness, sound design and music editing for video, each with its source.
Researched 2026-10-24 via agent-reach (Exa) web search. Every ffmpeg command below was run once on a test clip
(ffmpeg 6.1.1, testsrc2 + sine/pink-noise test audio) and is marked "(ran OK locally)".

## Dialogue & cleanup

276. **Record room tone** — capture 30-60 s of the location's "silence" (everyone quiet) at every recording place, because it is the glue for every later dialogue fix. [iZotope, https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/basics-of-room-tone-audio-editing, 2021-10-13] [ACX, https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/check-your-production-before-you-wreck-your-production, 2017-09-22]
277. **Fill edit gaps with room tone** — when cutting dialogue leaves holes, lay clean room tone under them so the background does not pop in and out with each line. [Frame.io Workflow Guide, https://workflow.frame.io/guide/roomtone]
278. **Choose clean room tone** — it must contain only the room (no cloth rustle, car horn, breath), and pieces shorter than about 10 frames are hard to use and sound looped. [Frame.io Workflow Guide, https://workflow.frame.io/guide/roomtone]
279. **Remove a click between words by hand** — mark it, cut it out, drop matching room tone into the hole with tiny crossfades, and leave a bit of the original reverb tail so the fill does not bump. [Waves, https://www.waves.com/audio-repair-101-clicks-crackles-pops, 2018-05-03]
280. **Cut on zero crossings** — a cut away from a zero crossing makes a discontinuous waveform that clicks; move the edit to the nearest zero crossing. [Music Technology Tips, https://www.musictechnologytips.co.uk/knowledge/audio-editing] [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/removing-clicks-crossfades-sonar]
281. **Micro-fade every cut** — from about 1 ms upward each doubling of fade length lowers the splice click by ~6 dB, and a 10 ms fade puts it ~61.5 dB below the tone; ~30 ms in/out is an inaudible click fix. Here: `afade=t=in:d=0.03` at clip starts (ran OK locally). [Lukas Wojcik, https://www.lukaswojcik.com/blog/en/music-production/crossfades-cuts-click-splice-level-hole-fade-length-measured/, 2026-09-25] [Toolbox365, https://www.toolbox365.net/tutorials/audio-fade-duration-curves-click-prevention/, 2024-05-12] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade]
282. **Crossfade length by material** — a few milliseconds hides a hard edit in speech, while a music transition usually wants one to two seconds. Here: `acrossfade=d=0.5:c1=qsin:c2=qsin` (ran OK locally). [Descript, https://www.descript.com/blog/article/crossfade-audio-what-crossfade-is-and-how-to-edit-it, 2022-08-25] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossfade]
283. **Equal-power crossfade** — two linear fades can dip in level mid-crossfade; equal-power ("cosine") curves avoid the surge or dip, and an edit inside a sustained note needs more than ~250 ms (typical edits 20-50 ms). Here: `c1=qsin:c2=qsin` in `acrossfade`. [Audacity Manual, https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/fade_and_crossfade.html, 2026-09-02] [ebrary "Crossfades", https://ebrary.net/300232/education/crossfades]
284. **High-pass the voice** — start a high-pass near 80 Hz and sweep up until the voice thins, then back off (typically 80-150 Hz for men, a little higher for women); it removes rumble, AC/fan noise and proximity boom. Here: `highpass=f=80` (ran OK locally). [Avid, https://www.avid.com/resource-center/how-to-eq-vocals, 2024-10-20] [Music Guy Mixing, https://www.musicguymixing.com/voice-eq/, 2024-09-04] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#highpass]
285. **Male vs female voice EQ map** — male: cut rumble <80 Hz and mud ~220 Hz, lift presence ~3 kHz and air >9 kHz; female: rumble <110 Hz, mud ~280 Hz, harshness ~7.5 kHz, presence ~4.2 kHz, air >10 kHz; moves of 1.5-3 dB. Here: `equalizer=f=3000:t=q:w=1:g=2,equalizer=f=250:t=q:w=1:g=-3` (ran OK locally). [Airtape Studio, https://airtape.co/tools/voice-eq] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#equalizer]
286. **Cut mud and boxiness** — pull 200-400 Hz down 2-4 dB for a thick voice, and find boxiness (300-600 Hz) with "boost, sweep, cut". [Violet Recording, https://violetrecording.com/how-to-eq-vocals-in-logic-pro/, 2026-08-06]
287. **Presence and air** — a wide 1-3 dB boost between 3 and 5 kHz carries consonants and intelligibility, plus a gentle high shelf above ~10-12 kHz for air; too much gets harsh. [Violet Recording, https://violetrecording.com/how-to-eq-vocals-in-logic-pro/, 2026-08-06]
288. **Order: denoise, EQ, then loudness** — remove noise before EQ (a presence boost also boosts hiss) and normalize loudness last (EQ changes level); EQ cannot remove echo. [Airtape Studio, https://airtape.co/tools/voice-eq]
289. **Dialogue compression ratio** — keep dialogue ratios around 2-2.5:1, max 3:1; Blackmagic lists 2.5:1-3:1 as common, with the threshold set to the actual input. Here: `acompressor=threshold=-18dB:ratio=2.5:attack=5:release=300:makeup=2` (ran OK locally). [Ronak Parmar, https://ronakparmar.com/compression-for-dialogues/] [DaVinci Resolve Club, https://davinciresolveclub.com/fairlight-compressor-dialogue/, 2026-08-04] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acompressor]
290. **Gentle dialogue preset** — a tutor's start point: threshold -18 dB, ratio 2.5-3:1, attack 5 ms, release 336 ms, knee 2, output +4 dB. [Vittorio Toscani (Univ. of Lincoln blog), https://audiovittorio.blogs.lincoln.ac.uk/2018/12/11/dialogue-compression/, 2018-12-11]
291. **Clip gain before compression** — fix big clip-gain jumps first; use makeup gain only to match the bypassed level, and back off if room noise starts breathing. [DaVinci Resolve Club, https://davinciresolveclub.com/fairlight-compressor-dialogue/, 2026-08-04]
292. **Manual de-essing first** — lower individual "ess" sounds with clip gain or automation before a de-esser; it often sounds more natural. [iZotope, https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/the-dos-and-donts-of-de-essing, 2025-05-07]
293. **De-esser frequency** — most "s/sh" energy is 5-8 kHz (default 7 kHz); try 4-5 kHz for deeper male voices and 8-10 kHz for bright voices; Waves' "Male Ess" preset sits at 4.5 kHz. [Adobe After Effects Help, https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/desktop/apply-effects-and-animation-presets/list-of-effects/audio-effects.html] [Waves DeEsser manual, https://assets.wavescdn.com/pdf/plugins/deesser.pdf]
294. **De-esser amount and place** — 3-6 dB of reduction is usually enough (more sounds like a lisp); put it early in the chain, before boosting EQ. Here: `deesser=i=0.4:m=0.5:f=0.5` (ran OK locally). [Peak-Studios, https://www.peak-studios.de/en/de-esser/] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#deesser]
295. **Gentle noise reduction** — learn the profile from ~2 s of silence in the same recording and stop at 6-9 dB of reduction; beyond ~8 dB the processing becomes audible (metallic word endings, washed-out tone). [Digital Mixing, https://digital-mixing.com/en/features/18762-voice-in-a-video-edit-the-processing-chain-most-editors-skip-12-09-2026/, 2026-09-12] [344 Audio, https://www.344audio.com/post/article-noise-reduction-how-much-is-too-much]
296. **afftdn in light passes** — start at `afftdn=nr=10:nf=-30` and raise only if needed; maximum settings give robotic "underwater" speech. Here: `afftdn=nr=10:nf=-30` (ran OK locally). [pproenca/dot-skills, https://github.com/pproenca/dot-skills/blob/HEAD/skills/.experimental/audio-voice-recovery/references/noise-avoid-overprocessing.md] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afftdn]
297. **Non-local-means denoise** — ffmpeg's `anlmdn` reduces broadband noise with the Non-Local Means algorithm, an alternative to `afftdn`. Here: `anlmdn=s=0.0001` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#anlmdn]
298. **Mains hum notch** — notch the hum fundamental (50 Hz in most countries, 60 Hz in the Americas) and then its harmonics; a Q around 4 is narrow, lower it if the hum wanders. Here: `bandreject=f=50:width_type=q:w=4,bandreject=f=100:width_type=q:w=4` (ran OK locally). [Audacity Manual, https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/notch_filter.html] [Soundscaper Handbook, https://soundscaper.org/docs/guides/cleaning-up/remove-mains-hum-with-a-notch-filter/] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#bandreject]
299. **De-reverb is the hardest fix** — reverb is correlated with the voice, so it is harder than hiss, noise or hum; ML dialogue de-reverb separates the reverb and lowers only that part. [Digital Mixing, https://digital-mixing.com/en/features/18762-voice-in-a-video-edit-the-processing-chain-most-editors-skip-12-09-2026/, 2026-09-12] [iZotope RX 10 Help, https://docs.izotope.com/rx10/en/dialogue-de-reverb/index.html]
300. **Repo voice cleaner** — `clean_voice.py` keeps only the person's voice (BS-RoFormer vocals + Mel-RoFormer denoise, optional de-reverb) and puts it back into the video as clean.mp4. [repo: library/README.md] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Voice and songs with words"]
301. **clean_voice.py measured results** — on a 26 s test, BS-RoFormer took pauses from -28 dB to -106 dB (SNR 5.7 -> 21.9 dB, ~8 min); `--fast` gave -69 dB / 18 dB in 43 s; a singing background song stays as "voice". [repo: CLAUDE.md "Voice and songs with words"]
302. **Use the owner's existing voice** — if a supplied video already has speech, clean that voice instead of generating a new one (skill `clean-video-voice`). [repo: CLAUDE.md "Voice and songs with words"]
303. **Declip with headroom** — lower the level before de-clipping because rebuilt peaks rise above 0 dB; `adeclip` threshold can be lowered for heavy distortion. Here: `volume=-3dB,adeclip` (ran OK locally). [salivity ffmpeg article, https://salivity.github.io/ffmpeg/article/repair-audio-clipping-with-f-fmpeg-adeclip-filter] [iZotope, https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/repairing-a-distorted-audio-track, 2026-06-15] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#adeclip]
304. **Declick and processing order** — run from least to most aggressive: high-pass, then `adeclick`, then noise reduction. Here: `adeclick` (ran OK locally). [pproenca/dot-skills, https://github.com/pproenca/dot-skills/blob/HEAD/skills/.experimental/audio-voice-recovery/references/noise-avoid-overprocessing.md] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#adeclick]
305. **Lav rustle needs special tools** — rustle is non-stationary and overlaps speech, so ordinary de-noise fails; ML de-rustle exists, and clipping the lav to cotton/wool (not synthetics/silk) or taping it reduces rustle. [iZotope Tech Blog, https://techblog.izotope.com/2017/04/24/derustle-removing-lavalier-microphone-noise-with-deep-learning/, 2017-04-24]
306. **Wind noise** — wind hitting the capsule usually cannot be repaired and must be cut and replaced; De-wind tools handle intermittent light-moderate gusts, constant wind needs spectral de-noise. [Audio-Technica, https://www.audio-technica.com/en-us/support/basic-audio-techniques-for-video-lavalier-microphone-windscreens] [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/rx6/, 2018-07-26]
307. **Plosive pops** — a dynamic EQ cut near 150 Hz that only engages on the pop (3-5 dB) tames "p" bursts while keeping body. [Music Guy Mixing, https://www.musicguymixing.com/voice-eq/, 2024-09-04]
308. **Lower breaths, don't delete them** — removing breaths completely often sounds unnatural; bring them down, and expect de-breath plugins to miss some breaths or hit words. [Sound On Sound, https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/podcast-dialogue-editing-cubase] [Podcast Engineering School, https://podcastengineeringschool.com/why-i-rarely-use-de-breath-plugins/]
309. **Fix a constant A/V offset** — find which way it is off, check stream start times with ffprobe, then delay the video with `-itsoffset` and stream copy. Here: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -itsoffset 0.1 -i in.mp4 -map 1:v -map 0:a -c copy out.mp4` (ran OK locally). [FFmpeg Cookbook, https://ffmpeg-cookbook.com/en/articles/audio-video-sync/, 2026-04-08] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Advanced-options]
310. **Delay early audio** — when sound arrives before the action, shift the audio later. Here: `adelay=100:all=1` (ran OK locally). [FFmpeg Cookbook, https://ffmpeg-cookbook.com/en/articles/audio-video-sync/, 2026-04-08] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#adelay]

## Mixing & loudness

311. **Split D, M, E tracks** — group the timeline into dialogue, effects and music (one dialogue track per speaker/location; whooshes/hits/risers on their own tracks) before mixing. [Frame.io Insider, https://blog.frame.io/2021/08/16/premiere-pro-audio-mixing-basics/, 2021-08-16]
312. **Element level table** — start points: dialogue peaks -6 to -3 dBFS, music under dialogue -18 to -24 dBFS, music alone -12 to -9, sound design -12 to -6, ambience/room tone -30 to -24 dBFS. [VideoEditingTips, https://videoediting.tips/en/techniques/audio/mixing-basics]
313. **Music under voice** — keep music 15-25 dB below the voice while someone talks and raise it in the pauses; under a voice choose instrumentals (lyrics compete with speech). [RFTracks, https://rftracks.com/guides/how-to-choose-background-music, 2026-10-17] [Versely, https://www.versely.studio/blog/your-music-ducking-pumps-instead-of-breathing]
314. **Duck depth and timing** — 9-12 dB of gain reduction with attack under ~300 ms and release of several hundred ms; one patch: attack 20 ms, release 500 ms, range 10 dB, ratio 4:1; past 12 dB the clash is spectral, go to EQ. [Versely, https://www.versely.studio/blog/your-music-ducking-pumps-instead-of-breathing]
315. **Don't over-duck** — start with 6-12 dB, attack 30-80 ms, release 250-700 ms; dropping music 15-20 dB on every line makes the automation audible. [CapCut, https://www.capcut.com/create/audio-ducking-for-clear-dialogue-in-video, 2026-08-11]
316. **Release decides the gaps** — in a measured `sidechaincompress` test (ratio 4:1, attack 5 ms) every release ducked ~10 dB during words; in 100 ms word gaps music sat at -5.4 dB with 250 ms release and -8.1 dB with 500 ms. Here: `[music][voice]sidechaincompress=threshold=0.05:ratio=4:attack=20:release=500` (ran OK locally). [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-ducking-music] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sidechaincompress]
317. **Duck the music, not the master** — put the keyed compressor on the music (or music bus) so SFX and voice are not ducked too; baseline 4:1, 10 ms attack, 200 ms release, no makeup gain. [SonusGearFlow, https://sonusgearflow.com/musicproduction/sidechain-compression-for-podcast-and-spoken-word, 2026-02-04]
318. **Automation vs sidechain** — a drawn volume lane is predictable; a sidechain is automatic but pumps when set hard; in HyperFrames start voice at `data-volume` 1.0, music alone 0.7-0.85, music under voice 0.18-0.3, effects during speech 0.25 max. [Motion Cheat Code, https://motioncheatcode.com/glossary/ducking/]
319. **Repo ducking tool** — `voice_over_music.py` places voice lines at film times, ducks the bed 8 dB under the voice and sets the voice 5.5 dB over the bed. [repo: library/README.md]
320. **Repo-tested reel levels** — music 1.0, impact-bass 0.35-0.4, whoosh 0.6-0.8, ring 0.5; at 0.8-0.9 impacts were 8 dB over the music; target about -16 LUFS. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Client phone-video edits"]
321. **Platform loudness targets** — YouTube, TikTok and Instagram -14 LUFS integrated at -1 dBTP; podcasts -16 LUFS stereo / -19 mono. [Level Rebel, https://levelrebel.net/specs/] [Video Volume Booster, https://videovolumebooster.com/tools/lufs-standards]
322. **Targets disagree, know the range** — APU lists Social Media (TikTok/Reels) at -16 LUFS, EBU R128 broadcast -23, ATSC A/85 -24, Netflix -27 LUFS. [APU Software, https://apu.software/loudness-standards/]
323. **YouTube only turns down** — YouTube normalizes loud uploads down but does not raise quiet ones, so a quiet mix stays quiet; Facebook targets -16 LUFS. [Video Volume Booster, https://videovolumebooster.com/tools/lufs-standards]
324. **Read the right meter** — momentary (400 ms), short-term (3 s), integrated (whole programme, gated), LRA and true peak; LUFS is K-weighted (less sensitive to lows, more above ~2 kHz). [iZotope, https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/mastering-for-streaming-platforms, 2025-07-23]
325. **True peak headroom** — the reconstructed waveform can exceed sample peaks (inter-sample peaks), and lossy re-encoding can clip a 0 dBFS file, so aim for -1 dBTP. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/loudness-and-lufs]
326. **loudnorm defaults** — single-pass `loudnorm` aims at -24 LUFS, -2 dBTP, LRA 7 and only estimates as it goes, so always set I/TP/LRA. Here: `loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1:LRA=11:print_format=json` (ran OK locally). [FFmpeg Cookbook, https://ffmpeg-cookbook.com/en/articles/loudness-normalization/, 2026-04-08] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#loudnorm]
327. **Two-pass loudnorm** — pass 1 measures (print_format=json), pass 2 feeds measured_I/TP/LRA/thresh and offset back with `linear=true` to avoid dynamic compression. Here: on the test clip pass 2 landed at exactly -14.0 LUFS (ran OK locally). [FFmpeg Cookbook, https://ffmpeg-cookbook.com/en/articles/loudness-normalization/, 2026-04-08] [AnimeThemes Wiki, https://www.animethemes.moe/wiki/encoding/workflow, 2022-02-15] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#loudnorm]
328. **Measure the render** — check the final file with `ebur128` (integrated LUFS and true peak) and per-0.5 s numpy RMS. Here: `ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -` (ran OK locally). [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#ebur128] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Client phone-video edits"]
329. **Verify hits with HyperFrames** — `npx hyperframes audio <file>` measures hits, loudness and silence, so sound placement can be checked without listening. [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
330. **Dialogue mono and centred** — dialogue is edited in mono and panned centre even in stereo deliveries; music and ambiences are stereo. Here: `-ac 1` for a voice stem, or `pan=mono|c0=0.5*c0+0.5*c1` (both ran OK locally). [April Tucker, https://apriltucker.com/dialog-editing-part-6/] [DV Info Net, https://www.dvinfo.net/forum/all-things-audio/322567-i-dont-understand-fundamentals-stereo-please-explain-print.html, 2009-08-25] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#pan]
331. **Check mono fold** — phones and smart speakers play mono; folding (L+R)/sqrt2 costs nothing for a centred voice, ~3 dB for unrelated channels and everything for out-of-phase content. [winberg.ie, https://winberg.ie/tools/mono-compatibility/] [Reddit r/editors, https://www.reddit.com/r/editors/comments/u8wj05/talking_heads_web_videos_still_panning_dx_mono/, 2022-04-21]
332. **Three-system check** — check on headphones, laptop and phone and fix only what is wrong on more than one; laptop speakers have almost nothing below 200 Hz, so hum is missed there. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/mixing-on-what-you-have]
333. **HyperFrames audio lanes** — every `<audio>` needs `data-duration` equal to the clip length, and overlapping clips need different `data-track-index` lanes. [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
334. **Fade-out after the last word** — an `<audio data-fade-out>` must start after the last spoken word or it swallows the final line; place lines in numpy because ffmpeg 6.1 `adelay` after `concat`+`atempo` silently fails. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Voice and songs with words"]

## Sound design & SFX

335. **Four sound families** — isolated everyday sounds, specialty (invented) effects, Foley performed to picture, and ambience; every scene needs background ambience or it sounds unnatural. [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/sfx-for-video.html]
336. **Ambience bed recipe** — a ~30 s loop of the right place on its own track for the whole scene, loop points crossfaded by half a second, set around -40 to -30 dBFS; if muting it makes the scene feel dead, the level is right. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/effects-and-ambience]
337. **Spot what is seen** — add sounds for visible events the viewer is looking at (cup set down in frame), not background walkers; a 5-minute piece usually needs 15-30 such sounds. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/effects-and-ambience]
338. **Duck ambience under close dialogue** — loop 30-60 s of room tone with gentle crossfades, low-pass it to sit under dialogue and dip it when close dialogue plays. [Krotos Studio, https://krotos.studio/blog/what-is-sound-design-in-video-editing, 2026-07-03]
339. **Layer, but not too much** — one punch = smack + weighty impact + cloth rustle, but too many layers or overlapping frequencies make design weaker. [Pro Sound Effects, https://blog.prosoundeffects.com/sound-layering]
340. **Foley sync grid** — sync footsteps and props on a +/-1/2-frame grid and judge sync with a couple of seconds of pre-roll. [A Sound Effect, https://www.asoundeffect.com/ultimate-foley-editing-guide/]
341. **Foley from household props** — towel waved fast or cardboard flicked for whooshes, spatula on a tin lid for hits, crumpled paper/zips for texture; record close and at 30-60 cm. [Krotos Studio, https://krotos.studio/blog/objects-for-movie-foley-sounds, 2026-08-24]
342. **Top, tail and micro-fade SFX** — trim silence and noise from each effect's start/end and add few-millisecond fades to every clip to stop pops. [SFX Engine, https://sfxengine.com/blog/foley-sound-effects-tutorial, 2026-01-21]
343. **Kill lead-in silence** — a sound placed on the right frame still lands late if its file starts with silence; zoom in and align the attack. Here: `silenceremove=start_periods=1:start_threshold=-50dB` trims the lead-in (ran OK locally). [Woven Video, https://www.woven.video/guides/add-sound-effects-to-video] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#silenceremove]
344. **Every SFX answers a visible moment** — whoosh on a cut, pop on a word, ding on a reveal; if you can't point to the frame, cut the sound. [Woven Video, https://www.woven.video/guides/add-sound-effects-to-video]
345. **Whoosh timing** — slide the whoosh so its loudest point sits on the first frame of the new shot; a 0.6 s whoosh peaking halfway starts 9 frames early at 30 fps; pan it with the on-screen motion. [Opus Sounds Directory, https://opussounds.directory/blog/whoosh-and-transition-sound-effects-for-video-editing, 2026-10-06]
346. **Impact exactly on the frame** — hits land on the hard cut, title or reveal frame; even 50 ms early reads as sloppy. [Zella Blog, https://zellahq.com/blog/sound-design-at-cuts-whoosh-impact-riser/, 2026-07-19]
347. **Riser before, then stop dead** — a riser runs 1-3 s into the moment, swells, and stops abruptly where the moment lands; the stop is the effect. [Zella Blog, https://zellahq.com/blog/sound-design-at-cuts-whoosh-impact-riser/, 2026-07-19] [YouTubeSFX, https://youtubesfx.com/guides/how-to-add-sound-effects-to-youtube-videos/, 2026-02-20]
348. **SFX level** — keep effects around -6 dB relative to speech (4-8 dB below dialogue); roll sub-bass off whooshes and tame harsh clicks at 3-5 kHz. [Zella Blog, https://zellahq.com/blog/sound-design-at-cuts-whoosh-impact-riser/, 2026-07-19] [Pippit, https://www.pippit.ai/blog/capcut-alternative/how-to-add-sound-effects-on-capcut, 2026-04-08]
349. **Five SFX kinds with lengths** — whoosh 0.3-0.8 s, hit 0.5-1.5 s (loudest briefly), pop/click <0.3 s, riser 1-3 s, ambience the whole scene; not on every cut, rotate 2-3 variants. [cut.express, https://cut.express/blog/sound-effects-for-youtube-videos, 2026-10-15]
350. **Bundled SFX quirks** — the `riser` file crests at 3.47 s and is silent after 4.5 s (use `data-media-start`), `error` starts 0.6 s in and needs `data-volume` ~2.5, `whoosh`==`whoosh-short`, `click`==`click-soft`. [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
351. **Where the repo SFX are** — 19 effects (whoosh, pop, impact-bass, riser, sparkle, ping, glitch ...) ship in `.claude/skills/media-use/audio/assets/sfx/`; own `phone-ring.wav` and `wheel-clicks-13-steps.wav` live in `library/sfx/`. [repo: library/README.md]
352. **J-cut and L-cut sound bridges** — J-cut: the next scene's audio starts before its picture; L-cut: the previous audio carries over the new picture; both smooth scene changes better than a dissolve. [Wikipedia "J cut", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_cut] [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/l-and-j-cut.html] [Filmmakers Academy, https://www.filmmakersacademy.com/glossary/audio-bridge/, 2021-07-16]
353. **Silence as a tool** — deliberately removing music (and even ambience) focuses attention on a performance or revelation; "silence has a sound", built from subtle winds, drones and tones. [parker.mov Editologica, https://www.parker.mov/tools/editologica/editing-with-silence] [Jonny Elwyn, https://jonnyelwyn.co.uk/film-and-video-editing/better-editing-through-sound-design/, 2018-10-18]

## Music editing

354. **Know music's jobs** — it sets emotional register, paces the edit, bridges cuts, builds and releases, and marks section changes; music doing none of these is decoration. [Lumira Studio, https://www.lumirastudio.com/blog/insights/guides/best-music-for-video-production/, 2024-02-01]
355. **Mood first, in one word** — name the scene's emotion (calm, tense, uneasy, triumphant) before searching; that one word removes most options. [Duende Sounds, https://duendesounds.com/choose-music-that-fits-your-edit/, 2026-06-02]
356. **Tempo bands** — 60-90 BPM slow vlogs/emotional, 90-120 talking head/tutorial/corporate, 120-150 montages/sports/ads/short videos. [RFTracks, https://rftracks.com/guides/how-to-choose-background-music, 2026-10-17] [Lumira Studio, https://www.lumirastudio.com/blog/insights/guides/best-music-for-video-production/, 2024-02-01]
357. **Find the BPM** — first trust the file/product page, then tap for 15+ s, then a detector; detectors often report double or half (170 -> 85), and 1 BPM off is a full beat off after about a minute. [beatmotion, https://beatmotion.io/blog/cut-music-video-to-the-beat, 2026-10-13]
358. **How detectors work** — they build an onset envelope and find the strongest repeating interval; a 70 BPM groove can read as 140 (metrical ambiguity), and steady-beat music is usually within 1-2 BPM. [BPM Detector, https://bpmdetector.io/] [Realtime BPM Analyzer, https://www.realtime-bpm-analyzer.com/]
359. **Bar length math** — a 4/4 bar lasts 240 / BPM seconds (60 / tempo x beats per bar); the repo's 72 BPM tracks have 3.333 s bars, so cut scenes on multiples of 3.333 s. [MixClap, https://www.mixclap.com/en/blog/where-to-cut-a-song-clean-transition, 2026-08-04] [MediaScaleUp, https://mediascaleup.com/post/editing-music-to-length-the-decisions-that-matter-audio, 2026-08-07] [repo: library/README.md]
360. **Cut on the downbeat** — the downbeat's burst of kick, bass and chord masks a splice; place the cut a few ms before the transient so the attack stays intact. [MediaScaleUp, https://mediascaleup.com/post/editing-music-to-length-the-decisions-that-matter-audio, 2026-08-07]
361. **Cut at phrase ends** — music is built in 4-bar phrases grouped into 16/32-beat sections; cut at the end of a phrase, at a zero crossing, with a 5-20 ms fade. [MixClap, https://www.mixclap.com/en/blog/where-to-cut-a-song-clean-transition, 2026-08-04]
362. **Join look-alike moments** — the downbeat of chorus 2 and chorus 3 are nearly identical, so cutting between them is undetectable; verse straight into outro is risky. [MediaScaleUp, https://mediascaleup.com/post/editing-music-to-length-the-decisions-that-matter-audio, 2026-08-07]
363. **Backtime the ending** — keep the composer's start and real ending (button/sting) and hide the edit in the middle instead of fading out. [MediaScaleUp, https://mediascaleup.com/post/editing-music-to-length-the-decisions-that-matter-audio, 2026-08-07] [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/cap-your-music-tracks-for-a-smooth-ending/, 2011-09-10]
364. **If the ending join sounds wrong, move back 4 bars** — attach the final bar at the end of a progression; if unnatural, cut 4 bars earlier, and use a short crossfade if a click remains. [RouteNote, https://licensing.routenote.com/blog/how-to-sync-music-to-visuals/, 2026-03-30]
365. **Never fade mid-phrase** — either fade over 2-4 s or cut so the track's natural ending meets the video's end. [RFTracks, https://rftracks.com/guides/how-to-choose-background-music, 2026-10-17]
366. **Fade lengths by use** — short-video BGM fade in 0.3-0.5 s, out 0.5-1 s; YouTube long video 1-2 s in, 2-3 s out; fades over ~3 s sound natural with log/qsin curves. Here: `afade=t=out:st=2:d=1:curve=qsin` (ran OK locally). [Toolbox365, https://www.toolbox365.net/tutorials/audio-fade-duration-curves-click-prevention/, 2024-05-12] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#afade]
367. **Shorten by cutting the middle on beats** — remove time from the middle and close the gap on the nearest beat at a section change; it does not reduce quality. [Audjust, https://www.audjust.com/tools/how-to-edit-a-song-to-make-it-shorter]
368. **Listen without picture** — play the finished music edit with the picture off; joins the visuals hid become audible. [MediaScaleUp, https://mediascaleup.com/post/editing-music-to-length-the-decisions-that-matter-audio, 2026-08-07]
369. **Few, well-chosen hit points** — mark only key actions; too many markers make the music needlessly complicated. [MusicTech, https://musictech.com/tutorials/logic-tutorial-producing-music-to-picture-part-two-working-with-hit-points/, 2014-11-19]
370. **Spot sync points** — in a spotting pass decide each cue's in/out and the frames where music must change (door slam, reveal). [Vectree, https://vectree.io/pdf/c/film-spotting-technicalities]
371. **Cut energy by song section** — verse: hold shots, cut on phrase ends; build: shorten cuts progressively; chorus/drop: cut hardest on the beat; use one locked reference mix so cuts never drift. [Hanna Eng, https://www.hanna-eng.com/guides/monter-un-clip-musical/, 2026-06-03]
372. **Picture 1-2 frames before the beat** — cut on downbeats in calm parts, every bar in builds, every beat only in short bursts at drops. [showtime docs, https://faviovazquez.github.io/showtime/docs/workflows/music-video.html]
373. **Score first, reveal on the drop** — pick the track first, mark intro/build/drop timecodes, put hard cuts on transients and the reveal on the drop frame, then add a riser into the drop and an impact on the reveal. [iart-ai ad-video-skills, https://github.com/iart-ai/ad-video-skills/blob/main/skills/launch-video/references/launch-structure.md]
374. **Repo bar-cut recipe** — pick a BPM and cut on bars (72 BPM = every 3.333 s), with clip speeds chosen per beat. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a professional edit"]
375. **CC0 vs public domain** — CC0 waives all rights as far as law allows (free for any use, no permission); the Public Domain Mark labels works already free of copyright. [Creative Commons, https://creativecommons.org/public-domain/]
376. **Owner's no-credit rule** — background music must need NO credit: CC0, public domain or made by us; CC BY (YouTube CC filter, incompetech, most FMA/Jamendo) is not acceptable, and copyrighted songs are never downloaded. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Environment facts"]
377. **Ready CC0 library** — `library/music/cc0` holds 29 OpenGameArt tracks verified CC0-only per page (background, cinematic, piano, desi/eastern), moods in its README. [repo: library/README.md]
378. **CC0 source sites** — sites that aggregate CC0/public-domain music include Freesound, Pixabay, Chosic, Free Music Archive and FreePD; nullrights re-checks each track's CC0 on the source page. [SoundSafari/CC0-1.0-Music, https://github.com/SoundSafari/CC0-1.0-Music] [nullrights, https://nullrights.com/]
379. **Check each track's licence** — FreePD closed in 2025, FMA is not all free, Jamendo/ccMixter mix licences, and one source can hold several licences, so verify per work. [Wikimedia Commons, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Free_media_resources/Music/en] [TrustFM, https://www.trustfm.net/ebooks/CCSources.php, 2026-10-19]
380. **Own music only when asked** — `music_pack.py` makes ACE-Step tracks (-14 LUFS, no credit), but the owner wants CC0 music made by others unless they ask for ours. [repo: library/README.md] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Environment facts"]
