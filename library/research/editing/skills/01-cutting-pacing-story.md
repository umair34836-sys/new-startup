## Cutting

1. **Hard (standard) cut** — switch straight from one shot to the next with no effect; keep most hard cuts inside a scene, because a hard cut from one scene to another can feel jarring. [MasterClass, https://www.masterclass.com/articles/essential-film-cuts, 2020-09-25] [Backstage, https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/types-of-cuts-in-film-75730/, 2023-05-23]
2. **Hard cuts for "cold" dialogue** — plain back-and-forth hard cuts keep the viewer on the conversation and can build tension (Pulp Fiction diner scene), where J/L cuts would feel softer and warmer. [Filmsupply, https://www.filmsupply.com/articles/film-editing-cuts/, 2022-11-09]
3. **Jump cut** — cut between two sequential shots where the camera barely moves but the subject jumps in the frame, to show time moving forward. [MasterClass, https://www.masterclass.com/articles/essential-film-cuts, 2020-09-25] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-editing-transitions-in-film/, 2021-11-28]
4. **Jump cut out dead air in talking heads** — cut pauses, fillers and mistakes instead of reshooting; viewers are used to it and it adds energy. Here: drop a section with `trim`/`atrim` + `setpts` + `concat` (kept 0-1 s and 2-3 s of a 3 s test clip, result 2.0 s) (ran OK locally). [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/jump-cut.html] [Alan Spicer, https://alanspicer.com/why-do-youtubers-jump-cut/, 2021-08-17] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#trim] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#concat]
5. **Make jump cuts look intentional** — a clearly deliberate jump cut with an obvious discontinuity reads as less jarring than a near-match that looks like a mistake. [NYI Constellations, https://www.nyiconstellations.org/articles/issue-no-1-summer-2023/discontinuity-as-prosody-meaning-and-form-of-jump-cuts-on-youtube-1, 2023-11-12]
6. **Jump cut as an aside** — in single-camera confessional videos a jump cut can mark the next shot as an aside, extra context or a qualification of the last line ("the set as a stage"). [Musée Magazine, https://museemagazine.com/features/2019/1/22/why-i-hated-what-i-loved-youtube-and-the-jump-cut, 2019-01-22]
7. **Punch-in on alternate jump cuts** — keep jump cuts short and frequent and add a subtle 105-110% zoom on every other cut so they read as style, not error. Here: wrap the video in a div and tween `scale`/`x`/`y` on the wrapper. [Pristine Podcasts, https://app.pristinepodcasts.com/blog/editing-talking-head-video-podcast] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a "professional" edit"]
8. **Switch angle at each trim** — with a second camera, cut to the other angle where you removed words; angle changes hide internal trims more honestly than B-roll. [Pristine Podcasts, https://app.pristinepodcasts.com/blog/editing-talking-head-video-podcast] [MediaScaleUp, https://mediascaleup.com/post/a-practical-guide-to-interview-and-talking-head-editing-video, 2025-09-12]
9. **Smash cut** — at a crucial moment where a cut is not expected, cut abruptly to a sharply different kind of scene (frenzied to tranquil, pleasant to tense) for drama, comedy or contrast. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smash_cut] [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/smash-cut.html]
10. **Gilligan cut** — a character insists they will never do something, then smash cut to them doing exactly that. [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/smash-cut.html] [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-smash-cut-definition/, 2022-09-25]
11. **Earn the smash** — hold shot A long enough to set its tone, then cut to B with a big change in brightness, loudness or shot size and no dissolve; use smash cuts sparingly, since between similar scenes they do nothing. [Melies, https://melies.co/cinematic-techniques/editing/smash-cut] [Ensemble Docs, https://ensemblewriter.com/docs/transitions-and-cut-to]
12. **Cross-cutting (parallel editing)** — alternate two or more actions in different places to show they happen at once and are linked (Jaws: shark POV vs swimmers). [BBC Bitesize, https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zxdmmnb/revision/3, 2017-01-30] [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-cutting, 2026-09-27]
13. **Speed up the cross-cut** — increasing the rapidity between the two actions may add tension; the length of time between cross-cuts sets the scene's rhythm, and cross-cutting is frequently used at a film's climax. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-cutting, 2026-09-27]
14. **Parallel editing across time** — intercut present and past to draw comparisons or contrasts between the two. [Unsplice, https://www.unsplice.com/what-is-parallel-editing/, 2020-06-30] [Yale Film Analysis, https://web.archive.org/web/20110723024728/http:/classes.yale.edu/film-analysis/htmfiles/editing.htm]
15. **Hidden cut behind a blocked frame** — end shot A when a body or dark object fills the lens and start shot B from the same blocked frame, so it looks like one take. [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/ideas/what-is-an-invisible-cut-in-film.html] [4K Shooters, https://www.4kshooters.net/2022/09/10/how-to-pull-off-a-long-take-with-hidden-cuts/, 2022-09-10]
16. **Whip-pan hidden cut** — end A and start B with a fast pan in the same direction and cut inside the blur. [DRIFF, https://www.driff.ca/diy-film-school/lesson1-the-whip-cut, 2022-02-04] [4K Shooters, https://www.4kshooters.net/2022/09/10/how-to-pull-off-a-long-take-with-hidden-cuts/, 2022-09-10]
17. **Black-out hidden cut** — hide the cut where the camera blacks out completely (1917: going down into the bunker and coming up from it), or where things moving in front of the camera (a cart, a line of soldiers) block the frame completely. [No Film School, https://nofilmschool.com/1917-camera-edits-oner, 2020-02-19]
18. **Cut on action** — when a movement spans two shots, cut during the movement, not before or after it; the eye follows the motion and misses the join. [Video101, http://video101course.com/300editing220.html] [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_on_action]
19. **Overlap the action 2-3 frames** — start the incoming shot 2-3 frames before where the outgoing shot left off; an exact-frame match often reads as a stutter or as slightly late. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/grammar-of-cuts] [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-when-to-cut]
20. **Match frame position across the cut** — keep the moving thing on the same side of frame in both shots (door on the right in the wide stays right in the close). [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/grammar-of-cuts]
21. **Exit/entrance cut** — subject leaves frame in A and enters in B with matching screen direction; Dmytryk: cut when the eyes leave frame and start B 3-5 frames before the eyes re-enter. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_on_action] [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html]
22. **Never cut without a positive reason** — stay on a shot while it best delivers the information; cut only when the new shot's size, composition or performance serves the scene better. [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html]
23. **Save the tightest close-up** — an unneeded close-up weakens later ones; build from wide to over-the-shoulder to close singles as the stakes rise. [Chicago Media Works (Dmytryk), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editing_docdymytryk.html] [Flashboards, https://flashboards.yaroflasher.com/learn/editing/editing-dialogue-scenes/, 2026-09-25]
24. **Match cut** — match visual or sound elements at the end of one scene with the start of the next to carry the viewer across. [MasterClass, https://www.masterclass.com/articles/essential-film-cuts, 2020-09-25] [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_cut, 2026-06-28]
25. **Graphic match** — link two similar but markedly different shots through similar visual elements (2001: bone to spaceship, both white and in roughly the same part of frame; Psycho: drain to eye); plan the shots, then align and colour-match the two frames. [BBC Bitesize, https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zxdmmnb/revision/2, 2017-01-30] [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/match-cut.html]
26. **Split edit** — let audio and picture change at different times so a conversation does not feel like a tennis match. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_edit] [MasterClass, https://www.masterclass.com/articles/essential-film-cuts, 2020-09-25]
27. **J-cut** — bring the next shot's sound in before its picture (the audio edit leads the video edit). [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/l-and-j-cut.html] [EditMentor, https://help.editmentor.com/en/articles/4753447-split-edits-j-cuts-vs-l-cuts, 2022-11-10]
28. **L-cut** — let the current shot's sound run over the next picture, e.g. the speaker's line continues over the listener's face. [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/post-production/cuts-in-film/l-and-j-cut.html] [Apple, https://support.apple.com/guide/final-cut-pro/create-split-edits-ver1632d82c/mac]
29. **Split edits to mix takes** — use the picture from one take with the better-read sound from another, letting picture cuts fall at different places. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_edit]
30. **Offset most dialogue joins** — after placing picture cuts, unlink and drag picture edges earlier/later than sound (J start point 6-20 frames); every join straight sounds like a slideshow. Here: separate `trim` and `atrim` chains cut picture at 2.0 s and sound at 2.5 s, then `concat` (ran OK locally). [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/cutting-a-dialogue-scene] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#atrim]
31. **Start on the speaker, overlap forward** — editor Tom Rolf: never let a line start off-screen; begin on the speaker, then L-cut to whoever reacts. [Starword (Salt), http://www.starword.com/Articles/ReactionTime.pdf]
32. **Smooth section seams** — viewers leave at seams that feel like "a new section starts"; bridge them with L-cuts, continuous music and connecting phrases. [Pixflow, https://pixflow.net/blog/youtube-video-retention-editing/, 2026-07-19]
33. **Cutaway** — leave the main action for a shot of supporting information, then return to it. [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/types-of-editing-transitions-in-film/, 2021-11-28] [Descript, https://www.descript.com/blog/article/guide-to-cutaway-shots-how-to-use-cutaway-shots-in-editing, 2022-04-18]
34. **Insert vs cutaway** — ask "is this detail part of the master shot?": yes = insert (stays inside the scene), no = cutaway (steps outside). [FilmDaft, https://filmdaft.com/what-is-a-cutaway-shot-in-film-definition-examples-uses/, 2025-03-14] [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/cutaways-and-inserts]
35. **Colour shots** — mood shots that are not part of the action (beach, surf, gulls) add feeling to a sequence. [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/f5/7615-the-wonderful-cutaway/, 2000-06-01]
36. **Cutaway as repair** — cover a dead spot, mismatched action or weak performance: cut to hands for two seconds and minutes of interview can go with no visible join. [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/f5/7615-the-wonderful-cutaway/, 2000-06-01] [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/cutaways-and-inserts]
37. **One cutaway per 30 s of interview** — a six-minute piece needs about twelve; they can repeat, but not next to each other. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/cutaways-and-inserts]
38. **Noddies** — place a listener/interviewer reaction shot over the start of the next clip to hide a jump cut. [DV 101 (flylib), https://flylib.com/books/en/4.193.1.36/1/]
39. **Three-point edit for cutaways** — mark in/out on the timeline, mark the in on the B-roll source, overwrite onto the track above. [CineD, https://www.cined.com/editing-cutaways-short-video-tutorial/, 2017-02-13]
40. **Shot-reverse-shot** — cut between setups facing opposite ways for dialogue, usually matching over-the-shoulders or singles. [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/shot-reverse-shot-cutaways-coverage/, 2019-05-13] [Learn About Film, https://learnaboutfilm.com/film-language/sequence/180-degree-rule/, 2013-02-01]
41. **Cut to whoever's reaction tells the story** — bad news often lands harder on the listener's face; staying on one person through the other's lines makes the scene theirs. [Flashboards, https://flashboards.yaroflasher.com/learn/editing/editing-dialogue-scenes/, 2026-09-25]
42. **Cut on the thought** — the best cut point is often not the end of a line but where a thought ends or starts: a blink, a glance away, a breath before an answer, or a visible reaction. [Flashboards, https://flashboards.yaroflasher.com/learn/editing/editing-dialogue-scenes/, 2026-09-25]
43. **Cut just before the blink** — Murch cuts just before an in-character blink, the "I get it" moment where a thought completes. [ProVideo Coalition, https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-bcpc/, 2020-04-08] [The Paris Review, https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2012/02/07/josh-melnick-and-walter-murch-in-conversation/, 2012-02-07]
44. **Eyeline match** — show a character looking off-screen, then cut to what they see, with gaze direction and height matched. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyeline_match] [Flashboards, https://flashboards.yaroflasher.com/learn/editing/eyeline-match/, 2026-09-25]
45. **Match specs for eyeline pairs** — same shot size, distance, focal length, horizon and depth of field in both shots. [StudioBinder, https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-an-eyeline-match/, 2025-02-20]
46. **Eye-trace** — put the next shot's point of interest where the viewer's eye already is; vital when shots are a fraction of a second long. [Derek Lieu, https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/4/6/good-eye-trace-for-smooth-editing, 2020-04-06] [EditMentor, https://help.editmentor.com/en/articles/4891638-eye-trace, 2022-11-11]
47. **Dissolve test for eye-trace** — park on the cut, add a dissolve, reposition or retime until the subjects overlap, then delete the dissolve. [Cut Daily, https://www.cut-daily.com/eye-trace-an-editors-secret-weapon/, 2021-04-13]
48. **Know what pulls the eye** — motion (an actor's movement), position (a character at the frame edge, then a cut to another on the same edge) and a bright or different colour catch the eye; discontinuity hides in a part of the frame the eye is not looking at in that moment. [EditMentor, https://help.editmentor.com/en/articles/4891638-eye-trace, 2022-11-11]
49. **180-degree rule** — keep every setup on one side of the line between two people so A always looks screen-right and B screen-left. [BFI, http://old.bfi.org.uk/education/teaching/cineminis/glossary.html] [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/grammar-of-cuts]
50. **Cross the line safely** — change screen direction with a moving shot that crosses the axis, a POV from the other side, or a head-on shot between. [Queen's FILM 250, https://web.archive.org/web/20050923165131/http:/www.film.queensu.ca/250/250Continuity.html]
51. **30-degree rule** — avoid consecutive shots whose angles differ by less than 30 degrees; each new shot needs a real change in angle or size. [BFI, http://old.bfi.org.uk/education/teaching/cineminis/glossary.html] [Queen's FILM 250, https://web.archive.org/web/20050923165131/http:/www.film.queensu.ca/250/250Continuity.html]
52. **Interview cameras on one side of the eyeline** — a B camera about 30 degrees round on the same side lets angle changes hide trims without the speaker flipping direction. [MediaScaleUp, https://mediascaleup.com/post/a-practical-guide-to-interview-and-talking-head-editing-video, 2025-09-12]
53. **Don't cut between lens-look and off-lens** — cutting from a guest looking into the lens to one looking away (and back) confuses the viewer's role. [Larry Jordan, https://larryjordan.com/articles/better-one-camera-editing-techniques/, 2020-04-05]
54. **Wide, medium, close** — "the wide tells where, the medium tells who, the tight tells what"; reverse it (close first) for a reveal. [Adobe, https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/production/cinematography/camera-shots-and-angles/sequence-shot.html] [LibreTexts, https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Theater_Film_and_Storytelling/Documentary_Culture/13%3A_Filming_Coverage/13.02%3A_Sequencing, 2026-01-13]
55. **Six-shot action sequence** — wide, medium, close on hands, insert of the object, reaction, then a wide of the world after. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/building-a-sequence]
56. **Join shots with "but/therefore"** — a sequence joined by "and then" is a list; join by cause and effect. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/building-a-sequence]
57. **Ripple trim** — shorten/lengthen one clip and move everything after it (Premiere: B); it changes total length and shifts downstream music and titles. [Adobe, https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/premiere-pro/using/trimming-clips.html] [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/trimming-tools]
58. **Roll trim** — move the cut point between two clips without changing total duration (Premiere: N). [Adobe, https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/premiere-pro/using/trimming-clips.html] [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/trimming-tools]
59. **Slip and handles** — slip changes a clip's in/out without moving it on the timeline; roll and slip need handles (unused frames beyond the in/out). [Adobe, https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/premiere-pro/using/trimming-clips.html] [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/trimming-tools]
60. **Lock structure before timed work** — ripple trims push music cut to picture and titles timed to words out of place, so settle structure first. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/trimming-tools]
61. **Separate mechanical and discourse fillers** — "um/uh/er" can always go; "like/you know/I mean" sometimes carry meaning, so check each. [Cubix, https://cubix.design/guides/remove-filler-words-naturally] [Descript, https://www.descript.com/blog/article/the-art-of-filler-word-removal, 2022-08-17]
62. **Numeric silence rule** — cut pauses of 0.5 s or more, keep about 0.12 s of breathing room and snap each cut to a word edge. Here: find them with `silencedetect=noise=-30dB:d=0.5` (it found the 1.0 s gap in a tone-silence-tone test file) (ran OK locally). [Valmera, https://valmera.io/docs/cuts-and-cleanup] [ffmpeg docs, https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#silencedetect]
63. **Shorten pauses, don't delete them** — e.g. bring pauses over 0.4 s down to 0.2 s and leave a fraction of the original gap so the join stays invisible. [Vyra, https://www.usevyra.com/prompts/remove-filler] [Optivox, https://optivox.app/learn/remove-filler-words]
64. **Lower breaths, keep some** — reduce loud breaths rather than delete them all; speech with every breath removed sounds robotic. [Studio Rescue, https://www.studiorescue.net/blog/how-to-edit-out-filler-words-and-breaths-in-reaper/, 2026-08-23]
65. **Leave some fillers in conversation** — scripted pieces can lose all fillers, but interviews keep the ones that mark real thinking. [Descript, https://www.descript.com/blog/article/the-art-of-filler-word-removal, 2022-08-17] [Optivox, https://optivox.app/learn/remove-filler-words]
66. **Cut on breaths, fill with room tone** — move speech cuts into the troughs between phrases and lay a matching room-tone bed under the joins. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/cutting-a-dialogue-scene] [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/article/c3/13514-editing-dirty-little-tricks/, 2008-12-01]
67. **Cut between neighbouring words** — when lifting a spoken line, cut in the gap between its last word and the next line's first; in our test a +0.3 s tail leaked the next line's first word. [repo: CLAUDE.md "Voice and songs with words"]
68. **Screen all the rushes first** — allow at least 2-3 times the footage length to watch, evaluate and take notes. [CCE Editor's Taskbook, https://cceditors.ca/wp-content/uploads/CCE_Editors_Taskbook_EN_v1.0.pdf]
69. **Assembly** — everything usable, in order, no music, transitions or titles; expect 2-4x the target length (a doc guide caps it at 140%). [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/the-assembly-and-the-rough-cut] [New Doc Editing, https://newdocediting.com/stages-of-documentary-post-production/]
70. **Watch the assembly through before touching it** — note where you drift or get confused; those problems become invisible after two days of work. [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-assembly-to-fine-cut]
71. **Rough cut** — fix structure and rough length (remove whole sections, reorder); it can start additive (two takes side by side until the context makes the choice clear) and, though unfinished, should already communicate the key message. [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/editing/workflow/video-editing-workflow-how-to-do-it-properly/, 2019-03-21] [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/the-assembly-and-the-rough-cut]
72. **Fine cut** — after the rough is approved, place and time every shot and cut precisely so every moment lands. [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/editing/workflow/video-editing-workflow-how-to-do-it-properly/, 2019-03-21] [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-assembly-to-fine-cut]
73. **Picture lock** — no more edit changes, so colour, sound and finishing can start; later changes cause rework and cost. [Frame.io, https://workflow.frame.io/guide/editing-stages]
74. **No grade, mix or titles before lock** — every later trim forces a regrade, remix or retime. [Addaly, https://addaly.com/learn/video-editing/the-assembly-and-the-rough-cut]
75. **Duplicate and date timelines** — name versions (e.g. "RadioCut 2-3 noon", "RadioCut EOD 2-3") to experiment without losing a cut. [LibreTexts, https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Theater_Film_and_Storytelling/Documentary_Culture/15%3A_Editing_the_Documentary/15.03%3A_Building_Assemblies, 2026-01-17]

## Pacing & rhythm

76. **Know the shot-length baseline** — Hollywood average shot length fell from about 10 s (1930s-40s) to under 4 s after 2000; a modern baseline is 4-6 s. [Cutting et al. (PMC), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485803/, 2011-09-30] [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_rhythm, 2026-10-03]
77. **Rhythm vs pacing** — rhythm is the timing of each shot (frame to cut on, placement, hold); pacing is the rate at which cuts occur. [Computational Film Analysis with R (Pearlman), https://cfa-with-r.netlify.app/editingii]
78. **Micro and macro pacing** — plan pace within each scene and across the whole film; go above or below the baseline on purpose. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_rhythm, 2026-10-03]
79. **Shot lengths across a film (revised view)** — the earlier finding that every act starts and ends with slightly longer shots was withdrawn by its authors as an artifact of their method; what remained in 143 films (1935-2005): shots are generally longer at the beginning of a film, and in the climax (act 4) shots get shorter and then lengthen again at the end, likely during the epilog. [Cutting, Brunick & DeLong, Projections 6(1), https://jordandelong.com/pubs/2012/RevisedView.pdf, 2012]
80. **Climax shape** — shorter shots and more motion early in the climax, then longer shots and less motion at the very end. [Cutting (PMC), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5256470/, 2016-12-19]
81. **Tension and release** — shape waves of tension and anticipation followed by waves of release; they guide the audience through the plot at the pace the editorial rhythm sets (Pearlman gives a whole chapter to tension, release and synchronization). [Pearlman, Cutting Rhythms (O'Reilly), https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/cutting-rhythms-3rd/9781040351369/xhtml/Ch05.xhtml, 2025-06-02] [Backstage, https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/film-rhythm-editing-guide-77147/, 2024-09-16]
82. **Metric cutting** — cut by absolute shot lengths in a music-like measure, and shorten pieces in proportion to build tension. Here: pick a BPM and cut on bars (72 BPM = every 3.333 s), one shot per bar for music reels. [Eisenstein, Film Form, https://cpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/4/2875/files/2017/01/MethodsOfMontageEisenstein_Sergei_Film_Form_Essays_in_Film_Theory_1977.pdf] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Editing a phone video into a "professional" edit"] [repo: CLAUDE.md "Client phone-video edits"]
83. **Rhythmic cutting** — let the content and movement inside each shot set its length instead of a fixed formula. [Chicago Media Works (Eisenstein), https://www.chicagomediaworks.com/2instructworks/3editing_doc/3editingeisenstein.html] [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/directing/film-history/sergei-eisenstein-the-man-the-method-the-montage/, 2020-10-29]
84. **Tonal and overtonal cutting** — cut by the emotional tone of shots (a sleeping baby = calm), then combine metric, rhythmic and tonal for a layered effect. [University of Denver, https://mysite.du.edu/~lmehran/download/montage.pdf] [Videomaker, https://www.videomaker.com/how-to/directing/film-history/sergei-eisenstein-the-man-the-method-the-montage/, 2020-10-29]
85. **Let it breathe (decompression)** — hold a shot a little longer after emotional moments so the audience can feel what the character feels. [No Film School, https://nofilmschool.com/2017/12/watch-why-its-important-let-your-edit-breathe, 2017-12-10] [parker.mov, https://www.parker.mov/tools/editologica/editorial-breathing-room]
86. **Cut dead air, keep meaningful breaths** — remove ums, fiddling and trailing "yeah", but keep the pause before a reveal and after a payoff, or nothing lands. [Van Pelt Studio, https://vanpelt.studio/learn/editing/cut-silence-keep-breath/]
87. **Add a beat after a dramatic moment** — manufacture a pause (even an extra shot) so the audience can read the reaction and empathise. [Film Editing Pro, https://www.filmeditingpro.com/timing-your-cut-to-enhance-the-drama/, 2023-02-03]
88. **Loud, quiet, loud** — put quiet stretches between high-energy sections so the next hit lands harder ("I let you exhale so I can punch you"). [parker.mov, https://www.parker.mov/tools/editologica/editorial-breathing-room]
89. **Use sudden silence for attention** — when sound suddenly drops out, or a cut lands in a moment of visual stillness, attention spikes and viewers start anticipating what comes next. [Editing Luke, https://www.editingluke.net/breathing-room-editors-embrace-silence/, 2026-09-05]
90. **"Get the air out" pass** — a final tightening pass that shortens pauses (editor Bill Lewthwaite's catchphrase). [Starword (Salt), http://www.starword.com/Articles/ReactionTime.pdf]
91. **Comedy: cut tight after the punchline** — trim silences that don't serve the joke, sometimes clipping the last frames of the final word. [Movie School Free, https://movieschoolfree.com/video-editing-course/digital-video-editing-for-a-comedy/, 2016-01-16]
92. **Comedy: leave laugh room** — give space after a joke so viewers don't miss the next line; it is often a matter of frames. [Movie School Free, https://movieschoolfree.com/video-editing-course/digital-video-editing-for-a-comedy/, 2016-01-16] [LBB Online, https://lbbonline.com/news/cutting-for-comedy-the-art-of-crafting-a-funny-moment, 2024-04-24]
93. **Comedy: biggest laugh last, cut the shoe leather** — arrive late, leave early, cut everything between setup and punchline, and end the scene on its biggest laugh. [PremiumBeat, https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/tips-cutting-comedy-comedians-cars/, 2018-04-17]
94. **Comedy: cut to the reaction** — cutting away to a reaction after a punchline helps the joke land. [Descript, https://www.descript.com/blog/article/learn-how-to-edit-a-comedy-clip-and-make-your-jokes-land, 2022-03-02]
95. **Pattern-interrupt rate: sources disagree** — quoted rates run from a visual change every 3-5 s (entertainment) or 7-10 s (talking head) to one interrupt every 45-90 s, or just 2-3 in a 30 s talking head; an interrupt works because it is rare compared with the baseline. [Editing Machine, https://editingmachine.com/blog/youtube-video-editor-for-retention-beyond-just-cuts, 2026-03-06] [PrePublish, https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-pattern-interrupts, 2026-03-05] [Headroom, https://headroomapp.ai/pattern-interrupts-for-reels]
96. **Reel shot budget** — talking-head reels change something every 1.5-3 s, story/vlog every 3-4 s; nothing static longer than about 4 s. [Reelyze, https://getreelyze.com/guides/reel-pacing-and-cuts, 2026-06-01]
97. **Interrupts must carry meaning** — change frame before sentence, never interrupt during the payoff sentence; meaningless zooms become noise. [Viral Hook Analyzer, https://viralhookanalyzer.com/guides/pattern-interrupt-hooks]
98. **Interrupt, explain, prove, reset** — a 20 s clip can lose attention at about 4, 9 and 15 s; reset the pattern before each drift. [CapCut, https://www.capcut.com/create/pattern-interrupt-technique-short-form-video, 2026-06-12]

## Story & structure

99. **Hook in the first 3 seconds, sound off** — watch only the first 3 s muted: can you tell what it is, who it's for and why to stay? Start mid-action with on-screen text. Here: the first frame is the thumbnail, so put a title or key image on screen at 0 s. [CapCut, https://www.capcut.com/create/short-form-video-hooks, 2026-06-12] [Click Pop, https://www.clickpop.ai/guides/hooks-for-the-first-three-seconds, 2026-10-14] [repo: CLAUDE.md "HyperFrames learnings"]
100. **Cold open: strongest line first** — mark the three strongest sentences in the take and move the best one to frame one: no greeting, no logo, setup as a caption. [Pixel8, https://www.pixel8production.com/blog/how-to-edit-engaging-hooks-first-3-seconds, 2026-07-02] [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_open, 2026-08-13]
101. **In medias res (scene lift)** — open in the middle of the action; show the result first (the cake being sliced), then go back to how it got there. [Derek Lieu, https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2019/10/2/editing-an-engaging-trailer-opening, 2019-10-02] [PrePublish, https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention, 2026-07-05]
102. **State the stakes, then open loops** — say within 15-30 s what the viewer will get, then plant 2-3 unanswered questions through the middle and close each before opening the next. [Infinite Creation, https://infinitecreation.io/tutorial-story-structure] [Asendify, https://www.asendify.co/blog/open-loops-video-editing/, 2026-10-11]
103. **Re-hook every 2-3 minutes** — finish a point, then pivot ("but there's one problem...") so no section resolves into silence. [Pixflow, https://pixflow.net/blog/youtube-video-retention-editing/, 2026-07-19]
104. **Hook, retain, reward** — hook in under 3 s, pay off curiosity in beats through the middle, end with a usable payoff that rewards the watch. [Selfstorming, https://www.selfstorming.com/tools/libraries/frameworks/hook-retain-reward] [aiskillstore (Hormozi), https://github.com/aiskillstore/marketplace/blob/36e07d5e13068e5be64447e8f20b427cf2cbd21a/skills/vyralcontent/viral-hooks/references/hook-archetypes.md]
105. **Three acts with a time budget** — 25/50/25 split (6 min = 90 s / 3 min / 90 s); in a 60 s short: hook 3-5 s, escalation 40-45 s, payoff 10-15 s; a 30 s script holds about 70-85 words. [Austen Menges, https://www.austenmenges.com/blog/three-act-story-structure-editing] [ScreenWeaver, https://www.screenweaver.ai/blog/writing-short-film-tiktok-youtube-shorts-reels, 2026-03-19] [WowReveal, https://wowreveal.com/blog/story-structure-30-second-shorts-hook-turn-payoff]
106. **AIDA and PAS for promos** — AIDA (attention 0-3 s, interest, desire with proof, action) for cold audiences; PAS (problem, agitate about 8 s, solution + CTA) for people who already feel the pain. [AdConvert, https://adconvert.org/blog/how-to-write-video-ad-scripts, 2025-08-05] [Cutroom, https://cutroomai.com/formats/problem-agitate-solve-ad] [AdMove, https://www.admove.ai/blog/how-to-make-a-video-ad-script]
107. **Selects, paper edit, radio edit** — transcribe with timecodes, pull more soundbites than needed into selects/string-outs, order them in a paper cut, then play the audio-only radio edit to prove the story before any picture. [NYFA, https://www.nyfa.edu/student-resources/how-to-edit-an-interview-for-a-documentary/, 2014-05-28] [Medium (Simon Says), https://medium.com/simon-says-transcription/what-are-assembly-edits-and-why-they-are-are-important-f0f17cef114f, 2021-02-25] [PaperCuts, https://papercuts.io/learn/what-is-a-radio-edit]
108. **Show, don't tell with B-roll** — cut to footage of what the speaker describes, enter it on the concrete noun, and keep the face on screen for the hook, emotion and final ask. [Wistia, https://wistia.com/blog/b-roll, 2015-08-11] [Zupum, https://zupum.com/learning-hub]
109. **Kuleshov effect, with a caveat** — the same neutral face reads as hungry, grieving or desiring depending on the shot it is cut against; a 2024 study confirmed it with real film clips, though the original experiment is poorly documented. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuleshov_effect, 2026-09-17] [PLOS One, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0308295, 2024-08-05] [Prince & Hensley, https://doi.org/10.2307/1225144, 1992-01-01]
110. **Murch's Rule of Six** — rank every cut by emotion (51%), story (23%), rhythm (10%), eye-trace (7%), 2D screen plane (5%), 3D space (4%), and when you can't have all six, drop them from the bottom up. [UC Berkeley iSchool, https://blogs.ischool.berkeley.edu/i290-viznarr-s12/the-rule-of-six-walter-murch/] [Web of Stories (Murch), https://www.webofstories.com/play/walter.murch/294] [No Film School, https://nofilmschool.com/2016/11/6-rules-good-cutting-according-oscar-winning-editor-walter-murch, 2016-12-01]
